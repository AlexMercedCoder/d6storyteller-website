## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** **Canis & Felis** is a high-fantasy political thriller built on the fundamental irony of its premise: dogs and cats-creatures whose relationship with each other in the mundane world is defined by reflex conflict, surprising friendship, and profound mutual misunderstanding-have built a civilization around their differences, formalized those differences into complementary systems of governance and magic, and are now in the complicated situation of having developed a grudging mutual dependency they refuse to acknowledge.

The **Canis Compact** runs on honor, loyalty, and collective action. Dog-folk knights genuinely believe in the causes they serve, form Pack Bonds that become the most important relationships of their lives, and organize their cities around communal spaces-great hall courts, training grounds, shared feast halls. Their magic is bound magic: oaths of protection, packbinding spells, banishment circles. Their weakness is their certainty: a dog-folk knight who is wrong about something is terrifyingly difficult to redirect.

The **Felis Sovereignty** runs on autonomy, observation, and individual excellence. Cat-folk wizards study centuries of arcane history before attempting original magic, operate in comfortable solitude punctuated by precisely chosen social moments, and organize their city-states around personal towers, private libraries, and cafes where solitary scholars meet by coincidence. Their magic is environmental: illusions, divination, spatial manipulation, and the legendary nine-lives casting system. Their weakness is their comfort with inaction: a Felis philosopher who sees a problem coming may watch it arrive with interest rather than move to prevent it.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** Twinmoor itself-the Sunward Side's warm golden architecture with hunting murals, heraldic banners, and communal plazas full of training knights; the Moonward Side's silver spires rising at architectural angles no Canis engineer would consider stable, connected by levitating bridges and lit by soft bioluminescent panels; the Threshold Districts in between, architecturally hybrid and politically unclaimed, where the most interesting people in the city live.
2. **Sound:** The Sunward Side: military drills, communal singing, the thump of heavy paws on stone training yards; the Moonward Side: the peculiar near-silence of spaces full of occupied, thinking minds, punctuated by the sudden purr of a successful spell or the crisp sound of a Tower Door opening. The Threshold: both sounds, intermingled, sometimes harmonious.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The Sunward Side smells of polish, leather, and warm fur in sunny courtyards; the Moonward Side has the complex, layered scent of libraries-old parchment, spell components, dusty velvet-with an undertone of something ozone-sharp from active arcane work. The Threshold Districts smell like both, and also like the particular combination of cooking from two entirely different culinary traditions happening simultaneously.

**The Core Tension:** Both sides are convinced the other assassinated the Treaty Warden. Both sides are wrong. The actual assassin, and their motive, is far more dangerous than either side's political rivalry-and the real threat requires Canis and Felis to cooperate at a level the century-old cold war has made psychologically almost impossible.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context

The **Three Century War** (which Canis histories call the Long Defense and Felis histories call the Unnecessary Affair) ended not in either side's military victory but in mutual exhaustion and the emergence of two brilliant individuals: **Field Commander Aldren Harrun** (Canis) and **Archphilosopher Sevra Nightglass** (Felis), who had spent the final decade of the war conducting a private correspondence through courier birds, arguing about the theoretical foundations of justice. When they finally met in person at Twinmoor-chosen for the meeting because neither side controlled it-they signed the Treaty of Twinmoor within three days.

Their descendants-the Harrun Pack and the Nightglass Tower-are still the most powerful families in their respective societies, and still corresponding, and still arguing about justice.

The **Treaty Wardens** are a neutral human-equivalent (there are populations of non-Canis, non-Felis peoples in the world, though they are a minority in Twinmoor) who administer the Divided City under Treaty authority. The current Treaty Warden's murder is the most significant crisis in a century.

## 3. Core Factions

### The Canis Compact (Honor-Bound Kingdoms)
Organized around Pack Loyalty and the knight traditions of the Great Houses.
- **The Harrun Pack:** The most powerful Canis noble house. Currently internally divided between the war-hawk faction and the Treaty defenders.
- **The Howler Guard:** Canis Compact's military intelligence unit. Believes the assassination was a Felis provocation. Has evidence. The evidence may have been planted.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"My Pack includes six of the most skilled knights in the Compact. I would die for any of them without hesitation. That is not a metaphor or a boast. It is simply true."*

### The Felis Sovereignty (Arcane Philosophy-States)
Organized around towers of individual magical excellence, loosely coordinated by the Council of Spires.
- **The Nightglass Tower:** The Archphilosopher's lineage. The most powerful Felis magical dynasty, currently in the uncomfortable position of producing the primary suspect in the Warden's murder.
- **The Silent Watchers:** Felis intelligence operatives. They observe everything and say nothing until the precise moment that saying something will accomplish the most.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I have lived three years in the Threshold District. I have studied both sides with equal interest. My conclusion: both sides are completely wrong about the other, in completely predictable ways, and neither would believe me if I told them."*

### The Threshold Communities
The mixed-heritage, politically unclaimed communities along the Treaty wall.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"My mother is Canis. My father is Felis. I have a Pack Bond and three Territories. The wall runs through our dining room. We eat on both sides."*

## 4. Unique Mechanic

**Pack Instinct (Canis-folk):**
Designate up to 3 Pack Members. Gain +1d6 to any action protecting/aiding them. At Pack Cohesion 3 (full Pack present and not separated), +2d6 to all Force actions in protection scenarios.

**Territorial Sense (Felis-folk):**
Designate 3 locations as Territory. +1d6 to all actions within them, +2d6 to defend them. Can move Territory designation once per session (Felis are territorial but not inflexible).

**Mixed Heritage:**
Choose one full or both at half effectiveness. Unique option: once per session, switch between Pack and Territory bonus as circumstances demand.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### Archetype 1: The Canis Treaty Defender
A senior Canis knight who served under the murdered Warden for twelve years and refuses to believe the Felis killed him-partly from the evidence, partly because she developed a genuine friendship with a Felis advisor during that time and can't accept that she misjudged them.
- **Quote:** *"The Pack tells me who I protect. My eyes tell me who did this. Right now my eyes and the Pack are in disagreement. I trust my Pack. I also trust my eyes. I need more information."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 3 | Finesse 1 | Mind 1 | Presence 3

### Archetype 2: The Felis Silent Watcher
A Felis intelligence operative who was surveilling the Warden at the time of the murder-for unrelated reasons she can't disclose-and knows exactly who the killer was. She hasn't told anyone yet because she's still calculating the optimal moment to do so.
- **Quote:** *"I know what happened. I am choosing when and how to reveal it with the precision that the situation demands. This is not obstruction. This is craft. The difference will be apparent in retrospect."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 3 | Mind 3 | Presence 1

### Archetype 3: The Threshold Investigator
A mixed-heritage private investigator in the Threshold District who has spent a career solving cases that neither Compact nor Sovereignty law covers. Knows both cultures intimately and is trusted by neither institution.
- **Quote:** *"I have Pack Bonds and three Territories. I also have a list of enemies in both halves of the city. Being trusted by individuals from both sides while being trusted by neither institution is the exact credential this case requires."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

## 6. Conditions & Starter Scenario

**Conditions:** *Pack-Broken* (separated from Pack Members, -1d6 to Force actions), *Territorial Violation* (another has entered a Felis Territory, triggering mandatory response), *Treaty Breach Suspicion* (officially suspect in diplomatic crisis), *Mixed-Heritage Conflict* (pulled between Pack and Territory instincts simultaneously).

**Starter Scenario: The Murder at the Meridian**
The Treaty Warden was found dead at the Meridian Stone-the precise geographic center of Twinmoor and the only point where both laws apply simultaneously. The murder weapon is a Canis military-grade blade enchanted with Felis illusion magic. It is forensically impossible without collaboration between both peoples. Both sides' intelligence arms are converging on the murder site. If they meet there without a neutral party present, the century of peace ends in the next ten minutes.

## 7. Why This Setting Matters

Dogs and cats have coexisted with humans for millennia and with each other for almost as long. Their relationship-reflexive tension, unexpected friendship, profound miscommunication, and genuine mutual benefit-is one of the oldest interspecies stories in human experience. Canis & Felis uses that familiar dynamic as a lens for examining political ideology: Pack Loyalty vs. Individual Autonomy are not merely dog-and-cat traits; they are real political philosophies with genuine strengths and genuine failure modes. The setting asks whether societies built on fundamentally different values can sustain peace, and what that peace actually costs the people who live inside it.
