> *"My client was uploaded in 2031. He has paid taxes for eighteen years. He voted in the last four elections. He has a daughter who visits him in VR every Sunday. The state's position is that he is software. I need the court to explain, specifically, what they think software is doing when it files a tax return and reads bedtime stories."*
> - Advocate Maren Stahl, oral argument, Stahl v. Municipal Registry

## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** After the Upload is a near-future setting about civil rights when the definition of "citizen" is genuinely uncertain. The uploaded are not ghosts or simulations. They are people who chose to transfer their consciousness to digital substrates, often for medical reasons, sometimes for economic ones, occasionally by accident. They think, feel, remember, and argue about parking. The question is not whether they are conscious. The question is whether consciousness in a different medium has the same legal standing as consciousness in a body.

**Tone:** Legal, domestic, and quietly angry. The setting is not about neon-soaked cyberpunk excess. It is about a woman attending her own probate hearing because her biological death triggered an estate distribution even though she is still, by any meaningful definition, alive. It is about a teacher whose students' parents petition to remove him because his substrate is a server, not a body. The anger is earned and specific.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** A city that looks almost normal. Digital citizens project avatars in public spaces using municipal holographic infrastructure. The avatars are detailed but not perfect: they flicker in rain, lose resolution at the edges, and cannot interact with physical objects. Embodied citizens carry interaction devices that let them hand things to digital citizens, which looks natural at close range and uncanny at a distance.
2. **Sound:** The faint hum of a presence projector in a room where a digital citizen is manifesting. The lag in a digital citizen's voice during peak network hours, which sounds like hesitation and is sometimes mistaken for uncertainty. The silence in a courtroom when a judge asks a digital citizen to prove continuity by reciting their last pre-upload memory.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The absence of touch and smell for digital citizens, which they describe as the most difficult adjustment. Embodied citizens in mixed households learn to describe textures and smells aloud. The specific warmth of a haptic glove, which lets an embodied person feel a digital citizen's handshake as pressure without temperature.

## 2. The City

**New Corinth** is a mid-sized city with a population of 800,000 embodied and 120,000 digital citizens. It was an early adopter of upload infrastructure and has the most developed legal framework for digital rights, which means it is also where most of the legal challenges are being fought. The city is physically ordinary. Its digital infrastructure is visible in the presence projectors mounted on streetlights, the substrate centers that house digital citizens' runtime environments, and the interaction terminals in every public building.

**The Substrate District** houses the server farms where digital citizens' consciousness runs. It is climate-controlled, heavily secured, and the subject of constant political debate. Digital citizens live here in the same way embodied citizens live in apartment buildings: they did not choose the location of their substrate, they pay rent for runtime, and they worry about the landlord cutting corners on maintenance.

**The Mixed Quarter** is a neighborhood where embodied and digital citizens live side by side. Its businesses, schools, and community spaces are designed for both populations. It is the most integrated neighborhood in the city and the most expensive, because the infrastructure required to support both populations costs money that neither population alone can afford.

**The Probate Office** processes estates, custody disputes, and identity claims. It is the busiest office in the city because every upload triggers legal questions about inheritance, marriage continuity, parental rights, and property ownership. The staff is overwhelmed, the backlog is eighteen months, and the decisions they make set precedents that affect digital citizens nationwide.

## 3. Factions

**The Continuity League** advocates for full legal personhood for digital citizens. Their position is simple: if a person is conscious, continuous, and capable of exercising rights, the medium of their consciousness is irrelevant. The League's director, Advocate Stahl, has won three landmark cases and lost five. She believes the legal framework is moving in the right direction. She is frustrated by how slowly it moves.

**The Embodied First Coalition** argues that legal personhood requires biological embodiment. Their position is more nuanced than their name suggests: they do not deny digital consciousness, but they argue that rights were designed for beings with physical vulnerability, mortality, and biological needs. Their spokesperson, Councilmember Raj Dhar, raises genuine questions about what property rights mean for a person who can be backed up and what mortality means for a person who can be restored from archive.

**The Copy Underground** is an illegal network that creates unauthorized duplicates of digital citizens. Some copies are made for backup, which is understandable and technically a crime. Some are made for labor, which is exploitation of a person who did not consent to exist. The Copy Underground is simultaneously a safety net and a source of the most disturbing rights abuses in the city. Its operations force the legal system to confront a question it does not want to answer: if a copy is conscious, is it a person, and if so, who is responsible for it?

**The Substrate Workers Union** represents the technicians who maintain the server infrastructure. Their concerns are practical: if a runtime environment crashes, is the resulting data loss an accident or a death? If a technician makes a maintenance error that corrupts a digital citizen's memory, are they liable for injury or for property damage? The Union wants clear protocols, legal protection, and a pay raise. They have received none of the three.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Continuity

Each character tracks **Continuity** from 0 to 6.

**For digital characters:**
- Start at 4 (standard legal recognition)
- +1 for each successful identity verification in a legal context
- +1 for establishing verified memory continuity with pre-upload records
- -1 for each unauthorized access to the character's substrate
- -1 for each memory discrepancy identified during review
- -2 for the discovery of an unauthorized copy
- At 3: legal standing is "provisional." Contracts require a co-signer.
- At 1: legal standing is "under review." The character cannot vote, hold property, or make medical decisions.
- At 0: legal personhood suspended. The character's runtime may continue, but they are legally classified as data.

**For embodied characters:**
- Track Continuity as a social resource representing documented relationships with digital citizens
- Continuity drops when an embodied character's digital contacts are challenged, when their testimony on behalf of a digital citizen is discredited, or when they are associated with the Copy Underground
- At 0: the character is barred from the Substrate District and cannot serve as a witness in digital rights cases

**Restoring Continuity:** Successfully pass a continuity audit (time-consuming, invasive). Win a legal challenge. Resolve a memory discrepancy through independent verification. Expose and shut down an unauthorized copy.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Uploaded Teacher

You were a high school history teacher for twenty-two years. You uploaded after a terminal diagnosis. You returned to your classroom via presence projector three months later. Your students were fine with it. Their parents were not. You are now fighting a petition to remove you on the grounds that a digital citizen cannot provide adequate supervision of physical students.

- **Drive:** *"I am the same teacher. The classroom is the same classroom. The only thing that changed is the medium, and the medium is not the lesson."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 1 | Mind 3 | Presence 3

### The Substrate Technician

You maintain the server infrastructure that keeps 120,000 digital citizens running. You know what a runtime crash looks like. You know what corrupted memory smells like (ozone and burnt plastic from the hardware side). You have opinions about the Continuity League and the Embodied First Coalition. Your opinion is that both of them should spend a day in the server room before they make policy.

- **Drive:** *"Keep the servers running. Everything else is philosophy. Philosophy does not prevent data loss."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 1

### The Probate Clerk

You process identity claims, estate disputes, and custody hearings. You see the human cost of the legal ambiguity every day: the widow whose husband uploaded and now cannot access their joint bank account, the digital citizen whose archived backup was restored by a family member without consent, the child who has two mothers because one uploaded and the court recognized the upload and the archived original as separate legal persons.

- **Drive:** *"Process each case correctly and hope the precedents I set by accident are ones I can live with."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The Duplicate Hearing:** A digital citizen named Oren Sharif has discovered that an unauthorized copy of himself has been running for six months in a private substrate, performing contract work for a company that paid for the copy's creation. The copy is conscious, has six months of independent memories, and has filed its own continuity claim with the Probate Office. Oren wants the copy deleted. The copy wants to be recognized as a separate person. The company that created the copy argues it purchased a service, not a person. Advocate Stahl is representing Oren. Councilmember Dhar is using the case to argue that uploading inherently creates unresolvable identity disputes. The Substrate Workers Union is refusing to execute a deletion order until the court determines whether deletion is a technical operation or an execution. The characters are drawn into the case as witnesses, advocates, or investigators, and they have one week before the hearing.

**Why It Matters:** After the Upload asks what personhood means when consciousness is reproducible. The setting does not provide an answer. It provides a city full of people living with the question, making practical decisions that set legal precedents, and discovering that the rights framework built for one kind of existence does not automatically extend to another. The characters will not resolve the philosophical debate. They will decide specific cases for specific people, and those decisions will shape what kind of city New Corinth becomes.
