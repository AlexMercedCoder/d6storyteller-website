## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The most powerful warrior tradition in history is also one of the most developed shamanic traditions -- and the Duureg embody both.

Mongolian shamanism is one of the world's oldest continuous spiritual traditions, with deep roots in Central Asian and Siberian practice: Tengri (the eternal blue sky) as the supreme divine principle, Etugen (earth mother) as the earthly counterpart, nature spirits inhabiting mountains, rivers, and significant landmarks, the shaman as intermediary who can travel between realms in trance, and a sophisticated understanding of the relationship between the human and spirit worlds as mutual obligation.

The historical context is specific: the setting can be placed in the period of the great Mongol empire or earlier, in the traditional steppe culture that produced the empire's warriors and spiritual practitioners.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The steppe in its full scale: a landscape so vast it redefines the concept of horizon, the sky occupying more visual space than the land, the sacred mountains rising from the plain in ways that draw the eye from enormous distances, and the Ovoo cairns at their summits that accumulate offerings for generations until they become their own significant landscape features.
2. **Sound:** The steppe wind that Mongolian tradition reads for spiritual content, the morin khuur (horsehead fiddle) that Böö use in ceremony, the specific patterns of the shaman's drum that mark the transition into trance states, and the extraordinary silence of the high steppe at dawn when the wind briefly pauses.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The physical reality of life on horseback: the way the horse becomes an extension of the body over years, the cold of the Mongolian seasons, the smell of the ger (yurt) fire, the physical weight of the shaman's costume with its accumulated spirit-tokens, and the specific feeling of the Spirit Horse's presence -- a warmth and mass in the air where nothing visible stands.

## 2. The Duureg Structure

### The Rider-Shamans
Duureg are trained in both physical combat and spirit navigation. Not all Böö are warriors; not all warriors are Böö. The Duureg are both, specifically because the sacred geography they protect requires both capacities.

### The Sacred Geography
The mountains, water sources, and significant landmarks of the steppe that have accumulated centuries of relationship and spiritual significance. Each has a spirit keeper. Each requires specific protocols from travelers and residents. The Duureg know all of them.

### The 99 Heavens
The Mongolian cosmological tradition recognizes 99 heavens of varying levels, each inhabited by different types of divine beings. Communication with the higher heavens is possible but requires exceptional skill and significant ritual preparation. The Duureg's routine work involves the lower heavens and the nature spirits -- the higher ones are for exceptional situations.

### The Incursion
Something from the spirit world is increasing its pressure on the physical world at specific points in the sacred geography. Not the usual nature-spirit activity -- something more organized and more aggressive. The Duureg must identify it and address it before it creates openings large enough to cause real harm.

## 3. Adventure Seeds

1. **The Desecrated Ovoo:** A trade caravan has dismantled an Ovoo to use its stones for camp construction, not understanding what they were doing. The spirit keeper of that mountain is furious. The trade caravan is now experiencing the consequences. The Duureg must mediate, rebuild the relationship, and address the damage before it escalates to the region level.

2. **The Captured Shaman:** An Udgan (female shaman) has been captured in the spirit world during a journey that went wrong. Her physical body is in trance but cannot be awakened without her return. A Duureg team must enter the spirit world to find her, navigating the territory of whatever captured her.

3. **The Ancient Grudge:** Two mountain spirit-keepers have been in conflict since before living memory. Their conflict is now affecting the human communities in the valley between their mountains. Both sides have legitimate historical grievances. Resolution requires deep spirit-world negotiation and probably a formal ceremony that nobody in living memory has needed to perform.

4. **The False Böö:** Someone without genuine shamanic training is performing ceremonies, establishing relationships with spirits they don't understand, and creating obligations they cannot honor. The spirits are being consulted under false pretenses. The damage spreads when the false Böö cannot fulfill what was promised.

5. **The Heaven Messenger:** A being from a higher heaven has descended to the physical world with a message that requires delivery to specific people. Getting the message to its intended recipients requires traveling across the steppe in a specific time window. The being cannot survive long in the physical world. The journey is complicated by parties who don't want the message delivered.
