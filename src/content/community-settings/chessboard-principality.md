I work through intermediaries rather than acting directly. The rules of my position have become the habits of my personality. I do not know whether I chose to be indirect or whether being a Bishop made me this way."*
> - Advisor Elene Marsh, House Aldren, assigned Bishop

## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

The Grand Court of Aldren is a massive chessboard: a 64-square floor of alternating black and white marble, each square large enough for a person to stand in. When noble houses dispute territory, trade, or succession, they deploy their retainers as pieces and play a match governed by the rules of chess, adapted for human scale. The matches are formal, witnessed, and binding. The consequences of losing a piece are social rather than physical: a taken piece is reassigned, their status reduced, their role in the house shifted to reflect the loss.

**Tone:** Formal, strategic, and laced with the specific tension of people who are both players and pieces. The setting is about the intersection of strategy and humanity: the moment when a player realizes that the optimal move requires sacrificing someone they care about, and the piece realizes they are being sacrificed.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Grand Court: marble floor, banners of the competing houses hanging from the gallery, spectators in formal dress observing from raised platforms. The pieces: retainers in ceremonial armor styled to match their chess roles, standing in their squares with the stillness that comes from knowing they cannot move until commanded. The strategist's platform, elevated above the board, where each house's commander views the positions and gives orders.
2. **Sound:** The strategist's commands, spoken clearly across the Court: "Bishop to E7," "Knight to C3." The sound of armored footsteps crossing marble as a piece moves to their new square. The silence when a piece is taken, which involves the taken piece stepping off the board and walking to the side of the court, their role in the match, and their current social standing, concluded.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The cold of the marble floor through ceremonial boots. The weight of chess-specific armor, which is lighter than battle armor but heavier than court dress. The smell of the Court: polished stone, beeswax from the gallery candles, and the sharp scent of anxiety from people standing in squares, waiting to be moved or taken.

## 2. The Principality

**The Grand Court** is the primary match venue: a permanent chessboard built into the floor of the Principality's central hall. It has hosted matches for two centuries. The black and white squares are worn smooth by generations of armored feet.

**The Strategists' Gallery** overlooks the Court. Each house's commander sits here, studying the board and issuing orders. The Gallery is where the intellectual combat happens: the reading of opponents, the calculation of sacrifice, and the decision to move a person into danger.

**The Reassignment Hall** is where taken pieces report after leaving the board. The Hall manages the social consequences: reduction in rank, reassignment to lesser positions, and the administrative processing of a human being who has just been moved from one social status to another because of a game.

**The Training Grounds** prepare retainers for board service. Each piece role requires specific training: Pawns learn to advance and hold, Knights practice the leaping combination of aggression and surprise, Bishops develop the indirect approach. The training shapes personality as well as skill, which is the system's most subtle effect.

## 3. Factions

**House Aldren** is the ruling house, currently defending its position against two challengers. Strategist Sera Vane commands the Aldren board with methodical precision. She is brilliant, careful, and aware that her pieces are people, which makes her a better strategist and a worse sleeper.

**House Korr** is the primary challenger: an ambitious trading house that wants territorial concessions. Strategist Dax Korr is aggressive and willing to sacrifice pieces for positional advantage. His pieces know this. Some consider it bold. Others are updating their wills.

**The Pieces' Assembly** is an informal organization of retainers who serve as board pieces and who have begun advocating for their own interests. Assembly speaker Mira Cole (assigned Pawn, which means she can only address problems by moving forward one step at a time) has been pushing for rules that allow pieces to refuse a move they consider suicidal. The strategists consider this mutiny. The pieces consider it self-preservation.

**The Arbiters** are the independent officials who judge matches. Chief Arbiter Jonas Fell ensures that moves are legal, takes are properly processed, and the social consequences are correctly administered. Fell is impartial, meticulous, and privately horrified by a system he enforces because the alternative, the civil war that the chess matches replaced, was worse.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Board Position

Each character is assigned a piece role that governs their approach to problems:

| Role | Movement Rule | Narrative Effect |
|------|--------------|------------------|
| **Pawn** | Forward only, one step. Captures diagonally. | Can only address problems directly ahead. Cannot retreat. Can be promoted to any role by reaching the opponent's back rank. |
| **Knight** | L-shaped: two steps in one direction, one step perpendicular. Leaps over obstacles. | Approaches problems from unexpected angles. Can bypass barriers that stop other pieces. Cannot move in straight lines. |
| **Bishop** | Diagonal only, any distance. | Works through indirect channels. Cannot confront problems directly. Effective advisors and intermediaries. |
| **Rook** | Straight lines (horizontal or vertical), any distance. | Direct, forceful, and inflexible. Excellent at straightforward tasks. Cannot adapt to diagonal or oblique situations. |
| **Queen** | Any direction, any distance. | Maximum flexibility but maximum responsibility. The Queen is the most valuable piece and the most targeted. |
| **King** | Any direction, one step. | Limited mobility but ultimate importance. The King's safety determines the match. All other pieces serve the King's survival. |

**Promotion:** A Pawn who reaches the opponent's back rank (accomplishes a goal against significant opposition) can be promoted to any role. This is the primary mechanism for social advancement.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Reluctant Pawn

You are a Pawn: the most expendable piece, the most numerous, and the only one that can be promoted. You advance one step at a time, you capture sideways, and you cannot retreat. Your strategist considers you disposable. Your Assembly friends consider you brave. You consider yourself a person standing in a square, waiting to be told where to go.

- **Drive:** *"Reach the far rank and earn promotion, or convince the Assembly that the rules of the board should not be the rules of my life."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 1 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

### The House Strategist

You command the board from the Gallery. You see every piece, every threat, and every opportunity. You also see the faces of the people you are about to move into danger. Your skill determines your house's fate. Your conscience determines how you sleep. Every match requires at least one sacrifice. The mathematics of chess demand it. The humanity of the situation protests.

- **Drive:** *"Win the match with the fewest sacrifices, and find a way to value my pieces as people without losing the strategic clarity that keeps them alive."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 1 | Mind 4 | Presence 2

### The Taken Knight

You were captured in the last match and reassigned. Your social standing dropped two ranks. You went from a respected position in House Aldren to a minor administrative role. You are not bitter (the rules are clear), but you are motivated: you want to return to the board, prove your value, and regain the position you lost. You are also starting to question whether a system that can take your life's work away in a single move is a system worth defending.

- **Drive:** *"Return to the board on my terms, and decide whether I am fighting to reclaim my position or to change the game entirely."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 1

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The Queen's Sacrifice:** Strategist Sera Vane has identified a sequence that will win the Aldren-Korr match in seven moves. The sequence requires sacrificing the Queen: Elene Marsh, the Bishop who has been reassigned to Queen for this match. Marsh is the most capable person on the board and the most valuable target. Sacrificing her wins the match but costs Aldren its most experienced piece. Mira Cole has invoked the Assembly's right to refuse, which has no legal standing but which has the moral weight of a person saying "I am not a piece." The characters must navigate the match, the sacrifice, and the emerging question of whether the pieces are players or property.

**Why It Matters:** The Chessboard Principality asks what happens when strategy requires treating people as pieces. The system is not cruel by design. It replaced a war. But it replaced one form of human expenditure with another, and the pieces, the people who stand in squares and wait to be moved, are beginning to ask whether being a game piece is better than being a casualty, and whether there is a third option.
