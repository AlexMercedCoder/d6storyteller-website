> *"Article 7, Section 3 of the Master-Minion Agreement is clear: henchmen who are dispatched to confront a known active adventuring party are entitled to a full briefing on the adventurers' known abilities, at least 48 hours in advance of the confrontation, and the option to designate a beneficiary for their hazard pay. Your client sent seventeen henchmen into a dungeon encounter with four words of briefing: 'They're in there, go.' Seventeen henchmen, four words, no beneficiary forms. We are filing a Tier 3 grievance."*
> - Sela Pell, Union Representative, Local 404

## 1. Setting Overview and Tone

The Union was organized thirty years ago after the Incident at Blackrock Tower, in which a villain sent forty henchmen into an encounter with an adventuring party without hazard pay, advance briefing, or casualty extraction plans, resulting in the complete loss of the squad. The survivors organized. The Villains' Council initially resisted recognition and then recognized the Union after a three-month general strike that left every major dungeon understaffed for an entire adventuring season.

The relationship has been complicated ever since, in the specific way of labor-management relationships in industries where the work is genuinely dangerous and both parties need each other.

**Tone:** Labor comedy with genuine solidarity underneath. The humor comes from the application of rigorous labor law to villainy. The Union representatives are the most professionally competent people in the setting. The villains are theatrical but ultimately rational economic actors. The henchmen are workers who want hazard pay, reasonable hours, and to not be used as dramatic cannon fodder.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Union hall: a practical building across the street from the Villains' Council's dramatic headquarters, deliberately unglamorous. Bulletin boards with grievance updates, job postings (three-month lackey position, medium-security lair, competitive hazard pay), and the Union's awards wall (Henchman of the Year, voted by members). The Union rep's desk: covered in case files organized with the thoroughness of someone who has learned that documentation is the only advantage a henchman has.
2. **Sound:** The specific language of labor negotiation applied to villainy: "workplace incident" for a dungeon encounter, "unauthorized task expansion" for being asked to commit crimes outside your job description, and "client-inflicted involuntary separation" for being used as a distraction for adventurers. The sound of a solidarity chant that the junior henchmen practice slightly out of sync.
3. **Touch and Smell:** The standard-issue uniform: matching outfits that the Union negotiated as a specific contract requirement after the Incident, on the grounds that uniformed henchmen are more likely to be treated as a professional workforce rather than expendable props. The Union rep's coffee: always present, always strong, because the grievance calendar is never empty.

## 2. The Union

**The Union Hall** is the operational center: a large main room for member meetings, three smaller rooms for grievance hearings, and the Union Rep's office, which is actually a suite of offices that Union Representative Sela Pell shares with four associates who handle different contract areas (safety, compensation, training, and the specialized unit that handles magical hazard claims).

**The Grievance Board** tracks active complaints against Villains' Council members. Currently: forty-seven open grievances, twelve in active hearing, and three escalated to the Joint Arbitration Panel. The Board is managed by Union Archivist Bren, whose memory for precedent is the Union's most valuable institutional resource.

**The Training Center** offers professional development for henchmen: combat readiness courses, first aid certification, the required "Adventurer Recognition and Evasion" seminar, and an elective series on career advancement (from basic lackey to technical specialist, with a dedicated track for those pursuing lair management positions).

**The Joint Arbitration Panel** is the final step in the grievance process: a three-member body with one Union representative, one Villains' Council representative, and one independent arbitrator from the Professional Guild of Neutral Parties. The independent arbitrator, a retired civil servant named Tam, has been hearing cases for fifteen years and has developed opinions about both parties that he expresses in his written decisions with increasing specificity.

## 3. Factions

**The Union Leadership** under Representative Sela Pell is effective, realistic, and constantly managing the tension between the militancy the membership wants and the institutional relationships that make the Union effective. Pell has won forty-seven arbitration cases. She has also made three significant concessions that the younger members consider sellouts and that Pell considers necessary compromises that preserved the Union's certification. Both assessments are correct.

**The Villains' Council** represents forty-two active dungeon operators and theatrical villains. Council Chair Mordak the Moderate (formerly Mordak the Merciless, but the branding consultant advised the change) manages the Council's relationship with the Union with the weary professionalism of someone who genuinely respects collective bargaining in principle and finds the specific application of it to his operations extremely inconvenient.

**The Henchmen's Membership** includes a wide range of workers: experienced veterans who have been in the industry twenty years, new recruits who joined for the hazard pay, technical specialists (trap mechanics, magical support, logistics), and a small but vocal group of members who believe the Union should be demanding full transparency about all planned villain schemes before assigning henchmen to them, which Pell considers a legitimate goal that is thirty years away from being achievable.

**The Adventurers' Guild** has a complicated relationship with the Union. Individual adventurers and henchmen who face each other in dungeon encounters often develop professional respect over repeated interactions. The Guilds cooperated on one specific joint initiative: the Henchman Identification Protocol, which requires that incapacitated henchmen be identified, reported to the Union, and allowed to surrender rather than being killed as a combat default. The protocol reduced casualty rates significantly and is the Union's proudest policy achievement.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Labor Action

Track **Solidarity Rating** from 0 to 6 (starts at 4).

**Raising Solidarity (+1 each):**
- Win a significant grievance at arbitration
- Successfully organize a workplace safety action
- Ratify a favorable new contract provision
- Public demonstration of Union effectiveness (successful collective action, visible member support)

**Lowering Solidarity (-1 each):**
- A significant grievance is lost at arbitration
- A Villains' Council member successfully recruits henchmen outside Union channels
- A contract negotiation produces terms the membership considers unfavorable
- A high-profile member crosses a picket line or publicly breaks with the Union

**Labor actions by Solidarity level:**
| Solidarity | Available Actions |
|------------|------------------|
| 5-6 | Full collective action available. Work-to-rule, grievance filing, organized work stoppages. |
| 3-4 | Standard operations. Grievances effective. No collective actions. |
| 1-2 | Division visible. Grievances harder to enforce. Members vulnerable to individual pressure. |
| 0 | Certification revoked. Union loses standing. Members unprotected. |

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Union Representative

You are Sela Pell. You have been representing henchmen for twelve years. You have won more arbitration cases than any representative in the Union's history. You have also made compromises that you stand behind professionally and carry personally, because every compromise was a choice between something you wanted and something you needed to protect the members you had. You currently have forty-seven open grievances, a contract negotiation starting in three months, and a junior member who has just filed a class action against a villain for "systematic dramatic endangerment," which is not a recognized category in the Master-Minion Agreement but which describes a real pattern of behavior that deserves one.

- **Drive:** *"Win the systematic endangerment case, establish precedent, and get it written into the next contract before Mordak's legal team realizes what you've done."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### The Veteran Henchman

You have been a dungeon operations specialist for twenty-two years. You have survived six full dungeon encounters with active adventuring parties (one was a misunderstanding, three were professional, two were genuinely unpleasant). You serve on the Union's safety committee and you have opinions about the Master-Minion Agreement's hazard pay schedule that you intend to raise at the next contract negotiation. You are also the most experienced member anyone asks when they want to know whether a new villain's operation is worth working for, and your informal reputation as an operator assessor is the most accurate intelligence service the Union has.

- **Drive:** *"Get the hazard pay schedule updated to reflect current adventurer threat levels, and evaluate whether the new villain applying for a Council seat is the kind of operator you're willing to send your members to work for."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

### The New Recruit

You joined Local 404 three months ago. You came to professional villainy support because the pay is good, the Union benefits are excellent, and the training program is the most comprehensive vocational program available to someone of your background. You have attended every required seminar. You have your first assignment: a three-month position as basic lackey at a medium-security lair run by a villain named the Crimson Administrator, who your veteran training partner has described as "a good operator who files all his paperwork" with the specific tone of someone praising a quality that most of their colleagues lack.

- **Drive:** *"Complete your probationary assignment successfully, demonstrate to the Union that you are worth the senior certification, and understand why everyone treats 'files all his paperwork' as the highest possible compliment."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 2 | Mind 1 | Presence 3

## 6. Starter Scenario and Why It Matters

**The Class Action:** A henchman named Dex has filed a class action against Villain Council member Zaravel the Dramatic, alleging that Zaravel systematically places henchmen in dramatically sacrificial positions: staging them as "the villain's disposable minions" in ways that guarantee adventurer attention, maximize their likelihood of being incapacitated, and produce the theatrical effects that Zaravel's reputation requires. Dex's filing documents eleven incidents over two years with forty-seven named members affected. The class action asks for back hazard pay, mandatory dramatic positioning disclosure before deployment, and a formal recognition that "theatrical atmosphere" does not constitute operational justification for elevated risk. Zaravel's counsel argues that drama is an inherent feature of the industry. The Joint Arbitration Panel has accepted the case. Tam, the independent arbitrator, has been seen reading the filing with increasing visible interest.

**Why It Matters:** Unionized Henchmen Local 404 is about the relationship between institutional power and worker protection in industries where the work is inherently dangerous and the power imbalance is extreme. The comedy is real. So is the underlying question: who protects the people doing the dangerous work when the dangerous work is, structurally, their entire function? The Union's answer is collective action and documentation, which is the same answer it always is.
