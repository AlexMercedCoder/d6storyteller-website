## 1. Setting Overview and Tone

The Dungeon Certification Act was passed in the Kingdom of Aldrath forty years ago after an adventuring party was killed by a dungeon that had improperly calibrated crushing walls, trapping them inside rather than providing the standard escape window. The resulting lawsuit established that dungeon operators have a legal duty of care toward the adventuring parties their dungeons attract.

The Act is deeply controversial. Villains resent the paperwork. Adventurers' guilds lobbied for it but now complain that certified dungeons are "too predictable." The Certification Board treats the whole situation as a straightforward professional obligation. The inspectors are the most matter-of-fact people in the kingdom.

**Tone:** Deadpan professional comedy. The humor comes entirely from the contrast between the inspectors' complete institutional normalcy and the complete abnormality of their workplace. Inspectors do not find dungeons alarming. They find dungeons that are improperly certified alarming.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The inspection kit: a standard-issue satchel containing measuring equipment, a violation citation pad, a copy of the Dungeon Safety Code (current edition, 847 pages), a headlamp, and a packed lunch. The inspector's expression while noting a minotaur enclosure that is missing two of its required safety bolts: the same expression a restaurant health inspector has when noting an improperly stored salad ingredient.
2. **Sound:** The specific bureaucratic language the inspectors use for extraordinary situations: "unlicensed animated remains" for a room full of undead, "uncontained pressure events" for an active magma flow, and "non-compliant patron extraction failure" for a dungeon that has been killing adventurers without the required casualty reporting. The sound of a violation citation being torn from the pad.
3. **Touch and Smell:** The dungeons themselves, which the inspectors have developed the professional detachment to navigate without responding to the atmosphere of menace that dungeon operators spend considerable effort cultivating. The smell of a properly maintained dungeon versus an uncertified one: experienced inspectors claim they can identify certification status by the smell of the first corridor.

## 2. The Certification Board

**The Regional Office** handles certification for approximately two hundred dungeons in its jurisdiction. Chief Inspector Maren manages eight inspectors and a case backlog that is currently four months long. She considers this normal.

**The Certification Board Tribunal** is the appeals body where dungeons contest closure orders. Tribunal proceedings involve the dungeon operator (or their legal representative), the issuing inspector, and three Tribunal members who have collectively heard every possible argument for why a particular violation should not count. Their patience is legendary. Their sympathy is not.

**The Standard Reference Library** contains the complete Dungeon Safety Code, all amendments, all tribunal decisions that established precedent, and a supplementary volume of Frequently Asked Questions that is more frequently read than the code itself because it is written in language that does not require a law degree. The FAQ was written by Inspector Bren, who has been with the Board for twenty years and who has seen every situation at least twice.

**The Field Inspection Route** is the monthly circuit that inspectors travel to perform scheduled inspections and respond to complaints. Unscheduled inspections occur when the Board receives a complaint from an adventuring guild, a neighboring property owner, or, occasionally, a dungeon operator who wants a competitor shut down.

## 3. Factions

**The Dungeon Safety and Certification Board** is the regulatory body: professional, underfunded, and committed to the mission. The inspectors take their jobs seriously because the alternative is adventurers dying in dungeons that could have been made safer, and that outcome is worse than the paperwork. They are not adversaries of the dungeon operators. They are professionals managing a compliance relationship.

**The Dungeon Operators' Association** represents the kingdom's licensed dungeon operators. Chair Vex (formerly Lord Vex the Terrible, retired from active villainy) has been lobbying for regulatory reform for twenty years. He has three specific asks: a simplified permit process for standard trap types, a reasonable timeline for violation remediation, and recognition that some dungeon features that technically violate code are essential to the dungeon's narrative purpose. The Board has declined all three asks on grounds of public safety.

**The Adventurers' Guild** lobbied for the certification requirement and now has complicated feelings about it. Guild representative Sela privately admits that certified dungeons are safer and that safer dungeons produce fewer guild insurance claims, which is good, but that the guild's membership considers certified dungeons "too soft" and that this is affecting recruitment. She would like the Board to consider a "Challenge Rating" certification tier that allows experienced adventuring parties to attempt uncertified sections of dungeons under a waiver.

**The Shadow Operators** are the dungeon operators who refuse to certify and operate illegally. The Board does not have enforcement authority: they can issue closure orders, but actual closure requires cooperation from the City Watch, which has other priorities. Inspector Bren has a list of seventeen known uncertified dungeons and a bureaucratic process for compelling Watch cooperation that takes approximately four months. Each uncertified dungeon is a liability she tracks carefully.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Violation Citations

During an inspection, track **Violation Count.**

**Violation severity levels:**
| Level | Description | Examples |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| Minor | Does not immediately endanger patrons | Signage too small, trap reset time 5% over limit |
| Moderate | Creates unnecessary risk | Missing escape route, unlicensed monster species |
| Major | Endangers patron life unreasonably | Uncontained lethal hazard, blocked emergency exits |
| Critical | Represents flagrant disregard for code | Trap designed to prevent patron escape, unreported casualties |

**Outcomes:**
| Violations | Result |
|------------|--------|
| 0 | Full certification, 12-month validity |
| 1-2 minor | Conditional certification, reinspection in 90 days |
| 3-4 any | Partial operation order, restricted to compliant zones |
| 5+ any, or any Critical | Full closure order |

**Appeals:** Operators can appeal closure orders to the Tribunal. Successful appeals reduce the order to partial operation. Failed appeals result in Watch referral.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Senior Inspector

You are Bren. You have inspected four hundred dungeons in twenty years. You have issued 1,847 violation citations. You have been threatened, cursed, turned into a small animal (twice, both resolved), and offered bribes in amounts that would make your monthly salary look embarrassing. None of this has made you less thorough. You are currently managing three active appeals, one complaint against a competitor dungeon from a dungeon operator, and a new inspection request from a dungeon that describes itself as "artisanal" and "narrative-forward," which you have not encountered as a certification category before and which you are approaching with professional caution.

- **Drive:** *"Complete the artisanal dungeon inspection, handle the competitor complaint without showing favoritism, and get through the appeals without being turned into a small animal a third time."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 3 | Mind 3 | Presence 1

### The Reformed Dungeon Operator

You were previously known as Lord Vex the Terrible. You are now Vex, chair of the Dungeon Operators' Association and operator of Vex's Challenge Complex, a fully certified dungeon in its eighth year of continuous operation. You are professionally committed to the certification system because it has been good for business: certified operators get better insurance rates, better adventurer foot traffic, and protection from liability claims. You are also quietly running for city council, which requires you to maintain a completely clean record, which means you are extremely anxious about the inspection your dungeon is due for this month.

- **Drive:** *"Pass the inspection cleanly, push through the regulatory reform you've been lobbying for, and do both without anyone discovering the thing in the sub-basement that you were planning to get permitted but haven't gotten around to yet."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

### The Adventurers' Guild Representative

You are Sela. You advocated for the certification system. You now spend most of your professional life managing the gap between "the certification system is good for adventurers' safety" and "adventurers find certified dungeons boring and are blaming the Guild for this." You have a practical solution: a voluntary "Challenge Tier" for elite parties who want to attempt uncertified sections under a waiver. You need the Board's cooperation, the operators' participation, and the Guild membership to stop filing complaints about your regulatory priorities long enough for you to implement something.

- **Drive:** *"Get the Challenge Tier framework approved and implemented before the Guild membership votes on your leadership position in three months."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

## 6. Starter Scenario and Why It Matters

**The Artisanal Dungeon:** A new dungeon has applied for certification. It describes itself as "an immersive narrative experience" and "artisanal" in its approach to challenge. The inspector assigned to the initial review, a newer hire named Tal, has returned from the preliminary walkthrough with a ten-page incident report and a request for Bren's assistance. The dungeon's design is genuinely unusual: it has no traps in the traditional sense, every monster has been hired as a performance contractor with a union agreement, and the "treasure" at the end is a detailed psychological assessment of the adventuring party. It appears to meet code in every technical respect. It violates the spirit of the certification system in a way that the current code does not have language for. The Tribunal is watching to see how the Board handles it.

**Why It Matters:** Dungeon Health Inspectors is about the specific comedy of professional normalcy applied to extraordinary situations. The inspectors' humor is their absolute commitment to treating dungeons as a compliance issue rather than an adventure. The setting is also, quietly, about the purpose of regulation: the certification system exists because people were dying, and the comedy cannot fully obscure the genuine stakes underneath.
