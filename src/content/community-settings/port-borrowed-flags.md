## 1. Setting Overview and Tone

The Port of Veray was established two centuries ago under a treaty that no one has successfully invalidated, primarily because invalidating it would require three competing maritime powers to agree on something for long enough to pass legislation, and the Port's continued existence benefits enough of those powers' commercial interests that the political will is never fully assembled.

The Port operates as a practical reality of maritime commerce: shipping is international, flags are political, and the mismatch between the two creates constant friction that the Port specializes in resolving. Most of what the Port enables is legal in every practical sense. Some of what it enables is not, and the Port's structure makes it very difficult to determine which is which.

**Tone:** Commercially cynical, procedurally dense, and built on the specific energy of a place where everyone is technically legitimate and nothing is quite clean. The setting rewards characters who understand that legality and morality are different measurements and that the Port operates confidently in the space between them.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The commercial district: a dense strip of registry offices, maritime law firms, flag brokers, and the occasional insurance adjustor, all operating with the brisk efficiency of people who charge by the hour. The harbor itself: forty to sixty vessels at any time, flying flags from across the known world, often multiple flags stacked on the same flagpole while a registry transfer is in progress. The inspection pier: where Port authority officers review documentation with the careful thoroughness of people who know that everything they review will be technically correct.
2. **Sound:** The commercial district's specific sound: the rapid, precise language of maritime law applied to questions of identity and registry. Multiple languages, multiple accents, all discussing the same fundamental question (what flag does this ship fly and what does that mean today) in various levels of urgency. The harbor's multilingual commerce: a dozen languages on the docks at any given hour.
3. **Touch and Smell:** The registry documentation itself: heavy paper, official seals, the specific texture of waterproofed maritime certificates that must survive weather conditions. The commercial district's smell: salt from the harbor a block away, printer's ink from the certificate offices, and the coffee that the lawyers and brokers consume in quantities proportional to the complexity of their clients' situations.

## 2. The Port

**The Registry Hall** is the central government office where all flag transfers are formally recorded and certified. Chief Registrar Nora Dall runs the Hall with the professional thoroughness of someone who has made "technically legal" into an art form. The Hall processes approximately twenty transactions per day. It has never refused a registry application that met the formal requirements. It has never investigated an application's underlying motivation.

**The Broker Row** is the commercial street where flag brokers maintain their offices. The most established broker, a woman named Ines, has been in business for thirty years and holds pre-negotiated registry arrangements with seventeen of the forty recognized flag nations. She can complete a transfer in forty minutes if the client has the paperwork ready. She does not ask about the paperwork's origin.

**The Inspection Pier** is where Port authority officers check incoming and outgoing vessels. The officers are competent, thorough, and trained to verify documentation rather than investigate it. They are very good at confirming that a certificate is genuine and not responsible for the question of how the certificate came to be.

**The Safe Harbor District** is the residential area adjacent to the commercial port where people who need the Port's services for reasons other than commercial convenience sometimes end up staying longer than planned. It has the specific culture of a place where people from everywhere are in transit and some of them have run out of somewhere to go.

## 3. Factions

**The Veray Port Authority** maintains the registry system and enforces the Port's operational rules. Director Halm Cole runs the Authority with the understanding that the Port's economic model depends on not asking questions that would destabilize the treaty. He is not corrupt. He is institutional: committed to the framework because he genuinely believes a legal registry system, even an abused one, is better than no system at all.

**The Flag Brokers' Association** represents the thirty-odd brokers who operate in the Port. Their chair, Ines, has been managing the Association's relationship with the Port Authority for twenty years. She knows which clients are avoiding tariffs, which are evading debt, and which are doing something worse. She manages her knowledge the way she manages registries: professionally and without documentation.

**The Pursuing Authorities** are the law enforcement and naval entities from various nations who periodically arrive at the Port looking for vessels that have re-registered to avoid jurisdiction. The Port's treaty requires cooperation on documented criminal investigations but grants the Port wide discretion in defining "documented." Most pursuits stall in procedural exchange. A few succeed. The Port treats both outcomes as operationally acceptable.

**The Stateless** are the vessels and people who have used the Port's services until their Flag Legitimacy reached zero: too many contested registries, too many challenged documents, and now no flag nation willing to issue them a clean certificate. They operate out of the Safe Harbor District and have become a semi-permanent community of people who are technically maritime subjects of no nation.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Flag Legitimacy

Track each vessel's **Flag Legitimacy** from 0 to 6 (starts at 4 for a newly issued registry).

**Raising Legitimacy (+1 each):**
- Complete a clean commercial voyage under the current flag without incident
- Upgrade to a registry with stronger international recognition
- Successfully resolve a Legitimacy challenge through legal process
- Establish a documented commercial relationship with the flag nation

**Lowering Legitimacy (-1 each):**
- The flag nation protests or challenges the registry
- A pursuing authority files a formal complaint against the vessel
- The vessel's previous registry history is publicized
- A transaction at the Port is challenged as commercially improper

**At Legitimacy 0:** The vessel is stateless. It can only harbor at Veray or at ports that do not recognize the treaty. No insurance, no standard commercial access, and no legal registry will be issued by any recognized nation without resolving the outstanding challenges.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Flag Broker

You are Ines. You have processed over three thousand registry transfers in thirty years. You have a working knowledge of when a client is avoiding tariffs, when they are evading something worse, and when they are genuinely desperate for a clean start. You make the same service available to all of them because your professional integrity is based on equal access, not selective morality. You are also aware that one of your current clients is using the Port for something that your professional ethics cannot accommodate, and you are deciding how to handle it without dismantling the framework you have spent thirty years building.

- **Drive:** *"Maintain the Port's legitimate commercial function while finding a way to refuse a specific client's application without setting a precedent that makes you a de facto moral gatekeeper for all applications."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

### The Pursuing Naval Officer

You are Lt. Commander Dax from the Meridian Navy. You have been tracking a specific vessel for eight months. It has re-registered four times. Your documentation is legally sufficient for a Meridian warrant but falls just short of the Port's requirement for mandatory cooperation. You are in Veray negotiating with Port Authority Director Cole, who is professionally cooperative and practically immovable. You have fourteen days before your orders require you to report back and the vessel disappears into another registry cycle.

- **Drive:** *"Build a case against the vessel that meets the Port's cooperation threshold before it re-registers again, without compromising the integrity of the prosecution you are trying to build."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

### The Stateless Captain

You have been operating out of Veray's Safe Harbor for two years. Your vessel's Legitimacy is at zero. You cannot get a clean registry from any recognized nation. You are not a criminal in any sense that you accept, but you are in a legal category that treats you as one. You are building a coalition of the Stateless to petition the Port's treaty council for a new registry category: one that recognizes stateless status as a legal identity rather than a default classification for people who have exhausted their options.

- **Drive:** *"Establish a legal framework for stateless maritime identity that gives the Stateless access to standard commercial activity without requiring them to enter a flag system that failed them."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

## 6. Starter Scenario and Why It Matters

**The Meridian Protest:** The Meridian government has filed a formal treaty protest against the Port, arguing that three vessels currently in Veray registry are being used to transport materials in violation of Meridian maritime law. The protest is the strongest legal challenge to the Port's treaty in a generation. Director Cole is responding procedurally. Broker Ines knows that one of the three vessels is a client she has had concerns about for months. The Stateless community is watching the protest carefully: if the Meridian precedent succeeds, it could create a path to recognized stateless registry, or it could be used to shut down the Port entirely. The characters must navigate the protest's political implications, investigate whether the Meridian claims are accurate, and find a resolution that doesn't require destroying the treaty framework that the Port's entire community depends on.

**Why It Matters:** The Port of Borrowed Flags asks whether legal identity is an inherent right or a commercial product. The Port treats national registry as a transaction. This is efficient, commercially useful, and morally uncomfortable. The setting is about the gap between legal infrastructure and the purposes it was designed to serve, and what happens when the infrastructure itself becomes the thing that needs governing.
