## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The forest is managed. By spirits. The Pajé's job is to interface with the management.

The setting draws on the rich spiritual tradition of Amazonian indigenous peoples: the Curupira is a well-documented spirit of multiple Amazonian and Brazilian traditions -- the guardian of the forest who protects animals from over-hunting, most recognizable by their feet pointing backwards (to confuse those who follow their tracks). The Boto (pink river dolphin) who can take human form at night to seduce humans is another documented tradition. The Mapinguari draws on traditions about an ancient giant sloth and cave-dwelling monster figure.

The Pajé tradition is the shamanic healing and mediation practice across many Amazonian cultures: the person trained to navigate between human and spirit worlds, heal illness with spiritual and herbal knowledge, and maintain the community's relationship with the forest's non-human inhabitants.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Amazon in its full ecological complexity: the flooded forest (igapó) where trees grow in permanent water, the várzea floodplains that shift with the river's annual cycle, the terra firme high forest where light reaches the ground only in scattered columns, and the river systems that are highways, food sources, and spirit roads; the Curupira's tracks in the mud at the forest edge -- pointing the wrong direction.
2. **Sound:** The overwhelming sound complexity of the Amazon: insects, birds, frogs, the river itself, and at night, the sounds that don't fit any familiar category; the Pajé's chanting that maintains the connection to the spirit world; and the specific sounds that mean the forest balance has shifted in one direction or another, readable to those trained to hear it.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The humidity that makes the Amazon feel like breathing water, the smell of the river which changes character completely between seasons, the feel of the forest in dry season versus wet season (two completely different physical environments), the physical reality of a Pajé ceremony which involves herbs and preparations whose effects are real.

## 2. The Spiritual Ecology

### The Curupira
Not a single spirit but a type: forest guardians who protect specific territories. Each has their own personality, specific concerns, and specific triggers. Building working relationships with them requires understanding each one individually. They are not hostile by default -- they are appropriate in their responses to human behavior.

### The Boto
River dolphins who take human form at night to engage with human communities. They are attracted to celebrations and music. Their interactions with humans can be generous or disruptive depending on their mood and the human's behavior. A community in good relationship with the Boto has excellent fishing. A community that has offended them has difficulty on the water.

### The Mapinguari
The deep forest figure: ancient, massive, representing the forest's most primal protective force. The Mapinguari does not engage with human communities under normal circumstances. When it moves toward a settlement, the Curupira's normal management has failed and the situation has become serious.

### The Outside Threat
The setting's central conflict can be the intrusion of extractive industry from outside the spirit compact's area, spiritual problems introduced by displaced communities from other forest zones, or the fragmentation of spirit territory by human activity that the compact's original terms didn't anticipate.

## 3. Adventure Seeds

1. **The Hunter's Transgression:** A hunter from the community took more than their acknowledged portion in a single season, rationalizing it for good reasons. The Curupira is now guiding them in circles when they enter the forest. Resolving this requires acknowledging the transgression to the Curupira directly, making the appropriate acknowledgment, and the community discussing how to prevent similar situations.

2. **The Boto Offense:** A celebration went wrong and a Boto was insulted. The fishing has declined dramatically since. Finding the specific Boto, determining what the offense was (it may not be what anyone thinks), and making appropriate acknowledgment requires navigating a spirit world that does not communicate in direct language.

3. **The Mapinguari's Approach:** The Mapinguari has been sighted within three days' travel. Nothing this close has been seen in living memory. The Pajé must determine what has reached the point of requiring the Mapinguari's direct attention, address it before the spirit arrives, and if it cannot be addressed in time, find a way to explain the situation to a being whose patience for human justification is limited.

4. **The New Community:** A community from a different part of the forest has moved into the area, displaced by flooding. They have their own spirit relationships, their own Pajé tradition, and their own compact. The two communities' spirit compacts are not compatible in all areas. Negotiating a shared territory requires both human and spirit-world diplomacy.

5. **The Healing Journey:** A community member is ill with something the Pajé's herbal knowledge cannot address -- the illness has a spirit component that requires a journey into the spirit world to identify and resolve. The journey is real and dangerous and the illness will progress while the Pajé is traveling. Speed and accuracy both matter.
