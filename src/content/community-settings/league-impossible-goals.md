## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

Goalball is a contact sport played with a leather ball on a field that is part grass, part enchanted stone, and entirely responsive to the emotional state of the spectators. The basic rules are simple: two teams, two goals, and a ball that must be carried, thrown, or kicked into the opposing goal. The advanced rules are determined in real time by the crowd's collective belief, which interacts with the stadium's old enchantments to make the impossible permissible and the ordinary occasionally illegal.

**Tone:** Exhilarating, physically intense, and built on the tension between athletic discipline and theatrical spectacle. The setting is a sports drama about people whose careers depend on being genuinely skilled and genuinely entertaining at the same time, in a game where the audience's enthusiasm can make you superhuman and their boredom can make you clumsy.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Colosseum of Thresh: a massive open-air stadium with stone tiers, enchanted field markings that glow and shift during play, and a crowd of 30,000 whose collective excitement produces a visible shimmer in the air above the stands. The players: muscular, acrobatic, wearing team colors that flash when the crowd's belief is high. The impossible plays: a forward leaping twenty feet into the air because the crowd believed she could, a goalkeeper deflecting a shot with a barrier of solidified sound because the fans were chanting his name.
2. **Sound:** The roar of the crowd, which is not just noise but a physical force: high belief produces a sound that vibrates the field and the players. The enchanted whistles, which sound different depending on the crowd's interpretation of the rules. The crack of a ball striking a goal post, which in Thresh echoes longer than physics allows because the crowd holds onto the sound.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The grass of the pitch, which is warm when belief is high and cold when the crowd is disengaged. The ball itself, which feels heavier or lighter depending on how many spectators are watching the player holding it. The smell of the stadium: sweat, grass, roasted food from the vendor stalls, and the ozone-sharp scent that accompanies high-belief moments.

## 2. The City

**The Colosseum of Thresh** is the primary stadium: a structure older than the city's written records, built on foundations that contain the enchantments responding to crowd emotion. The Colosseum seats 30,000 and is full for every match. Its groundskeeper, an old woman named Birch, maintains the enchantments through methods she describes as "gardening" and refuses to explain further.

**The Training Grounds** are located outside the city, where teams practice without crowd influence. Practices here follow the basic rules only, which means players must train for two games: the one they play without the crowd's help and the one they play with it. The disconnect between practice and match performance is a constant frustration.

**The Players' Quarter** is a neighborhood near the Colosseum where most professional players live. The Quarter is lively, well-funded, and carries the specific energy of a community whose residents are celebrities with short careers and unpredictable bodies. The Quarter's pub, The Broken Hoop, is where retired players gather to watch matches, critique technique, and nurse injuries that the crowd's belief made possible and ordinary medicine cannot fully heal.

**The Bookmakers' Row** is the city's gambling district, where betting on Goalball is the primary industry. The bookmakers must account for crowd belief as a variable, which makes odds-setting more art than mathematics. The most successful bookmaker, a woman named Ode Garrett, employs a team of "belief scouts" who survey crowd sentiment before each match.

## 3. Factions

**The Thresh Ironhawks** are the city's dominant team, led by captain Renn Salazar, a forward whose combination of athletic skill and crowd charisma has made her the league's most valuable player. Salazar is talented, competitive, and increasingly aware that her body cannot sustain the impossible plays the crowd demands. Her best performances happen when the crowd believes most strongly, which means her most spectacular moments are also her most physically dangerous.

**The League Commission** governs Goalball and manages the tension between sport and spectacle. Commissioner Aldric Voss believes the game should prioritize athletic merit over crowd influence. His proposed rule changes (fixed regulations that override crowd belief) are deeply unpopular with fans and bookmakers. He is not wrong about the safety concerns. He is wrong about the politics.

**The Crowd Shapers** are an informal guild of professional audience influencers: people who sit in strategic sections of the stadium and lead chants, reactions, and emotional responses that direct crowd belief. The best Shaper, a man called Forge, can shift a crowd's belief by two points in a single play. He works for whichever team pays his fee. He considers himself an artist. The Commission considers him a threat to competitive integrity.

**The Broken Players** are retired athletes whose careers ended because of injuries sustained during impossible plays. Their advocate, former goalkeeper Dax Orin, is lobbying for a players' union that would negotiate safety protocols. The problem: safety protocols require limiting crowd belief, and limiting crowd belief makes the game less spectacular, which reduces attendance, which reduces revenue, which threatens the careers of current players.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Crowd Belief

Track **Crowd Belief** from 0 to 10 for each team, starting at 5.

**Raising Belief (+1 each):**
- Execute a spectacular play (acrobatic, creative, or visually impressive)
- Score a goal in a dramatic fashion
- A Crowd Shaper successfully leads a chant or emotional response
- Overcome an adversity (playing through injury, recovering from a deficit)

**Lowering Belief (-1 each):**
- Execute a boring or predictable play
- Commit unsportsmanlike conduct (the crowd turns on players it considers unfair)
- A player is injured and removed (reminds the crowd of the game's real cost)
- Lose momentum (multiple failed plays in succession)

**Belief effects:**
| Belief | Field Response |
|--------|---------------|
| 8-10 | Impossible plays permitted. Players can attempt superhuman feats. The field assists (surfaces become springy, distances compress). |
| 5-7 | Standard enhanced play. Players are slightly stronger, faster, and more precise than baseline. |
| 3-4 | Basic rules only. The enchantments provide no advantage. Standard athletics apply. |
| 1-2 | Crowd doubt. The field resists: distances feel longer, the ball feels heavier, the goal seems smaller. |
| 0 | Dead crowd. The enchantments shut down. The game becomes ordinary sport in a very large stadium. |

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Star Forward

You are the player the crowd comes to see. Your combination of athletic talent and performative instinct makes you the most valuable asset on the field. You know how to read a crowd, how to time a play for maximum dramatic impact, and how to make the impossible look effortless. You also know that every impossible play costs your body something the crowd does not pay for.

- **Drive:** *"Play the best game of my career before my career ends, and figure out whether the crowd loves me or the version of me it creates."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 3 | Finesse 2 | Mind 1 | Presence 2

### The Crowd Shaper

You sit in the stands and shape what the crowd believes. You are not a player. You are a conductor, orchestrating 30,000 people into a force that can bend the rules of the game. You work for hire, which means your loyalty is contractual. You have shaped belief for both the Ironhawks and their rivals. You consider this professional neutrality. The players consider it treachery.

- **Drive:** *"Prove that the crowd is the real player, and negotiate a role in the league that recognizes what I do as essential rather than exploitative."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

### The Retired Goalkeeper

You played for eleven years. Your right shoulder was reconstructed twice because of impossible saves the crowd believed you could make. You made them. Now you cannot lift your arm above your head. You are advocating for a players' union and safety protocols. You are fighting for the careers of players who do not want your protection because they believe, like you believed, that their bodies will hold.

- **Drive:** *"Get the safety protocols passed before another player's career ends the way mine did, and accept that the players I am trying to protect may not thank me."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 1 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The Championship Match:** The Thresh Ironhawks face the Dunwall Lancers in the league championship. Salazar is carrying a knee injury she has not disclosed. Forge has been hired by the Lancers, which means the crowd influence will be working against the Ironhawks for the first time. Commissioner Voss is threatening to implement fixed rules for the championship, which would eliminate impossible plays and reduce the match to standard sport. Dax Orin has gone public with a list of injuries sustained during impossible plays, and the press is asking whether the league is responsible. The characters must navigate the match, manage crowd belief, deal with Salazar's injury, and decide whether winning the championship is worth the cost the game demands.

**Why It Matters:** The League of Impossible Goals asks what happens when an audience's desire becomes a force that physically affects the performers. The players are not just athletes. They are sacrifices to spectacle, performing feats their bodies cannot sustain because the crowd's belief makes the impossible feel easy. The setting is about the relationship between performers and audiences, the cost of entertainment, and the specific cruelty of a system where the more spectacular you are, the shorter your career.
