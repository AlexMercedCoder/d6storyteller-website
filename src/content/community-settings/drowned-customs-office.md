## 1. Setting Overview and Tone

The Drowned Customs House is the most unusual office in the maritime world and the least unusual thing about working in Velas harbor. The staff have been underwater for a century and a half. They are unchanged. Their paperwork requirements have increased in proportion to the inconvenience of submitting it. The city's legal framework treats the Office as fully operational, because the alternative requires admitting that the city's primary customs authority has been technically dead for a hundred and sixty years, and no one in the city government has been willing to open that particular administrative review.

**Tone:** Dry bureaucratic comedy with a genuine surreal underpinning. The humor is in the Office's commitment to procedure in impossible circumstances, not in mockery of the staff, who are managing their situation with admirable professionalism. The horror, when it surfaces, is subtle: what does it mean that they are still there?

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The harbor surface above the Office: a cluster of buoys marking the document line, a dedicated dock where submitters lower their cases into the water, and the speaking tube terminals (brass pipes emerging from the harbor floor at a gentle angle) where conversations with the clerks can be conducted. Below the surface: the Customs House itself, visible in clear water at about thirty feet, its windows intact and lit by a light source the divers who occasionally inspect it have not been able to identify.
2. **Sound:** The speaking tube communication system: voices from forty feet below arriving slightly compressed and with the particular quality of sound that has traveled through standing water. The bell system that the Office uses to signal processing completions: a specific pattern of chimes that experienced harbor workers can interpret without checking the codebook.
3. **Touch and Smell:** The document cases: waterproof cases of brass and treated leather, filled with forms and manifests, lowered on a rope through forty feet of salt water. The waterproofing compound has a specific smell that Velas harbor workers associate with bureaucratic completion. The cases come back slightly wet and often with a faint smell of old wood from the Customs House below.

## 2. The Office

**The Document Line Dock** is the primary interface between the harbor and the Office: a long dock with twelve lowering stations, each with a rope, pulley, and communication bell. The Line has a queue system that is enforced with institutional seriousness by the Dock Coordinator, a surface employee who technically works for the Office but has never been underwater.

**The Speaking Tube Terminal** allows direct verbal communication with specific clerks. The tubes were installed in 1891 by a city engineer who was given detailed instructions by the Office and who declined to explain afterward how he had received those instructions. There are fourteen tubes, each labeled with a clerk's name.

**The Surface Liaison Office** is a small building at the harbor's edge where the Office's surface representative, Customs Agent Wren, handles cases that cannot be processed through the standard document line. Wren is employed by the Office, visits the underwater facility weekly via diving equipment, and considers his commute a manageable inconvenience.

**The Commissioner's Chamber** is visible through the Customs House's main window in clear water: the Commissioner's desk, the Commissioner's lamp, and the Commissioner's paperwork. The Commissioner has not directly communicated with the surface in forty years. All Commissioner communications are issued through the Office's standard document processing system.

## 3. Factions

**The Drowned Staff** are the Customs House's original employees: approximately thirty clerks, inspectors, and the Commissioner, who have been processing maritime filings since 1847. Their correspondence is professional, their procedures are meticulous, and their institutional knowledge of maritime law is extensive because they have had a century and a half to refine it. They do not discuss their situation. They process paperwork.

**The Merchant Community** has adapted to the Office's operational requirements with the pragmatic flexibility of people who need their cargo cleared and cannot afford an indefinite wait. There is an informal market for "filing expertise": traders who know which clerks respond fastest, which queries can be preemptively addressed, and how to format a document to minimize the likelihood of a Depth Review hold.

**The City Customs Authority** is the above-water administrative body that technically supervises the Drowned Office. Commissioner General Tomas Dale has been filing supervisory reports on the Office for fourteen years without ever directly communicating with the underwater Commissioner. His reports describe the Office's operations as "irregular but functional" and recommend against administrative review on grounds of operational continuity.

**The Reform Advocates** are a small group of lawyers and commercial interests who have been arguing that the city needs a new customs office: one that is on land, staffed by people with regular life spans, and subject to normal administrative oversight. Their leader, attorney Sela Pell, has filed three petitions with the city council. All three have been referred to the Commissioner General for review. He has not responded.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Processing Depth

Track **Processing Depth** for each customs transaction.

**Depth Delay categories:**
| Filing Type | Base Delay |
|-------------|------------|
| Standard cargo manifest, clean documentation | 4 hours |
| Complex cargo (mixed origins, multiple stops) | 2 days |
| Contested filing (disputed duty category, conflicting documentation) | 1-2 weeks |
| Exceptional filing (unusual cargo, first-time filer, missing documentation) | "Pending Depth Review" - indefinite |

**Reducing Depth Delay:**
- Relationship with specific clerk (Presence roll): -1 day
- Pre-filed supplementary documentation: -half base delay
- Surface Liaison Agent Wren facilitates: -1 step down from current category
- Bribe (technically a "processing facilitation fee" but the Office has no record of accepting these): mechanical effect unclear, historically mixed results

**Depth Review:** When a filing is placed in Depth Review, a character must investigate to determine what the Office needs. This requires either diving to the Office, communicating via speaking tube, or waiting for a query to come up on the document line. Each query answered correctly reduces the hold. Each query answered incorrectly adds a new query.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Filing Expert

You have been clearing cargo through the Drowned Office for fifteen years and you know more about its operational quirks than the City Customs Authority does. You know which clerks prefer which document formats, which tube to use for urgent requests, and how to word a filing to avoid the three most common query triggers. You make a very comfortable living teaching this knowledge to traders who cannot afford indefinite processing delays. You have never been to the Commissioner's chamber. You have never asked why the staff are still there.

- **Drive:** *"Maintain the practical relationships and procedural knowledge that make you the most valuable person in the harbor, while keeping enough distance from the Office's deeper nature to sleep comfortably."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### The Reform Lawyer

You are Sela Pell. You have filed three petitions and received three referrals to a Commissioner General who has no functional authority over an office that has been underwater for a hundred and sixty years. You are building a legal case for requiring the city to establish a new, land-based customs authority. The Merchant Community is split: some want reform, some benefit too much from the current system's predictable irrationality to want it changed. You need a specific incident to trigger the council into action.

- **Drive:** *"Demonstrate that the current arrangement is legally untenable and operationally dangerous in a way specific enough to force council action, without accidentally destroying the functional customs system that currently exists."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### The Surface Liaison

You are Wren. You dive to the Customs House every week. You have sat across from the Commissioner's window. You have not been invited into the Chamber. You deliver documents, answer queries, and return to the surface with the completeness of someone who has compartmentalized his understanding of his own job into "what I do" and "why I do not think too hard about what I do." Something in the last three visits has suggested that the Commissioner wants to communicate something that the standard document system does not accommodate. You are not sure you want to find out what it is.

- **Drive:** *"Determine what the Commissioner is trying to communicate and decide whether you are the right person to carry that message to the surface, or whether some messages are better received at depth."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

## 6. Starter Scenario and Why It Matters

**The Commissioner's Query:** A filing came up the document line with an unusual notation: instead of a standard query about the submitted cargo manifest, it is addressed directly to the Surface Liaison Office with the notation "For Director Attention - Not for Standard Processing." Inside is a document that appears to be a formal request from the Commissioner to the city council, describing a situation in the Office that requires "surface administrative intervention" and requesting a meeting. This is the first direct communication from the Commissioner in forty years. Reform Lawyer Sela Pell has heard about the document and is asking to see it. The Merchant Community's filing expert is worried that any administrative intervention will disrupt the processing system they depend on. The City Customs Authority Commissioner General has been notified and has called a meeting for which he has not yet determined an agenda. The characters must determine what is happening in the Customs House, manage the competing interests that want to control the response, and decide whether the Commissioner's request represents an opportunity for reform or the beginning of something none of the current frameworks can handle.

**Why It Matters:** The Drowned Customs Office is about the institutional persistence of systems past their original context. The Customs House is operating. The question of why it is still operating, and what it means that the staff are still there, has been administratively avoided for a hundred and sixty years because the system works and addressing the underlying situation would require confronting something the city's legal framework was not designed to accommodate. The setting is about the specific inertia of institutions that function despite being fundamentally impossible.
