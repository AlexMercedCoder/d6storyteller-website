## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** Spirits are not the enemy -- the new spirits are, and the old spirits are asking for help.

The setting draws on the Zar spirit possession tradition of the Horn of Africa, practiced alongside Ethiopian Christianity in a complex spiritual ecology where possession by spirits is real, healers specialize in negotiation rather than exorcism, and the spirit world has its own community dynamics.

The Ethiopian context is rich: the ancient Aksumite civilization, the tradition of the Ark of the Covenant's presence at Axum, the Kebra Nagast (Ethiopian holy text), Lake Tana's monasteries, the Blue Nile's source at the highlands, and the distinctive Christian tradition that pre-dates Rome.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Ethiopian highland plateau at its most dramatic: volcanic landscape, the shimmer of Lake Tana, the Blue Nile cutting through deep gorges, ancient churches carved from living rock, and Zar healing ceremonies lit by incense and attended by community members who understand their purpose even if they do not all participate.
2. **Sound:** The distinctive liturgical chant of the Ethiopian Orthodox Church that forms the spiritual backdrop, the drumming and incantation of a Zar ceremony, the specific sound of a person whose speech is influenced by a riding spirit (different from their normal voice in ways the healer recognizes), and the deep silence of the highlands before dawn.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The highland chill, the smoke of incense used in both church and healing ceremony, the physical reality of a Zar healing negotiation (a communal event, not a private one, with physical contact and community witness), and the smell of coffee prepared in the traditional Ethiopian ceremony that opens many important conversations.

## 2. The Zar Tradition

### The Balazar (Zar Healers)
Specialists who have developed Rapport with the Zar spirit community through years of practice. They diagnose possession, identify the specific spirit, negotiate its demands, and facilitate the ongoing relationship between spirit and host. The balazar does not remove spirits unilaterally -- they facilitate agreement.

### The Zar Spirits
A diverse community of spirits with different origins, personalities, and needs. Some come from specific geographic locations. Some represent historical figures or events. Some are ancient beyond memory. They have their own hierarchy, their own politics, and their own concerns -- including significant concern about the new foreign spirits disrupting their traditional arrangements.

### The Church
The Ethiopian Orthodox Church exists alongside the Zar tradition in complex relationship -- officially opposed, practically entangled, with many community members practicing both. Some priests can diagnose possession. Some balazar have deep church training. The relationship between traditions is one of the setting's living tensions.

### The Foreign Spirits
Something new: spirits that do not respond to traditional negotiation, that arrive with no established relationship to the local spirit community, and that ride their hosts in ways that damage rather than challenge. Their origin and the person who introduced them are the mystery at the heart of the setting.

## 3. Adventure Seeds

1. **The Merchant's Cargo:** A merchant who recently returned from a distant trade route is possessed by something none of the local balazar recognize. The spirit will not communicate in any known spirit language. Its behavior is escalating. The merchant's family is desperate. The players must determine the spirit's origin and find a way to negotiate with something outside their tradition.

2. **The Village Spread:** Multiple people in a single village have been ridden by unfamiliar spirits within a week. The pattern suggests a single source. Investigation reveals someone in the village specifically invited these spirits in, without understanding what they were doing or what they were dealing with.

3. **The Spirit Testimony:** A long-established Zar spirit with whom a healer has excellent Rapport approaches them voluntarily: the spirit has information about where the foreign spirits came from, but sharing it requires the healer to do something the spirit has been waiting years to ask. The request is difficult. The information is critical.

4. **The Priest's Dilemma:** A local priest has been possessed. He knows it. He is maintaining his duties, his possession not yet visible to his congregation. He has come to the balazar for help but is demanding complete secrecy -- the damage to his community if the possession becomes known would be severe. How does the healer serve both the man and the community?

5. **The Source:** Following the foreign spirits back to their origin reveals a human agent who acquired them from a distant tradition and introduced them without understanding the local spiritual ecology. The agent is not malicious -- they thought they were bringing healing tools. The damage they've done is real. What is the appropriate response?
