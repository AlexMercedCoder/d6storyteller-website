## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

The Undercourt occupies a converted cistern beneath the financial district: a vaulted stone space with a competition floor, spectator seating carved into the walls, and a betting board that tracks odds in real time. The operation is managed by a woman known as the Conductor, who selects the games, verifies the disputes, and ensures that the results are honored.

**Tone:** Tense, morally gray, and built on the irony that the city's most powerful people solve their problems in its most illegal venue. The Undercourt is a crime. It is also the only institution in the city where disputes are settled honestly, because the participants cannot afford the exposure of disagreeing with the result.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Undercourt at game time: stone walls lit by industrial floodlights, a competition floor that changes configuration between matches, and an audience of well-dressed people who should not be there. The champions: professional athletes, retired soldiers, and hired specialists who compete under aliases.
2. **Sound:** The echo of the cistern, which amplifies every impact and muffles every conversation. The crowd's restrained reactions (applause is quiet; these people do not want to be heard). The Conductor's announcements, delivered in a formal tone that treats illegal combat like a corporate arbitration.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The cold damp of underground stone. The heat of the competition floor under the floodlights. The smell of old water, sweat, and the expensive cologne of spectators who dressed for their offices and came underground.

## 2. The Undercourt

**The Competition Floor** adapts to each game: padded for combat, configured with obstacles for agility trials, or cleared for endurance contests. The floor's versatility is the Conductor's innovation.

**The Sponsors' Gallery** is a raised platform where the disputing parties sit, watching their champions compete for their interests. The Gallery is separated from the floor by enough distance for deniability.

**The Champions' Quarters** are a series of rooms where competing athletes prepare. The quarters are functional: a cot, a locker, and a mirror. Champions are paid well, treated well, and understood to be disposable if they talk.

**The Vault** stores the documentation of every dispute and its result. The Vault is the Undercourt's insurance: if any participant reneges on a result, the documentation can be released to the press, the police, or the tax authority. The Conductor calls this "mutual assured discretion."

--

## 3. Factions

**The Conductor** runs the Undercourt with the precision of a corporate executive. Her real name is not known. Her reputation is: fair, ruthless, and absolutely reliable. She takes a percentage of every dispute and maintains the Vault. She considers herself a public servant for people who cannot use public services.

**The Champions** are the athletes who compete. Most are professionals: former military, competitive fighters, or specialists recruited for specific games. Champion coordinator Ellis Vane manages the roster and handles the ethical gray area of hiring people to fight other people's battles.

**The Clients** are the powerful people who bring their disputes underground. They include corporate executives, political operatives, crime bosses, and occasionally diplomats. They pay well. They expect discretion. They treat the Undercourt as a necessary service, which it is.

**Detective Raya Patel** of the city police has been investigating unusual patterns in dispute resolution among the city's elite. She does not know the Undercourt exists. She knows that corporate rivals are settling disagreements with suspicious speed and without legal proceedings, and she is following the money.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Heat Clock

Track **Heat** from 0 to 6, starting at 1.

**Heat rises (+1 each):**
- A participant discusses the Undercourt outside its walls
- A game produces injuries visible enough to require public medical treatment
- Detective Patel discovers a new lead
- A client reneges on a result, creating a dispute about the dispute

**Heat drops (-1 each):**
- The Conductor successfully contains a security breach
- A client honors a result that was costly, demonstrating the system works
- The champions maintain discretion despite external pressure
- Misleading information is fed to Detective Patel's investigation

**At Heat 6:** The Undercourt is compromised. Law enforcement raids the cistern. All documentation in the Vault becomes evidence. Every client, champion, and spectator faces exposure.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Professional Champion

You fight other people's battles in an underground arena. You are paid well, treated respectably, and understood to be a tool. You are good at what you do. You are also a person with opinions about the disputes you settle, opinions your clients do not pay for and do not want.

- **Drive:** *"Win enough matches to retire, and decide whether the Undercourt's version of justice is better than the one it replaced."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 3 | Finesse 2 | Mind 1 | Presence 2

### The Investigating Detective

You know powerful people are settling disputes without lawyers. You do not know how. You are methodical, persistent, and increasingly close to discovering an institution that several hundred influential citizens need to remain hidden.

- **Drive:** *"Find the Undercourt, and then decide whether shutting it down serves justice or just sends the disputes back to a legal system that was not resolving them either."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### The Match Coordinator

You manage the Undercourt's operations: scheduling games, recruiting champions, and maintaining the security protocols that keep the operation hidden. You work for the Conductor. You believe in the Undercourt's function. You are also watching Heat rise and calculating how many more games you can run before the operation is compromised.

- **Drive:** *"Keep the Undercourt running long enough for it to matter, and find a way to reduce Heat without reducing the service that powerful people clearly need."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The Political Match:** A sitting senator and a rival political operative have brought a dispute to the Undercourt: control of a voting district. The stakes are higher than any previous match. The senator's champion is a former military officer. The operative's champion is a street fighter with no professional record and an unusual combat style that Ellis Vane cannot assess. Detective Patel has independently discovered that the senator's recent political moves correlate with outcomes of unknown origin. Heat is at 3. The Conductor is considering whether to run the match (the fee is enormous) or refuse it (the exposure risk is too high).

**Why It Matters:** The Stadium Below asks whether justice can exist outside the law. The Undercourt is illegal. It is also honest, efficient, and used by the same powerful people who fund the legal system that was not resolving their problems. The setting is about the gap between legitimate and effective, and the specific irony of a criminal operation that provides the fairest outcomes in the city.
