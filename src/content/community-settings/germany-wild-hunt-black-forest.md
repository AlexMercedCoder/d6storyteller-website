## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The **Thirty Years' War** (1618-1648) killed an estimated eight million people in the German-speaking lands-roughly a third of the population. Entire regions were depopulated. Villages were burned. Mercenary armies roamed without allegiance, plundering and murdering at will. Into this catastrophe, the **Black Forest** (Schwarzwald) serves as both refuge and trap: a vast, ancient woodland so dense that sunlight rarely reaches the forest floor, where the old Germanic spirits never truly left despite centuries of Christianization.

In this setting, the war has done more than kill humans. The sheer volume of death, grief, and spiritual anguish has torn the boundary between the mortal world (*Mittelerde*) and the old spirit realm. **Wodan** (the southern Germanic form of Odin) leads the **Wild Hunt** (*Wilde Jagd*) across the winter sky, collecting the unburied war dead for his spectral host. The creatures of fairy tales-not the gentle versions published by the Brothers Grimm centuries later, but the original, brutal oral traditions-are walking the forest: wolves that speak in human voices, grandmothers with iron teeth, children made of gingerbread who eat people back, and a spinning woman at the crossroads who trades futures for names.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** Cathedral-scale pine and fir trees so tall and dense they create a permanent twilight at ground level, the rare beam of sunlight cutting through like a golden blade; the Wild Hunt visible as a streak of blue-white lightning across the winter sky, with the shapes of horsemen and hounds visible in the afterimage; a gingerbread house that is undeniably beautiful, undeniably warm, and undeniably wrong, standing in a clearing where no house should be; the glowing eyes of a wolf that is far too large, watching from exactly the edge of firelight range.
2. **Sound:** The howling of the Wild Hunt's spectral hounds, a sound like wind through empty churches that makes dogs whimper and horses bolt; the eerie silence of a forest clearing where even the birds have fled; the distant crack of musket fire from an unknown battle that may or may not still be happening; the voice of a child singing a counting rhyme in a dialect that hasn't been spoken for five hundred years.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The sharp, resinous scent of Black Forest pine and the smoky warmth of a Kachelofen (tiled oven) in a half-ruined farmhouse; the bone-deep cold of a German winter that kills as efficiently as any army; the unsettling warmth of gingerbread walls that pulse slightly, like breathing; the iron-tang scent that warns of Frau Holle's proximity.

**The Core Tension:** Civilization vs. the Wild. The war has destroyed the structures that kept the old powers contained: churches, monasteries, villages, the daily rituals of normal life. The forest is reclaiming what was taken from it. The characters must decide whether to rebuild the boundaries or accept that the old powers are part of the world and learn to live alongside them.

## 2. Historical & Mythological Context

The Thirty Years' War was a catastrophe of almost unimaginable scale for the German-speaking world. Started as a religious conflict between Catholic and Protestant states, it devolved into a general European war that devastated the Holy Roman Empire. The Black Forest region (in modern Baden-Württemberg) was particularly hard-hit, with population losses exceeding 60% in some areas.

Germanic folklore preserves a rich pre-Christian mythology that survived Christianization in fragmented form. The **Wild Hunt** (*Wilde Jagd*) is one of the most widespread motifs in Northern European folklore-a spectral procession of the dead, led by Wodan/Odin, that rides the winter storms. **Frau Holle** (Mother Holle) is a powerful earth-goddess figure who rewards the industrious and punishes the lazy, associated with spinning, snowfall, and the boundary between life and death. The fairy tales collected by the **Brothers Grimm** in the early 19th century draw from oral traditions centuries older, originally far more violent and psychologically complex than the published versions.

> **Note on Respectful Portrayal:** Germanic folklore, Romani cultural traditions, and the historical trauma of the Thirty Years' War are treated with respect in this setting. The Romani character archetype is presented with sensitivity to the historical persecution of Romani people in Europe.

## 3. Core Factions & Traditions

### The Wise Women's Circle (Kräuterfrauen)
Herbalist healers and folk practitioners who maintain the oldest spiritual traditions of the Black Forest.
- **Cultural Association:** The *Kräuterfrauen* (herb-women) are the living repository of pre-Christian plant lore, midwifery, weather-reading, and spirit negotiation. They are not witches in the inquisitorial sense (they practice no diabolism) but are routinely targeted by witch-hunters who cannot distinguish between folk healing and sorcery. They maintain a network of hidden herb gardens, forest shelters, and coded communication through carved symbols on trees.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I know 200 medicinal plants by touch in complete darkness, and which twelve will kill you"*
  - *"The forest speaks to me through the behavior of its fungi. When the toadstools grow in circles, something old is waking."*
  - *"I survived three witch-trial interrogations by knowing more about anatomy than the inquisitor's physician"*

### The Waldläufer (Forest Runners)
Deserters, refugees, and outlaws who have retreated into the Black Forest to escape the war.
- **Cultural Association:** A loose, desperate community of former soldiers, displaced villagers, and fugitives who have learned to survive in the deep forest. They know the paths, the dangers, and the old rules (never leave the path after dark, never answer a voice calling your name from the trees, always leave bread for Frau Holle at the crossroads). They are not romantic outlaws; they are traumatized survivors making impossible choices.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I deserted after Magdeburg. I have seen what armies do to cities. I will never serve a general again."*
  - *"I know every hidden path in the Schwarzwald because I have been running for three years"*
  - *"The fairy-tale wolves do not attack me anymore. I think they have decided I am one of them."*

### The Romani Seers (Sinti Drabarni)
Romani travelers who possess hereditary second sight and serve as intermediaries between the mortal world and the spirit realm.
- **Cultural Association:** The Sinti Romani communities of Central Europe maintained oral traditions of divination, healing, and spirit communication that predated their arrival in the German lands. Their *drabarni* (healers/seers) possess genuine spiritual sight and are the few people who can perceive the Wild Hunt's riders clearly. They are doubly persecuted-by the war and by anti-Romani prejudice-yet their spiritual gifts make them invaluable guides through the supernatural chaos.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I see the Wild Hunt as clearly as you see the sun. It is not a metaphor. It is an army of the dead, and it is recruiting."*
  - *"My grandmother taught me the old roads-not the physical ones, but the spirit roads that the Romani have walked for centuries"*
  - *"I can read futures in fire, and lately every fire shows the same thing: the forest is hungry"*

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Waldgeist (Forest Spirit Bond)

Characters track a **Waldgeist Meter** (1-6):

- **1-2 (Outsider / Fremdling):** The character is a stranger to the forest. The forest is merely trees. +1d6 to Presence and Mind in human social situations. The character cannot perceive fairy-tale entities or spirit paths. Safe from forest spirits but also unable to navigate the deep woods (easily lost, vulnerable to natural hazards).
- **3-4 (Forest-Touched / Waldkind):** The character is accepted by the forest as a resident. They can find hidden paths, identify which clearings are safe and which are claimed by old powers, and perceive fairy-tale entities in their true forms. +1d6 to Finesse in forest terrain. Costs 1 Momentum per scene. **Risk:** The character's moral compass begins to shift toward the forest's values: patience, ruthlessness, and indifference to human political conflicts.
- **5-6 (Forest-Bound / Waldseele):** +1d6 to Force and Finesse in forest terrain. The character can command plant growth, speak with animals, and walk spirit paths through the deep forest that bypass physical distance. They can negotiate with the Wild Hunt as a peer. **Risk:** The character is becoming a forest entity. They lose interest in human affairs. They begin to view the war, the suffering, and even their own companions with increasing detachment. At Waldgeist 6, the character must choose: leave the forest and lose all connection, or merge with the Waldgeist and become a permanent forest spirit.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### Archetype 1: The Root-Woman of Todtnau
- **Concept:** A wise woman herbalist who has served the village of Todtnau for twenty years. The village was burned by Swedish mercenaries. She led the survivors into the deep forest and has kept them alive for two years with her plant knowledge, her weather sense, and her willingness to negotiate with entities that no priest would acknowledge. She is practical, exhausted, and carrying the weight of forty lives.
- **Quote:** *"God helps those who help themselves, and Frau Holle helps those who leave bread at the crossroads. I am not particular about which heaven my help comes from."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Twenty years of Black Forest herbalism-I can cure plague symptoms with pine bark and spider silk"*, *"Led forty survivors into the deep forest and kept them alive for two winters"*, *"Frau Holle accepted my offering and now the snow falls lighter on our camp"*, *"I have been denounced as a witch three times. Each time, the accuser died of something entirely natural."*
- **Drive:** *"Keep my people alive until the war ends, using whatever power the forest offers."*
- **Flaw:** *"I am making bargains with forest powers I do not fully understand, and the debt is accumulating."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### Archetype 2: The Deserter of Magdeburg
- **Concept:** A former Imperial soldier who witnessed the sack of Magdeburg in 1631-the worst massacre of the Thirty Years' War, where over 20,000 civilians were killed. He deserted, walked into the Black Forest, and has been living as a Waldläufer for three years. He is a skilled fighter but refuses to kill humans. He will fight fairy-tale monsters, wolves, and Wild Hunt riders without hesitation, but the sight of a uniform-any uniform-makes his hands shake.
- **Quote:** *"I used to think the worst thing in the forest was the wolf. Then I remembered what I saw at Magdeburg. The wolf is honest about what it is."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Veteran swordsman who refuses to draw steel against another human being"*, *"Three years in the deep forest have given me the survival instincts of a wild animal"*, *"The fairy-tale wolves recognize me as a fellow predator, which is deeply uncomfortable"*, *"I sleep with one eye open because the Wild Hunt passed over our camp last Thursday"*.
- **Drive:** *"Protect the innocent refugees in the forest, and find redemption for what I failed to prevent at Magdeburg."*
- **Flaw:** *"PTSD from the massacre renders me unreliable in any situation that echoes organized human violence."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 3 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 1

### Archetype 3: The Romani Fire-Reader
- **Concept:** A young Sinti Romani woman with powerful second sight who can see the Wild Hunt clearly, read futures in fire, and perceive the true forms of fairy-tale entities. Her family's caravan was attacked by mercenaries. She survived alone and joined the Root-Woman's refugee band in the forest. She is the most spiritually gifted person in the group and the most socially vulnerable.
- **Quote:** *"I told the soldiers their futures in fire. I showed them flames. They saw their own deaths. They ran. This does not make me a witch. This makes me honest."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Second sight lets me see the Wild Hunt, fairy-tale entities, and the dead as clearly as the living"*, *"Fire-reading shows me possible futures, but I cannot control which possibilities I see"*, *"I know the old Romani spirit roads that predate every kingdom in Europe"*, *"The wise women's herbs ground me; without them, the visions overwhelm me"*.
- **Drive:** *"Find the other survivors of my family's caravan and build a new community that is safe from both armies and inquisitors."*
- **Flaw:** *"My visions are becoming stronger and harder to control, and I am terrified of what happens when I can no longer tell the vision world from the real one."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

## 6. Condition Palette

**Physical Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Winter Chill* (hypothermia onset), *Fairy-Thorn Scratch* (from enchanted brambles), *Smoke-Lungs* (from hiding in burning villages).
- **Moderate:** *Wild Hunt Wind-Burn* (frost damage from the spectral gale), *Mercenary Sword Wound*, *Iron-Tooth Bite* (from a Grimm entity).
- **Severe:** *Claimed by the Hunt* (the Wild Hunt has marked you for collection on the next ride), *Forest Consumption* (the Waldgeist is physically absorbing you-bark growing on skin), *War Madness* (complete psychological collapse from accumulated trauma).

**Social Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Deserter's Shame* (former comrades view you with contempt), *Witch Suspicion* (villagers whisper about your herb knowledge).
- **Moderate:** *Inquisitor's Warrant* (a witch-hunter has your name), *Forest Exile* (the refugee community has cast you out for fraternizing with fairy-tale entities).
- **Severe:** *Romani Persecution* (targeted specifically for ethnic identity), *Denounced as Diabolist* (formal witch-trial proceedings have begun in absentia).

**Psychological Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Grimm Dread* (fairy tales are supposed to be stories, and the fact that they are not is deeply unsettling), *Wild Hunt Insomnia* (you cannot sleep during winter storms because of the spectral sounds).
- **Moderate:** *Magdeburg Flashbacks* (any scene of organized violence triggers full sensory recall), *Forest Detachment* (high Waldgeist is eroding your emotional connections to humans).
- **Severe:** *The Root-Woman's Debt* (the forest powers you have bargained with are collecting, and the price is always more than you expected), *Wodan's Eye* (the leader of the Wild Hunt has noticed you personally, and his one-eyed gaze follows you even in daylight).

## 7. Starter Scenario: The Twelve Nights of the Wild Hunt

**Framing:** It is December 1634. The Root-Woman's refugee camp of forty souls is hidden in a deep Black Forest valley. A Swedish raiding party of twenty mercenaries is approaching from the north, following trails of smoke. From the south, a lone witch-hunter and his assistant are tracking the Root-Woman by following rumor and denunciation. And overhead, Wodan's Wild Hunt is about to begin its annual Twelve Night ride (from Christmas Eve to Epiphany), during which it will pass directly over the valley every night for twelve consecutive nights, collecting any soul that is outdoors and unprotected.

The characters have twelve nights to protect the camp from three simultaneous threats: the mercenaries, the inquisitor, and the Wild Hunt. The forest itself may help-but its help always has conditions.

**3-Layer Scene Design:**

1. **Immediate Threat:** The Swedish mercenaries will reach the camp in 3 beats. They are experienced, well-armed, and desperate for food and shelter. They will kill or enslave anyone they find. The Deserter knows how mercenary companies operate and can predict their movements, but confronting them directly risks breaking his oath against human violence.

2. **Ticking Clock:** The Wild Hunt rides overhead tonight at midnight. Every night for twelve nights, anyone outdoors without protection will be swept up. The Root-Woman can create a protective ward, but it requires herbs that only grow in a fairy-tale glade guarded by a wolf that speaks in riddles. The Fire-Reader can see which nights will be the worst, but her visions are fragmentary.

3. **Opportunity:** Frau Holle has appeared at the crossroads near the camp. She offers a bargain: she will shroud the valley in snow so deep that neither mercenaries nor inquisitors can reach it for twelve days. In exchange, she wants the Root-Woman to spin flax at her spinning wheel for one night-during which Frau Holle will judge the Root-Woman's character. If judged worthy, the snow falls as protection. If judged unworthy, the snow falls as burial.

**Antagonist:** ***Frau Holle*** - Not a villain but a cosmic judge. She is one of the oldest entities in Germanic mythology-possibly a remnant of a pre-Christian earth goddess. She rewards the hardworking and punishes the lazy with absolute, impersonal fairness. She does not care about the war, the armies, or human politics. She cares about whether a person does their work honestly. She is testing the Root-Woman, and through her, the entire refugee community.
- **Traits:** Power over snow, ice, and the boundary between life and death; sees through all lies and pretension; rewards genuine effort and punishes genuine laziness; indifferent to human suffering (she is a natural force, not a sympathetic ally); weakness to genuine humility and honest labor.
- **Condition Track:** ☐ Observing (testing the Root-Woman through the spinning trial) / ☐ Judging (evaluating the community's worthiness based on how they treat each other) / ☐ Worthy (the snow falls as protection, sheltering the valley) / ☐ Unworthy (the snow falls as punishment, burying the valley).
- **Shadow Cost:** Frau Holle's protection lasts exactly twelve days. After that, the mercenaries and the inquisitor will still be searching. The long-term survival of the community requires a permanent solution that Frau Holle will not provide-she helps once, not forever. The characters must use the twelve days of protection to build a more lasting defense.

## 8. Why This Setting Matters

The Black Forest and its fairy tales represent one of humanity's oldest attempts to make sense of the terrifying power of nature and the darkness in the human heart. The Grimm fairy tales, in their original forms, are not children's stories; they are survival guides for a world where wolves speak, forests swallow travelers, and the line between the living and the dead is a matter of seasonal weather. Setting these tales during the Thirty Years' War-the moment when European civilization nearly consumed itself-creates a powerful resonance: when human civilization fails, the old powers return, and the question is not whether to engage with them but how to do so without losing what makes us human. The Root-Woman's answer-practical wisdom, community bonds, and the willingness to negotiate with powers she cannot control-is a model for surviving any catastrophe.
