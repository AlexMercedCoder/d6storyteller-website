> *"The sky rained for free for ten thousand years. Then someone figured out how to catch it in a jar, and the rest of us have been paying ever since."*
> - Farmer's saying, Lower Pelarch

--

## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

Pelarch's economy runs on water. Not water from rivers or wells, which still exist, but the rain itself: the storms, the seasonal showers, the gentle overnight drizzle that feeds crops and fills reservoirs. Stormcallers learned to harvest clouds three centuries ago. Within a generation, the Crown established the Rain Market, where precipitation is auctioned to the highest bidder. The market is efficient. It is also deeply unjust: wealthy cities and noble estates receive reliable rainfall while farming villages in the lower valley watch their wells drop and their fields crack.

**Tone:** Earthy, political, and infused with the particular anger of people who watch rain fall on someone else's land. The setting is a rural drama as much as a fantasy one. Characters argue about water rights at village meetings, calculate whether they can afford this month's allotment, and make the kind of compromises that farming communities make when the alternative is watching crops die.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Rain Market at auction: an open-air pavilion where brokers bid on clouds visible overhead, each one tagged with a Stormcaller's seal and a volume estimate. The contrast between Upper Pelarch (green, irrigated, prosperous) and Lower Pelarch (brown, cracked, stubborn). A Stormcaller at work: standing on a hilltop with arms raised, pulling moisture from the air into a visible column that they direct toward a paying customer's land.
2. **Sound:** Thunder directed rather than wild, rolling precisely over a purchased field. The auctioneer's cadence at the Market, calling lot numbers and cloud grades. The silence in a dry village at midday, when the heat presses conversation flat and the well is two buckets from empty.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The difference between purchased rain (which arrives on schedule, at a consistent temperature, and smells clean) and wild rain (unpredictable, warm, and carrying the smell of the earth it has crossed). Cracked soil under bare feet. The coolness of a newly filled reservoir, which in Lower Pelarch is a community event worth celebrating.

## 2. The Valley

**The Rain Market** occupies a plateau at the center of the valley. Its auction floor is open to the sky so bidders can see the available clouds. Surrounding the floor are brokerage offices, Stormcaller guildhalls, Crown tax offices, and the licensed weighing stations where rain volume is verified after delivery. The Market operates daily. Prices fluctuate with season, demand, and political mood.

**Upper Pelarch** is the wealthy district: noble estates, Crown facilities, and commercial farms that have long-term rain contracts. The land is green year-round. Its residents consider regular rainfall a natural condition rather than a purchased service, which is technically true and practically dishonest.

**Lower Pelarch** is the farming country that cannot afford Market prices. Its villages rely on well water, seasonal runoff, and whatever wild weather escapes the Stormcallers' nets. Lower Pelarch feeds Upper Pelarch. Upper Pelarch waters itself first. The arrangement has been stable for decades and is currently breaking down.

**The Dry Marches** are the outer edge of the valley, where Stormcaller harvesting has depleted the natural weather cycle entirely. No rain falls in the Marches. Ever. The few communities that remain survive on deep wells and traded water. The Crown considers the Marches uninhabited. The Marches disagree.

## 3. Factions

**The Crown Weather Office** regulates the Market, licenses Stormcallers, and collects a 15% tax on every rain sale. Its director, Lord Sable, is a competent administrator who genuinely believes market-based water distribution is more efficient than the pre-Market chaos. He is aware that the system creates hardship in Lower Pelarch. He considers the hardship a pricing problem, not a structural one.

**The Stormcallers' Guild** harvests and sells weather. Its members are skilled, well-paid, and increasingly uncomfortable with the work. Senior Stormcaller Dessa Wren has proposed a public rain reserve, funded by Guild contributions, that would guarantee minimum rainfall to every community. The Guild's commercial faction has blocked the proposal because a rain reserve would lower Market prices.

**The Dry Council** represents Lower Pelarch and the Dry Marches. Its speaker, a farmer named Carth Oakes, has been petitioning the Crown for subsidized rain for three years. The petitions have been acknowledged and ignored. Carth is running out of patience and beginning to listen to the people in his movement who argue that petitions are a tool for people who already have water.

**The Rain Runners** are smugglers who steal rain from purchased storms and redirect it to communities that cannot afford Market prices. Their methods are crude, their success rate is inconsistent, and they are universally beloved in Lower Pelarch. The Crown considers them thieves. The communities they serve consider them infrastructure.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Water Debt

Communities and characters accumulate **Water Debt** when they cannot pay Market prices.

**Gaining Water Debt (+1):**
- A community misses a scheduled rain purchase
- A well drops below sustainable levels
- A competing bid diverts rain from the community's allotment
- The Crown increases the tax on rain sales

**Thresholds:**
| Debt | Effect |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Crops stress. Livestock need supplemental water. |
| 2 | Rationing begins. Social tensions rise between families. |
| 3 | Community conflict: arguments over allocation, distrust of leadership. |
| 4 | Displacement begins. Families leave for wetter districts. |
| 5 | Crisis. The community faces dissolution or revolt. |

**Reducing Water Debt (-1 each):**
- Negotiate a discounted rain purchase from a sympathetic Stormcaller
- Receive smuggled rain from the Rain Runners
- Win a political concession from the Crown (subsidized allotment)
- Discover or restore a natural water source (well, spring, aquifer)
- Challenge the Market system directly through public action

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Dry Farmer

You grow wheat in Lower Pelarch on land that your family has worked for six generations. The land was productive when rain fell for free. Now you bid at the Market every month and lose to commercial farms that can outspend you. You know how to grow food in poor conditions. You do not know how to grow food in no conditions, which is where the trend is heading.

- **Drive:** *"Keep my village fed through one more season while someone figures out whether the Market can be fixed or needs to be burned."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

### The Rogue Stormcaller

You left the Guild because they asked you to divert a spring storm away from a village that could not pay. The village lost its crop. You lost your license. You still know how to call rain. You do it for the communities that need it, in exchange for food, shelter, and the knowledge that the Guild wants you arrested.

- **Drive:** *"Put rain where it belongs: on the land that needs it, not the land that can pay."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

### The Market Broker

You sell rain. You are good at it. You have made a comfortable living matching buyers and sellers, and you have watched the system's inequities from the inside long enough to know that the Market works exactly as designed. That is the problem. You are beginning to use your position to route favorable contracts toward Lower Pelarch communities, which is not illegal but is certainly not profitable.

- **Drive:** *"Use the Market's own rules to move water downhill before someone with more principle and less leverage breaks the system entirely."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The Auction of Last Resort:** Lower Pelarch's largest farming community, Millbrook, has pooled its savings for one final Market bid. If they win, the village survives the growing season. If they lose, 600 people will need to relocate. The bid is scheduled for tomorrow's auction. Tonight, a Rain Runner brings word that Lord Sable's estate has entered a competing bid for the same storm, for ornamental gardens. The Stormcallers' Guild has a member willing to redirect the storm outside of Market channels, but doing so would void Millbrook's contract and give the Crown legal grounds to seize the village's water rights entirely. Carth Oakes wants to march on the Market. The Rain Runners want to steal the storm. The characters must find a way to keep Millbrook alive without giving the Crown the excuse it needs to end Lower Pelarch's independence.

**Why It Matters:** Crown of the Rain Market asks who owns the weather. The answer, in Pelarch, is that the sky belongs to whoever organized first and charged second. The Market is not a conspiracy. It is a system that distributes resources efficiently and unjustly, and the people who built it believe those are separate problems. The characters will not end the drought. They will decide whether rain is a commodity, a right, or something that belongs to the land itself. Every storm they redirect, purchase, or steal is a vote on what kind of kingdom Pelarch becomes.
