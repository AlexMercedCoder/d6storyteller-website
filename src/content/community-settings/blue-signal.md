## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The Blue Signal is a modern-day supernatural thriller about information, trust, and responsibility. The signal is real. Its predictions are not always right. The setting is not about discovering the signal or proving it exists. It is about what ordinary people do when they have access to knowledge that is powerful, imperfect, and impossible to verify through any other channel.

**Tone:** Grounded, anxious, and morally complicated. The characters are not chosen ones. They are hobbyists, engineers, journalists, and worried parents who started listening and could not stop. The setting borrows from conspiracy thriller and investigative journalism, but the conspiracy is not hidden. Everyone knows the signal exists. They disagree about what it means and what they owe to the people who have not heard it.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** A basement full of radio equipment, frequency monitors casting blue light on the walls. A prediction log on a whiteboard, dates circled in red for confirmed, crossed out in black for failed. The ordinary look of a street that the signal says will not exist in two weeks.
2. **Sound:** The signal itself: a low, rhythmic pulse that resolves into data patterns when processed through the right filters. The static between broadcasts, which some listeners swear contains additional information. The sound of a phone ringing at 3 AM with a fellow listener on the other end saying, "It gave a date."
3. **Touch & Smell:** The warmth of radio equipment that has been running all night. The smell of stale coffee in a monitoring room. The weight of a printed prediction in your hand, knowing that the address on it is your neighbor's house.

## 2. The Listener Network

**The Forum** is an encrypted online space where listeners share predictions, debate interpretations, and argue about methodology. It has roughly 4,000 active members worldwide. Most are passive observers. About 200 are serious analysts. Maybe 30 have acted on predictions in ways that affected other people. The Forum has no formal leadership, which means its norms are enforced through reputation, argument, and occasional schism.

**Local Cells** are small groups of listeners in the same geographic area who meet in person. The characters belong to one. Their cell has seven members, a rented storage unit full of equipment, and a shared fund that covers expenses. The cell's internal disagreements about how to handle predictions are the setting's primary social tension.

**The Source** is unknown. The signal broadcasts from no fixed location. Multiple governments have attempted to triangulate it. The results are contradictory. Some analysts believe the signal originates from multiple synchronized sources. Others believe it originates from no physical location at all. The source has never responded to any attempt at communication.

**The Skeptics Bureau** is the informal name for a government office that monitors the listener network. It is not hostile. It employs two full-time analysts who read the Forum, attend public listener events, and publish quarterly reports concluding that the signal is a natural phenomenon being over-interpreted. The Bureau's reports are careful, well-researched, and wrong about 30% of the time in exactly the ways that matter.

## 3. Factions

**The Analysts** believe the signal can be decoded into reliable predictions through better methodology. They want more data, more equipment, and more time. Their leader, Dr. Youssef Karim, has developed a scoring system for predictions that improves accuracy to roughly 80%, but his model requires waiting 72 hours before acting, which is often 72 hours too many.

**The Actors** believe that a prediction you do not act on is a prediction you wasted. They evacuate buildings, warn communities, and call emergency services. When they are right, they save lives. When they are wrong, they create panic, waste resources, and give the Skeptics Bureau evidence that listeners are a public safety hazard. Their most visible member, Carmen Alvarez, has been arrested twice for filing false emergency reports based on predictions that were accurate but early.

**The Silencers** believe the signal should not be shared with the public. Their argument is pragmatic: imperfect predictions in the hands of untrained people produce more harm than good. They want the listener network to provide information to governments and emergency services privately, without public broadcasts. The Silencers include three former intelligence analysts who understand information control and are uncomfortable with how comfortable they are with it.

**The Seekers** want to find the source. They are less interested in predictions and more interested in what is producing them. Their methods range from serious engineering to fringe science. The Seekers' current project involves building a transmitter powerful enough to send a response signal, which the other factions consider either pointless or dangerous.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Conviction

Each character tracks **Conviction** from 0 to 6.

**Rising:** +1 when a prediction the character heard comes true. +1 when the character witnesses a prevented disaster that matches a prediction. +2 when the character personally experiences a predicted event.

**Falling:** -1 when a prediction fails. -1 when acting on a prediction causes measurable harm (evacuation panic, false arrest, resource waste). -2 when the character discovers a prediction was manipulated or misinterpreted.

**Thresholds:**
- **0-1 (Skeptic):** The character treats predictions as unreliable. No mechanical bonus. The character is resistant to social pressure from believers.
- **2-3 (Cautious):** The character verifies before acting. May spend 1 Conviction as a resource to add +1d6 when acting on a verified prediction (the Conviction point returns next session if the prediction was accurate).
- **4-5 (Committed):** The character acts quickly on predictions. +1d6 on all actions aligned with a current prediction, but the Storyteller introduces a complication related to insufficient verification.
- **6 (True Believer):** +2d6 on prediction-aligned actions. The character cannot voluntarily ignore a prediction. If a prediction involves someone the character knows, they must act or resist with a Presence roll.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Reluctant Analyst

You joined the network because a prediction described your apartment building. You stayed because the data is genuinely interesting. You are a statistician by training and you know the signal's accuracy rate does not justify the certainty that some listeners bring to it. You also know the accuracy rate is too high to dismiss.

- **Drive:** *"Build a model that tells me when to trust it and when to ignore it, before someone gets hurt by guessing."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### The First Responder

You are a paramedic who started listening after a prediction described a highway pileup that happened on your shift. Now you pre-position your ambulance based on the signal. Your supervisor does not know why your response times have improved. You are not sure how to explain it.

- **Drive:** *"Use every tool I have. The signal is a tool. I do not need to understand it to use it."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 3 | Mind 1 | Presence 2

### The Journalist

You are writing a long-form piece on the listener network. You started as a skeptic. You are now six months in, and your notes include four predictions you heard before they happened. Your editor wants a debunking story. Your notes do not support that conclusion. You have not told your editor yet.

- **Drive:** *"Tell the truth about what the signal is, even if the truth makes me sound like the people I came to profile."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 1 | Mind 3 | Presence 3

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The School Prediction:** The signal has issued a prediction describing structural failure at a public school in the characters' city. The date is four days away. Dr. Karim's model gives the prediction a 78% confidence score, which is high but not certain. Carmen Alvarez wants to go public immediately. The Silencers want to notify the fire department privately and let them handle it. The Seekers want to use the prediction's specificity to narrow down the signal's source. The school principal, when contacted, dismisses the warning as a hoax. The building's last inspection was two years ago and passed. The characters have four days to investigate the building independently, decide how to warn 600 students without causing panic or legal liability, and confront the possibility that acting on this prediction may do more damage than the collapse it describes.

**Why It Matters:** The Blue Signal is about the weight of knowing something you cannot prove. Every prediction places the listener in a moral position: act and risk being wrong, or stay silent and risk being right. The setting does not resolve whether the signal is benevolent, neutral, or something else entirely. It asks the characters to make decisions with incomplete information and live with the results, which is the same challenge the rest of the world faces without a signal.
