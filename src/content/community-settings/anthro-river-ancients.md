## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** **River of Ancients** is high fantasy built around the radical premise that power doesn't require aggression. The four Savanna Kindred are among the largest, most physically powerful beings in any fantasy world—and they have built a civilization on the values of memory, protection, mediation, and patience. This is not a naive utopia: the Realm has genuine conflicts, political tensions, and the looming catastrophe of an ancient drought. But the tools the Kindred reach for first are the tools of relationship, not conquest.

The tone is **warm, deep, and occasionally majestic**—the characters are physically enormous beings with quiet authority, and every scene should feel like interacting with beings who have thought carefully about everything for a very long time.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The River Realm at dawn—mist rising from four rivers converging at the delta, the great reed-island platforms carrying the Kindred's cities slowly rotating with the current's seasonal shift; an Elaphkin Memory-Circle, where elders sit in a ring and reconstruct a historical event in unified recall, their tusks catching the firelight; Hippokind warrior-guardians rising from the river at night, their bulk enormous and their presence immediately calming to the delta communities they protect.
2. **Sound:** The deep, subsonic communication of the Elaphkin—Realm-songs felt more than heard, carrying emotional weight that smaller creatures experience as a sense of deep time; the territorial bellow of a Hippokind guardian declaring a river highway open (the signal that transit is safe); the surprisingly gentle voice of a Capybara Accord-Keeper conducting a ceasefire negotiation.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The warm wet smell of the delta—rich with biological life, river-mud, flowering water-grasses, the specific smell of vast gentle creatures in sunlight; the texture of reed-island platforms underfoot, slightly giving, always warm from sun-absorption; the cool weight of river water in the deep channels where the Hippokind spend their rest periods.

## 2. The Four Kindred

**Capybara Kindred (The Accord-Keepers):** The most socially diplomatic of the four, the Capybara have a natural quality of ease-with-others that other species find immediately disarming. They share space with everyone. They are impossible to alarm. They radiate the specific quality of a being who has decided that this moment, with these people, is perfectly acceptable. In political terms, this makes them extraordinary mediators—they are constitutionally incapable of bringing threat or urgency into a negotiation space, which means negotiations they facilitate tend to reach their actual issues rather than defensive posturing.

**Hippokind (The River Guardians):** The most physically formidable of the four, Hippokind are also the most territorial—but their territory is defined not by land they own but by river-highways they protect. A Hippokind guardian's claim is: this river is safe while I am in it. They hold this claim with their enormous bodies and their even more enormous willingness to enter violent conflict if the river is threatened. They are not aggressive; they are precise and threshold-based. Do not threaten the river.

**Rhinokind Paladins (The Sacred Horn):** The Realm's holy warrior tradition. The Rhinokind follow the Great Horn Code—a set of warrior ethics so ancient and so specific that law scholars have spent centuries arguing about edge cases. The Code requires protection of the vulnerable, prohibits violence against non-combatants, mandates that a challenged Rhinokind must accept the challenge (they cannot decline a declared trial by combat), and requires any Rhinokind witness to an injustice to intervene immediately regardless of personal risk. The Sacred Charge—their signature combat technique—is considered a holy act.

**Elaphkin Memory-Keepers (The Living Archive):** The most philosophically significant of the four, Elaphkin carry the Realm's history in their perfect recall. An Elaphkin Elder has personally witnessed, through the chain of Memory-Keeping, events going back four thousand years—each Elder learned the memories of their predecessors as if living them. They are gentle, measured, and quietly devastating in any situation that requires truth. The ancient drought memory they carry is fragmentary—the civilization that fell left records only in the memories of Elaphkin who witnessed its end, and those memories are deeply traumatic.

## 3. Factions

**The Accord Council:** The governing body of the River Realm, with representatives from all four Kindred. Currently in emergency session over the drought indicators.

**The Hippokind Wardens' Brotherhood:** The informal collective of river guardians. They know the drought is real because the rivers have already dropped 3% below historical baseline—which they know because they remember the historical baseline.

**The Memory Fragmentation Circle:** A group of Elaphkin Elders who have identified the traumatic gap in their memory chain—the exact point where the previous civilization's memories become discontinuous. They believe the trauma was deliberate: someone caused the memory break to prevent the previous civilization's survivors from passing on what destroyed it.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Kindred Gifts

**Capybara — Accord Presence:** Once per session, call an immediate ceasefire in any conflict scene. All parties pause for one beat. No roll required. Works on any sentient being.

**Hippokind — River Guardian:** In or adjacent to water: +2d6 to all Force actions. All incoming physical damage reduced by 1 step.

**Rhinokind — Sacred Charge:** Once per scene, charging attack that cannot be parried or interrupted. Automatic Moderate damage regardless of roll result. Using Sacred Charge in a clearly unjust cause requires a Rhinokind to make a Code Check or temporarily lose access to the ability.

**Elaphkin — Perfect Witness:** Never forget anything directly witnessed. Can ask Storyteller factual questions about past witnessed events. Once per session: invoke a Remembered Precedent—a past event the Storyteller must acknowledge as fact in the current scene.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### Archetype 1: The Capybara Accord-Keeper
A senior mediator who has spent thirty years negotiating between Kindred factions and is the only person in the Accord Council who genuinely believes the drought can be survived without inter-Kindred conflict—if the right precedents are invoked and the right agreements made in the next six days.
- **Quote:** *"I have sat with warring parties and made them tea and asked them about their children and watched the war drain out of the room before anyone realized what was happening. A drought is a different kind of emergency, but the principle is the same: first, someone has to be calm."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

### Archetype 2: The Hippokind Guardian
A senior river guardian whose protected highway has run 40% below capacity for the first time in their forty-year tenure. They know the drought is catastrophic because they've been counting every fish for four decades. They are not panicking—they are preparing.
- **Quote:** *"The fish count is down forty percent. The water level is down three. The migration patterns are wrong. I have protected this river for forty years. I am not going to stop now because there is less of it. I need to know what is taking it."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 3 | Finesse 1 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

### Archetype 3: The Elaphkin Memory Healer
A young Elaphkin who has been trained specifically to enter the traumatic memory gap and navigate it—using a method the Memory Circle developed after determining that the break was deliberate and therefore might be repairable. This has never been attempted before.
- **Quote:** *"The memory breaks like a scar—the tissue is there, but the connection is interrupted. I was trained to heal exactly this. I was trained in a method that has never been used. The Elders are very confident in the theory. I am mostly confident. There is a difference."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The Drying Council:** The Accord Council has convened all four Kindred representatives plus whatever outside consultants the characters represent. The Elaphkin Elder on the council begins by doing something unprecedented: she invokes a Remembered Precedent from 4,000 years ago—a specific sequence of events in the previous civilization's final years that maps identically to the current situation. The memory chain ends before the resolution. The characters must go to the Memory Fragmentation Circle to attempt the healing of the break—while also managing the real-world drought response that cannot wait for the historical investigation to conclude.

**Why It Matters:** The Savanna Kindred setting uses animals we associate with peaceful power—the capybara's legendary calm, the elephant's memory, the hippo's river territory, the rhino's solitary dignity—to build a civilization model based on protection, mediation, and truth-telling rather than conquest and hierarchy. In doing so, it asks what governance might look like if the most powerful members of a society were also the most committed to using their power to protect rather than control. The ancient drought stakes give the philosophical framework immediate urgency.
