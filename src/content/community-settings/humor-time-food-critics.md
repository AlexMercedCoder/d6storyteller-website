## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** History is edible and someone has to review it. The problem is the reviewing keeps changing it.

The **Temporal Culinary Institute (TCI)** was founded on the principle that human culinary history deserves rigorous documentation and evaluation, and that this documentation would be best performed by trained critics with access to actual time travel. What followed was several centuries (from the TCI's perspective, operating outside normal time) of increasingly elaborate incident reports about what happens when people with opinions visit kitchens in the past.

The players are TCI field critics, equipped with time displacement technology, extensive culinary training, and briefing documents detailing exactly what they're not supposed to do (the list grows every assignment).

**Tone:** This is a comedy of professional exactitude meeting existential absurdity. The critics are genuinely skilled and genuinely passionate about their work. The problem is their work is literally dangerous to history. Every meal is a minefield where the mines are historical significance.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** A Roman banquet in full imperial splendor, the critics seated at the edge of the room with TCI-issue period clothing (good research, slight fit issues), the senior critic making microscopic notes while maintaining a completely neutral expression despite the fact that this is the greatest garum they've ever encountered, and the junior critic visibly struggling not to audibly react.
2. **Sound:** The noise of history's kitchens: ancient spits, clay pots over open fires, the sounds of cooking technique unchanged across centuries and profoundly changed in every detail, and the very quiet hiss of the senior critic whispering corrections to the junior critic's field notes before they become part of the official record.
3. **Touch & Smell:** Actual historical food in actual historical preparation: the smoke and char of ancient cooking fires, spices at their original sources before global trade routes diluted them, the dense richness of medieval feast cooking, and occasionally something that doesn't translate well to modern palates and must be evaluated professionally regardless.

**The Core Tension:** The critics' expertise makes them want to engage. Their mission requires them not to. Every assignment is a test of professional discipline against genuine passion.

## 2. The TCI Structure

### The Rating System
The TCI uses a modified star rating that accounts for historical context: food is rated both on absolute quality and relative excellence for its era. A technically imperfect meal might rate higher than a technically superior one if it represents an extraordinary achievement given its historical context.

### The Non-Interference Protocols
An ever-growing list of prohibited behaviors including: making eye contact with kitchen staff, accepting complimentary dishes, commenting on food to anyone within earshot, visibly grimacing, visibly enjoying food beyond professional necessity, and the recently added prohibition on "temporal selfies near significant culinary moments."

### The Review Board
Senior critics who evaluate field reports and determine interference penalties. They are extremely rigorous. They have also all been on the 1973 buffet posting at least once.

### The Archivists
The TCI's record-keeping staff who compile all field notes into the Great Culinary History of All Time, an ongoing document that has never been publicly released and probably never should be.

## 3. Adventure Seeds

1. **The Signature Dish:** A dish critical to cultural history has disappeared from the historical record between visits. Somewhere in the timeline, a previous critic's report changed something. The players must locate the change, evaluate the culinary damage, and determine if correction is possible without making it worse.

2. **The Chef Problem:** The greatest chef in 12th century France recognizes the players from a previous TCI visit they absolutely did not make. Investigation reveals a temporal loop: the players are destined to influence this chef despite every protocol. How do they close the loop while minimizing interference?

3. **The Competitive Review:** A rival critical organization with different temporal ethics has sent their own team to the same historical meal. Their approach is less concerned with non-interference. The players must complete their professional evaluation while managing a team that is actively improving the food they're supposed to be neutrally assessing.

4. **The Catastrophic Review:** A junior critic's field note from three assignments ago has propagated through time and significantly altered the development of a major cuisine. The damage is done. The question now is whether the altered cuisine is, on its own merits, actually better than the original.

5. **The Final Meal:** The TCI has identified what their records indicate is the single greatest meal ever prepared in human history, occurring in the kitchen of a private home in 1927 Marseilles. Getting to evaluate it requires navigating severe access restrictions, non-interference protocols for an extraordinarily intimate setting, and the discovery that one of the guests at this private dinner is a TCI founder who has no idea the Institute exists yet.
