## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** Beneath the most distinctive landscape in Anatolia, an ancient deal between humans and fire-spirits is fraying -- and what's disturbing it is not human at all.

The setting draws on Turkish folklore traditions surrounding djinn (ancient Arabic and Anatolian spirit tradition) and situates them in the extraordinary historical landscape of Cappadocia: the real underground cities (Derinkuyu and Kaymakli are genuine archaeological sites extending eight or more levels underground), the volcanic rock formations called fairy chimneys, and the layered history of Hittite, Greek, Byzantine, and Ottoman cultures all living in this landscape.

The Djinn of Anatolian tradition are complex: some are Muslim, some pre-date Islam, some are bound by ancient pacts, and some are entirely free and enormously powerful. Their relationship to humans is one of coexistence -- sometimes harmonious, sometimes contentious.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Cappadocian surface: volcanic plains studded with the strange organic shapes of the fairy chimneys, cave churches carved into the rock faces with their faded Byzantine frescoes still visible, the entrance holes of the underground cities covered by millstones that can be rolled closed, and on certain evenings, the fire-glow from the Djinn passage points visible in the chimney summits.
2. **Sound:** The constant wind of the Anatolian plateau, which the locals read for Djinn presence and mood; the echo of voices in the deep underground chambers where sound behaves strangely; the call to prayer from a mosque built over an older sacred site; and the very specific sound of a Djinn choosing to be heard, which is different in quality from wind.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The cool dry air of the underground tunnels, the volcanic rock smooth from generations of hands, the warmth of a Djinn presence even in cold air, the smell of sulfur from the volcanic vents that the Djinn use as passages, and the smell of the offerings burned at Djinn-pact maintenance sites.

## 2. The Underground World

### The Known Levels
The documented underground city: storage rooms, communal spaces, churches, ventilation shafts, wells. Occupied at various points in history by communities fleeing invasion. Currently maintained by a small community of pact-keepers who understand the city's dual function.

### The Deep Levels
Below the documented sections, accessible only to those with Djinn permission or Djinn blood. The Djinn Courts occupy vast natural cavern systems that the volcanic activity of millions of years has created. Each Djinn lord rules a specific section according to rules that predate human recording.

### The Unknown Level
Something below even the Djinn Courts. The Djinn don't discuss it. The fact that they don't discuss it is itself alarming.

--

## 3. Adventure Seeds

1. **The Pact Review:** The Djinn are demanding a formal renegotiation of the ancient pact. Their stated reason is that humans have not honored the offering requirements consistently. Their actual reason, which requires investigation to discover, is that they need expanded terms to deal with what's in the unknown level. Getting to the actual reason requires navigating Djinn politics.

2. **The Bound Djinn:** A Djinn has been trapped by a competing sorcerer who wants to use its power for their own purposes. The Djinn is relatively minor but its absence from the pact network is weakening the overall integrity. Finding and releasing the Djinn requires tracking a very careful human who has taken significant precautions.

3. **The Ascending Thing:** Something from the unknown level has reached the Djinn Courts. The Djinn are mobilizing in ways they haven't in centuries. They will not share information with humans directly -- but they will allow one party of humans to accompany them, bound by the Djinn war-pact, to observe and assist. The players are that party.

4. **The Community Fracture:** The human community that maintains the pact has split into two factions: those who believe the pact is sustainable and those who believe it must be ended. Ending the pact would leave the underground cities unprotected. Maintaining it requires resources the community is struggling to provide. The Djinn have opinions about this dispute that they're expressing through weather patterns.

5. **The Lost Passage:** A ventilation shaft that serves as a Djinn passage has been sealed by a construction project whose planners didn't know what it was. The Djinn using that passage are trapped on the wrong side. Finding and reopening the passage before the trapped Djinn become sufficiently frustrated to open it themselves is time-sensitive.
