## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The Spanish Main, the Caribbean, the Golden Age of Piracy -- all real, all dangerous, all now subject to maritime commercial law and the terrifying expertise of financial buccaneers who realized the pen (and ledger) was mightier than the cutlass.

The world of **Swashbucklers & Spreadsheets** treats piracy as a legitimate, if morally ambiguous, financial services profession. The most successful pirates aren't necessarily the most violent -- they're the ones who can identify a merchant vessel's undeclared assets, calculate the exact percentage of cargo that constitutes acceptable privateer compensation under maritime law, and file the correct paperwork with whatever colonial authority will recognize their letters of marque.

The **Balanced Ledger** is the most profitable ship in the Caribbean not because its crew fights better but because they negotiate better, audit better, and exploit every legal gray area available to them. Captain Doubloon holds letters of marque from four different colonial powers, two of whom are currently at war with each other.

**Tone:** This is genuinely swashbuckling adventure filtered through the comedic lens of financial services. The accounting is played straight. The piracy is played straight. The combination is played for laughs. Characters have actual sword fights AND argument-fights about depreciation schedules.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** A ship's cabin crammed with ledgers, counting frames, and rolled maritime commercial codes alongside cutlasses, navigation charts, and a very nice compass that was definitely legitimately acquired, the captain reviewing a seized merchant's financial statements while the crew celebrates on deck, and a prize vessel being escorted to port with its crew signing a consulting engagement letter.
2. **Sound:** The creak of the Balanced Ledger's rigging, the scratch of quill on account books, the very specific silence of a merchant captain who has just been told his ship is being seized for regulatory non-compliance rather than in a conventional pirate attack, and the captain's calm tone explaining exactly which regulations are being violated.
3. **Touch & Smell:** Salt air and cannon smoke mixing with the faint smell of ink and parchment, the rough texture of account books that have been through storms, the weight of a ledger that contains more gold value than most people will see in their lives, and the satisfying heft of properly audited treasury records.

**The Core Tension:** The crew must decide whether their expertise is ultimately in service of a fundamentally ethical goal (correctly applying financial justice) or merely a more sophisticated form of the piracy they're nominally improving upon.

## 2. The Balanced Ledger

### Captain Celeste Doubloon
Former Crown auditor who discovered that piracy was more profitable than government service and also more honest. Holds degrees in maritime law, accounting, and swordsmanship. The swordsmanship was a late addition after her third boarding action.

### The Crew Departments
- **The Audit Team:** Board target vessels and review their financial records for actionable non-compliance
- **The Legal Division:** One actual lawyer and four people who have read enough maritime law to be dangerous
- **Prize Operations:** Converts seized assets into transferable value (this involves some traditional piracy)
- **Crew Morale:** This department does not exist; this is an ongoing tension

### The Admiralty Problem
The actual Crown Navy cannot quite decide if the Balanced Ledger is legal. Technically, Captain Doubloon's letters of marque are valid. Technically, her audit seizures comply with maritime commercial code. Technically, she's paying taxes (on her declared income). This is a ongoing headache for several admirals.

## 3. Adventure Seeds

1. **The Merchant Who Wouldn't:** A shipping conglomerate has hired actual pirates to protect their vessels from the Balanced Ledger. A conventional pirate battle breaks out but the players are better at negotiating than fighting. This doesn't mean they can't fight. It means they'll try negotiation first and fighting second.

2. **The Hostile Audit:** A rival CPA pirate has appeared, claiming their audits are more rigorous and their fees are lower. This is war. Financial war. Possibly also actual war.

3. **The Lost Invoice:** Someone has stolen the Balanced Ledger's own financial records. Without them, Captain Doubloon cannot prove she's paid her taxes and the Crown Auditors are closing in.

4. **The Colonial Governor's Books:** The colonial governor of Port Royale has been skimming from the Crown. The players have the evidence. The players also need the governor's cooperation for their next prize seizure. Negotiation required.

5. **Retirement Planning:** A veteran crew member wants to retire and needs their share of the communal prize fund. Calculating their actual share requires auditing 12 years of the Balanced Ledger's records, some of which are intentionally obscure for tax purposes.
