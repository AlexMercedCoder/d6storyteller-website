## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

The Embassy is three years old. In that time, humanity has established forty verified shared concepts with the Correspondents, which is a remarkable achievement and a dangerously thin foundation for a relationship between two species that do not share biology, communication methods, or assumptions about what constitutes a promise. The diplomats are exhausted, underfunded, and aware that every interaction could be the one that collapses the relationship.

The characters are embassy staff: diplomats, translators, security officers, scientists, support crew, or advisors from Earth governments jockeying for influence over a relationship they do not understand. The embassy is a small space with a large mandate, and the people inside it are making decisions that will define interspecies relations for centuries.

**Tone:** Tense, cerebral, and occasionally absurd. The setting takes first contact seriously as a diplomatic challenge rather than a military one. The humor comes from the gap between the enormity of the task and the humanness of the people doing it: a translator arguing with a colleague about whether a color shift means "hello" or "your shoes are offensive," a diplomat trying to explain Earth's political system to an alien that communicates in geometry.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The embassy interior: clean, modular, designed for function. The Correspondents' section: curved walls that change color in patterns the translation team records but does not fully understand. The view from the observation deck: Earth, the Moon, and the Correspondents' vessel, which is a geometric shape that does not correspond to any human architectural tradition and which rotates slowly on an axis that changes every few days.
2. **Sound:** The hum of life support. The click of recording equipment documenting every interaction. The silence during a Correspondent exchange, because their communication is visual and chemical, not auditory. The sound of a translator's voice, carefully modulated, narrating observations into a log that will be analyzed by seventeen committees on Earth.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The dry, filtered air of the station. The specific chemical smell when the Correspondents are communicating: faint, sharp, and different each time, like a perfume that carries meaning. The weight of a diplomatic pouch sealed with both human and Correspondent authentication, which feels heavier than its contents should account for. The cold of the station's exterior hull, felt through the viewport glass.

## 2. The Embassy

**The Common Chamber** is the only room where humans and Correspondents meet directly. It is climate-controlled to a compromise temperature and atmosphere that is slightly uncomfortable for both species. The walls are equipped with color-display panels for Correspondent communication and audio-visual equipment for human recording. Every session in the Common Chamber is logged, analyzed, and archived by both sides.

**The Translation Suite** is where the human team processes Correspondent communications. It contains spectral analyzers, chemical sensors, and a database of every verified concept. The team works in shifts because the Correspondents do not appear to sleep. The suite's lead translator, Dr. Rahn, has developed a personal notation system for Correspondent color-meanings that the rest of the team considers either brilliant or dangerously idiosyncratic.

**The Earth Wing** houses the diplomatic offices, crew quarters, and communication equipment linked to Earth. Messages take approximately 1.3 seconds each way, which is fast enough for conversation and slow enough to create awkward pauses that Earth officials interpret as hesitation.

**The Threshold** is the sealed boundary between the human and Correspondent sections of the station. It can only be opened by mutual agreement from both sides. The Threshold has been opened 847 times in three years. Each opening is recorded as a diplomatic event. The Correspondents treat the Threshold with a formality that the human team has learned to mirror, even when the reason for the opening is a routine supply exchange.

## 3. Factions

**The Embassy Staff** is the core team: twelve diplomats, translators, and support crew who live and work on the station. Ambassador Tanaka leads with patience, precision, and a growing concern that Earth's governments are about to replace her with someone who prioritizes speed over accuracy. The staff is united by the work and divided by the pressure: some want to slow down and deepen understanding, others want results they can show the budget committee.

**Earth's Advisory Council** is a committee of representatives from major governments who issue directives to the embassy. The Council wants trade agreements, technology exchanges, and a formal treaty. It does not want to hear that the Correspondents' concept of "agreement" may not overlap with the human one. The Council's liaison on the station, Director Calloway, is a career diplomat who understands the constraints but is under orders to produce deliverables.

**The Correspondents** are not a monolithic entity. The human team has identified at least three distinct subgroups within the Correspondent delegation, each with slightly different communication patterns. Whether these represent factions, departments, or something entirely different is unknown. One subgroup has been consistently warmer in its interactions. Another has been more formal. The third has been silent for six weeks, which may be significant or may be routine. The team does not know.

**The Independent Science Advisory** is a group of researchers from various universities who have been granted observer status on the station. They want to study the Correspondents as a scientific phenomenon. Their methods sometimes conflict with diplomatic protocol: a researcher who collects chemical samples from the Common Chamber without permission could be conducting valuable science or committing a diplomatic offense. The line between the two is not always clear.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Protocol Confidence

The embassy maintains a shared **Protocol Confidence** score from 0 to 10, starting at 5.

**Raising Confidence (+1 each):**
- Successfully resolve a miscommunication without escalation
- Complete a cultural exchange that both sides acknowledge
- Honor a commitment made in a previous session
- Discover and verify a new shared concept

**Lowering Confidence (-1 each):**
- Miscommunication that reaches the Correspondents before correction
- Unilateral action by either side without consultation
- Earth directive that contradicts an established protocol
- A researcher or staff member acts outside diplomatic channels

**Thresholds:**
| Confidence | Status |
|------------|--------|
| 7-10 | Productive relationship. Threshold opens on request. New concepts explored. |
| 4-6 | Standard operations. Threshold opens on schedule. Interactions are formal. |
| 2-3 | Reduced contact. Correspondents limit exchanges to essential matters. |
| 1 | Crisis. The Correspondents recall their delegation to their vessel. One final session is offered. |
| 0 | Embassy evacuated. Contact suspended. |

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Color Translator

You have spent three years learning to read Correspondent color-communication. You can distinguish 400 shades of blue and you know what 60 of them mean. You are the best translator on the station and you are acutely aware that "best" means "slightly less wrong than everyone else."

- **Drive:** *"Translate accurately enough that the next mistake is a surprise, not a pattern."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 4 | Presence 1

### The Protocol Officer

You manage the embassy's daily operations: scheduling sessions, enforcing communication protocols, and ensuring that every interaction is documented. You are the person who says "we cannot do that yet" to Earth directives and "we are not ready for that" to enthusiastic researchers. You are also the person who keeps the Common Chamber functional, which means you spend more time with the Correspondents than anyone except the translators.

- **Drive:** *"Maintain the relationship long enough for both sides to learn what it could become."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

### The Earth Liaison

You represent the Advisory Council on the station. Your job is to produce results for governments that measure diplomatic success in quarterly reports. You understand that the Correspondents operate on a different timescale. You also understand that your career depends on delivering something concrete before the next budget review. You are not hostile to the translation team's caution. You are hostile to the possibility that caution will be used as an excuse to avoid decisions.

- **Drive:** *"Bring something home that justifies the cost without destroying what we have built."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The Gift:** The Correspondents have placed an object in the Common Chamber. It is geometric, approximately the size of a human head, and it changes color in response to proximity. The translation team cannot determine whether it is a gift, a proposal, a test, or a boundary marker. Ambassador Tanaka wants time to study it. Director Calloway wants to reciprocate immediately to demonstrate good faith. Dr. Rahn believes the object's color pattern matches a concept the team translated six months ago as "obligation," which may mean the Correspondents are offering a commitment or demanding one. The silent subgroup has resumed communication for the first time in six weeks, and their color patterns are focused on the object. Earth's Advisory Council, informed of the object by Calloway, has instructed the embassy to accept it formally within 24 hours. The characters must determine what the object means, decide whether to accept it on Earth's terms or the Correspondents', and manage the possibility that their response will define the relationship for the next decade.

**Why It Matters:** Embassy at Lagrange Point asks whether humans can build a relationship with something they do not understand without reducing it to something they do. The Correspondents are not hostile, friendly, or indifferent. They are different in ways that the human conceptual framework cannot fully capture. The setting is about the discipline of uncertainty: doing careful work under pressure, admitting what you do not know, and making decisions that affect billions of people based on sixty verified words and a color that might mean "hello" or might mean "we are watching to see if you deserve to stay."
