## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

The Severing is a natural barrier that no army has crossed by any means other than the Red Bridges. There are four bridges, each maintained by a small garrison of keepers, and each one is the economic lifeline, diplomatic channel, and potential invasion route for two nations that have been alternating between cold war and cautious trade for centuries.

The keepers are not warriors. They are diplomats, engineers, toll collectors, and mediators who live on the bridges themselves, belonging to neither realm. Their authority comes from the Compact, and the Compact expires in 90 days. After that, the bridges revert to whichever realm can claim them first.

**Tone:** Politically tense, geographically dramatic, and centered on the specific anxieties of people who live between two powers and belong to neither. The setting is about borders, neutrality, and the practical work of maintaining a peace that both sides resent.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Severing: a canyon so deep the bottom is permanently shrouded in mist. The Red Bridges: enormous stone structures stained with iron-rich mineral deposits, spanning the gap in graceful arches. Each bridge is wide enough for wagon traffic and long enough that crossing takes thirty minutes on foot. The keepers' quarters built into the bridge's support structures, hanging over the void.
2. **Sound:** Wind through the canyon, which produces a low moan that the keepers have learned to read for weather changes. The rumble of wagon wheels on bridge stone. The formal bell that rings when a diplomatic party arrives, distinct from the trade bell and the emergency bell. The echo of a voice shouted from one side of the bridge, which takes four seconds to reach the other.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The rough texture of the bridge's iron-stained stone, warm in sun and cold at night. The updraft from the canyon, which carries moisture and a mineral smell. The specific vibration of the bridge during heavy traffic, felt through the feet. The smell of the keeper's quarters: stone, lamp oil, and the particular staleness of enclosed spaces over open air.

## 2. The Crossings

**The First Bridge** is the oldest and most heavily trafficked. It carries commercial trade and is the primary revenue source for the keepers. Its garrison is the largest and its toll booths the busiest. First Bridge Keeper Solana has managed the crossing for fifteen years and considers neutrality not a principle but a practice: something you do every day through hundreds of small decisions.

**The Second Bridge** serves diplomatic traffic. It is narrower, more formal, and its crossing requires appointment. The diplomatic suites built into the Second Bridge's towers are the only neutral meeting space on the continent. More treaties have been signed here than in either realm's capital.

**The Third Bridge** is a military crossing, used only during formally declared emergencies. It has been closed for twelve years. Both realms maintain garrisons on their respective sides. The keepers maintain the bridge and the neutrality of the span between the garrisons. The Third Bridge is the most likely flashpoint if the Compact expires.

**The Fourth Bridge** is the smallest and most remote. It serves pilgrims, scholars, and anyone who cannot afford First Bridge tolls. The Fourth Bridge keeper is a single person, an elderly engineer named Hatch, who maintains the structure alone and has been doing so for thirty years. Hatch does not care about politics. Hatch cares about stone integrity and resents visitors who scuff the mortar.

## 3. Factions

**The Keepers' Council** governs the bridges and is negotiating the Compact's renewal. Council speaker Solana wants independence maintained through a renewed agreement that both realms sign voluntarily. She is a skilled diplomat and an increasingly exhausted one. The Council is divided between those who want to negotiate from a position of neutrality and those who want to negotiate from a position of leverage, which would require threatening to destroy the bridges.

**The Eastern Realm (Aurelia)** wants control of the First and Second Bridges for economic and diplomatic advantage. Their ambassador, Lord Tavish, has been stationed at the Second Bridge for three years, building relationships with keepers and quietly assessing the bridge defenses. Tavish is charming, patient, and operating under instructions that include military contingencies.

**The Western Realm (Castellan)** wants control of the Third Bridge for military advantage and considers the other bridges negotiable. Their commander, General Maren Holt, has reinforced the western Third Bridge garrison and is making no effort to hide it. Holt considers the Compact an anachronism that served its purpose and should be replaced by a bilateral defense agreement.

**The Bridge Workers** are the laborers who maintain the structures: masons, engineers, and toll collectors who live on the bridges and have no citizenship in either realm. They want the Compact renewed because it protects their homes and livelihoods. Their steward, a mason named Jory, has been conducting an informal census of bridge residents and has discovered that the keepers' population is larger than either realm realizes. He is considering whether that population is large enough to matter politically.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Bridge Trust

Each bridge maintains a **Trust** score from 0 to 6, starting at 4.

**Gaining Trust (+1 each):**
- Fair enforcement of crossing rules applied equally to both realms
- Successful mediation of a cross-border dispute
- Maintained neutrality during a provocation
- Physical maintenance of the bridge (keeping the structure sound reinforces its symbolic authority)

**Losing Trust (-1 each):**
- Favoritism toward one realm in toll assessment or access
- Accepting a bribe or making a deal with one side secretly
- Blockading or restricting access without mutual agreement
- Violence on bridge grounds

**Thresholds:**
| Trust | Status |
|-------|--------|
| 5-6 | Both realms respect the crossing. Disputes are brought to the keepers for mediation. |
| 3-4 | Functional neutrality. Both sides are testing boundaries but cooperating. |
| 1-2 | Contested. Both realms station observers on the bridge. Commercial traffic drops. |
| 0 | The bridge is no longer neutral. Both realms treat it as contested territory. Military occupation becomes likely. |

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Bridge Keeper

You have lived on the First Bridge for fifteen years. You collect tolls, mediate disputes between merchants from both realms, and maintain the crossing in all weather. You have no citizenship, no national loyalty, and no home that is not suspended over a canyon. The Compact is your constitution. Without it, you are a squatter on a contested military asset.

- **Drive:** *"Renew the Compact on terms that keep the bridges independent and keep my home from becoming a battlefield."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

### The Eastern Diplomat

You represent Aurelia at the Second Bridge. Your instructions are to secure economic control of the First and Second Bridges through negotiation if possible, other means if necessary. You have spent three years learning how the keepers think. You respect them. You also have a job to do, and your government's patience has an expiration date that aligns uncomfortably with the Compact's.

- **Drive:** *"Secure Aurelia's interests without destroying the institution that makes those interests worth having."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

### The Bridge Mason

You repair the Red Bridges. You know every stone, every joint, every stress point. You also know that the bridges were built to be destroyed from within: the original architects included demolition chambers in the support structures, sealed and forgotten by everyone except the people who maintain the stonework. You have not told anyone. You are deciding whether this knowledge is leverage or a last resort.

- **Drive:** *"Keep the bridges standing. Use what I know about their destruction only if there is no other way to keep them free."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 1

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The 90-Day Clock:** The Compact expires in 90 days. Both realms have submitted renewal proposals. Aurelia's proposal gives the keepers continued independence in exchange for economic concessions (reduced tolls, priority access). Castellan's proposal replaces the keepers with a joint military commission. Both proposals are unacceptable to the keepers as written. Solana needs a counter-proposal that both realms will accept, and she needs it before General Holt's patience runs out and Lord Tavish's charming suggestions become demands. The characters must negotiate, investigate both realms' true intentions, and decide whether the bridges' independence can be preserved, whether it should be, and what they are willing to risk to keep the only crossings on the continent free.

**Why It Matters:** The Red Bridge Compact asks what neutrality costs and who pays for it. The keepers have maintained peace for centuries by standing between two powers and belonging to neither. That position is structurally fragile: it depends on both sides preferring a neutral crossing to a controlled one, and that preference is not permanent. The characters live on the border itself, and the setting asks whether a border can be a home, whether peace is a product that someone must maintain, and whether the people who do the maintaining deserve a say in what the peace looks like.
