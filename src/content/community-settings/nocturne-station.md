> *"I have ridden the Nocturne Line four times. I used to think about going back to school. I used to consider moving to another city. I used to imagine a version of my life where I learned to paint. None of those feel possible now. Not difficult. Not unlikely. Impossible, the way flying is impossible. I know I had those options. I can remember wanting them. I cannot want them anymore."*
> - Regular passenger, Nocturne Line support group

## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

The Nocturne Line appears on the transit map as a standard commuter route: seven stops, circular, operating midnight to 4 AM. The train arrives on time. The cars are clean. The fare is standard. The conductors are polite. Everything about the Nocturne Line is normal except the effect it has on the people who ride it: each trip removes a possible future from their lives, pruning the branching tree of what they might become until only one path remains.

**Tone:** Melancholic, intimate, and built on the specific horror of losing something you did not know you had. The setting is not about monsters on a train. It is about the slow reduction of possibility, the way a person becomes less of themselves without any single dramatic event, just a series of late-night train rides that felt perfectly ordinary at the time.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The platform at midnight: fluorescent lights, concrete walls, and a handful of passengers waiting for a train that looks exactly like every other train in the system. The interior of the Nocturne cars: clean, well-lit, and populated by passengers who sit quietly and stare at the windows, which show the city passing but also, briefly and at certain stops, show something else: a flash of a life the passenger will no longer live, visible for a moment in the glass before the train moves on.
2. **Sound:** The arrival announcement, which is the standard automated voice saying the standard things. The hum of the train between stops, which is slightly lower in pitch than a standard train and which passengers describe as "comfortable in a way that makes you not want to get off." The silence between passengers, which is the silence of people who are not talking because they are watching the windows and seeing futures dissolve.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The warmth of the seats, which are always warm regardless of how long the car has been empty. The vibration of the train, which is soothing and rhythmic and which produces a drowsiness that makes passengers miss their intended stops. The smell of the Nocturne cars: clean, metallic, and carrying a faint sweetness that regular transit does not have, like the smell of a new book or an empty room.

## 2. The Line

**Platform 9** is the Nocturne Line's origin: a standard subway platform that is unremarkable during the day and becomes the departure point at midnight. The platform has a bench, a schedule display, and a map that shows the seven stops. The map is accurate. It does not mention what happens at each stop because the map was printed by the transit authority, which does not know.

**The Seven Stops** correspond to real locations on the transit map, but the Nocturne train passes through each one differently. At each stop, the doors open, passengers board or depart, and one possible future is removed from everyone on the train. The removal is invisible. The stop looks normal. The passengers feel a brief moment of calm, a sense of something settling into place, which is the sensation of a possibility closing.

**The Conductor's Car** is the last car on the train, separated from the passenger cars by a door that passengers are not supposed to open. The conductor, identified by a name tag as "Ellis," has been working the Nocturne Line for an indeterminate period. Ellis is polite, professional, and evasive about the Line's nature. When asked directly, Ellis says: "The train goes where the tracks go. The tracks go where they need to."

**The Terminal** is not on the map. Passengers who ride past all seven stops without exiting arrive at an eighth location: a station with no name, no signage, and no exit. The Terminal is clean, well-lit, and empty. Passengers who reach the Terminal have exhausted their futures. They sit on the benches and wait for the next train, which is always the Nocturne Line, which takes them back to Platform 9 to begin again.

## 3. Factions

**The Regular Riders** are passengers who take the Nocturne Line repeatedly despite (or because of) its effects. Some ride because the reduction of possibility is comforting: fewer futures means fewer decisions, and fewer decisions means less anxiety. Others ride because they are addicted to the calm the train produces. Support group facilitator Nadia Voss has been documenting the riders' experiences and has identified a pattern: most regular riders began with a single late-night ride during a period of stress, and each subsequent ride made the stress feel more manageable because it made the future feel simpler.

**The Transit Authority** operates the Nocturne Line as a standard service and has no idea that it is unusual. Supervisor Orla Chen manages the midnight routes and has noticed that the Nocturne Line's ridership has been growing steadily for six months, which she considers a scheduling success. She has not connected the ridership growth to the increasing number of passengers who report feeling "directionless" to the transit system's customer service line.

**Conductor Ellis** is the only crew member on the Nocturne train. Ellis has been working the line for longer than the current transit authority records cover. Ellis is not hostile. Ellis is not helpful. Ellis is a professional who runs the train, announces the stops, and ensures that the service operates on schedule. When pressed about the Line's nature, Ellis will say one thing that is true and one thing that is evasive, and the passenger must determine which is which.

**The Eighth Riders** are passengers who have reached the Terminal: people with no remaining futures who ride the Nocturne Line continuously. They are quiet, calm, and difficult to engage. Their faces are familiar to the regular riders. Their names appear in the transit authority's records as passengers who purchased monthly passes and never stopped using them. Nadia has tried to help them. They do not want help. They do not want anything, which is the problem.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Future Tally

Each passenger tracks a **Future Tally** starting at 0.

**Tally increase:** +1 per Nocturne Line ride (one future removed per trip)

**Tally effects:**
| Tally | Passenger Experience |
|-------|---------------------|
| 0 | Full range of futures. Decisions feel open, uncertain, and anxiety-producing. |
| 1 | One future removed. The passenger cannot identify which one, but a vague option has stopped feeling available. |
| 2 | Two removed. A specific life path (career change, relationship, relocation) no longer feels possible. The passenger can name what they have lost. |
| 3 | Decision paralysis narrows. The passenger's remaining options are fewer. Roll -1d6 when attempting something outside their narrowing path. |
| 4 | The passenger's personality simplifies. Friends notice reduced spontaneity, creativity, or curiosity. |
| 5 | One future remains. The passenger is locked into a single path. All actions outside that path are at -2d6. |
| 6 | No futures remain. The passenger becomes an Eighth Rider: calm, directionless, and permanently aboard the Nocturne Line. |

**Reducing Tally (-1 each):**
- Stop riding the Nocturne Line for an extended period (one session without riding)
- Deliberately pursue a path that feels impossible (force a closed future open through action and will)
- Understand the mechanism of the Line well enough to resist it during a ride (Mind roll during the trip)
- Reach the Terminal and refuse to board the return train

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Addicted Rider

You have been riding the Nocturne Line every Tuesday for three months. Each ride makes your life feel simpler. You used to agonize over decisions. Now the decisions feel made. You know you are losing something. You know the calm is a symptom, not a cure. You cannot stop riding because the alternative is returning to a life full of options you no longer feel capable of choosing.

- **Drive:** *"Understand why the train makes the future feel smaller, and decide whether I want my anxiety back if it comes with my possibilities."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

### The Support Group Leader

You run a group for Nocturne passengers who have noticed the effects. You have never ridden the train yourself, which gives you perspective and limits your credibility. Your members describe losing futures in specific, heartbreaking terms: a musician who can no longer imagine performing, an entrepreneur who has lost the ability to envision a business, a young parent who cannot picture their child's graduation. You are documenting the pattern. You are also watching your most affected members drift toward the Terminal.

- **Drive:** *"Help riders recover what the train has taken, and find a way to shut the Nocturne Line down before it reduces anyone else."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 1 | Mind 3 | Presence 3

### The Transit Investigator

You work for the transit authority and you have noticed the anomaly: ridership on the Nocturne Line is growing but the route does not appear in the system's original planning documents. The line has no construction record, no commissioning date, and no maintenance schedule. It runs every night, on time, on tracks that the engineering department cannot account for. You are investigating a ghost route that carries real passengers, and Conductor Ellis knows your name without being introduced.

- **Drive:** *"Find out who built the Nocturne Line, why it was built, and how to take it off the schedule."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 3 | Mind 3 | Presence 1

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The Empty Passenger:** An Eighth Rider has been recognized by a regular rider as someone who went missing from the neighborhood six months ago. The missing person's family filed a report. The police found nothing. The Eighth Rider is sitting on the Nocturne train every night, calm, present, and unreachable. Nadia has brought the family to the platform. They can see their loved one through the train window. The train doors are open. The Eighth Rider does not respond to their names, their voices, or their touch. Conductor Ellis says: "All passengers are free to disembark at any stop." The family says their loved one is not disembarking. Ellis says: "That is correct." The characters must find a way to reach the Eighth Rider, determine whether a person with no remaining futures can be restored, and decide what to do about a transit line that the city does not know it is operating.

**Why It Matters:** Nocturne Station asks what happens when the future is not taken violently but reduced gently, one comfortable ride at a time. The train does not threaten. It soothes. The passengers do not lose futures through trauma but through a calm, repetitive process that feels like relief. The setting is about the seduction of simplicity, the cost of avoiding uncertainty, and the specific danger of a system that makes people feel better by making them less. The characters must decide whether the Nocturne Line is a predator, a service, or something in between, and whether the passengers who ride it every night are victims or volunteers.
