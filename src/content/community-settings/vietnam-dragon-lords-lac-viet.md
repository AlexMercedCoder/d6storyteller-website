## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** Every Vietnamese person knows the origin myth: **Lac Long Quan**, the Dragon King of the sea, married **Au Co**, the Mountain Fairy. She laid a sac of one hundred eggs from which hatched one hundred children. Fifty followed their mother to the mountains; fifty followed their father to the coast. Together they became the **Lac Viet**--the Vietnamese people. In this setting, that myth is literal history, and the dragon blood still flows.

Set during the 1st century CE, when the ancient kingdom of **Nam Viet** has been absorbed into the Han Chinese Empire, the Lac Viet people live under foreign occupation. Rice paddies are taxed to starvation. Bronze Dong Son drums--the sacred instruments of Lac Viet identity--are being confiscated and melted. The old shrines to Lac Long Quan are being demolished and replaced. But the dragon blood cannot be taxed or melted. When **Trung Trac** and **Trung Nhi** (the historical Trung Sisters) raise the banner of rebellion in 40 CE, they do not merely rally an army--they awaken the dormant dragon heritage in thousands of Lac Viet descendants, and the sleeping Long dragons hidden within the limestone karst pillars of **Ha Long Bay** ("Bay Where the Dragons Descend") begin to stir.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The otherworldly beauty of Ha Long Bay's 1,600 limestone karst pillars rising from emerald water like the petrified spines of sleeping dragons, each one containing a dormant Long spirit visible as faint jade-green luminescence in the stone; war elephants decorated with bronze Dong Son drum patterns wading through flooded rice paddies; the crimson and gold silk banners of the Trung Sisters' rebel army reflected in the Red River's muddy waters; a Vietnamese Long dragon--sinuous, antlered, maned with flowing whiskers, four-clawed--uncoiling from the mist between two karst pillars and stretching across the dawn sky.
2. **Sound:** The deep, resonant boom of a Dong Son bronze drum (the largest in the world, over a meter across) being struck to call the Lac Viet to arms; the eerie acoustics of hidden caves within limestone karsts, where whispered prayers to Lac Long Quan echo for minutes; the roar of monsoon rain on banana leaf roofs; the distinctive splash-pattern of rice paddy water being displaced by something very large moving beneath the surface.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The cool, mineral-rich water of Ha Long Bay against sun-warmed skin; the heavy, fertile scent of the Red River Delta's alluvial soil during planting season; the tingling, electric sensation of dragon blood activating in the veins--like drinking ginger tea that heats the body from the bones outward; the acrid smoke of burning imperial tax records.

**The Core Tension:** Heritage vs. Erasure. The Han occupation does not merely tax the Lac Viet; it systematically replaces their identity. Names are changed to Chinese forms. Customs are banned. The dragon blood origin story is declared "barbaric superstition." The characters must decide not only whether to fight the occupation but how to preserve what it means to be Lac Viet when the empire wants to erase the concept entirely.

## 2. Historical & Mythological Context

The Trung Sisters' Rebellion (40-43 CE) is one of the most celebrated events in Vietnamese history. Trung Trac and Trung Nhi were daughters of a Lac Viet lord. When Trung Trac's husband was executed by the Han governor, the sisters raised an army that included many women warriors and commanders, liberated 65 citadels, and established an independent kingdom for three years before the Han reconquest. They are national heroes of Vietnam, honored with temples, holidays, and streets named in their honor across the country.

The Lac Long Quan and Au Co origin myth is foundational to Vietnamese national identity. The hundred eggs symbolize the unity of the Vietnamese people across geography (mountain and sea). The Long dragon is distinct from Chinese dragons--typically depicted with flowing mane-whiskers, deer antlers, and a sinuous serpentine body. Ha Long Bay's name literally translates to "Descending Dragon Bay," referencing the legend that dragons sent by the gods spat jewels that became the limestone islands, protecting the coast from invasion.

Dong Son bronze drums (dating from approximately 600 BCE to 200 CE) are masterpieces of Bronze Age metallurgy unique to the Lac Viet/Vietnamese cultural sphere. Their surfaces are decorated with intricate patterns depicting boats, warriors, birds, and ritual scenes. They functioned as ceremonial instruments, status symbols, and are believed to have had spiritual significance.

> **Note on Respectful Portrayal:** The Trung Sisters, the Dragon King origin myth, and Dong Son drum culture are central pillars of Vietnamese national identity and cultural pride. This setting presents Vietnamese mythology and history with deep reverence, centering Vietnamese heroism and cultural genius against imperial erasure.

## 3. Core Factions & Traditions

### The Dragon-Blooded (Con Rong Chau Tien)
Direct descendants of the hundred eggs of Lac Long Quan and Au Co whose dormant dragon heritage can be awakened through ritual, extreme emotion, or proximity to sleeping Long dragons.
- **Cultural Association:** The dragon-blooded come from all walks of Lac Viet life--fisher folk, rice farmers, bronze workers, warriors. What distinguishes them is a hereditary sensitivity to water, a resonance with bronze Dong Son drums, and the potential for draconic transformation. Fully awakened dragon-blooded can breathe mist, command river currents, and in rare cases, partially transform (scaled skin, elongated limbs, water breathing).
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"When I submerge in the river, I can hold my breath for an hour without training--the blood remembers the sea"*
  - *"My skin hardens to bronze scales when my family is threatened"*
  - *"The Dong Son drum in our village shrine hums when I place my hands on it, and the river rises"*

### The Elephant Commanders (Tuong Ba Voi)
Elite military tacticians of the Trung Sisters' rebellion who ride war elephants into battle against Han cavalry.
- **Cultural Association:** Master strategists, elephant trainers, and swamp warfare specialists. Vietnamese war elephants were smaller and more agile than their Indian counterparts, adapted for jungle and rice paddy terrain. Elephant commanders use the flooded landscape as a weapon, luring heavy Han cavalry and chariots into waterlogged paddies where they become mired.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"My elephant, Jade Mountain, has charged through Han shield walls three times and never stumbled"*
  - *"I can read the depth of a rice paddy by the way the water moves and know exactly where a horse will sink"*
  - *"The Trung Sisters appointed me because I won a battle with forty elephants against two thousand infantry"*

### The Bronze Drum Keepers (Nguoi Giu Trong Dong)
Artisan-priests who forge, maintain, and play the sacred Dong Son bronze drums that serve as spiritual beacons for the Lac Viet.
- **Cultural Association:** Master metallurgists, ritualists, and acoustic engineers. Each drum is a unique instrument with specific tonal qualities that can resonate with dragon blood, call rain, and transmit messages across river valleys. The Han occupation has targeted the drums for destruction, making the keepers fugitives protecting their nation's sonic heritage.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I carry the smallest surviving Dong Son drum on my back; it weighs forty pounds and can call rain from a clear sky"*
  - *"My hands know the twelve sacred rhythms that awaken different aspects of the dragon blood"*
  - *"I have hidden seven great drums in caves across Ha Long Bay, and each one guards a sleeping Long"*

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Dragon Blood Awakening

Characters of Lac Viet descent track a **Dragon Blood Meter** (1-6):

- **1-2 (Dormant):** The dragon heritage sleeps. The character is fully human with no supernatural abilities but is also invisible to celestial bureaucrats and imperial spirit-hunters. +1d6 to Mind (human adaptability and cunning are at their peak when the blood is quiet).
- **3-4 (Stirring):** The blood begins to warm. The character can breathe underwater for extended periods, sense the location of the nearest body of water, and communicate with river spirits. Dong Son drums resonate in their presence. +1d6 to Presence when rallying Lac Viet people. Costs 1 Momentum per scene. **Risk:** The character's eyes occasionally flash jade-green, making them identifiable to Han spirit-hunters.
- **5-6 (Awakened):** Full draconic manifestation. +1d6 to Force and Finesse. The character can command water currents (redirecting rivers, creating waterspouts), breathe a mist that conceals allies, and partially transform (scaled forearms, clawed hands, elongated jaw). In proximity to a sleeping Long dragon, they can attempt to wake it. **Risk:** The Jade Emperor's celestial bureaucracy dispatches a spirit-auditor to investigate unauthorized dragon activity. Prolonged awakening (more than 3 scenes at level 5-6) triggers an imperial celestial warrant.

**Recovering Dragon Blood Balance:** Immersion in natural water (rivers, sea, rain) stabilizes the blood. Playing or hearing a Dong Son drum can raise or lower the meter by 1 (player choice). Consuming plain rice and river water grounds the blood; consuming alcohol or heavily spiced food inflames it.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### Archetype 1: The Daughter of the River (Trung Trac's Lieutenant)
- **Concept:** A young rice farmer's daughter who discovered her dragon blood when Han soldiers tried to burn her village's paddies. She walked into the flames, and the river rose to meet her. Now she serves as one of the Trung Sisters' most trusted lieutenants, leading a guerrilla company through the flooded delta lands.
- **Quote:** *"They burned my rice. So I drowned their garrison. The river and I have an understanding."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"The Red River parts for me when I walk into its current"*, *"Can read the health of a rice paddy by touching the water"*, *"Commander of the 'Mist Company'--forty fighters who move through river fog like ghosts"*, *"My dragon blood runs hottest when someone threatens farmers"*.
- **Drive:** *"Drive the Han from the Red River Delta and return the land to the people who tend it."*
- **Flaw:** *"My rage at the occupation overwhelms my tactical judgment when I see villages burning."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 2 | Mind 1 | Presence 3

### Archetype 2: The Last Dong Son Drummer
- **Concept:** An elderly bronze worker who has spent twenty years hiding the great drums from Han confiscation. He has mapped every cave in Ha Long Bay, and each cave holds a drum. Each drum guards a sleeping Long dragon. He is the only person alive who knows all twelve sacred rhythms--and he is running out of time to teach them to someone.
- **Quote:** *"When I die, twelve songs die with me. And when the songs die, the dragons will never wake. So I will not die until I find a student."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Keeper of the twelve sacred Dong Son rhythms"*, *"Mapped every cave in Ha Long Bay by sound alone"*, *"My hands have forged bronze for sixty years and can feel impurities by touch"*, *"The Han have destroyed ninety of the hundred great drums; I have hidden the last ten"*.
- **Drive:** *"Pass the twelve rhythms to a worthy successor before the Han find the hidden drums."*
- **Flaw:** *"My standards for a successor are impossibly high, and time is running out."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 1 | Mind 4 | Presence 2

### Archetype 3: The Ha Long Bay Fisher-Pirate
- **Concept:** A fisher who knows Ha Long Bay's two thousand karst islands better than anyone alive. She runs a smuggling network through the bay's hidden caves, moving Dong Son drums, weapons, and dragon-blooded refugees past Han naval patrols. Her boat is fast, her crew is loyal, and the Long dragon sleeping inside her favorite karst pillar sometimes generates helpful fog when she asks politely.
- **Quote:** *"Two thousand islands, ten thousand caves, one Han patrol boat. I like those odds."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Navigates Ha Long Bay's karst maze blindfolded by current and echo"*, *"Captain of the smuggling junk 'Jade Phantom'"*, *"The sleeping Long in Pillar 847 creates fog when I bring it offerings of bronze"*, *"Can outrun any Han warship in shallow water because I know where the rocks are"*.
- **Drive:** *"Keep the smuggling routes open so the rebellion can be supplied, and maybe wake a dragon or two along the way."*
- **Flaw:** *"I trust the sea more than I trust people, and that makes me a poor leader on dry land."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 4 | Mind 2 | Presence 1

## 6. Condition Palette

**Physical Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Paddy Fever* (infection from wading in flooded fields), *Bronze Rash* (skin irritation from dragon blood interacting with non-Dong Son metals), *Monsoon Chill*.
- **Moderate:** *Han Crossbow Bolt*, *Dragon Blood Surge* (painful partial transformation that leaves burns), *War Elephant Trampled*.
- **Severe:** *Jade Scale Lock* (dragon blood transformation freezes mid-change, locking limbs in scaled form), *Drowned in the Red River* (lungs filled during an underwater battle), *Celestial Brand* (a spirit-auditor has marked you for collection).

**Social Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Imperial Suspicion* (Han authorities are watching you), *Drum Keeper's Burden* (you carry a secret that isolates you from your community).
- **Moderate:** *Bounty Posted* (the Han governor has priced your head), *Dragon Blood Exposed* (your transformations have been witnessed by informants).
- **Severe:** *Family Held Hostage* (the Han have taken your kin as leverage), *Celestial Warrant* (the Jade Emperor's court has authorized your arrest by spirit-agents).

**Psychological Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *River Longing* (the dragon blood pulls you toward water constantly), *Drum Echo* (you hear phantom bronze rhythms in silence).
- **Moderate:** *Ancestor Overload* (voices of a hundred dragon-blooded generations crowd your thoughts), *Transformation Dysphoria* (the dragon blood changes your body in ways that feel alien).
- **Severe:** *Identity Dissolution* (you can no longer tell whether you are human with dragon blood or a dragon wearing human skin), *The Hundred Voices* (all one hundred original children of Au Co speak through you simultaneously).

## 7. Starter Scenario: The Waking of Ha Long

**Framing:** A Han punitive fleet of twelve warships sails into Ha Long Bay to destroy a rebel supply cache hidden in the karst caves. The rebels have three smuggling junks and forty fighters. The Han have crossbow batteries, fire ships, and a Chinese court sorcerer who can bind water spirits. The only way to survive is to wake one of the sleeping Long dragons inside the karst pillars. The Last Dong Son Drummer knows which rhythm to play. The Daughter of the River has enough dragon blood to serve as a conduit. The Ha Long Bay Fisher-Pirate knows which pillar holds the most powerful Long. But waking a dragon in Ha Long Bay will send a shockwave through the spirit world that the Jade Emperor's bureaucracy cannot ignore.

**3-Layer Scene Design:**

1. **Immediate Threat:** The Han fleet has cornered the three rebel junks in a narrow channel between karst pillars. Fire ships are approaching from the east. The court sorcerer is chanting a binding that will freeze the water around the rebel boats. The characters must break the binding (Mind), destroy the fire ships (Force/Finesse), or navigate through a hidden karst cave passage (Finesse) to escape the trap.

2. **Ticking Clock:** The Dong Son Drummer needs 5 beats of uninterrupted playing to wake the Long dragon. Each beat, he must be protected from Han boarding parties, crossbow fire, and the court sorcerer's disruption attempts. If interrupted, the rhythm must restart from Beat 1.

3. **Opportunity:** Inside the karst pillar, the sleeping Long dragon will only wake if three conditions are met: the correct rhythm is played (the Drummer), a dragon-blooded descendant places their hand on the dragon's jade-scaled brow (the Daughter of the River), and the dragon is given a reason to care about the human world again (the hardest part--the dragon has been sleeping for a thousand years and has forgotten why mortals matter).

**Antagonist:** ***Governor Ma Yuan's Court Sorcerer, Wu Lingzhi*** -- A genuinely brilliant and not entirely unsympathetic Han court magician who serves the empire because he believes that unification under Han civilization is the only path to peace. He has studied dragon lore extensively and understands (correctly) that waking a Long dragon could destabilize the entire celestial order.
- **Traits:** Water-binding sorcery, calligraphy-based ward magic (can write binding characters on water that freeze it solid), genuinely erudite and willing to debate philosophy mid-battle, physically frail.
- **Condition Track:** ☐ Binding Active (water freezing around rebel boats) / ☐ Binding Disrupted (dragon blood resonance interferes with his calligraphy) / ☐ Confronted with Truth (shown evidence that the Lac Viet dragon heritage is real, not superstition) / ☐ Defeated or Persuaded (subdued in combat, or convinced that the Long dragon's awakening is a force for cosmic good, not chaos).
- **Shadow Cost:** If the Long dragon wakes, the Han fleet is destroyed--but a celestial spirit-auditor arrives within one session to investigate. If Wu Lingzhi is killed rather than persuaded, the Han response will be ten times harsher (historical: Ma Yuan's brutal reconquest). If he is persuaded, he becomes a double agent who feeds misinformation to the Han, buying the rebellion years.

## 8. Why This Setting Matters

Vietnamese mythology centers on one of the most powerful origin stories in world folklore: that an entire nation descends from the union of a dragon and a fairy. This is not merely a fairy tale--it is a declaration of identity that has sustained the Vietnamese people through millennia of foreign occupation, from the Han Chinese to the Mongols to the French. The Trung Sisters' rebellion is one of history's earliest recorded instances of women-led military resistance, and their legacy resonates in Vietnamese culture to this day. In a tabletop RPG context, this setting offers something rare: a heroic mythology where the characters are not exceptional individuals in an ordinary world, but ordinary people discovering that their entire nation carries the extraordinary within its bloodline. The dragon does not choose the hero; the hero discovers they were always a dragon.
