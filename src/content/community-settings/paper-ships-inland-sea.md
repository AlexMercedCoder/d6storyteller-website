## 1. Setting Overview and Tone

The Inland Sea of Taro is a body of fresh water large enough to require three days to cross by conventional boat, surrounded entirely by mountains that make overland trade prohibitively expensive. The paper ship tradition has made the Inland Sea an efficient trade route for four centuries: guild folders craft vessels from specific paper, according to specific measurements, speak the activation phrase over the completed fold, and launch them at the appropriate lunar moment. By dawn, the cargo is on the far shore.

The tradition is not magic in any academic sense. The guild masters who have studied it for generations cannot explain the mechanism. They know the process, they know the failure conditions, and they know that something has been wrong for the past twenty years that the guild's knowledge base has not been able to diagnose.

**Tone:** Quietly mysterious and culturally rich. The setting is not about dramatic magic. It is about craft, tradition, and the specific anxiety of watching something you have inherited stop working as well as it used to, without knowing whether the problem is in the practice or in the world.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The guild folding rooms: large, dry, and temperature-controlled, with long tables where folders work in precise coordination. The launch ceremony at the shore: the completed paper vessel floating on the water, and then the transformation as the lunar conditions are met, the paper taking on volume and solidity over the course of a few minutes. The successful ship floating in full size at the launch point, waiting for its cargo to be loaded.
2. **Sound:** The silence during folding: the guild maintains quiet rooms because vocal errors during the folding process are believed to contaminate the vessel. The launch words themselves: a specific phrase in an old dialect that most guild members have learned phonetically rather than linguistically. The sound of a failed launch: the paper vessel simply floating without transformation, until the water eventually takes it.
3. **Touch and Smell:** The paper: a specific material made from Taro mountain cedar, dense and slightly waxy, very different from ordinary paper. The fold itself: experienced folders can assess a vessel's quality by how it feels at the final crease, a specific resistance that indicates successful internal structure. The smell of a successful transformation: something like lightning-struck wood, present for a few seconds and then gone.

## 2. The Inland Sea

**The Guild Hall of Taro** is the central facility where master folders train apprentices, maintain the fold instruction archive, and manage the launching schedule. Guild Master Oren Sull has led the Hall for eighteen years and has been quietly tracking the declining success rate for fourteen of them.

**The Three Launch Shores** are the points from which paper ships are launched: one on the northern shore, one on the western, and one on the east. Each shore has a guild station with a small resident staff who manage the launches and receive arrivals. The eastern shore has experienced the highest failure rate in the current decline.

**The Archive Vault** contains the complete fold instruction set: the original manuscript, believed to be four centuries old, and all subsequent guild additions and refinements. The Vault is restricted to master folders. The original manuscript is in an old script that four masters can partially read and no one can fully translate.

**The Mountain Passes** are the overland alternative to the paper ship route. They are expensive, slow, and controlled by the three mountain kingdoms that surround the Inland Sea. If the paper ship tradition fails entirely, the mountain kingdoms will control all trade access to the sea. Guild Master Sull is aware of this. The mountain kingdoms are aware that the guild is aware.

## 3. Factions

**The Folder Guild** is the institution managing the tradition. Its master folders are skilled craftspeople who have devoted their careers to a process they understand practically but not theoretically. The Guild is internally divided about how to respond to the declining success rate: some want to investigate the original manuscript for missed instructions, some believe the paper quality has declined and want to change suppliers, and some quietly suspect that the tradition itself is ending.

**The Mountain Kingdom Envoys** have been appearing at guild functions more frequently in the past five years. Their interest in the guild's operational challenges is professionally courteous and politically transparent. Trade Envoy Mira of the Eastern Kingdom has offered twice to "assist" with the guild's archival research. The guild has declined both times.

**The Traditional Practitioners** are an informal group of older guild members and retired folders who believe the decline is the result of departures from the original practice: modern paper stock, modified launch timing, or the increasing commercial pressure to launch more ships per cycle than the tradition was designed to support. Their leader, retired Master Dax, maintains a personal practice that uses only the oldest materials and the most conservative interpretation of the launch procedure.

**The Young Folders** are the apprentice generation facing a career in a tradition that is working at roughly half the rate it did when their teachers trained. The most vocal among them, Sela Dar, has been documenting the failure pattern in detail and has identified correlations that the masters have not acknowledged. Her data suggests the decline is not uniform: specific fold patterns fail consistently while others remain reliable. This implies the problem is structural rather than material.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Lunar Craft

Track the **Lunar Phase** during each launch attempt.

| Phase | Base Success |
|-------|-------------|
| Full Moon | Automatic success |
| Waxing Moon | Finesse roll, difficulty 2 |
| Waning Moon | Finesse roll, difficulty 3 |
| Dark Moon | Finesse roll, difficulty 5; success produces an unpredictable ship |

**Declining reliability:** Each in-game season (four sessions), the failure threshold increases by 1. Full Moon eventually requires a Finesse roll. What was reliable becomes uncertain.

**Failure consequences:**
- Paper ship fails to transform: cargo delayed, launch materials lost
- Paper ship partially transforms: vessel is unstable, potentially dangerous to load
- Dark moon success: ship arrives but with wrong cargo, wrong size, or from an unexpected direction, sometimes carrying things that were not loaded

**Investigation:** Characters can research the decline by:
- Reading the original archive manuscript (Mind roll to interpret old script)
- Testing specific fold variations under controlled conditions
- Consulting Traditional Practitioners about historical practice differences

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Documenting Apprentice

You are Sela Dar. You have been tracking failure data for three years and you have identified a structural pattern that the masters have not acknowledged. You do not know whether they have not seen it or have chosen not to discuss it. You are an apprentice in an institution where speaking above your station is a significant professional risk. You are also watching the tradition your career depends on deteriorate in a way that somebody should be urgently investigating.

- **Drive:** *"Present your findings to the Guild in a way they cannot dismiss, and find out whether the pattern points to a fixable problem or to something the masters already know and are managing in silence."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### The Master in Denial

You are one of the senior masters who has been aware of the declining rate but has been managing it through conservative public statements and internal optimism. You remember when the tradition worked with near-perfect reliability. You remember your teacher telling you the same thing about the generation before. You have been telling yourself that every generation sees a dip and recovers. The data Sela is collecting is making that self-reassurance harder to maintain.

- **Drive:** *"Determine whether the decline is cyclical and recoverable or structural and permanent, before the mountain kingdoms use the Guild's uncertainty to take control of the passes."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### The Traditional Practitioner

You are retired Master Dax. You have maintained the original practice for forty years and your personal success rate has not declined. You believe you know why. You also believe that telling the Guild what you know will require an admission that the past two generations of refinement and modernization have compounded the problem rather than improved it, and you are not certain the Guild is ready to hear that.

- **Drive:** *"Find a way to demonstrate that the original practice is the recovery path without destroying the careers of the masters who implemented the changes that caused the problem."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

## 6. Starter Scenario and Why It Matters

**The Full Moon Failure:** A paper ship launched on a Full Moon night, which should be automatic, has failed to transform. This has never happened before in the Guild's four-century record. Guild Master Sull has called an emergency session. Sela's data now includes this incident. Master Dax has requested permission to demonstrate his traditional technique to the full council. The Mountain Kingdom envoy has sent a diplomatic message expressing "concern for the Guild's operational challenges" and offering to facilitate a "partnership arrangement" while the Guild's situation stabilizes. The characters must investigate the Full Moon failure, navigate the Guild's internal politics, and determine whether the tradition can be recovered before the mountain kingdoms' diplomatic pressure becomes a territorial claim.

**Why It Matters:** Paper Ships of the Inland Sea is about the relationship between tradition and understanding. The guild has maintained a practice that works without ever fully understanding why it works. When it starts failing, they have no theoretical framework for diagnosis, only the practice itself. The setting asks what happens when craft knowledge is not enough, and whether understanding a tradition is necessary to preserving it.
