## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The Netherlands is the only nation on earth built largely *below sea level*. A quarter of the country's land area was literally created by draining seawater and lake beds through an extraordinary system of dykes, windmills, and canals. This is not merely an engineering achievement; it is an ongoing act of defiance against the natural order. The Dutch word for a catastrophic flood-**waterwolf** (*waterwolf*)-personifies the sea as a predator.

In this setting, the Waterwolves are real. They are ancient water spirits-primordial entities of the North Sea-that view the polder system as a theft of their territory. Every dyke is a wall in a war. Every windmill is a weapon. The drowned dead of the great floods (the St. Lucia's Flood of 1287, the St. Elizabeth's Flood of 1421, dozens of others) still linger in the waterlogged polder soil, pulled between the living world and the sea's dominion. A secret society called the **Dijkgraafschap** (Dyke Mastership) maintains both the physical dykes and the spiritual wards sealed into their foundations.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The flat, endless Dutch landscape where the sky occupies three-quarters of the visual field; rows of windmills turning against grey storm clouds, their sails inscribed with protective symbols visible only at dusk; a Waterwolf manifesting during a storm surge-a massive, wolf-shaped wave crashing against a dyke with visible malice, its foam-formed eyes burning with pale light; the drowned dead rising from polder mud during a high tide, waterlogged corpses of sailors and farmers from centuries past.
2. **Sound:** The rhythmic creak of windmill sails and the splash of their pump machinery-the heartbeat of Dutch survival; the warning toll of the *noodklok* (emergency bell) announcing a dyke breach; the subsonic groan of a dyke under pressure from a storm surge; whispers rising from canal water at night in languages dead for centuries.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The salt-wind taste of North Sea air; the distinctive Dutch smell of peat, canal water, and brine; the cold, sucking grip of polder mud that can swallow a person to the waist; the warm, yeasty comfort of a Dutch *bruin café* tavern against the perpetual damp.

**The Core Tension:** Civilization vs. Nature. The Netherlands is humanity's most audacious claim against the natural world. The Waterwolves are nature's response. The characters must decide whether to maintain the war against the sea or find an accommodation that both sides can survive.

## 2. Historical & Mythological Context

Dutch water management is one of humanity's greatest collective engineering achievements, developed over centuries and requiring constant, cooperative maintenance. The *poldermodel*-the Dutch tradition of consensus-based decision-making-was literally born from the necessity of water management: if the dyke fails, everyone drowns, so everyone must agree on how to maintain it.

Dutch folklore includes **Waterwolves** (storm floods personified as predators), **witte wieven** (white women spirits of the mist), the legend of the **Flying Dutchman** (a ghost ship condemned to sail forever), and various water spirits associated with canals and rivers.

> **Note on Respectful Portrayal:** Dutch water management traditions and the national trauma of flooding are treated with respect. The *poldermodel* of cooperative governance is presented as a genuine cultural achievement.

## 3. Core Factions & Traditions

### The Dijkgraafschap (Dyke Mastership)
The secret society of dyke-keepers who maintain both physical and spiritual defenses against the sea.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"Every dyke I build has protective runes sealed into the foundation stones with salt and iron"*
  - *"I can feel a dyke weakness through the vibration of the earth. The sea tests every wall, every night."*
  - *"My family has maintained this stretch of dyke for nine generations. The sea knows our name and hates us personally."*

### The Molenaar Guild (Windmill Sorcerers)
Windmill operators who use the rotational energy of their mills to power spiritual wards.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"The windmill is not just a pump. It is a prayer wheel. Every rotation pushes the sea back, physically and spiritually."*
  - *"I inscribe the sail patterns with ward-sigils that are only visible when the sails are spinning at full speed"*

### The Verdronkenen (The Drowned)
The restless dead of centuries of flooding who exist in the waterlogged soil beneath the polders.
- **Descriptor Examples (for intermediaries):**
  - *"I hear the drowned when the water table rises. They whisper in Old Dutch and Middle Frisian."*
  - *"The drowned are not angry. They are lonely. They have been underwater for centuries and they miss daylight."*

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Polder Balance

Unlike individual character meters, **Polder Balance** is a communal meter (1-6) shared by the entire party:

- **6 (Droog / Dry):** The dykes hold perfectly. +1d6 to all Presence rolls. No supernatural threats. The sea is calm. Complacency sets in.
- **4-5 (Stabiel / Stable):** Normal operations. Minor leaks and seepage. The drowned whisper but do not manifest. Standard play.
- **2-3 (Gevaarlijk / Dangerous):** Dykes are stressed. Waterwolves probe the defenses. The drowned begin to manifest. Flooding threatens low-lying areas. -1d6 to all Presence in affected areas.
- **1 (Doorbraak / Breach):** Catastrophic dyke failure. Waterwolves flood through. The drowned rise en masse. The polder reverts to sea. -1d6 to all rolls. Emergency survival mode.

**Raising Polder Balance:** Repairing dykes, renewing spiritual wards, appeasing the drowned, maintaining windmill operations. **Lowering it:** Storm damage, neglected maintenance, spiritual desecration, Waterwolf attacks, complacency.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### Archetype 1: The Dijkgraaf of Zeeland
- **Concept:** The Dyke Master of the most flood-prone province in the Netherlands. She has maintained her stretch of dyke through three major storms and personally sealed the wards into thirty foundations. She is the most competent person in any room and the most exhausted.
- **Quote:** *"The sea never sleeps. Neither do I."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 1

### Archetype 2: The Windmill Sorcerer of Kinderdijk
- **Concept:** Operator of one of the nineteen windmills at Kinderdijk, the most famous pumping station in the Netherlands. His mill is the spiritual anchor for the entire polder system-if it stops turning, the wards across three provinces weaken.
- **Quote:** *"When the wind stops, we all drown. So I have learned to make the wind obey."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### Archetype 3: The Speaker for the Drowned
- **Concept:** A woman who nearly drowned as a child and was pulled back from death with the voices of the drowned still in her ears. She can communicate with the dead beneath the polders and has learned that they are not the enemy-they are prisoners of the sea, and they want to be freed as much as the living want the dykes to hold.
- **Quote:** *"The drowned do not want to destroy the polders. They want to rest. But the sea will not let them, and neither will we."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 1 | Mind 2 | Presence 4

## 6. Condition Palette

**Physical:** *Polder Mud Trapped*, *Waterwolf Impact* (hit by a wave with intent), *Hypothermia* (North Sea cold), *Dyke Collapse Injury*.
**Social:** *Dijkgraaf's Censure* (failed your dyke duty), *Accused of Sabotage* (blamed for a breach), *Drowned Debt* (you promised the dead something you cannot deliver).
**Psychological:** *Flood Anxiety* (rain triggers panic), *Sea Voice* (the Waterwolf whispers offers of peace if you open the dyke), *Polder Guilt* (the knowledge that your dry land is built on drowned dead).

## 7. Starter Scenario: The Night the Wind Stopped

**Framing:** A freak calm has settled over the Netherlands. No wind means no windmills. No windmills means no pumping. The water table is rising. The wards powered by windmill rotation are failing. The Waterwolves sense the weakness and are massing for an assault on the Zeeland dykes. The Dijkgraaf must organize emergency defenses. The Windmill Sorcerer must find a way to power the wards without wind. The Speaker for the Drowned must negotiate with the dead to serve as an early-warning system.

**Antagonist:** ***The Eldest Waterwolf*** - Not a creature but a force: the accumulated rage of the North Sea over centuries of land reclamation. It does not think in human terms. It simply wants what was taken. It can be bargained with-the Dutch can return some polder land to the sea in exchange for peace-but this means flooding villages, displacing families, and admitting that the great engineering project has limits.

## 8. Why This Setting Matters

The Netherlands exists because of collective action. The *poldermodel*-cooperate or drown-is the oldest functioning democratic tradition in Europe. This setting turns that pragmatic reality into mythology: the dyke is both an engineering structure and a spiritual ward, and maintaining it requires both technical skill and moral commitment. The question at the heart of the setting-can humanity claim territory from nature without eventually paying the price?-is the defining environmental question of the 21st century, set in the 17th.
