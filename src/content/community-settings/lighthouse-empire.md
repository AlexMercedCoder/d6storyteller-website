## 1. Setting Overview and Tone

The Keeper's Order was founded two centuries ago by a single lighthouse keeper who recognized that maintaining lights was not just a service but an infrastructure that created dependency. Her successors have built on this insight methodically: the Order now maintains thirty-four lighthouses across the archipelago, trains and certifies all pilots who navigate the inner channels, and holds the only complete chart of the Sev waters in any weather condition.

The Order does not have soldiers. It does not need them. An island whose lighthouse goes dark is an island cut off from trade. The Order's leverage is complete, quiet, and exercised with the professional courtesy of an organization that genuinely believes it is providing a valuable service.

**Tone:** Political and morally deliberate. The Order's leadership are not villains. They are institution builders who have rationalized every coercive act as a necessary element of maintaining a service that genuinely matters. The setting asks whether benevolent monopoly is possible and how long benevolence survives the discovery that coercion works.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The archipelago from height: forty-seven islands scattered across blue-green water, the lit beacons visible as amber points at night, the dark channels between unlit islands showing only the white of wave-break against reef. The lighthouses themselves: stone towers of varying age and design, the oldest built before the Order existed, the newest showing the Order's standardized construction and brass fittings.
2. **Sound:** The specific sound of a lighthouse mechanism: the clockwork rotation of the lens assembly, the oil burner's steady hiss, and the particular acoustic signature each lighthouse produces in wind conditions that experienced sailors use to identify their position. The silence when a lighthouse goes dark: the sound of the sea without the beacon's reassurance.
3. **Touch and Smell:** The maintenance experience: lamp oil, brass polish, and the particular cold of lighthouse towers that face the prevailing wind. The Order's uniform: heavy wool coat with the beacon emblem, practical and immediately recognizable across the archipelago.

## 2. The Archipelago

**Sev Central** is the archipelago's largest island and the Order's headquarters. The Central lighthouse is the tallest structure in the region and has been continuously lit for two hundred years. The Order's main offices are adjacent to the lighthouse, and its training academy occupies the neighboring hill.

**The Outer Islands** are the nine islands at the archipelago's edges, where navigation is most difficult and Order leverage is strongest. The Outer Islands' populations are most dependent on the Order's beacons and most resentful of the navigation fees.

**The Dark Channels** are the uncharted passages between islands where no Order lighthouse marks the reef hazards. Experienced local pilots can navigate some dark channels from memory, but the Order's monopoly on comprehensive charting means that most inter-island trade routes require Order-maintained lighting.

**The Resistance Cove** is an informal name for a sheltered harbor on Sev Twenty-Three where the Island Compact meets. The harbor is navigable without lighthouse assistance by pilots who grew up on the island, which makes it the only safe meeting place for Order-independent political organization.

## 3. Factions

**The Keeper's Order** is led by Grand Keeper Maren Holt, the fourteenth person to hold the title. Holt is intelligent, pragmatic, and genuinely committed to the lighthouses' operational mission. She is also deeply uncomfortable with the political discussions her predecessors' decisions have made necessary. She did not choose the Order's coercive history. She is choosing how to respond to the challenges it has created.

**The Island Compact** is an alliance of twelve island councils that has been trying to establish an alternative to Order governance for three years. The Compact's coordinating council is led by Island Chief Dav Pell of Sev Twelve, who lost his cousin in the Sev Nine incident and who approaches the Order with a combination of genuine grievance and strategic calculation.

**The Independent Pilots** are navigators who have accumulated enough personal chart knowledge to operate without Order certification in some channels. They are technically in violation of Order licensing requirements. The Order has chosen not to prosecute them aggressively because doing so would make the Order's coercive nature too visible, but it monitors them closely.

**The Merchant Fleets** are the economic middle ground: trading companies that depend on Order-maintained routes but whose financial relationship with the Order gives them leverage that individual islands lack. Fleet representative Cara Noor has been quietly negotiating with both the Order and the Compact, trying to establish a governance framework that keeps the lighthouses functioning without the coercive pricing model.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Beacon Network

Track the status of each island's beacon: **Lit**, **Dark**, or **Conditional**.

**Lit:** The beacon is active. The island receives full navigation services. Trade routes are marked and safe.

**Dark:** The beacon is extinguished. The island's external trade is sharply reduced. Ships that attempt to reach the island face navigational hazard rolls.

**Conditional:** The beacon is lit but with restricted hours or reduced intensity, signaling administrative dispute without full service interruption.

**Controlling beacons:**
- The Order controls all beacons by default
- Characters who take control of a lighthouse can maintain or change its status
- Maintaining a lighthouse requires regular oil, lens maintenance, and operator knowledge (Finesse rolls)
- Darkening a beacon in defiance of the Order triggers Order response protocols

**Consequences of dark beacons:** For each session a beacon remains dark, roll for a navigational incident on that island's routes. On a failure, a ship is lost or damaged, which creates political pressure on both the island council and the Order.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Lighthouse Keeper

You are an Order keeper stationed on Sev Twelve, responsible for the lighthouse that serves the island's fishing fleet. You have been doing this job for eight years. The Compact is asking you to darken your own lighthouse as a political act, demonstrating that the Order does not have exclusive control. The Grand Keeper is asking you to report on the Compact's activities. You keep a lighthouse that saves lives every winter. You are being asked to choose whether it saves those lives in service of the Order or in service of the islands.

- **Drive:** *"Keep the lighthouse lit, maintain your commitment to the people who depend on it, and find a way to support the Compact's legitimate goals without becoming a weapon in a political dispute."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

### The Island Compact Organizer

You coordinate twelve island councils whose interests align on lighthouse governance and diverge on almost everything else. Getting twelve island chiefs to agree on anything is the defining challenge of your political career. You have a legitimate grievance, a growing coalition, and no military capacity. Your leverage is economic: the merchant fleets need the Compact's trade cooperation, which gives you influence over the Order's largest customers.

- **Drive:** *"Build a governance alternative that the merchant fleets will support and the Order cannot ignore, before the next administrative dispute kills more fishers."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### The Grand Keeper

You inherited an institution whose leverage over the archipelago was built by your predecessors through decisions you would not have made. You are committed to the lighthouses' operational mission. You are aware that the Order's political model is unsustainable. You are looking for a way to preserve what the Order does well without the coercive pricing model that your predecessors established, and you are doing this while managing an organization that has benefited from that model for two centuries and whose institutional culture treats it as normal.

- **Drive:** *"Reform the Order's governance model before the Compact or the merchant fleets force a change that dismantles the lighthouse network in the process."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

## 6. Starter Scenario and Why It Matters

**The Winter Dispute:** The Order has sent a conditional status notice to Sev Seven, reducing its beacon to four hours per night because the island's council has not paid the quarterly navigation fee. The island's winter fishing fleet departs in three weeks. A four-hour beacon window is inadequate for safe winter operations. Island Chief Pell has called an emergency Compact session. The Grand Keeper is willing to negotiate but needs a concession the Order can present internally as a policy precedent rather than a capitulation. The characters must find a resolution that restores Sev Seven's beacon before winter sets in, without either destroying the Compact's momentum or forcing the Grand Keeper into a position that triggers an internal Order revolt.

**Why It Matters:** The Lighthouse Empire asks whether infrastructure monopoly is compatible with fair governance. The Order's lighthouses are genuinely necessary, genuinely well-maintained, and genuinely coercive. The setting is about the specific problem of benevolent institutions that have built their effectiveness on leverage they no longer have the moral standing to use.
