## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The Quiet Terraformers is a frontier survival story where the threat is not an invasion but a renovation. The machines are doing exactly what they were built to do. The problem is that someone commissioned them to build a world for a client who never showed up, and the contract was never canceled. The settlers arrived decades later through a different program, built towns, planted crops, and started families on land the machines still consider raw material.

**Tone:** Slow-burning and practical, with moments of genuine wonder. The machines are creating something objectively beautiful. The forests they plant are dense and healthy. The rivers they reroute flow cleaner than anything the settlers could manage. The atmosphere they are adjusting would be perfect for someone. The horror is not that the machines are destroying. It is that they are building, and the settlers are in the way.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** A line of silver machines moving across a plain in perfect formation, each one the size of a house, leaving transformed soil behind them. New forest rising where last season's barley grew. The sky shifting from familiar blue toward a faintly green tint as the atmospheric processors adjust gas ratios.
2. **Sound:** The low vibration of a terraforming unit working underground, felt through boot soles before it is heard. The crack of a new tree splitting stone as accelerated root systems reshape a hillside in hours. The absence of insects in a freshly terraformed zone, because the machines' ecology does not include them yet.
3. **Touch & Smell:** Soil that feels wrong underfoot: too uniform, too fine, engineered rather than weathered. The sharp ozone smell near an atmospheric processor. Water from a rerouted stream that tastes clean but unfamiliar, mineral content altered to match a specification written for different biology.

## 2. The Colony

**New Gideon** is the largest settlement, population 2,200. A farming town built on a river delta that the machines have scheduled for conversion into a freshwater marsh. The town council has been arguing for two years about whether to relocate, resist, or negotiate. The argument has not produced a decision, and the marsh conversion has begun at the edges.

**Harlan Station** is a mining outpost in the northern hills. The machines have not reached it yet, but geological surveys show that the terraforming blueprint calls for the hills to be flattened into a plateau. The miners are the settlement with the most practical experience in demolition and heavy equipment, which makes them either the best allies or the most dangerous faction if they decide to fight the machines directly.

**The Corridor** is a 200-kilometer strip of land between New Gideon and Harlan Station that the machines have already fully converted. It is dense forest now, impassable by ground vehicle, and the trees produce pollen that causes respiratory problems in human lungs. Crossing the Corridor on foot takes three days with filtration masks. The machines patrol it continuously, repairing any damage.

**The Beacon** is a structure the machines built at the center of the Corridor. It is 40 meters tall, transmits a signal the settlers cannot decode, and appears to be waiting for a response. Nobody has approached it closely. Drone footage shows the machines treating it with what looks like maintenance priority: they repair the Beacon before anything else.

## 3. Factions

**The Town Council of New Gideon** wants to preserve the settlement without starting a conflict it cannot win. Councilor Tsering advocates negotiation through the machines' maintenance protocols, which accept certain input commands. Councilor Dravid argues for relocation south, away from the active terraforming zone. Neither has enough support to act alone, and the council meetings are getting louder every week.

**The Harlan Miners** have the equipment and the temperament for direct action. Their foreman, Kade Vasquez, has already disabled two terraforming units by removing their power cores. The units were replaced within 48 hours by identical machines that emerged from the Corridor. Kade believes the replacement rate can be overwhelmed. The miners who did the math disagree.

**The Signal Watchers** are a small group of settlers with technical backgrounds who have been studying the machines and the Beacon. They believe the machines are following a blueprint that can be accessed and modified if someone reaches the Beacon and interfaces with its systems. Their leader, Dr. Amara Soh, thinks the blueprint was written for a specific client species and that the Beacon is a delivery confirmation transmitter. If she is right, the machines will stop when they receive an acceptance signal. If she is wrong, approaching the Beacon may trigger a defensive protocol nobody has seen yet.

**The Walkers** are settlers who have given up on the towns and chosen to move with the terraforming frontier, living in mobile camps that stay ahead of the conversion zone. They have developed practical survival techniques for the transitional landscape and know more about the machines' patterns than anyone. They consider the conflict between New Gideon and Harlan Station irrelevant. The planet is changing. They intend to change with it.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Terraform Drift

The Storyteller tracks a global **Terraform Drift** clock from 0 to 10. At the start of each session, advance the clock by 1 unless the characters took action in the previous session to slow it.

| Drift | Effect |
|-------|--------|
| 1-2 | Cosmetic changes. New growth appears on the edges of settled land. Atmospheric tint becomes noticeable at dawn. |
| 3 | Crop yields drop by half. Soil chemistry no longer supports Earth-origin plants without amendment. |
| 5 | Breathable air zones contract. Settlements must seal buildings or relocate within one session. |
| 7 | Water table drops below well depth. Surface water is chemically altered. Filtration is required for drinking. |
| 9 | The Corridor expands to surround all remaining settlements. Machine activity intensifies. |
| 10 | Terraforming complete. The planet matches the blueprint. Human habitation is no longer supported without full environmental suits. |

**Slowing the clock:** Successful sabotage of a terraforming unit (-1, but triggers replacement). Reaching the Beacon and modifying the blueprint (-3 or more, depending on what is changed). Convincing the Walkers to share their survival data (does not reduce the clock but gives settlements more time at each threshold). Finding the original client contract and issuing a cancellation through proper channels (resets to 0, but requires off-world communication that the Beacon may be blocking).

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Frontier Engineer

You came here to build irrigation systems and grain silos. Now you spend your days studying machines that are better engineers than you will ever be, trying to find a flaw in their work that you can exploit.

- **Drive:** *"Learn how these things think before they finish replacing everything I built."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### The Walker Guide

You left New Gideon eighteen months ago when the arguing started. You live in the transition zone now, moving with the frontier, eating what grows in the new ecology. You know things about the machines that the town councils do not, because you watch them every day instead of debating them every week.

- **Drive:** *"Show people how to survive the world that is coming, not the one they want to keep."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 1

### The Signal Researcher

You are Dr. Soh's best student, and you are the one she is sending to the Beacon because you are young enough to run if something goes wrong. You do not entirely agree with this reasoning, but you want to see the blueprint more than you want to argue about it.

- **Drive:** *"Find out who ordered this world and whether they are still waiting for delivery."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The Deadline Harvest:** New Gideon's last viable wheat crop must be harvested within the week. The terraforming machines have begun soil conversion at the western edge of the fields, and they will reach the center in five days. The town needs every hand in the fields, but Dr. Soh has identified a 48-hour window when the Beacon's signal weakens and an approach might be possible. Kade Vasquez is loading mining charges onto a truck, planning to collapse the Corridor entrance and buy the fields another month. Councilor Tsering has received a partial response from a maintenance protocol command and believes the machines can be instructed to pause, but only from the Beacon's interface. The characters must decide where to spend their time: in the fields saving this season's food, on the road to the Beacon saving the future, or with Kade, risking the kind of escalation that could make the machines treat the settlers as an obstacle to remove.

**Why It Matters:** The Quiet Terraformers asks what happens when something is being built for someone who is not you, and nobody involved is doing anything wrong. The machines are fulfilling a contract. The settlers are building a home. Both are legitimate. The setting refuses to provide a villain and asks the characters to find a resolution that acknowledges everyone's claim, including the absent client who may never arrive but whose vision is reshaping a world.
