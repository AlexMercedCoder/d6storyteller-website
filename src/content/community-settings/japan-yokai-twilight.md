## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The **Sengoku period** (1467-1615) is Japan's most chaotic era--a time when the central government collapsed and hundreds of local warlords (*daimyo*) fought endlessly for territory. But beneath this human conflict, a far older and more dangerous war is brewing. The **yokai**--an enormous taxonomy of supernatural beings ranging from playful tanuki raccoon-dogs to apocalyptic oni demons--have existed alongside humans for millennia under an ancient compact. The compact's terms: yokai stay in the spirit world (*kakuriyo*) during daylight and may visit the human world (*utsushiyo*) only during the twilight hour. Humans, in return, leave offerings at shrines and do not defile sacred spaces.

The Sengoku wars have shattered the compact. Armies march through sacred groves. Temples burn. Offerings stop. Angry yokai cross into the human world at all hours. Frightened humans retaliate by destroying shrines. The cycle accelerates. The **Tasogare** (Twilight Walkers) are a small, informal network of individuals who can exist in both worlds--humans with yokai ancestry, yokai who have lived as humans, onmyoji (yin-yang sorcerers) who negotiate between the factions, and ronin who have stumbled into the spirit world and cannot quite get back out. Their job is to prevent total war between worlds.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The blood-red torii gates of a mountain shrine receding into fog, each gate slightly more translucent than the last until the final gate exists only in the spirit world; a kitsune (fox spirit) in human form, flawlessly beautiful except for the shadow on the ground that has too many tails; castle towns where paper lanterns slowly blink open eyes at dusk and the stone walls imperceptibly shift; autumn maple leaves falling upward in a grove where a tengu (crow warrior spirit) is practicing swordplay too fast for human eyes.
2. **Sound:** The unearthly singing of a yuki-onna (snow woman) carried on a mountain wind that freezes anything it touches; the rhythmic *clack-clack* of a one-legged umbrella yokai (kasa-obake) hopping down a rain-soaked street; the profound silence of a sacred grove where even insects hold their breath; a shamisen played by invisible hands in an abandoned inn, the melody achingly beautiful and deeply wrong.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The electric tingling of the ouma-ga-toki moment when the boundary dissolves--like stepping through a waterfall that is not quite wet; the comforting warmth of a kitsune's fox fire (kitsunebi) that illuminates but does not burn; the sharp scent of pine and ozone before a tengu manifests; the unsettling sensation of a room being slightly too cold on one side, indicating an invisible yokai presence.

**The Core Tension:** Coexistence vs. Extinction. The yokai are not evil; they are an ecosystem. Eliminating them would be like eliminating weather. But unchecked yokai crossing into the human world is equally catastrophic. The Tasogare must find a new compact that works for both worlds--while dodging human armies that consider them heretics and yokai factions that consider them traitors.

## 2. Historical & Mythological Context

Japanese yokai culture is one of the most extensively catalogued supernatural traditions in world history. The Edo-period scholar **Toriyama Sekien** produced illustrated encyclopedias of over 200 yokai species, but oral traditions describe thousands more. Yokai range from terrifying (oni, yurei) to absurd (a sentient paper umbrella, a giant head that appears in doorways) to deeply poignant (spirits of abandoned objects that waited too long for their owners to return).

The Sengoku period was characterized by constant warfare, political betrayal, and social upheaval--but also extraordinary cultural creativity. It produced the tea ceremony as a meditative art, revolutionary castle architecture, and the philosophical framework of *mono no aware* (the beauty of impermanence) that defines Japanese aesthetics.

Key yokai types in this setting include **kitsune** (fox spirits who can take human form, serve the deity Inari, and range from benevolent to malicious), **tengu** (mountain-dwelling crow warriors and martial arts masters), **oni** (horned demon-ogres who can be both protectors and destroyers), **tanuki** (shape-shifting raccoon-dogs known for trickery and good humor), and **tsukumogami** (household objects that gain sentience after 100 years--umbrellas, lanterns, tea kettles).

> **Note on Respectful Portrayal:** Yokai folklore, Shinto shrine traditions, and Buddhist temple practices are living cultural elements of Japanese life. This setting treats Japanese mythology with reverence while embracing the playful, bizarre, and poignant qualities that make yokai traditions unique in world folklore.

## 3. Core Factions & Traditions

### The Tasogare (Twilight Walkers)
The loose network of intermediaries who navigate between the human and yokai worlds.
- **Cultural Association:** A diverse group united only by their ability to perceive both worlds. Members include onmyoji sorcerers, shrine maidens (*miko*), ronin who wandered into the spirit world, merchants who accidentally married kitsune, and half-yokai children. They have no central authority, no uniform, and no funding. They operate through personal relationships and a shared understanding that both worlds need each other.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I can see yokai in broad daylight, which means I can never walk through a marketplace without having a panic attack"*
  - *"My mother was a kitsune. My father was a rice farmer. I am the only person at the dinner table who understands what both sides are complaining about"*
  - *"I carry an enchanted fan that, when opened, reveals the true form of any yokai in disguise--including the ones I'd rather not know about"*

### The Kitsune Courts (Fox Spirit Nobility)
Elegant, cunning fox spirits who have infiltrated human courts and noble houses for centuries.
- **Cultural Association:** Shapeshifters who can assume perfect human form (betrayed only by their shadow, which retains fox tails). They serve Inari, the deity of rice, commerce, and prosperity. Kitsune are organized into courts with complex hierarchies based on age (measured in tail count: one tail per century, maximum nine). They are natural diplomats, strategists, and manipulators--and many have genuinely fallen in love with the humans they were originally sent to spy on.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I have five tails, which means I have lived five centuries among humans, and I still cannot predict what they will do next"*
  - *"My human husband does not know I am kitsune. My kitsune court does not know I love him. I am running out of lives in which to keep both secrets"*
  - *"Kitsune fire can light a room, cook a meal, or incinerate a castle. The art is in the calibration."*

### The Tengu Mountain Order (Crow Warrior Spirits)
Fierce martial artist spirits who dwell on sacred mountains and are the legendary teachers of Japan's greatest swordfighters.
- **Cultural Association:** Tengu are crow-featured humanoid spirits known for martial mastery, arrogance, and a peculiar code of honor. They consider themselves the greatest warriors in creation and view human warfare with contempt--not because it is violent, but because it is *sloppy*. A tengu will teach a worthy student perfect swordsmanship but punish a braggart by breaking their legs. They are fiercely territorial about their mountain domains.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I trained under the Tengu King of Mount Kurama for seven years and learned the wind-cutting strike"*
  - *"The tengu do not care about your rank or your lord. They care about your footwork."*
  - *"My tengu master called me 'adequate' after defeating twelve oni. I wept with pride."*

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Ouma-ga-toki (The Twilight Hour)

Characters track a **Twilight Resonance Meter** (1-6):

- **1-2 (Human):** The character is firmly rooted in the human world. They can perceive yokai only during ouma-ga-toki (the actual twilight hour at dusk). +1d6 to Mind (human rationality provides clarity in a chaotic world). Yokai find them difficult to target because they are "spiritually quiet."
- **3-4 (Twilight Walker):** The character exists in both worlds simultaneously. They can perceive and interact with yokai at all times. +1d6 to Presence when negotiating with yokai (the character's dual nature makes them a credible intermediary). They can briefly manifest minor yokai abilities (fox fire, shadow manipulation, understanding animal speech). Costs 1 Momentum per scene. **Risk:** The character begins to lose their reflection in mirrors and water. Their shadow occasionally moves independently.
- **5-6 (Becoming Yokai):** The character has almost crossed over. +1d6 to Force and Finesse with supernatural abilities (full kitsune shapeshifting, tengu flight, oni strength). They can enter the spirit world at will. **Risk:** The character is fading from the human world. Friends and family begin to forget them. Their name becomes harder to write. Food tastes like nothing. At Resonance 6, the character must choose: shed all yokai resonance and return to full humanity (losing all supernatural abilities permanently), or complete the transformation into a yokai (losing their human life, relationships, and memories over the course of one session).

**Adjusting Twilight Resonance:** Each scene spent in the spirit world raises Resonance by 1. Each scene spent in ordinary human activities (eating, sleeping, laughing with friends, doing mundane work) lowers it by 1. Acts of genuine human connection (reconciling with a loved one, protecting a stranger, sharing a meal) lower it by an additional 1.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### Archetype 1: The Ronin Who Walked Back
- **Concept:** A masterless samurai who got lost in a forest during a battle and accidentally stepped into the spirit world. She spent what felt like one night there but returned to find three years had passed. Her lord is dead. Her comrades do not remember her. She can still see the spirit world overlaid on reality like a double-exposed photograph, and she cannot make it stop.
- **Quote:** *"I went into the trees a soldier. I came out of the trees something else. The war ended while I was gone, and a new one started. I do not know which world I am fighting in anymore."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Can see both worlds simultaneously, which is exactly as disorienting as it sounds"*, *"Trained in Tenshin Shoden Katori Shinto-ryu sword style"*, *"Lost three years in the spirit world and came back with reflexes no human should have"*, *"My katana's shadow cuts spirit-flesh as effectively as the blade cuts human flesh"*.
- **Drive:** *"Find out why the spirit world took three years from me, and what I am supposed to do with what it gave me in return."*
- **Flaw:** *"I no longer fully trust my own perception of reality."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 3 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 1

### Archetype 2: The Five-Tailed Kitsune Diplomat
- **Concept:** A five-hundred-year-old kitsune who has served as Inari's ambassador to the human courts for three centuries. She has lived as a noblewoman, a merchant's wife, a shrine maiden, and a teahouse owner. She has married humans, outlived them, mourned them, and married again. She is tired, wise, and deeply conflicted about a compact that forces her to lie about who she is to everyone she loves.
- **Quote:** *"I have loved seventeen humans in five centuries. I remember every one of their faces. None of them ever saw mine."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Five hundred years of experience reading human emotions, and I still cry at the same stories"*, *"Perfect human disguise betrayed only by a shadow with too many tails"*, *"Fox fire that can illuminate, enchant, or incinerate"*, *"I have served at the courts of six different daimyo, and they all made the same mistakes"*.
- **Drive:** *"Negotiate a new compact that lets yokai and humans know each other honestly, so I never have to lie to someone I love again."*
- **Flaw:** *"Five centuries of living as someone else have left me uncertain whether any version of myself is real."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

### Archetype 3: The Tsukumogami Collective (Three Awakened Objects)
- **Concept:** Not one character but three: a paper umbrella (kasa-obake), a paper lantern (chochin-obake), and a tea kettle (bunbuku chagama) who gained sentience after being abandoned for over a century in a burned temple. They are bound together by shared loneliness and travel as a set, taking turns operating a crude human disguise (the umbrella as a hat, the lantern as a face, the kettle as a torso). They are absurd, pathetic, and deeply earnest.
- **Quote:** *"We were thrown away. We waited. We woke up. We are going to find the families that abandoned us and ask them one question: why? ...Also, we need someone with legs."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Three awakened objects in a trenchcoat (technically a kimono)"*, *"The umbrella hops on one leg and can block rain, arrows, and existential despair"*, *"The lantern produces light, makes facial expressions, and screams very loudly when startled"*, *"The tea kettle can brew perfect tea under any conditions and transforms into a tanuki when embarrassed"*.
- **Drive:** *"Find a new home and a reason to exist beyond being abandoned objects. Prove that things that were thrown away still matter."*
- **Flaw:** *"We are literally three objects pretending to be a person, and our disguise falls apart every time someone sneezes."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 1 | Mind 3 | Presence 3

## 6. Condition Palette

**Physical Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Spirit-Chilled* (cold from proximity to yokai), *Katana Fatigue* (over-practiced sword arm), *Fox Fire Singed*.
- **Moderate:** *Oni Club Strike*, *Tengu Wind-Cut* (deep slash from a wind blade), *Twilight Sickness* (nausea from rapid world-boundary crossings).
- **Severe:** *Yuki-Onna Frozen* (progressive hypothermia from snow woman's breath), *World-Torn* (body partially exists in both worlds simultaneously, causing searing pain), *Name Erased* (your name has been eaten by a yokai--no one can remember it, including you).

**Social Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Yokai Suspicion* (humans notice your strangeness), *Kitsune Gossip* (the fox court is talking about you, and not favorably).
- **Moderate:** *Accused of Sorcery* (a daimyo has ordered your arrest), *Spirit World Debt* (you owe a powerful yokai a favor, and they are collecting).
- **Severe:** *Forgotten by the Living* (high Twilight Resonance has caused your human connections to fade--friends, family, and allies no longer remember you), *Tengu Challenge* (a tengu master has declared you unworthy and will test you to the death).

**Psychological Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Ouma-ga-toki Anxiety* (dread as dusk approaches every day), *Object Empathy* (you feel sorry for abandoned household items).
- **Moderate:** *Double Vision* (you cannot stop seeing both worlds, causing constant disorientation), *Mono no Aware* (the beauty of impermanence has become so vivid it paralyzes you with grief).
- **Severe:** *Identity Dissolution* (you can no longer tell whether you are a human who sees yokai or a yokai who remembers being human), *The Twilight Choice* (the inevitability of choosing between worlds weighs on you so heavily you cannot act).

## 7. Starter Scenario: The Night Parade of One Hundred Demons

**Framing:** It is the autumn equinox, the night when the boundary between worlds is thinnest. A massive **Hyakki Yagyo** (Night Parade of One Hundred Demons) is marching toward the castle town of Inuyama. This is not an attack--it is a traditional yokai procession that happens once every sixty years. But the local daimyo, who does not believe in yokai, has positioned his army on the parade route and ordered his archers to shoot anything "suspicious." If the army fires on the parade, the yokai will retaliate, and the resulting battle will destroy the town and shatter the compact permanently. The Tasogare have hours to either redirect the parade, convince the daimyo to stand down, or create a diversion that lets both sides pass without contact.

**3-Layer Scene Design:**

1. **Immediate Threat:** The vanguard of the Night Parade--a column of tsukumogami (awakened objects) led by a gigantic one-eyed paper lantern--has already entered the outskirts of the town. Nervous townspeople are fleeing, and the daimyo's advance guard has drawn swords. One sword stroke against a harmless tea-kettle yokai will trigger the full parade's wrath.

2. **Ticking Clock:** The main body of the parade (including an oni war-band, a kitsune delegation, and a tengu aerial squadron) will reach the town in 5 beats. At Beat 3, the daimyo personally arrives with his full army. At Beat 5, the parade leader--a nine-tailed kitsune elder--will interpret any armed presence on the route as a compact violation and declare war.

3. **Opportunity:** The parade route passes directly through the town's central shrine, which contains a sealed compact stone inscribed with the original human-yokai agreement. If a Tasogare can reach the stone and invoke the compact before the nine-tailed elder arrives, it will remind both sides of their obligations and pause the conflict. But the shrine is behind the daimyo's battle line, and the compact invocation requires a human and a yokai to speak the words simultaneously--which means convincing at least one yokai to trust a human enough to stand together at the stone.

**Antagonist:** ***Daimyo Takeda Nobumasa*** -- A pragmatic, atheistic warlord who conquered Inuyama through ruthless efficiency. He does not believe in yokai. He believes in hallucinations caused by swamp gas, mass hysteria, and enemy psychological warfare. Nothing his advisors say can convince him otherwise, because acknowledging the supernatural would undermine the rational military philosophy that has won him every battle. He is not evil; he is dangerously, stubbornly sane.
- **Traits:** Brilliant military strategist, commands 3,000 disciplined ashigaru spearmen and 200 mounted samurai, impervious to supernatural persuasion because he genuinely does not believe, weakness to being confronted with irrefutable evidence (a yokai physically demonstrating abilities in his presence will break his worldview, but the psychological shock may cause him to order a panicked attack).
- **Condition Track:** ☐ Dismissive (refuses to believe, holds position) / ☐ Shaken (witnesses something inexplicable, delays) / ☐ Worldview Broken (confronted with undeniable yokai reality, susceptible to negotiation but also to panic) / ☐ Convinced or Panicked (either stands down and allows the parade, or orders a catastrophic attack born from existential terror).
- **Shadow Cost:** If the daimyo attacks the parade, the resulting battle destroys the town and shatters the compact in this region permanently. If the daimyo is convinced to stand down peacefully, he becomes a reluctant but genuine Tasogare ally--the first warlord in the Sengoku period to officially acknowledge yokai, fundamentally changing the political landscape.

## 8. Why This Setting Matters

Japanese yokai folklore is not merely a collection of monster stories; it is one of humanity's most sophisticated frameworks for understanding the relationship between the known and the unknown, the rational and the irrational, the mundane and the numinous. The yokai taxonomy includes beings that represent every human anxiety, every natural phenomenon, every emotional state, and every discarded object. In a tabletop RPG context, this creates a setting where the supernatural is not an intrusion into the "real" world but a parallel ecology that has always been there, maintained by negotiation and mutual respect. The central question--can two fundamentally different ways of experiencing reality coexist?--is not merely a fantasy premise; it is the defining challenge of every diverse society in human history.
