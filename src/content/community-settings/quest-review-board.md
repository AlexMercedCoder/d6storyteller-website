Please resubmit with supplementary documentation. Estimated processing time: four to six weeks."*
> - Quest Review Board, Application Status Communication

## 1. Setting Overview and Tone

The Quest Review Board was established after the Third Bridge Incident, in which an adventuring party's quest to recover a magical artifact resulted in the permanent displacement of an entire river, the destruction of a protected habitat, and the accidental creation of a minor new mountain range. The Board's mandate is to prevent this kind of collateral damage through advance review.

The Board is not anti-adventure. Board members have to review six hundred quest applications a year, and most of them were adventurers themselves. They are anti-unchecked adventure: the specific belief that someone's heroic intent excuses them from considering the consequences of their methods.

**Tone:** Bureaucratic comedy with genuine environmental and historical stakes. The Board is right: unreviewed quests do cause significant collateral damage. The Board is also genuinely difficult to deal with. The setting rewards characters who can navigate institutional processes without losing their minds or their applications.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Board's application desk: a long counter behind which eight clerks process applications with the thoroughness of people who have seen what happens when thorough processing doesn't occur. The waiting area: adventuring parties in full gear sitting on regulation-width benches designed by someone who was not accounting for armor. The Board's wall display: a large map of the kingdom showing all active approved quests, all pending applications, and, in red, all known active unapproved quests, which the Board passes to the Watch for enforcement action.
2. **Sound:** The specific language of the Board's correspondence: precise, formal, and crammed with references to regulations that most applicants have never read. The sound of an adventuring party receiving its approval: a counter bell and the specific sound of a stamped permit being slid under the window, which experienced adventurers have learned to associate with the beginning of actually getting to go on the quest.
3. **Touch and Smell:** The application forms: twelve pages, double-sided, with supplementary forms for each specialized category. The permit itself: a laminated card on a lanyard that must be displayed during the quest and surrendered on return for the mandatory post-quest debrief.

## 2. The Board

**The Application Processing Center** is the main public-facing operation: eight clerks, a supervisor, and the application queue that is currently fourteen weeks long for standard applications and six weeks for expedited (with the expedited fee, which is enough to fund two weeks of dungeon provisions for a party of four).

**The Review Committee** is the body that evaluates applications that have passed initial processing: three Board members who review the complete application file and make approval determinations. Committee Chair Maren has been making these determinations for eleven years and has developed a document called the "Informal Guidance on Common Application Problems" that she distributes to applicants whose applications are technically incomplete rather than substantively flawed.

**The Appeals Panel** handles denials that applicants contest. The Panel is currently fourteen months backlogged, which is a problem the Board is aware of and which the Board's internal budget request for two additional panel members has been pending for three years.

**The Post-Quest Debrief Office** processes returning adventurers' reports: what they found, what they destroyed, what they moved, and how the quest's actual outcomes compared to the approved application. The Debrief Office's data is the Board's most valuable resource and is used for continuously improving the application criteria.

## 3. Factions

**The Quest Review Board** is staffed by people who take their mandate seriously. Committee Chair Maren is pragmatic: she wants to approve quests, she wants the quests to go well, and she wants to receive the post-quest debrief so she can improve the process. She has no interest in blocking heroism. She has significant interest in heroism not accidentally destroying river ecosystems.

**The Adventurers' Guild** has a complicated relationship with the Board. The Guild supported the Board's establishment after the Third Bridge Incident because Guild members were being held liable for quest collateral damage. The Guild now objects to the processing timeline (six to fourteen weeks) and to specific application requirements (the Environmental Impact Assessment is a particular point of conflict). Guild liaison Dax has been negotiating an expedited review track for Guild-certified parties for eighteen months without resolution.

**The Ministry of Antiquities** is the Board's most frequent collaborator on Protected Artifacts cases. Antiquities representative Sela has different priorities than the Board: she is less concerned about environmental impact and very concerned about artifacts being removed from registered locations, which affects the Ministry's historical record. She and Chair Maren have a professional relationship that is cordial and frequently in tension.

**The Unapproved Adventurers** are the parties currently operating without permits. The Board passes their names to the Watch, which has a mixed enforcement record. The unapproved parties are the category the Board is most concerned about: not because they are necessarily worse adventurers, but because their quests produce no advance review, no permits, and no post-quest debrief, which means the Board has no data to improve on.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Approval Rating

Track each quest application's **Approval Rating** from 0 to 6.

**Raising Rating (+1 each):**
- Submit required supplementary documentation
- Address a specific flagged concern with appropriate evidence
- Obtain a specialist endorsement (druid certification, Antiquities exemption, etc.)
- Demonstrate in a pre-application consultation that the quest has low collateral damage potential

**Lowering Rating (-1 each):**
- A supplementary document reveals new concerns
- An environmental or historical flag is raised during review
- The applicant attempts to resubmit an unchanged application after a previous denial
- Evidence arrives that the applicants have begun the quest without approval (automatic denial, Watch referral)

**Approval at 5-6:** Standard permit issued. Quest may proceed. Post-quest debrief mandatory.

**Approval at 3-4:** Conditional permit. Specific restrictions on methods, routes, or artifact handling.

**Approval at 1-2:** Additional review required. Pre-quest consultation with Committee Chair mandatory.

**Approval at 0:** Denial. Appeals Panel, fourteen months. Unless the characters find a creative path through the Board's informal guidance.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Board Clerk

You have been processing quest applications for six years. You have seen every application type and several that the application system was not designed for (the party of three that applied to quest for a quest was particularly memorable). You have a genuine respect for adventurers who do their paperwork correctly, which is about 30% of applicants, and a professional patience for the 70% who are experiencing the application process for the first time and treating it as an obstacle rather than a procedure. You are also the person who drafted the supplementary guidance that Chair Maren distributes, because you got tired of writing the same explanation in individualized correspondence.

- **Drive:** *"Process the current application backlog without losing anyone's quest to a procedural error that could have been caught at intake, and find a way to help the genuinely urgent quest that came in this morning navigate the timeline without going unapproved."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### The Frustrated Hero

You have a quest that is genuinely urgent: a threat that will become significantly worse if the quest is delayed by four to fourteen weeks. You have applied correctly. Your application is in the queue. The Board has sent you two requests for supplementary documentation, both of which you have provided. The threat is escalating. You are sitting in the waiting area in full plate armor because there is nowhere to put it while you wait. You have read the Informal Guidance document three times. You are considering the unapproved route and you know the Board passes those names to the Watch.

- **Drive:** *"Find a legitimate path to expedited approval before the threat escalates to the point where the quest becomes a post-disaster cleanup rather than a prevention."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 2 | Mind 1 | Presence 3

### The Committee Chair

You are Maren. You have approved 4,847 quests in eleven years. You have denied 341. You are currently managing a timeline problem: the expedited track the Guild has been requesting would genuinely improve outcomes for time-sensitive quests, but implementing it requires a budget approval that has been in administrative review for eight months and a staffing change that requires union consultation. You also have a genuinely urgent application in your queue from a party whose quest you believe is legitimate and time-sensitive, and you are exploring whether the Informal Guidance provisions allow you to move them forward without either the expedited track or the standard fourteen-week timeline.

- **Drive:** *"Get the urgent application approved through a legitimate process before the threat it addresses escalates, and use the case to make the argument for the expedited track that would prevent the same situation from recurring."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

## 6. Starter Scenario and Why It Matters

**The Urgent Application:** A party has submitted an emergency quest application for a threat that the Board's Environmental Impact Assessment process will take six weeks to process. The threat is a creature that has established residence in an Environmental Preserve and whose presence is causing collateral damage that the Preserve's wildlife management office has documented. The irony is explicit: the thing the Board's process is designed to prevent (quest-related environmental damage) is currently being caused by the thing the quest is designed to address. Chair Maren is looking for a process path. The Guild liaison is arguing for waiver authority. The Antiquities representative has identified two artifacts at the site that require Class B exemptions. The applicants are sitting in the waiting area and the creature's impact reports are arriving every forty-eight hours with increasing urgency. The characters must navigate the Board's process creatively, establish the case for expedited approval, and determine whether the Board's institutional framework can accommodate genuine urgency without the entire permit system losing its purpose.

**Why It Matters:** The Quest Review Board is about the genuine tension between process and urgency. The Board's process exists for good reasons: unchecked quests cause real damage. The Board's process also creates real problems: some things cannot wait fourteen weeks. The setting asks whether institutional process can respond to genuine urgency without collapsing into ad hoc decision-making, and what the right answer is when the system and the situation are genuinely in conflict.
