## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The **Majapahit Empire** (c. 1293-1500 CE) was the greatest maritime power in Southeast Asian history, unifying the Indonesian archipelago under a syncretic Hindu-Buddhist court culture of extraordinary artistic refinement. At the heart of Javanese spiritual life stood the **dalang**--the shadow puppet master who performed all-night **wayang kulit** shows using intricately carved leather puppets, an oil lamp, and a white cotton screen. In Javanese cosmology, the screen represents the boundary between the seen world (*alam nyata*) and the unseen world (*alam gaib*). The dalang is not merely an entertainer but a spiritual gatekeeper.

In this setting, that metaphor is literal. The wayang screen is a genuine portal, and shadows themselves are a manipulable force. As Merapi erupts and the old raksasa (demon-giants from the Ramayana and Mahabharata) stir beneath the volcanic ring of fire, the boundaries between worlds thin. Shadows begin to move independently. Puppets whisper. Temple relief carvings shift position between dawn and dusk. Only those trained in the art of shadow weaving can repair the screen before the Alam Gaib floods through.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The hypnotic dance of wayang kulit shadow puppets on a backlit cotton screen, their articulated leather arms casting stories in silhouette; the geometric perfection of Borobudur's 2,672 relief panels, where carved figures appear to shift position when viewed from different angles; the terrifying beauty of Merapi's lava rivers flowing in luminous orange lines down the volcano's flanks at night; keris daggers with distinctive wavy blades whose damascene patterns seem to ripple like water.
2. **Sound:** The shimmering metallic cascade of a full Javanese gamelan orchestra--bronze gongs, xylophones, and drums creating an intricate interlocking rhythmic pattern that serves as the heartbeat of every wayang performance; the dalang's voice shifting between dozens of distinct character voices during an all-night show; the deep subterranean rumble of Merapi tremors felt through the soles of the feet before they become audible; the sudden silence when all the insects stop before a volcanic eruption.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The smooth, oiled surface of a hundred-year-old wayang puppet carved from buffalo hide, warm from hours of proximity to the oil lamp; the heavy, humid air of the Javanese wet season carrying the scent of clove cigarettes, frangipani blossoms, and sulfurous volcanic steam; the tingling vibration of a gamelan gong struck at the precise resonant frequency that opens a shadow portal; volcanic ash falling like warm, gritty snow.

**The Core Tension:** Performance vs. Reality. In Javanese philosophy, the boundary between the story being told and the world itself is fluid. The dalang shapes reality through narrative. But what happens when the characters in the story refuse to follow the script? When the raksasa puppet on the screen steps off the cotton and into the world? The characters must decide whether to contain reality within the story or let the story reshape reality.

## 2. Historical & Mythological Context

The Majapahit Empire (based in eastern Java) was a maritime superpower whose influence extended across modern-day Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Brunei, and parts of the Philippines and Thailand. Its court culture produced some of the most sophisticated art, literature, and architecture in world history, including the epic poem *Nagarakretagama* and the monumental temples of Prambanan (Hindu) and Borobudur (Buddhist).

Wayang kulit shadow puppetry is a UNESCO-recognized Masterpiece of Oral and Intangible Heritage. Performances typically retell episodes from the *Ramayana* and *Mahabharata* epics, adapted with distinctly Javanese characters and philosophical themes. The dalang memorizes thousands of hours of narrative, performs all voices, directs the gamelan orchestra, and serves as a community spiritual guide. The keris (or kris)--the distinctive asymmetric dagger with a wavy blade--is considered a living spiritual object (*pusaka*) that possesses its own soul (*empu*) forged into the metal by master smiths.

Indonesian mythology features a vast pantheon including Dewi Sri (rice goddess), Nyai Roro Kidul (Queen of the Southern Sea), Batara Kala (lord of the underworld and time), and the Punakawan clown-servants (Semar, Gareng, Petruk, Bagong) who are secretly the most powerful beings in the wayang cosmos.

> **Note on Respectful Portrayal:** Javanese wayang kulit is a living art form practiced by master dalang across Java and Bali. The keris tradition and gamelan orchestral arts are sacred cultural practices. This setting draws from the rich mythology of the Ramayana and Mahabharata as interpreted through Javanese culture, with deep respect for Indonesian artistic traditions and the philosophical depth of Javanese mysticism (*kejawen*).

## 3. Core Factions & Traditions

### The Dalang's Circle (Shadow Weavers)
Master shadow puppeteers who understand that the wayang screen is a literal threshold between worlds.
- **Cultural Association:** Performers, spiritual gatekeepers, and narrative sorcerers. They command the gamelan orchestra, which provides the vibrational energy that stabilizes the screen. A dalang in mid-performance can reshape local reality by altering the story being told--but only if the audience believes.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I can perform all 177 voices of the Ramayana cycle without repeating a single inflection"*
  - *"My hands move faster than sight when the shadow war begins behind the screen"*
  - *"The gamelan's bronze song obeys my cues, and through the gamelan, the boundary holds"*

### The Empu Smiths (Keris Forgers)
Master metallurgists who forge enchanted keris blades layered with meteorite iron (*pamor*), each blade containing a bound spirit.
- **Cultural Association:** Blacksmiths, spiritual engineers, and guardians of the volcanic forge. The empu process requires folding iron and nickel hundreds of times while reciting mantras at specific volcanic vents. Each keris has a name, a personality, and a destiny. Some keris choose their wielders; others refuse to be drawn.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I have folded this blade 500 times at the vent of Merapi; it carries volcanic fire in its pattern"*
  - *"My keris, Naga Sasra, whispers the location of hidden raksasa even through stone walls"*
  - *"I know the seven forbidden pamor patterns that should never be forged--and why three of them were"*

### The Punakawan (Sacred Clowns)
Based on the beloved wayang characters Semar, Gareng, Petruk, and Bagong--servant-clowns who are secretly the most powerful spiritual beings in the Javanese cosmos.
- **Cultural Association:** Tricksters, truth-tellers, and cosmic janitors. In wayang tradition, the Punakawan scenes (called *goro-goro*) provide comic relief but also deliver the deepest philosophical truths. In this setting, those who embody the Punakawan spirit gain access to absurdist abilities that break the normal rules--but only through humor, humility, and self-deprecation.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I tripped over a temple step and accidentally sealed a raksasa portal. You're welcome."*
  - *"My jokes are terrible, but they make demons laugh so hard they forget to eat people"*
  - *"I look like a fool. That is my greatest weapon."*

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Wayang Mastery (Shadow Weaving)

Characters track a **Shadow Weave Meter** (1-6):

- **1-2 (Mundane Shadow):** Shadows behave normally. The character has no shadow manipulation ability but is completely invisible to Alam Gaib entities. Safe, but powerless against supernatural threats.
- **3-4 (Shadow Touched):** The character can harden their own shadow into a physical shield or extend it as a grappling tendril (+1d6 to Finesse in dim or dark environments). They can perceive the Alam Gaib as flickering shapes in peripheral vision. Costs 1 Momentum per use. **Risk:** The character's shadow occasionally acts independently--reaching for objects, pointing at things, or mimicking emotions the character is trying to hide.
- **5-6 (Shadow Master):** +1d6 to Force and Finesse in any shadow-rich environment. The character can step into the Alam Gaib through any shadow large enough to fit their body, create shadow constructs (weapons, barriers, decoys), and animate wayang puppets as autonomous agents. **Risk:** The character's shadow has become a fully conscious entity with its own goals. It obeys the character during scenes but makes one independent action per session that the Storyteller controls. If Shadow Weave reaches 6, the shadow attempts to replace the character entirely during the next wayang performance.

**Recovering Shadow Weave Balance:** Exposure to direct sunlight (which destroys shadow constructs), performing a wayang show that tells a story of humility, or receiving a Punakawan blessing (delivered through genuine laughter) reduces Shadow Weave by 1.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### Archetype 1: The Court Dalang of Trowulan
- **Concept:** The royal shadow puppeteer of the Majapahit capital, Trowulan. She has performed the complete Ramayana cycle for three successive kings and knows that each performance slightly reshapes the spiritual landscape of Java. With Merapi's eruption destabilizing the screen, her nightly performances have become desperate holding actions against an Alam Gaib invasion.
- **Quote:** *"Every story I tell is a wall. Every voice I perform is a lock. If I stop, the shadows walk."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Keeper of the Royal Wayang Collection (200 puppets, each a bound story)"*, *"Voice that can shift between 177 characters without pause"*, *"The gamelan responds to my breathing, not my cues"*, *"I have seen the true face of the Alam Gaib behind the screen, and I kept performing"*.
- **Drive:** *"Hold the screen together through the eruption, even if it means performing the longest wayang show in history--one that never ends."*
- **Flaw:** *"I have spent so long speaking in other voices that I am no longer sure which voice is my own."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

### Archetype 2: The Volcano Empu of Merapi
- **Concept:** A keris smith who forges blades at the volcanic vents of Mount Merapi itself. His keris blades are the most powerful in the archipelago because they contain actual volcanic spirit-iron. The eruption has supercharged his forge but also released the imprisoned raksasa that his predecessors sealed in the mountain's magma chambers centuries ago.
- **Quote:** *"The mountain is not angry. The mountain is in labor. Something is being born in the fire, and it is not friendly."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Forges keris blades at the lip of Merapi's active crater"*, *"Each of my blades carries a fragment of volcanic consciousness"*, *"Burns do not register on my skin anymore--I have been too close to the fire for too long"*, *"The raksasa sealed in the mountain know my family name and scream it during eruptions"*.
- **Drive:** *"Forge the seven keris blades needed to reseal the raksasa before Merapi's eruption releases them all."*
- **Flaw:** *"My obsession with the forge has cost me every personal relationship I have ever had."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 3 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 1

### Archetype 3: The Punakawan Fool of the Crossroads
- **Concept:** A wandering comedian and street performer who discovered that making people laugh at crossroads (sacred liminal spaces in Javanese cosmology) accidentally seals minor Alam Gaib breaches. She has no formal training, no prestigious lineage, and no idea why her terrible jokes have supernatural power. She just knows that when she tells the one about the farmer and the water buffalo, demons flee.
- **Quote:** *"I don't understand any of this. But the scary shadow thing ran away when I did my chicken impression, so I'm going to keep doing my chicken impression."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Worst comedian in Java; best demon repellent in Java"*, *"Accidentally sealed three Alam Gaib portals with pratfalls"*, *"Can make anyone laugh, including raksasa, which confuses them long enough to escape"*, *"Carries a broken wayang puppet of Semar that she found in a ditch and talks to"*.
- **Drive:** *"Figure out why my terrible comedy has cosmic power, and maybe learn to use it on purpose."*
- **Flaw:** *"I deflect every serious moment with humor because genuine emotion terrifies me more than any raksasa."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 3 | Mind 1 | Presence 3

## 6. Condition Palette

**Physical Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Gamelan Deafened* (ears ringing from prolonged bronze percussion), *Volcanic Ash Cough*, *Oil Lamp Burn*.
- **Moderate:** *Keris Wound* (wavy blade cuts resist healing), *Shadow-Grappled* (bruising from autonomous shadow attack), *Merapi Scalding*.
- **Severe:** *Raksasa Mauled* (massive demon claws), *Shadow Consumed* (a limb partially absorbed into the Alam Gaib), *Volcanic Burial* (trapped in cooling lava flow).

**Social Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Court Gossip* (whispered accusations of practicing dark shadow arts), *Gamelan Discordance* (your orchestra refuses to play for you).
- **Moderate:** *Royal Disfavor* (the Majapahit court has revoked your performance license), *Puppet Rebellion* (your wayang collection refuses to be manipulated).
- **Severe:** *Declared Shadow Sorcerer* (hunted by the royal guard), *Empu Exile* (no smith will forge for you or repair your keris).

**Psychological Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Screen Anxiety* (flinching at every shadow), *Voice Confusion* (speaking involuntarily in wayang character voices).
- **Moderate:** *Shadow Paranoia* (your own shadow's independent movements terrify you), *Alam Gaib Bleed* (you see the unseen world overlaid on reality at all times).
- **Severe:** *Identity Dissolution* (you can no longer distinguish your own thoughts from the voices of the characters you perform), *Shadow Replacement* (your shadow has begun acting as you during sleep, and people prefer it).

## 7. Starter Scenario: The Performance That Must Not End

**Framing:** Mount Merapi has entered a major eruption phase. The court dalang has been performing a continuous wayang show for three days and nights at Prambanan Temple to keep the screen stable, but she is collapsing from exhaustion. If the performance stops, the Alam Gaib breach behind the screen will tear open permanently. The characters must either take over the performance (requiring Presence and Mind), repair the screen from the Alam Gaib side (requiring Shadow Weave and Force), or find and seal the volcanic source of the breach inside Merapi (requiring an expedition into the erupting mountain).

**3-Layer Scene Design:**

1. **Immediate Threat:** Three raksasa have already pushed through the screen and are loose in the Prambanan Temple complex. They are reshaping the stone relief carvings to tell a different story--one where the demons win--and each altered relief weakens the screen further.

2. **Ticking Clock:** The dalang will collapse in 5 beats. Each beat she weakens, one more raksasa pushes through. At Beat 5, the screen tears completely and the Alam Gaib floods the temple.

3. **Opportunity:** The Punakawan puppet of Semar (the most powerful being in the wayang cosmos) is hidden somewhere in the temple's 240 relief panels. Finding and activating it allows a single cosmic joke--a moment of divine absurdity that can reset the screen. But Semar only responds to genuine humility.

**Antagonist:** ***Batara Kala* (Lord of Time and Destruction)** -- The great raksasa god imprisoned beneath Merapi, stirring in his volcanic prison. He does not need to escape physically; he is rewriting the wayang narratives from inside the mountain, changing the stories so that the demons always win.
- **Traits:** Reality-warping narrative control, volcanic fire breath, immune to physical weapons (can only be defeated by being written out of the story through a superior narrative performance), weakness to Punakawan humor (divine comedy disrupts his rewrites).
- **Condition Track:** ☐ Narrative Influence (stories begin changing) / ☐ Shadow Manifestation (a shadow-avatar appears at the screen) / ☐ Partial Emergence (volcanic arm reaches through the screen) / ☐ Rewritten or Resealed (the dalang tells a story that contains him, or the Punakawan make him laugh at himself).
- **Shadow Cost:** Defeating Batara Kala through force alone is impossible. Resealing him through narrative requires the dalang to write herself into the story as a sacrifice (she becomes a wayang puppet forever). The Punakawan path--making a god laugh--requires no sacrifice but demands the most difficult roll in the game: Presence at Desperate position, requiring a 6.

## 8. Why This Setting Matters

Indonesian mythology and performing arts represent one of humanity's most sophisticated explorations of the boundary between story and reality. The wayang kulit tradition holds that the dalang does not merely perform a story--the dalang maintains the cosmic order through narrative. In a tabletop RPG context, this creates a setting where the very act of storytelling (which is what every RPG session fundamentally is) becomes the central mechanic and theme. The players are not just playing characters in a story; they are playing characters who understand that they are in a story, and that the story's integrity is what keeps reality from unraveling. It is a setting about the power and responsibility of narrative itself--which is, ultimately, what every tabletop RPG is about.
