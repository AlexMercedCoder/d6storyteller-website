> *"In the beginning, there was the word. In Finland, the word is still the beginning. The tietäjä who knows the origin-song of iron can command every blade and nail. The one who knows the origin-song of fire can warm or burn the world. The one who knows the origin-song of death... that one is Väinämöinen, and he is still singing."*

## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The **Kalevala** is Finland's national epic, compiled from centuries of oral folk poetry. Unlike the martial epics of Greece or Scandinavia, the Kalevala's heroes win through *singing*. **Väinämöinen**, the eternal sage, defeats enemies by out-singing them. **Ilmarinen**, the divine smith, forges the Sampo by singing metal into shape. Magic in the Kalevala is *word-magic*: knowing the true name and origin of something gives you power over it. This is a setting where bards are the most powerful beings in creation, and the most dangerous weapon is a song you cannot stop hearing.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The infinite boreal forest of silver birch and dark spruce, reflected perfectly in the mirror-still surface of one of Finland's 188,000 lakes; the Sampo radiating rainbow light from its island stronghold; the aurora borealis as the visible manifestation of sung magic.
2. **Sound:** The haunting, resonant twang of the kantele (Finnish stringed instrument) whose music literally reshapes reality; the call-and-response singing of two tietäjä in a magical duel; the profound silence of a Finnish winter forest so deep you can hear your own heartbeat.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The dry, enveloping heat of a Finnish sauna where spiritual purification happens; the clean, metallic scent of fresh snow; birch leaves against skin during the sauna *vihta* (birch whisk) ritual.

**The Core Tension:** Knowledge vs. Wisdom. Knowing the origin-song of a thing gives you absolute power over it. But absolute power without wisdom is catastrophe. The Kalevala's greatest tragedy is that even the wisest sage, Väinämöinen, makes devastating mistakes. The characters must decide how much power to seek and whether they are wise enough to wield what they find.

## 2. Historical & Mythological Context

The Kalevala was compiled by Elias Lönnrot from Finnish and Karelian oral folk poetry in 1835. It was foundational to Finnish national identity and directly inspired Tolkien's Elvish languages and Silmarillion mythology. The epic features three main heroes: Väinämöinen (sage-singer), Ilmarinen (divine smith), and Lemminkäinen (reckless warrior), and their antagonist Louhi, the witch-queen of Pohjola. Finnish folk magic (*tietäjä* tradition) was genuinely based on knowing origin-songs (*syntylaulu*) for natural phenomena.

> **Note on Respectful Portrayal:** The Kalevala is central to Finnish national identity. Finnish folk magic traditions and the closely related Sami spiritual practices are treated with deep cultural respect.

## 3. Core Factions & Traditions

### The Tietäjät (Sage-Singers)
Masters of word-magic who can reshape reality through song.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I know the origin-song of iron: how it was born from the milk of three sky-maidens and hardened in the swamp of Tuonela"*
  - *"My kantele is strung with the hair of Väinämöinen's horse. Its music opens doors that have no hinges."*
  - *"I have sung for three days without stopping. The forest rearranged itself to match my melody."*

### The Sepät (Divine Smiths)
Forge-masters who sing metal into shape, following Ilmarinen's tradition.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I forge by singing to the iron. The iron tells me its name, and I hammer it into the shape it wishes to become."*
  - *"My forge burns with fire I sang into existence from the origin-word of flame."*

### The Pohjola Court (The Frozen North)
Louhi's domain, where the eternal winter holds and stolen treasures accumulate.
- **Descriptor Examples (for intermediaries or defectors):**
  - *"I served Louhi for seven years and heard her sing the sun into a box. She is the most powerful singer alive. She is also mad."*
  - *"Pohjola is not evil. It is cold. Everything there is about survival, and Louhi survives better than anyone."*

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Sana (Word-Power)

Characters track a **Sana Meter** (1-6):

- **1-2 (Silent):** No song-magic ability. +1d6 to Force (physical competence compensates). The character's words have no supernatural weight.
- **3-4 (Singer):** Can sing minor enchantments: calming animals, warming a room, finding lost objects. +1d6 to Presence when singing. Costs 1 Momentum. **Requirement:** The character must know the origin-song of the thing they wish to affect. Learning an origin-song requires research, travel, or being taught.
- **5-6 (Tietäjä):** Full word-magic. +1d6 to all rolls when singing. Can sing objects into existence, reshape terrain, bind spirits, and heal mortal wounds. **Requirement:** The singer must speak only truth. A single deliberate lie drops Sana to 0 and inflicts the *Broken Voice* condition. **Risk:** At Sana 6, the character's voice becomes the dominant force in their personality--they begin to sing involuntarily, and their songs reshape reality around them without conscious intent.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### Archetype 1: The Wandering Tietäjä
- **Concept:** An elderly sage-singer who has traveled Finland for decades, collecting origin-songs from every village, lake, and forest. She knows 200 origin-songs and can sing most natural phenomena into obedience. She is searching for the one song she cannot find: the origin-song of death, which would give her power over mortality itself.
- **Quote:** *"I know the song of iron, the song of fire, the song of birch and bear and salmon. I do not know the song of death. Väinämöinen knows it. He will not teach me. I must find out why."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 1 | Mind 3 | Presence 3

### Archetype 2: The Forge-Singer of the Copper Mountain
- **Concept:** A young smith who sings to metal as she works. Her blades are extraordinary because they are sung into their shapes rather than hammered. She dreams of forging a new Sampo, but the original forging required sacrifices she is not willing to make.
- **Quote:** *"Ilmarinen forged the Sampo from a swan's feather, a barren cow's milk, a single grain of barley, and a summer ewe's wool. I have three of the four. The fourth requires a cruelty I refuse."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 3 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 1

### Archetype 3: The Lemminkäinen (Reckless Hero)
- **Concept:** A brash young warrior who has more courage than sense and more charisma than skill. He has died once (drowned in the River of Tuonela) and been resurrected by his mother's songs. He is reckless because he has already died and found it boring.
- **Quote:** *"I have been to the land of the dead. The food is terrible and there is no music. I am in no hurry to go back."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 3 | Mind 1 | Presence 2

## 6. Condition Palette

**Physical:** *Tuonela Chill* (lingering cold from the underworld river), *Voice Strain* (overuse of song-magic), *Forge Burn* (singing metal into shape is hot work), *Pohjola Frostbite*.
**Social:** *Louhi's Notice* (the witch-queen knows your name), *Song-Debt* (you promised a song you cannot deliver), *Origin-Word Stolen* (someone has learned your personal origin-song).
**Psychological:** *Eternal Singer's Fatigue* (the compulsion to sing never stops), *Sampo Obsession*, *Death Memory* (you remember drowning, and water terrifies you).

## 7. Starter Scenario: The Theft of the Sampo

**Framing:** The Sampo sits on Louhi's island in Pohjola, grinding out prosperity for the frozen North while Kalevala starves. Väinämöinen, Ilmarinen, and Lemminkäinen attempted to steal it once (as told in the Kalevala) and it shattered, spreading its fragments across the sea. In this version, the characters are the *second* expedition--attempting to recover the fragments before Louhi can reassemble them. Each fragment grants a specific power (grain, salt, gold) and each is guarded by a different challenge that can only be overcome through song.

**Antagonist:** ***Louhi, Mistress of Pohjola*** -- The most powerful singer in the world. She is not evil; she is a survivor who has used every resource--including stolen ones--to keep her people alive in the eternal winter. She will negotiate. She will trade. But she will not surrender the Sampo voluntarily because without it, Pohjola freezes.

## 8. Why This Setting Matters

The Kalevala is unique among world epics because its heroes are poets and singers, not warriors. Power comes from knowledge, specifically the knowledge of origins. This is a mythology that treats the bard as the most important person in the room--which, in a tabletop RPG where storytelling is the primary activity, is the most meta-appropriate mythology possible. It is also the direct ancestor of Tolkien's Middle-earth, and playing in the Kalevala's world is playing in the source that inspired modern fantasy.
