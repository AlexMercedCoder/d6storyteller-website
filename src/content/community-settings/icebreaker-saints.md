## 1. Setting Overview and Tone

The Northern Passage runs through two thousand miles of polar water between the eastern and western landmasses. Without it, global trade is fractured: the overland alternatives take months and the southern sea route adds three weeks and significant storm risk. The Passage has been continuously maintained by the Icebreaker Saints for two centuries, originally commissioned by the Northern Church and now funded by a consortium of trading nations who have agreed on very little except the necessity of the Passage.

The Saints are a closed community: they recruit from their own families and from coastal towns who understand polar conditions. Their culture is intensely practical, built on the knowledge that the margin for error in polar icebreaking is very small and that the ice, in recent years, has been acting in ways that none of the Saints' operational history accounts for.

**Tone:** Claustrophobic, viscerally cold, and built on the specific intensity of a small community doing essential work in conditions that punish every mistake. The setting is not horror; it is the drama of expertise at the edge of its limits.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Passage in winter: pack ice as far as visible in every direction, with the narrow channel behind the icebreaker gradually refreezing. The Saint vessels themselves: reinforced hulls painted the red and white of the northern church, looking incongruously small against the scale of the polar ice. The northern lights, which in the Passage have specific patterns that experienced Saints read for ice condition changes in the same way they read weather.
2. **Sound:** The sound of icebreaking: a grinding, rhythmic percussion as the hull rides up onto pack ice and breaks it by weight. The specific silence that follows when the engines cut and the ice closes around the vessel. The crew's voice protocols: short, precise, no unnecessary communication, because in polar conditions cognitive bandwidth is a resource as limited as heat.
3. **Touch and Smell:** The cold that penetrates everything despite the ship's heating systems. The smell of the engine room: diesel and machine oil, which the crews associate with safety the way others associate warmth with safety. Frozen rope: a texture that experienced crews can assess by feel to determine whether it is safe to use or has been compromised by ice stress.

## 2. The Passage

**The Channel Network** is the system of maintained routes through the pack ice that the Saints keep open. The channels are not permanent: the ice refreezes constantly and must be regularly broken and re-broken. The network requires continuous operations to remain navigable.

**Icebreaker Command** is the operations center at the eastern end of the Passage, where Admiral Pell coordinates the fleet's movements, receives distress signals, and manages the seasonal replacement of crews. The Command is the logistical hub and the place where news from the interior of the pack arrives last.

**The Mid-Passage Station** is a fortified supply point halfway through the Passage, kept open year-round by a rotating crew of twenty. The Station provides fuel, emergency repair capacity, and the most complete picture of current conditions in the central pack. It is also the most isolated posting in the Saints' operation, and the crews who spend full winter seasons there develop a particular relationship with the ice that other Saints regard with a mixture of respect and wariness.

**The Ice Interior** is the deep pack beyond the maintained channels, which no vessel enters deliberately. The Saints have operational orders that prohibit entering the ice interior without rescue authorization. Recent navigation records show that two Saints vessels have entered the interior independently, on the same heading, three weeks apart.

## 3. Factions

**The Icebreaker Saints** are the fleet's crews: seven vessels, approximately forty crew each. The fleet has a strict hierarchy based on experience, and the senior captains have an informal council that operates parallel to Admiral Pell's formal command. Captain Sela of the Saint Mira has been the fleet's most experienced officer for eight years. She went into the pack three days ago on what was logged as a standard channel maintenance operation and has not returned.

**Icebreaker Command** under Admiral Pell manages the fleet's operations. Pell is a competent administrator who has never served aboard an icebreaker, which is a source of quiet friction with the fleet's captain council. He follows the operational protocols carefully and is currently managing the first simultaneous loss of contact with two vessels in the fleet's history.

**The Funding Consortium** represents the trading nations that pay for the Passage's maintenance. Their representative, a merchant named Ines, is stationed at the eastern port and communicates weekly with Pell. The Consortium's concern is purely operational: the Passage must remain open, costs must remain within the negotiated range, and the Saints must not create political complications. Ines is currently composing a very carefully worded message about the two missing vessels.

**The Ice** is the setting's antagonist in the way that weather is an antagonist: impersonal, powerful, and recently behaving in ways that the Saints' two centuries of operational history do not explain. The ice formations in the deep pack have been changing in patterns that experienced crews recognize as non-standard, and the two missing vessels were both investigating those formations when they went dark.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Channel Integrity

Track **Channel Integrity** from 0 to 6, starting at 4.

**Maintaining Integrity (+1 each):**
- Complete a successful icebreaking pass through a closing section
- Resupply and repair at the Mid-Passage Station
- Weather a storm without losing maintained channels
- Investigate and resolve an anomalous ice formation before it closes a channel

**Losing Integrity (-1 each):**
- A storm closes a maintained section
- A Saints vessel is disabled or lost, reducing operational capacity
- An anomalous ice event closes a section faster than standard operations can address
- Political disruption forces a vessel to leave the Passage for diplomatic reasons

**At Integrity 0:** The Passage is closed. Vessels waiting to use the route divert to alternatives. The Funding Consortium activates emergency provisions. The Saints have a narrow window to restore Integrity before the Consortium begins exploring whether to continue funding the operation.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Ice-Reading Mate

You have spent twelve winters on the Saints and you read ice the way other people read faces. You know what the current formations are telling you, and what they are telling you is that something has changed in the deep pack that does not correspond to any seasonal pattern in the fleet's operational history. You are not senior enough to redirect a vessel without authorization. You are senior enough to know that the two missing captains were investigating the same formation.

- **Drive:** *"Get authorization to enter the ice interior and investigate the anomaly before it closes enough channels to make the Passage untenable for the season."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 1

### The Admiral

You run Icebreaker Command and you have two vessels missing and a Funding Consortium representative asking for reassurance you cannot honestly provide. The protocol for lost vessels says you send a rescue mission after a 72-hour window. The rescue mission goes to the same location the missing vessels went. You are managing operational crisis from a desk in a port building and every captain in the fleet knows you have never been in the pack, and every one of them is watching to see whether you have the sense to know what you don't know.

- **Drive:** *"Make the right operational decisions without the hands-on experience your captains have, and find a way to lead people who are better than you at the specific skills this crisis requires."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### The Missing Captain's Second

Your captain went into the interior three days ago and has not come back. You are currently commanding the Saint Mira. You can hear her hull beacon. She is moving deeper into the pack. You have no orders that cover this situation. You have strong opinions about what to do. The Admiral has not yet authorized a rescue mission. The ice outside is doing something that none of your training described.

- **Drive:** *"Go in after your captain with or without authorization, and bring back whatever she found that made her keep going deeper instead of coming home."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 3 | Finesse 2 | Mind 1 | Presence 2

## 6. Starter Scenario and Why It Matters

**Deeper Into the Pack:** The Saint Mira's beacon is moving toward a deep ice formation that shows up on sonar as a structure rather than a natural accumulation. The Saint Elke is being authorized for rescue operations. The characters are crew who must navigate the rescue mission, manage their own vessel's Channel Integrity obligations, and determine what the Saint Mira's captain found that was worth going deeper rather than coming home. Meanwhile, three sections of the maintained channel are degrading faster than normal operations can address, and the Funding Consortium's next communication window opens in forty-eight hours.

**Why It Matters:** The Icebreaker Saints is about institutional commitment and the specific cost of essential work. The Saints keep the Passage open because someone has to, because they have done it for two centuries, and because the community and culture that has grown around that commitment gives it meaning beyond mere employment. The setting asks what you owe to the work you have inherited and the community you are part of, when the work itself is showing you something it has never shown before.
