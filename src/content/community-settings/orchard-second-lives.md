## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

The orchard calendar governs Edrin more closely than any royal decree. Families gather when a relative's tree bears fruit, village tenders record every harvest, and priests prepare the planting rites that begin another life. The Crown's new Rebirth Charter changes all of this by treating fertile groves as strategic property. A generation of souls may become royal assets before it becomes anyone's children.

**Tone:** Pastoral, tender, and politically charged. The setting is about gardens and genealogy, harvest rituals and family meals, held against the backdrop of a government that has decided to control the most intimate process in human experience. The emotional weight comes from the specific: a grandmother's tree flowering in spring, a family argument about which stories to tell at First Water, a child born with a smile that belongs to someone everyone remembers.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** Terraced orchards stepping down hillsides, each tree marked with a family name carved into the bark. Soul fruit that glows faintly at twilight, each one a different color corresponding to the life it carries: warm amber for a life lived in contentment, deep violet for grief, pale green for a life cut short. The Crown's survey markers, steel posts driven into orchard soil, claiming trees for the state.
2. **Sound:** The hum of a healthy tree in growing season, which tenders say is the soul remembering something it loved. The crack of a branch under the weight of ripe fruit, which must be harvested within hours before the soul begins to fade. The formal reading of the Rebirth Charter at a village meeting, followed by the particular silence of people deciding whether to obey.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The warm, yielding weight of a soul fruit in the palm, heavier than its size suggests, with a skin that pulses faintly like a heartbeat. The smell of an orchard in bloom: sweet and earthy with an undertone of something older, like turning soil in a graveyard. The rough texture of bark under a Rootkeeper's hands, reading the tree's health the way a doctor reads a pulse.

## 2. Orchard Life

**First Water** is the family rite held when a tree begins to flower. Relatives bring stories, unfinished apologies, and objects that mattered to the dead. No one agrees whether the fruit remembers those offerings, but everyone agrees that neglect leaves a mark. A First Water gathering is part funeral, part family reunion, and part negotiation about who the returning soul will be.

**The Graft Houses** preserve damaged soul trees. A graft can save a fruit, but it may mingle memories from two family lines. Crown officials call this an efficient public service. Families who have lived through it speak of children with dreams that belong to strangers. The most experienced grafter in Edrin, an old woman named Dola, refuses Crown contracts because she has seen what happens when grafts are done for political convenience rather than necessity.

**The Seed Road** connects Edrin's major orchards. It is crowded at harvest and watched by tax collectors, smugglers, and midwives carrying illegal fruit to a place where it can be planted without registration. The Road passes through three Crown checkpoints. Each checkpoint has a weighing station where fruit is assessed, valued, and occasionally seized.

**The Withering Hollow** is a grove where neglected trees stand. Fruit that is not harvested in time does not simply spoil; it withers, and the soul within it dissipates. The Hollow is a place of genuine sorrow. Some trees here were abandoned by families too poor to maintain them. Others were deliberately neglected by families who did not want the return of the person the soul belonged to. Rootkeepers tend the Hollow as a matter of duty, but it is not a place anyone visits willingly.

## 3. Factions

**The Crown Orchard Office** promises equal rebirth for all and collects fruit by force. Its officials can point to genuine inequities: wealthy families have long kept fertile groves for their own descendants while poor families tend rocky slopes with thin soil. Their answer, however, is a system in which the Crown decides whose next life is strategically useful. The Office's director, Lord Ashby, genuinely believes centralized rebirth management is fairer. He is also building an army of souls cultivated for loyalty.

**The Rootkeepers** defend local groves and know which trees are older than the kingdom. They are gardeners, midwives, and genealogists. Their elder, Hanna Thornfield, has tended orchards for forty years and can read a tree's history in its growth rings. Some Rootkeepers believe every family must control its own fruit. Others think their traditions have protected landowners as effectively as they have protected the dead. The argument is honest and unresolved.

**The Unplanted** are adults whose fruit was withheld, rented, or lost, and they refuse to let the system define them as incomplete. They organize tenant farmers and children born from state-managed groves. Their leader, Maren Cole, is determined to prevent the rebellion from becoming nostalgia for an older form of control. She does not want to return to the old system where wealthy families hoarded fertile groves. She wants a new one where nobody owns a soul.

**The Orchard Clergy** maintains the theological framework of reincarnation. Their doctrine says rebirth is a sacred process that should not be accelerated, manipulated, or denied by any secular authority. Archbishop Selwin has publicly opposed the Rebirth Charter, which makes him a hero to the Rootkeepers and a problem for the Crown. Privately, Selwin is aware that the clergy's own history includes centuries of choosing which families received the best grove plots, and he is not sure his institution has the moral standing to lecture anyone.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Ripening Clocks

Assign a **six-segment clock** to a soul fruit. Mark it when time passes, the tree is stressed, or outside claims on the fruit become more urgent.

**Before it fills,** characters can influence the soul's carried memory through stories, care, confession, or sacrifice. Name the quality being tended: courage, gentleness, a useful skill, or a dangerous loyalty.

**At 3 segments,** choose a complication:
- The fruit attracts a claimant (Crown official, rival family, or Clergy representative)
- The fruit begins showing memories in its skin (images visible beneath the surface)
- The fruit demands a visit from someone connected to the previous life

**When the clock fills,** the fruit must be planted, preserved at a cost (consuming resources or favors), or surrendered to decay. Each choice leaves a concrete trace in the new life. A planted soul is not a returned person. Players should decide together what continuity means at their table.

**Crown seizure:** The Crown can seize a fruit with a legal claim. Resist through law (challenge the charter), stealth (move the fruit along the Seed Road), public support (rally the community), or a different kind of care (demonstrate that the soul has already been tended and the Crown's intervention would damage it). Each approach changes who is trusted when the child eventually arrives.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Rootkeeper's Apprentice

You can tell a healthy fruit from one shaped by fear, and you have been asked to certify a harvest you know is wrong. The fruit belongs to a family that cannot afford to contest the Crown's claim. The certification would make the seizure legal. Your refusal would make you a criminal.

- **Drive:** *"Keep one child from being born as somebody else's political project."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### The Unplanted Organizer

You have no record of a previous life, which makes the Crown both suspicious of you and eager to use your story. You organize tenant farmers and Crown-born children who were raised without orchard ties. You are building a political movement that does not fit neatly into either the old family system or the new Crown one.

- **Drive:** *"Win a future where nobody needs paperwork to be treated as fully alive."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 2 | Mind 1 | Presence 3

### The Seed Road Courier

You transport soul fruit along back routes, avoiding Crown checkpoints and delivering harvests to families who have been separated from their groves. You carry the most important cargo in Edrin in a padded satchel, riding through the night, knowing that if you are caught, the fruit will be confiscated and the family will lose their chance at reunion.

- **Drive:** *"Deliver the fruit to the family it belongs to before the Crown decides it belongs to the state."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 3 | Mind 1 | Presence 2

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The Royal Harvest:** The Crown has claimed a grove whose fruit carries the rebirth of an executed rebel leader. The family wants one quiet chance to plant it in their home soil, away from politics. The court wants to cultivate that soul into a loyal heir, raised in the palace, educated by Crown tutors. The Rootkeepers want the fruit left alone until it ripens naturally. The Unplanted want to use the case to challenge the Rebirth Charter in public court. The characters have one harvest night to reach the grove, decide who gets to shape the future, and plant or protect the fruit before dawn brings the Crown's collectors.

**Why It Matters:** The Orchard of Second Lives asks who owns a soul. The old system says families do, which protected some and excluded others. The new system says the state does, which promises equality and delivers control. The characters will not resolve the question. They will decide specific cases for specific people: this fruit, this family, this child who does not yet exist. The answers they give will shape what rebirth means in Edrin, whether it remains a family inheritance, becomes a state resource, or grows into something neither system has imagined.
