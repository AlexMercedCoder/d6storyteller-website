## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The supernatural world in Imperial China has the same bureaucracy as the mundane world. The crime investigation is the same kind of problem.

Chinese folklore's celestial bureaucracy is one of the great imaginative achievements of mythological tradition: heaven mirrors earth, with divine officials holding ranks equivalent to earthly ones, corruption and virtue distributed similarly, and the whole system maintained by the Jade Emperor as a cosmic civil service. Fox spirits (Huli Jing) are among the most familiar figures in this tradition: shapeshifters of varying cultivation, ranging from minor tricksters to powerful beings who have earned heavenly status.

The Lingtan (Spirit Investigators) are an original institution for this setting: human officials trained in both earthly and celestial law, able to operate in both jurisdictions, and responsible for the cases that fall between them.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** An imperial city with its full complexity: the administrative quarter with its registries and courts, the market quarter with its mixing of every social class, the temple quarter where the celestial bureaucracy interfaces most directly with the earthly, and the night quarter where unlicensed spirit activity concentrates; fox spirits in human form visible to the Lingtan's trained eye through subtle signs that uninitiated humans miss.
2. **Sound:** The formal language of imperial court proceedings adapted for celestial jurisdiction, the specific protocols of a joint tribunal, the sound of incense offerings that carry formal celestial documents (the celestial bureaucracy prefers this method), and the sound of a Huli Jing's voice, which has a quality that experienced Lingtan can recognize.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The physical weight of dual-court documentation, the smell of the specific incenses used in celestial communication, the warm feel of a genuine celestial document versus a forged one (the difference is in the paper's response to light), and the cold that experienced Lingtan associate with celestial court officials' presence.

## 2. The Dual Court System

### The Earthly Courts
Standard imperial justice: magistrates, evidence protocols, punishment tables. Efficient for human crimes with human victims. Completely without authority over celestial beings who haven't acknowledged earthly jurisdiction.

### The Celestial Courts
Bureaucratically equivalent to the earthly system but governed by celestial law and staffed by divine officials. Authoritative over spirit matters but slow (celestial time is different) and prone to the same political complications as any large bureaucracy.

### The Lingtan Office
A small office (twelve investigators in the capital, scattered across major cities) that handles the gap. They have limited authority in both courts and extensive expertise in navigating between them. Their cases are the ones nobody else can handle.

### The Huli Jing Community
A diverse population of fox spirits ranging from fully cultivated nine-tails with celestial standing to first-tails who are barely more than clever animals who can take human form. They have their own social structures and their own law -- and their cases are the most complex for the Lingtan.

## 3. Adventure Seeds

1. **The Nine-Tail Suspect:** A nine-tail Huli Jing with significant celestial standing has been accused of a crime in the earthly jurisdiction. The evidence is strong. The celestial court is claiming exclusive jurisdiction. The earthly court is claiming the victim was human. Both are right. A joint tribunal must be convened, and someone high in the celestial court doesn't want that to happen.

2. **The Unlicensed Practitioner:** A first-tail fox spirit is operating in the capital without celestial registration, practicing benevolent magic for a neighborhood community that has come to depend on them. Reporting them to the celestial authorities means their likely return to spirit form. Not reporting them is a violation of Lingtan protocol. Finding a solution requires creative use of celestial law.

3. **The Forged Celestial Document:** A celestial document granting unusual permissions has been forged and is being used by someone to operate outside normal restrictions. Identifying the forgery requires deep celestial procedural knowledge. Determining who forged it requires earthly investigative work. Both courts want to handle this case exclusively.

4. **The Possession Contract:** A Huli Jing has a legitimate celestial-law contract with a human allowing partial possession for purposes of magical practice. The human has died and their heir claims the contract was not legally inheritable. The Huli Jing disputes this. Celestial contract law and human inheritance law are in direct conflict.

5. **The Missing Official:** A mid-rank celestial official who handles Lingtan liaison has disappeared. Both courts are treating this as an internal matter. The Lingtan investigators, who needed this official's testimony in an ongoing case, have the authority to investigate disappearances that affect their caseload. Following the investigation reveals something both courts have been concealing.
