## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** American Idol but the music is magic and the elimination rounds cause structural damage.

The **Great Bard-Off** is the musical Olympics of a world where music can reshape reality. The competitors are the world's most powerful musical practitioners. The competition is technically about musical excellence and practically about everything else: rivalries, politics, feuds, unresolved emotional material, and the elaborate competition meta-game of who performs when and in what order to maximize advantage.

The players are in this world, at this competition, either competing (with their own genuine musical magic and their own genuine unresolved material) or working around it (in the industry that supports and documents and debates it).

**Tone:** This is music competition drama filtered through magical absurdism. The performances are genuinely moving and also literally dangerous. The competition politics are as petty as any talent show and also have geopolitical implications. The judges are brilliant critics and also beings whose emotional states affect the magical field of the competition.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Grand Concordance Hall: a performance space designed to channel and contain magical music, its architecture modified several times after previous Bard-Offs revealed structural inadequacies, the stage itself enchanted with performance-enhancement and resonance-containment runes, the judges' panel of five legendary critics who collectively represent more musical history than any library, and the audience of 10,000 people who paid significant amounts for the privilege of being in the same space as this much concentrated musical magic.
2. **Sound:** Obviously. Music that is also magic, ranging from technically perfect and emotionally calculated to raw, genuine, reality-threatening expression. The different sonic signature of each competitor's style. The Concordance Hall's acoustic enchantments making every note exactly as intended. And the very specific silence after a performance that has affected everyone present, during which the judges write in complete quiet.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The charge in the air of a heavily magical space, the physical sensation of a powerful performance (warmth, resonance, the involuntary emotional response the audience came to experience), the backstage smell of performer stress and magical preparation, and the smooth chill of the competitor bracelet that monitors Performance Power expenditure.

**The Core Tension:** The competition requires the performers to be vulnerable (authentic emotional material powers the best performances) in a context that rewards competitive advantage. The best performances are also the most dangerous.

## 2. The Competition Structure

### The Field
30 competitors across three categories: Solo (individual performance), Ensemble (group acts), and Collaborative (acts that were enemies last week and must now perform together; this category was added after the Cold War incident and is controversial).

### The Judges
Five legendary figures whose assessments carry enormous professional weight and whose emotional states affect the magical environment of the Hall. They are impartial. They are also human. The combination creates its own competitive dynamic.

### The Schedule
Drawn by lot with some modifications for magical safety (certain competitor pairs cannot perform consecutively without resonance risk). The draw is also a competition, as position in the schedule affects Performance Power recovery time and audience emotional state inheritance from previous acts.

### The Press
Coverage of the Bard-Off is its own industry. Critics, journalists, and the magical-music equivalent of gossip columnists are all present. Their coverage will shape the competitors' careers. Their presence also changes what competitors feel comfortable risking on stage.

## 3. Adventure Seeds

1. **The Schedule Conflict:** The schedule draw has placed two competitors with a documented magical resonance incompatibility in consecutive slots. The Concordance says it's safe if containment protocols are in place. The containment team says they need more time. The competitors' agents are negotiating. The audience is seated.

2. **The Stolen Song:** A competitor's signature piece has been performed by another competitor in a preliminary round. The piece is emotionally personal, magically powerful, and technically belongs to the composer -- but the competitor who performed it claims independent creation. The Concordance must rule before the finals.

3. **The Judge's Secret:** One of the five judges is carrying a secret that is actively affecting their emotional state and, therefore, the magical environment of the Hall. The secret is related to one of the competitors. The other judges are aware something is wrong. The competition's integrity requires resolution.

4. **The Comeback:** A competitor who was disqualified from the previous Bard-Off for a Resonance Event that caused the 1270 situation has been allowed to return. The Hall's structural team has prepared specifically. The audience has strong opinions. The competitor has changed. The magical material they've accumulated since 1270 is unprecedented in scale.

5. **The Final Performance:** Two competitors remain. Both have been building toward this moment for the entire competition. Their unresolved material with each other is the most significant magical event in the Hall. The final performance will be the most powerful musical moment in a generation. It may also cause problems. The Concordance is prepared. The audience is not.
