## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** **Chiloé Island** is the second-largest island in South America, sitting off Chile's southern coast in a labyrinth of channels, fjords, and smaller islands perpetually wrapped in thick maritime fog. Its culture is a unique fusion of Huilliche Mapuche indigenous traditions and Spanish colonial folk Catholicism, producing one of the richest supernatural folklore systems in the Americas. At the center of this mythology sits the **Caleuche**: a spectral ghost ship that glows with eerie light, sails against the wind, and carries the souls of the drowned. It is crewed by the dead and controlled by the **Recta Provincia**-a secret society of brujos (male witches) who meet in a hidden underground cave and enforce their authority through fear, transformation magic, and control of the island's spiritual ecology.

This is not a setting about fighting monsters. It is a setting about power, secrecy, and the fog that hides both. The brujos are not cackling villains; they are a parallel government that has maintained order on the island for generations through a combination of genuine healing, terrifying enforcement, and absolute opacity. The question the characters must answer is not "how do we destroy the brujos?" but "what happens to the island if we do?"

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Caleuche materializing from dense fog-first as scattered lights, then as a full three-masted ship glowing blue-green from every porthole, with spectral crew dancing on deck to accordion music that carries across still water; Chiloé's iconic wooden churches (*palafitos*) on stilts over the tidal flats, their colors muted by perpetual mist; the Trauco (a malevolent forest dwarf) standing motionless between moss-covered trees, barely distinguishable from a gnarled stump until it blinks; the Invunche-a horrifically twisted human guardian of the brujos' cave, its head rotated backward on its shoulders-silhouetted against firelight.
2. **Sound:** The distant, melancholic sound of accordion music drifting across the water on a windless night (the Caleuche's siren song); the rhythmic thud of wooden churches being dismantled and rebuilt by *minga* (communal labor) teams; the eerie silence of the *monte* (dense temperate rainforest) broken only by dripping water; the sharp cry of a *chucao* bird, whose call from the right means good fortune and from the left means disaster.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The constant damp chill of Chiloé's maritime air that seeps through wool and wood alike; the rich, earthy scent of peat bogs and decomposing alerce wood; the warm, smoky comfort of a *curanto* (traditional earth-oven feast) steaming from a pit of hot stones; the electric tingling of walking into a fog bank that is slightly too cold and slightly too quiet.

**The Core Tension:** Transparency vs. Secrecy. The brujos maintain power through secrecy. The community survives through communal trust (*minga*). The Caleuche operates in fog. The characters must decide whether to tear the fog away and expose the brujos' system-which may protect the community but could also destroy the spiritual infrastructure that has kept the worst entities contained.

## 2. Historical & Mythological Context

Chiloé's mythology is uniquely dense and internally consistent, more closely resembling a complete supernatural ecosystem than a collection of isolated legends. The **Recta Provincia** (Righteous Province) is documented in historical witch trials from the 19th century as a genuine secret society operating on Chiloé, complete with initiation rituals, a hierarchical structure, and an underground cave headquarters.

Key entities in Chiloé mythology include the **Caleuche** (ghost ship), the **Trauco** (a forest dwarf who seduces and impregnates women), the **Fiura** (the Trauco's hideous female counterpart), the **Invunche** (a kidnapped child transformed by brujos into a monstrous cave guardian by breaking and resetting its bones), the **Imbunche** cave (the brujos' headquarters), and the **Basilisco chilote** (a serpent-rooster hybrid that drains life force).

The **Huilliche** Mapuche people of Chiloé maintain their own spiritual traditions parallel to and distinct from the colonial-era witch mythology, including machi healing practices and reverence for the *ngen* (nature spirits) of the temperate rainforest.

> **Note on Respectful Portrayal:** Chiloé's folklore is a living cultural tradition. The Recta Provincia represents a complex historical phenomenon that reflects colonial-era power dynamics and Indigenous-European cultural synthesis. This setting treats Chilota culture with deep respect, particularly the *minga* tradition of communal labor and mutual aid that defines the island's social fabric. The Invunche legend, which involves the violent transformation of a kidnapped child, is presented as the brujos' most horrific act and a moral line that drives the narrative.

## 3. Core Factions & Traditions

### The Recta Provincia (The Brujos' Coven)
The secret society of sorcerers who control the Caleuche and maintain a shadow government over Chiloé's spiritual affairs.
- **Cultural Association:** The Recta Provincia operates from a hidden cave accessible only by transformation or fog-walking. Members undergo brutal initiation rites (including standing under a freezing waterfall for forty nights) and gain access to transformation magic (*macuñ*-a vest made from the skin of a virgen del mar that grants flight), communication with the dead, and command authority over the Caleuche. The society is internally hierarchical, with a King, a Council, and regional enforcers. Not all members are evil-some joined to protect their families, and the society does provide genuine spiritual services. But its methods, particularly the creation of the Invunche, are unforgivable.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I wear the macuñ vest under my poncho. It lets me fly, but the cost of the skin it is made from haunts my dreams."*
  - *"I know the twelve secret words that summon the Caleuche to any shore on a foggy night."*
  - *"I joined the Recta Provincia to protect my village. I stayed because leaving means becoming an Invunche."*

### The Minga Circle (Community Healers and Builders)
The communal labor tradition that is the spiritual and social backbone of Chiloé.
- **Cultural Association:** *Minga* is the Chilota tradition of communal labor-when a house needs to be moved, an entire community comes together to physically carry it to a new location. When a church needs repair, the community builds it together. Minga is not merely practical; it is spiritual. The act of communal labor generates a protective energy that the brujos cannot penetrate. Characters aligned with the Minga Circle use community bonds as both social support and supernatural defense.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I have helped carry seventeen houses across the island. The act of carrying together makes us untouchable."*
  - *"My curanto feeds forty people and generates enough communal warmth to keep the Caleuche fog at bay for a full night."*
  - *"The brujos fear the minga because they cannot corrupt what belongs to everyone."*

### The Huilliche Machi (Southern Mapuche Healers)
Spiritual healers who maintain the older, pre-colonial spiritual traditions of southern Chile.
- **Cultural Association:** The machi tradition on Chiloé predates the brujo mythology and operates on fundamentally different spiritual principles. Where brujos manipulate and control, machi restore and balance. The machi uses the *rewe* (sacred carved post), the *kultrun* drum, and herbal medicine to heal spiritual afflictions, commune with *ngen* (nature spirits), and provide an alternative to the Recta Provincia's coercive spiritual services.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"My rewe stands at the edge of the alerce forest and keeps the wekufe contained where the brujos cannot."*
  - *"I heal the people the brujos have cursed. This makes me their enemy."*
  - *"The ngen of the old-growth forest speak to me. They say the brujos' power is borrowed and their debt is coming due."*

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Niebla (Fog Communion)

Chiloé's fog is not merely atmospheric. It is a spiritual medium-a suspension of water, memory, and unresolved death that blankets the archipelago and serves as the operating environment for both the Caleuche and the brujos.

Characters track a **Niebla Meter** (1-6):

- **1-2 (Clear-Sighted / Despejado):** The character is firmly grounded in the physical world. +1d6 to Mind and Presence in social situations with living humans. The fog is simply fog. The character cannot perceive the Caleuche, fog passages, or brujo enchantments. Safe, but spiritually blind.
- **3-4 (Fog-Touched / Neblina):** The character can perceive shapes and lights in the fog that others cannot. They can hear the Caleuche's music and determine its direction. +1d6 to Finesse when navigating fog-covered terrain or water. They can enter fog passages (shortcuts between locations that exist only when fog is thick enough). Costs 1 Momentum per scene. **Risk:** The Caleuche can perceive the character in return. The brujos' enchantments begin to work on them.
- **5-6 (Fog-Bound / Perdido en la Niebla):** +1d6 to Force and Finesse in all fog environments. The character can walk on fog-covered water as if it were solid ground. They can board the Caleuche voluntarily and communicate with the drowned crew. They can see through brujo illusions completely. **Risk:** The character is becoming part of the fog. Their body temperature drops. Their skin becomes pale and damp. Living people find it increasingly difficult to remember their face. At Niebla 6, the Caleuche extends a permanent invitation-and the ship does not take no for an answer.

**Adjusting Niebla:** Exposure to thick fog raises Niebla by 1 per scene. Sunlight (rare on Chiloé) lowers it by 1. Participating in a minga (communal labor) lowers it by 2. Eating a communal curanto lowers it by 1. Being alone in fog for extended periods raises it by 2.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### Archetype 1: The Caleuche Survivor
- **Concept:** A fisherman who was pulled overboard in a storm, drowned, and woke up on the deck of the Caleuche. He served as crew for what felt like years-dancing, singing, fishing the spectral waters-before managing to escape during a rare moment when the ship passed close enough to shore. He is alive, but he is not sure he is *entirely* alive. He no longer casts a full reflection. His skin is always cold. And he can hear the ship's accordion music every single night, getting louder.
- **Quote:** *"The worst part of the Caleuche is not the death. The worst part is that the dead are happy. They dance and sing and feast, and they want you to stay because they are lonely. Escape is not running from a prison. Escape is leaving a party that your dead friends are enjoying."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Drowned and resurrected by the Caleuche, carrying death's cold in my bones"*, *"Can breathe underwater for extended periods-a gift from the ghost ship I did not ask for"*, *"I hear the Caleuche's accordion music every night, and it is always closer"*, *"My reflection shows the Caleuche crew standing behind me"*.
- **Drive:** *"Sever the Caleuche's hold on me before it pulls me back aboard permanently."*
- **Flaw:** *"Part of me misses the ship. The dead were kind to me. The living are not always so generous."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 3 | Mind 1 | Presence 2

### Archetype 2: The Renegade Bruja
- **Concept:** A woman who was inducted into the Recta Provincia as a young girl and trained in transformation magic, fog-walking, and Caleuche summoning. She obeyed for years until she was ordered to help create an Invunche-to participate in the kidnapping and magical mutilation of a child. She refused, fled, and has been hunted ever since. She still has her powers (the macuñ vest, the summoning words, the fog-walking ability) but using them risks revealing her location to the Recta Provincia.
- **Quote:** *"I can fly. I can walk through fog. I can call a ghost ship. But I cannot make a child into a monster. That is where my obedience ended and my war began."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Escaped the Recta Provincia and carries their secrets like stolen fire"*, *"Owns a macuñ vest that grants flight-I try not to think about what it is made of"*, *"Knows the twelve words that summon and dismiss the Caleuche"*, *"Every brujo on the island has orders to bring me back alive"*.
- **Drive:** *"Destroy the Recta Provincia from the outside, using the very powers they taught me."*
- **Flaw:** *"I am terrified that my time with the brujos corrupted me more deeply than I want to admit."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

### Archetype 3: The Machi of the Alerce Grove
- **Concept:** An elderly Huilliche machi who has maintained the spiritual health of the southern Chiloé coast for forty years. She is the brujos' oldest enemy-not because she fights them directly, but because she offers the community an alternative. Every person she heals is a person who does not need to ask the Recta Provincia for help. Every minga she organizes is a demonstration that communal power works without coercion. The brujos cannot kill her because the community would revolt. So they have decided to discredit her instead.
- **Quote:** *"The brujos offer power. I offer balance. Power runs out. Balance does not. This is why they fear me."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Forty years of machi practice in the alerce groves of southern Chiloé"*, *"My rewe post has stood for three generations and the ngen spirits guard it"*, *"I have healed 200 people the brujos cursed, and I keep count"*, *"The old-growth forest tells me when strangers approach. The trees have known me longer than any human."*
- **Drive:** *"Heal enough of the community that the Recta Provincia becomes irrelevant, and the island no longer needs to fear the fog."*
- **Flaw:** *"My patience with the long game sometimes blinds me to immediate dangers that require faster action."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 1 | Mind 3 | Presence 3

## 6. Condition Palette

**Physical Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Fog Chill* (persistent cold from extended fog exposure), *Caleuche Resonance* (phantom accordion music causing headaches), *Peat Bog Twisted Ankle*.
- **Moderate:** *Trauco Enchantment* (disorientation and weakness from the forest dwarf's gaze), *Brujo Curse Mark* (burning sigil on the skin), *Drowned Lungs* (residual water in the lungs from Caleuche drowning).
- **Severe:** *Invunche Sight* (witnessing the Invunche causes lasting physical tremors), *Fog Dissolution* (the body is partially dissolving into fog), *Caleuche Claim* (the ghost ship has marked you-your skin glows faintly blue-green in darkness).

**Social Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Village Gossip* (rumored to be a brujo sympathizer), *Chucao Warning* (the bird called from the left-bad luck follows you).
- **Moderate:** *Recta Provincia Marked* (the brujos have put a price on your silence), *Community Suspicion* (the village doubts your motives).
- **Severe:** *Brujo Excommunication* (you are a renegade targeted for recapture), *Minga Rejection* (the community has refused to work with you-the worst social death on Chiloé).

**Psychological Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Fog Anxiety* (dread when visibility drops), *Accordion Earworm* (the Caleuche's music loops in your head).
- **Moderate:** *Caleuche Homesickness* (the ghost ship's warmth and companionship seem preferable to the cold, lonely shore), *Invunche Nightmares* (you dream of the twisted child guardian every night).
- **Severe:** *Fog Identity* (you are no longer sure whether you are a person who walks in fog or fog that remembers being a person), *The Renegade's Guilt* (the weight of the brujo training you received, the things you did before you refused, and the question of whether refusal at the end absolves compliance at the beginning).

## 7. Starter Scenario: The Minga of Defiance

**Framing:** A widow's house on the southern coast of Chiloé needs to be moved-the tidal erosion is undermining its stilts. The community has organized a *minga* (the traditional communal house-moving, where the entire structure is lifted onto log rollers and dragged by dozens of people to a new location). But the Recta Provincia has forbidden the minga. The widow refused to pay the brujos' "tithe" (a portion of her husband's fishing catch, demanded after his death on the Caleuche), and the brujos have declared that anyone who helps her will be cursed.

The Machi is organizing the minga anyway. The Renegade Bruja has learned that the brujos plan to summon the Caleuche to the shore during the minga as a demonstration of power. The Caleuche Survivor knows that the widow's husband is on board the ghost ship-and that seeing him might break her will to resist.

**3-Layer Scene Design:**

1. **Immediate Threat:** The minga begins at dawn. Forty islanders have come to help carry the house despite the brujos' threat. As they lift the house onto rollers, a thick, unnatural fog rolls in from the channel. The brujos are fog-walking into the crowd, disguised as participants, ready to sabotage the minga from within.

2. **Ticking Clock:** The Caleuche will arrive at the shore in 5 beats. Beat 2: The accordion music becomes audible. Beat 3: Spectral lights appear in the fog. Beat 4: The widow sees her husband on deck and begins walking toward the water. Beat 5: The Caleuche drops anchor and extends a gangplank. Anyone who steps onto it joins the crew of the dead.

3. **Opportunity:** The Renegade Bruja knows the twelve words that can dismiss the Caleuche, but speaking them will reveal her identity to every brujo on the island. The Caleuche Survivor can board the ship and convince the widow's husband to release her from the ship's call, but boarding risks his own recapture. The Machi can generate enough communal spiritual energy through the minga itself to push back the fog, but only if all forty participants keep working together despite their fear.

**Antagonist:** ***El Rey de la Recta Provincia*** (The King of the Brujos) - The leader of the Chiloé coven. He is ancient, possibly centuries old, sustained by the Caleuche's spiritual energy. He genuinely believes that the Recta Provincia is necessary-that without the brujos' control, the spiritual entities of the archipelago would overwhelm the human population. He has a point. The question is whether his methods (coercion, Invunche creation, Caleuche conscription of the dead) are justified by the protection they provide.
- **Traits:** Fog-walking mastery, transformation magic, command of the Caleuche, centuries of accumulated knowledge, weakness to genuine communal resistance (the minga generates energy he cannot corrupt because it belongs to no individual), physically frail without his magic.
- **Condition Track:** ☐ Fog Assault (brujo agents infiltrate the minga) / ☐ Caleuche Summoned (the ghost ship arrives as demonstration of power) / ☐ Authority Challenged (the minga succeeds despite his threats, undermining his credibility) / ☐ Confronted or Negotiated (the characters face him directly, choosing between destroying the Recta Provincia and negotiating reform).
- **Shadow Cost:** Destroying the Recta Provincia removes the brujos' oppression but also removes their containment of the Trauco, the Basilisco, and other entities they have been managing. Negotiating reform is harder but preserves the spiritual infrastructure while removing the coercive elements. The Invunche cannot be reformed-only freed, and freeing it raises the question of what happens to a person whose body has been that thoroughly broken.

## 8. Why This Setting Matters

Chiloé's mythology is among the most internally consistent and richly textured folklore systems in the world-a complete supernatural society with its own government, economy, and moral code existing alongside and intertwined with the human community. The Caleuche is not merely a ghost story; it is a metaphor for the way communities process collective grief (the drowned fishermen who never came home). The Recta Provincia is not merely a villain; it is a mirror of every institution that maintains order through fear while providing genuine services. And the *minga*-the communal house-carrying tradition-is one of the most powerful symbols in any folklore tradition: the idea that when the whole community lifts together, no individual force, natural or supernatural, can bring the house down.
