## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The **Kingdom of Ayutthaya** (1351-1767) was one of the most powerful and cosmopolitan states in Southeast Asian history-a golden city of over a million people, its skyline pierced by hundreds of gilded temple spires (*prang*), its canals thronging with merchant boats from China, India, Persia, Portugal, and Japan. In April 1767, after a fourteen-month siege, the Burmese Konbaung Dynasty sacked Ayutthaya, burning the city and scattering its population. It is one of the most traumatic events in Thai history.

In this setting, the siege of Ayutthaya is not merely a military catastrophe but a spiritual one. The Naga Kings-seven-headed serpent deities who dwell beneath the Chao Phraya River and guard the submerged foundations of Ayutthaya-have been slumbering for centuries, lulled by generations of peaceful offerings. The violence of the siege disturbs their rest. A hidden order of **Muay Boran warrior monks** (practitioners of the ancient predecessor to Muay Thai) bears Sak Yant sacred tattoos that allow them to channel supernatural combat abilities-but only while maintaining strict Buddhist moral precepts. As the walls crumble and the city burns, these monks must descend into the flooded temple foundations to wake the Naga Kings and plead for intervention, while holding the walls with sacred fists.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The breathtaking skyline of Ayutthaya's golden temple spires reflecting in the Chao Phraya's brown waters at sunset, slowly being consumed by siege fires that turn the reflections to blood-orange; the terrifying beauty of a seven-headed Naga rising from the river, each head crowned with a golden hood, water cascading from its coils like a living waterfall; the geometric perfection of Sak Yant tattoos glowing with white-gold light on a warrior monk's back as the sacred power activates; lotus flowers blooming spontaneously on floodwater wherever a Naga has passed.
2. **Sound:** The primal rhythm of hemp-wrapped fists striking flesh in Muay Boran combat-each impact accompanied by a warrior's shout (*kiai*) that carries spiritual force; the deep, subsonic vibration of Naga movement beneath the river, felt in the chest before it is heard; temple bells ringing in the smoke as monks chant protective sutras over collapsing walls; the roar of the Burmese army's war drums answering with ten thousand throats.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The tight binding of hemp rope around the hands and forearms (*kard chuek*) before combat, each wrap a meditation; the warm glow of Sak Yant ink pulsing beneath the skin like a second heartbeat; the choking mixture of temple incense and burning teak that fills the air during the siege; the sudden cool of the river flooding through underground temple passages, carrying the metallic scent of Naga.

**The Core Tension:** Discipline vs. Desperation. The Sak Yant tattoos demand moral purity. The siege demands survival. Every shortcut, every act of cruelty, every lie told to save a life risks burning out the sacred power that is the last defense. The characters must decide: can you fight a total war while maintaining total virtue?

## 2. Historical & Mythological Context

Ayutthaya was founded in 1351 on an island at the confluence of three rivers. At its peak, it was one of the largest cities in the world. European visitors described it as more magnificent than any city in Europe. Its destruction by Burma in 1767 remains a defining moment in Thai national memory.

**Muay Boran** ("ancient boxing") is the ancestor of modern Muay Thai, historically practiced by soldiers and monks. The legendary fighter **Nai Khanom Tom** is said to have defeated ten Burmese champions in succession after the fall of Ayutthaya, earning his freedom and the respect of the Burmese king. He is celebrated annually on National Muay Thai Day.

**Naga** (Thai: *Nak*) are sacred serpent beings deeply embedded in Thai Buddhism and Hinduism. They are guardians of water, treasure, and sacred knowledge. The seven-headed Naga is a common motif on temple staircases, representing the bridge between the earthly and divine realms. According to tradition, the Naga King Mucalinda sheltered the Buddha during a great storm by coiling beneath him and spreading his seven hoods as an umbrella.

**Sak Yant** (sacred tattoo) is a living tradition in Thailand, Cambodia, Laos, and Myanmar. Buddhist monks and *ajarns* (lay masters) inscribe sacred geometric designs with bamboo needles or metal tips, using ink infused with prayers. The tattoos are believed to confer protection, strength, and good fortune-but only while the bearer maintains specific moral precepts.

> **Note on Respectful Portrayal:** The fall of Ayutthaya, Muay Boran martial traditions, Naga reverence, and Sak Yant practice are integral to Thai cultural identity. This setting presents these traditions with deep respect for Thai spirituality, martial heritage, and the profound grief of Ayutthaya's destruction.

## 3. Core Factions & Traditions

### The Order of the Broken Spire (Sak Yant Warrior Monks)
A hidden monastic order that combines Muay Boran combat mastery with Sak Yant sacred tattoo activation. They guard the subterranean temple passages that connect Ayutthaya's foundations to the Naga realms beneath the river.
- **Cultural Association:** Buddhist monks who have taken additional vows to serve as spiritual warriors. Each monk bears between five and fifteen Sak Yant designs, each conferring a specific ability (invulnerability, enhanced strength, fear projection, pain suppression). They practice a form of Muay Boran that incorporates spiritual breathing techniques (*anapanasati*) to channel tattoo power through strikes.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"My Hah Taew (five-line) tattoo has turned aside sword blows seventeen times; it has never failed me because I have never broken a precept"*
  - *"I can shatter a teak pillar with a single elbow strike when my Sak Yant is fully charged"*
  - *"The abbot tattooed the Naga Paed Tidt (eight-directional Naga) on my back-it protects me from harm from all eight compass points, but only while I tell truth"*

### The River Naga Court (Nak Badan)
The Naga Kings and their retinue who dwell in a submerged palace beneath the confluence of the three rivers under Ayutthaya.
- **Cultural Association:** Ancient serpent beings of immense power and alien morality. They are neither good nor evil but operate on a timescale and value system incomprehensible to humans. They protect the foundations of Ayutthaya not out of love for humans but because the city was built on their sacred grounds, and its temple foundations serve as the roof of their palace. The siege is destroying their ceiling.
- **Descriptor Examples (for Naga-blooded human intermediaries):**
  - *"I can breathe underwater and speak the sibilant language of the Naga Court"*
  - *"My skin shifts to iridescent scales when submerged, and I can feel the Naga King's mood through the river current"*
  - *"The Naga Court accepted me as an ambassador because my grandmother made them an offering every day for sixty years"*

### The Siamese Royal Elephants (Chang Si Pheuk)
Sacred war elephants and their mahouts who serve as the armored cavalry of Ayutthaya's defense.
- **Cultural Association:** The white elephant is the supreme symbol of Thai royalty. War elephants carry howdahs (fighting platforms) and are trained in *elephant duel* combat-a formalized style where kings and generals fight from elephant-back. The mahouts share a spiritual bond with their elephants that borders on telepathy.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"My elephant, Plai Rachaphat, has charged Burmese siege lines four times and refuses to retreat before I do"*
  - *"I can feel my elephant's heartbeat through the back of my legs and know her thoughts"*
  - *"A mahout who abandons their elephant in battle loses their soul; this is not metaphor"*

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Sak Yant (Sacred Tattoo) Power

Characters who bear Sak Yant tattoos track a **Sak Yant Charge Meter** (1-6):

- **1-2 (Dormant):** The tattoos are beautiful but inert. No supernatural benefits. This state occurs when the bearer has recently violated a precept or is spiritually exhausted. Standard combat rules apply.
- **3-4 (Awakened):** The tattoos pulse with warm light. +1d6 to Force in unarmed combat. The bearer's skin hardens against bladed weapons (reduce severity of physical Conditions by one step). Pain is muted. Costs 1 Momentum per scene to maintain. **Precept Requirement:** The bearer must not have lied, stolen, harmed an innocent, or consumed intoxicants since the last dawn.
- **5-6 (Blazing):** The tattoos glow visibly through clothing. +1d6 to Force AND Finesse in all combat. Complete invulnerability to mundane weapons (swords, arrows, spears pass through or around the bearer). Strikes carry spiritual force that can damage supernatural entities. **Precept Requirement:** All precepts must be maintained AND the bearer must be fighting in defense of others, not for personal gain or revenge. A single violation drops the meter to 1 immediately and inflicts the *Sak Yant Burn* Condition (Severe).

**The Five Precepts (Sila):**
1. Do not kill (harming in defense is permitted; killing the helpless is not)
2. Do not steal
3. Do not engage in sexual misconduct
4. Do not lie
5. Do not consume intoxicants

**Violation Consequence:** Breaking any precept causes an immediate and painful reaction. The tattoo lines burn white-hot, inflicting the *Sak Yant Burn* Condition and dropping the charge to 0. The bearer cannot recharge until they have performed sincere atonement (confessing to a senior monk, performing a selfless act, or meditating through a full night without sleep).

## 5. Character Archetypes

### Archetype 1: The Last Monk of Wat Chaiwatthanaram
- **Concept:** A middle-aged warrior monk who has spent twenty years perfecting both meditation and Muay Boran in the great temple of Wat Chaiwatthanaram. His body bears twelve Sak Yant tattoos-more than any living monk. He has never broken a precept. He has also never fought outside the training yard. Now the Burmese are burning his temple, and the ancient prayer beads he clutches are the only thing keeping his hands from shaking.
- **Quote:** *"I have trained my entire life for a fight I prayed would never come. The prayer failed. The training must not."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Twelve Sak Yant tattoos that have never been tested in true combat"*, *"Muay Boran elbow technique that can crack stone"*, *"Twenty years of daily meditation grant absolute calm-until the first scream"*, *"The only monk who memorized the complete Naga summoning sutra"*.
- **Drive:** *"Protect the monks, the scriptures, and the temple treasures from the siege-and find the courage to fight for the first time in my life."*
- **Flaw:** *"My lifelong commitment to non-violence has left me paralyzed by the reality of lethal combat."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 3 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 1

### Archetype 2: The Naga Ambassador's Granddaughter
- **Concept:** A young woman whose grandmother served as the human intermediary to the Naga Court for forty years. Her grandmother died last monsoon season, and the Naga have not accepted a new ambassador. She can breathe underwater and speak fragments of the Naga language, but the Court considers her unproven. With Ayutthaya falling, she must convince beings who measure time in centuries that the next fourteen months matter.
- **Quote:** *"The Naga King asked me why he should care about a city that has only existed for four hundred years. I told him: because your ceiling is on fire."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Can breathe underwater and speak broken Naga court language"*, *"Skin shifts to subtle iridescent scales when submerged"*, *"Grandmother's jade Naga pendant opens the river-gate to the Court"*, *"Understands Naga etiquette well enough to not get eaten during negotiations"*.
- **Drive:** *"Convince the Naga Kings to intervene before both their palace and our city are destroyed."*
- **Flaw:** *"I am terrified of the Naga Court and doubt I am worthy of my grandmother's legacy."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

### Archetype 3: The Mahout of the Last White Elephant
- **Concept:** The royal mahout of Ayutthaya's last surviving white elephant-a sacred animal whose loss would symbolize the spiritual death of the kingdom. As the siege tightens, he must smuggle the elephant out of the city through the Burmese lines while the warrior monks hold the walls.
- **Quote:** *"This elephant is the kingdom. If she falls, the kingdom falls. If she lives, everything we are survives somewhere."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Bonded since birth to the white elephant Phra Sri Maha Chaiya"*, *"Can communicate with her through touch and breath alone"*, *"Veteran of three elephant duels against Burmese war-elephants"*, *"Carries the last royal seal of Ayutthaya strapped to the elephant's howdah"*.
- **Drive:** *"Get the white elephant to safety, even if I must ride through the entire Burmese army to do it."*
- **Flaw:** *"I will sacrifice any human life, including my own, to protect this elephant-and that inflexibility may cost us allies we cannot afford to lose."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 3 | Mind 1 | Presence 2

## 6. Condition Palette

**Physical Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Hemp-Burned Knuckles* (raw skin from Muay Boran hand wraps), *Smoke Inhalation* (from burning temples), *River Chill* (from submerged Naga Court visits).
- **Moderate:** *Burmese Sword Slash*, *Sak Yant Burn* (tattoo violation backlash-scorched skin along tattoo lines), *War Elephant Trampled*.
- **Severe:** *Naga Venom* (sacred serpent poison that turns blood cold), *Siege Collapse* (buried under falling temple masonry), *Precept Shattered* (all Sak Yant tattoos burned black, requiring complete re-inscription).

**Social Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Questioned Courage* (monks whisper that you hesitated), *Naga Court Dismissal* (the serpents found you unimpressive).
- **Moderate:** *Monastic Censure* (the abbot has revoked your senior status), *Burmese Informant Suspicion* (accused of collaboration).
- **Severe:** *Sacred Bond Broken* (your mahout bond with your elephant has shattered), *Naga King's Wrath* (you offended the Court and are banned from the river).

**Psychological Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Siege Tremors* (flinching at every loud sound), *Precept Anxiety* (constant fear of accidental violation).
- **Moderate:** *Golden Ruin Grief* (witnessing the destruction of Ayutthaya's beauty overwhelms you), *Naga Time Disorientation* (after visiting the Court, human time feels meaninglessly fast).
- **Severe:** *Warrior's Paradox* (the contradiction between Buddhist non-violence and the necessity of combat has broken your spiritual center), *The Last Spire Falls* (you have internalized the kingdom's death as your personal failure).

## 7. Starter Scenario: The Burning of the Golden Spires

**Framing:** It is April 7, 1767. The Burmese army has breached the eastern wall of Ayutthaya. Fires have engulfed the palace quarter. The Order of the Broken Spire has retreated to Wat Chaiwatthanaram, where they are making a last stand around the temple's central prang (tower). Beneath the temple, a staircase descends into flooded passages that lead to the Naga Court. The Naga Ambassador's Granddaughter believes she can reach the Court and negotiate. The Last Monk can hold the temple long enough for her to try. The Mahout must move the white elephant through the chaos to the western river gate. All three objectives are happening simultaneously, and they share a ticking clock: the Burmese are bringing fire ships up the river to burn the western gate.

**3-Layer Scene Design:**

1. **Immediate Threat:** Thirty Burmese soldiers have entered Wat Chaiwatthanaram. The warrior monks (five remaining) are fighting hand-to-hand in the cloisters. The Burmese commander has ordered the central prang demolished with gunpowder charges. If the prang falls, the temple floor collapses into the Naga passage below, trapping the Ambassador's Granddaughter.

2. **Ticking Clock:** Fire ships will reach the western river gate in 5 beats. Beat 1: smoke visible upriver. Beat 2: the fleet rounds the bend. Beat 3: archers on fire ships begin shooting at anyone on the river bank. Beat 4: fire ships contact the gate. Beat 5: the gate burns and the river route is sealed-trapping the white elephant inside the city.

3. **Opportunity:** The Naga King will flood the Burmese siege works-but only if presented with an offering that demonstrates the Siamese people are worth saving. The offering is not gold or jade; it is a demonstration of the precept of compassion. Someone must perform an act of genuine mercy toward a Burmese enemy-heal a wounded soldier, spare a commander, or protect a Burmese prisoner from a vengeful mob-while the city burns around them. This is almost impossibly difficult in the midst of the siege, which is precisely the point.

**Antagonist:** ***General Maha Nawrahta*** - The Burmese siege commander. He is not a villain but a professional soldier executing his king's orders. He respects Siamese martial tradition (he personally witnessed Nai Khanom Tom's legendary fights) and would prefer to accept a surrender rather than destroy the city. But his orders are absolute, and he has no authority to offer terms.
- **Traits:** Master strategist, commands overwhelming force (40,000 troops), personally skilled with the Burmese *dha* sword, honorable within the framework of his duty, weakness to displays of extraordinary martial virtue (will pause to observe a worthy fight).
- **Condition Track:** ☐ Siege Proceeding (overwhelming force, no reason to negotiate) / ☐ Impressed (witnesses Sak Yant combat or Muay Boran mastery, opens to dialogue) / ☐ Intervention (Naga flooding disrupts siege works, forcing tactical withdrawal) / ☐ Stalemate or Negotiated Retreat (the Burmese pull back from the temple quarter, buying time for evacuation).
- **Shadow Cost:** If the characters cannot demonstrate compassion toward a Burmese enemy, the Naga King's flood destroys Burmese and Siamese positions alike. If they can, the flood is precisely targeted. If General Maha Nawrahta is killed, the Burmese army responds with total annihilation orders. If he is shown mercy, he quietly delays the final assault by three days-enough time for the monks to evacuate the scriptures and the elephant to escape.

## 8. Why This Setting Matters

The fall of Ayutthaya is to Thai cultural memory what the fall of Troy is to the Western imagination: a golden civilization destroyed by war, its survivors carrying its spirit into a new era (the founding of Bangkok). But unlike Troy, Ayutthaya's spiritual traditions survived. Muay Thai is practiced by millions worldwide. Sak Yant tattoos are sought by people of every nationality. The Naga remains a central symbol of Thai sacred art. This setting explores the question that defines all survivor cultures: when everything is burning, what do you save? The answer, this setting proposes, is not the gold or the buildings. It is the discipline, the art, the compassion, and the stories. Those are the things that survive the fire.
