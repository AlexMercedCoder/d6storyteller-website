## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** **Yoruba** civilization produced one of the most sophisticated theological and philosophical systems in human history. The **Ifa** divination corpus contains 256 *odu* (chapters), each containing hundreds of verses (*ese*) that encode Yoruba philosophy, medicine, law, history, and spiritual practice. The **orisha** are not distant gods; they are intimate spiritual forces that interact with humans daily through ritual, divination, and possession. Every person has a personal orisha who guides their destiny and an **Ori** (inner head) that represents the destiny they chose before incarnation.

Set in the 15th-century Yoruba city-states (Ile-Ife, Oyo, Benin) during the golden age of Yoruba civilization--when the bronze-casters of Benin were creating artworks that rival anything in Renaissance Europe and the Ifa system was being codified into its mature form--this setting places players at the intersection of mortal politics and divine cosmology. The orisha are real, active, and often in conflict with each other. Eshu, the divine trickster, controls all crossroads and communications, making him simultaneously the most important and most dangerous orisha to deal with.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The magnificent bronze-walled city of Benin, its walls stretching for miles, its palace towers adorned with brass plaques depicting the Oba's divine authority; the sacred grove of Osun-Osogbo, a dense forest where every tree, river, and stone is home to an orisha; Eshu's crossroads marked by a mound of laterite clay adorned with cowrie shells and palm oil, shimmering with the trickster's barely contained energy; the Ifa divination tray dusted with sacred powder, marked by the babalawo's precise finger strokes.
2. **Sound:** The thunderous beat of *bata* drums calling the orisha to descend during a ceremony; the measured chanting of the babalawo reciting the 256 odu of Ifa from memory (a feat requiring years of study); the laughter of Eshu, which sounds like it is coming from everywhere and nowhere; the rushing water of the Osun River carrying Oshun's blessings.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The smooth, warm surface of freshly cast bronze still radiating heat from the forge; the sweet, pungent scent of palm oil offerings at the crossroads; the cool darkness of the sacred grove contrasting with the heat of the open savanna; the weight of coral beads signifying spiritual authority.

**The Core Tension:** Destiny vs. Free Will. Every person chose their Ori (destiny) before birth, but they cannot remember what they chose. The Ifa oracle can reveal fragments of this destiny, but the interpretation is always ambiguous. The characters must decide whether to submit to their chosen destiny (which they do not remember choosing) or fight for a different future (which means fighting themselves). Eshu's role is to ensure that this decision is never simple.

## 2. Historical & Mythological Context

The **Yoruba** are one of Africa's largest ethnic groups, with a civilization centered in modern southwestern Nigeria that has produced influential art, philosophy, and spiritual traditions. The **Kingdom of Benin** (distinct from modern Benin Republic) was one of the most powerful states in West Africa, famous for its bronze-casting tradition. **Ife** (Ile-Ife) is considered the spiritual homeland of the Yoruba and the place where creation began.

The **Ifa** divination system is recognized by UNESCO as an Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity. It requires 10-15 years of study to master and encodes the entire philosophical, medical, and historical corpus of Yoruba civilization. The **orisha** number in the hundreds, but key figures include **Ogun** (iron, war, technology), **Oshun** (love, beauty, rivers, fertility), **Shango** (thunder, justice), **Yemoja** (ocean, motherhood), **Obatala** (creation, purity), and **Eshu** (crossroads, communication, trickery).

> **Note on Respectful Portrayal:** Yoruba spiritual traditions (Ifa, orisha worship) are living religions practiced by millions of people worldwide, including in the diaspora traditions of Candomble (Brazil), Santeria (Cuba), and Vodou (Haiti). This setting treats Yoruba cosmology as a sophisticated philosophical and spiritual system deserving of deep respect.

## 3. Core Factions & Traditions

### The Babalawo Council (Keepers of Ifa)
The high priests of Ifa divination who maintain the sacred corpus and interpret the orisha's will.
- **Cultural Association:** The babalawo (*father of secrets*) is the highest spiritual authority in Yoruba society, trained for 10-15 years in the memorization and interpretation of the 256 odu of Ifa. They divine using the *opele* (divination chain) or *ikin* (sacred palm nuts), interpreting the patterns they produce.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I have memorized 200 of the 256 odu. Each odu contains 800 verses. The knowledge is a library I carry in my skull."*
  - *"When I cast the opele chain, the pattern it makes reveals which odu speaks to the questioner's situation. The odu never lies. But it always requires interpretation."*
  - *"Eshu delivered my last message to Ogun instead of Oshun. The iron god's response to a love question was... educational."*

### The Oshun Devotees (Daughters of the River)
Priestesses of the orisha of love, beauty, freshwater, and diplomacy.
- **Cultural Association:** Oshun is one of the most beloved orisha--associated with the Osun River, gold, honey, and the power of feminine grace and strategy. Her devotees are healers, diplomats, and custodians of the sacred groves. They are also formidable: Oshun once saved the world when all the other orisha failed, precisely because they underestimated her.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"Oshun does not fight. Oshun negotiates. And when negotiation fails, Oshun reminds you that rivers carve canyons."*
  - *"My brass fan channels Oshun's authority. When I dance the Oshun dance, even Ogun pauses."*
  - *"The river tells me everything. It carries the secrets of every village upstream and the prayers of every village downstream."*

### The Benin Bronze Guild (Igun-Eronmwon)
The royal bronze-casters of the Kingdom of Benin, whose works capture spiritual power in metal.
- **Cultural Association:** The Benin bronze-casters are among the finest artists in human history. Their works--portrait heads, plaques, and ceremonial objects--were created using the lost-wax casting method. In this setting, the bronze-casting process also captures and contains spiritual energy: a bronze portrait can hold a fragment of its subject's Ori, and a ceremonial bronze can serve as a vessel for an orisha's attention.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"When I cast bronze, the metal remembers the face I shape. The face remembers the person. The person's Ori remembers their destiny. My bronzes are destiny made visible."*
  - *"I forged a bronze plaque depicting the war between Ogun and Oshun. Both orisha appeared at my workshop that night to critique my composition."*

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Ori (Destiny Alignment)

Characters track an **Ori Meter** (1-6):

- **1-2 (Misaligned / Ori Buruku):** Fighting against destiny. -1d6 to all rolls. The orisha do not respond to prayers. Divination produces contradictory results. Everything the character attempts encounters unexpected obstacles.
- **3-4 (Seeking / Ori Mimi):** In transition. Standard rolls. The character is searching for their destiny. Ifa divination reveals fragments. The orisha respond but demand offerings before granting guidance. +1d6 to Mind when consulting the oracle.
- **5-6 (Aligned / Ori Ire):** Living in harmony with chosen destiny. +1d6 to all rolls. The orisha speak clearly. Doors open. Fate arranges coincidences in the character's favor. **Risk:** An aligned Ori attracts the attention of Eshu, who delights in testing alignment. At any crossroads, Eshu can challenge the character to a riddle, a game, or a choice. Failure flips Ori to 1.

**Adjusting Ori:** Making choices consistent with one's inner nature raises Ori. Making choices that betray one's values lowers it. Performing the appropriate rituals and offerings to one's personal orisha raises it. Neglecting spiritual obligations lowers it. Eshu can flip it at crossroads.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### Archetype 1: The Babalawo of Ile-Ife
- **Concept:** The youngest babalawo to complete the full Ifa training in a generation. He can recite 200 of the 256 odu from memory and interpret the opele chain with extraordinary precision. His problem: the Ifa oracle keeps giving him the same answer to every question, an odu that translates roughly as "the crossroads are closing." He does not know what this means, but Eshu is laughing.
- **Quote:** *"The Ifa never lies. But sometimes the Ifa tells you a truth so large that you cannot see the edges of it."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Youngest fully trained babalawo in a generation--completed 15 years of study at age 30"*, *"Can recite 200 odu from memory, each with 800 verses"*, *"My opele chain was blessed by Orunmila (the orisha of divination) himself in a vision"*, *"Eshu visits my crossroads offerings personally, which is either an honor or a warning."*
- **Drive:** *"Understand the 'closing crossroads' prophecy before it is too late."*
- **Flaw:** *"I trust the oracle more than my own judgment, and the oracle is telling me something I do not want to hear."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 1 | Mind 4 | Presence 2

### Archetype 2: The Oshun Priestess of Osogbo
- **Concept:** The chief priestess of the Osun-Osogbo sacred grove, where the river itself is the orisha's living body. She has served Oshun for thirty years and embodies the orisha's dual nature: graceful diplomat and devastating force of nature. She has been called to Benin because the Oba (king) reports that the bronze sculptures in the palace are weeping.
- **Quote:** *"Oshun is honey, gold, and cool river water. Oshun is also the flood that drowns cities. People who only see the honey learn about the flood."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Thirty years as chief priestess of the Osun-Osogbo sacred grove"*, *"When I dance Oshun's dance, the river rises to greet me"*, *"I carry Oshun's brass fan, which can redirect spiritual currents like river water"*, *"Oshun speaks through me during ceremony. I say things I do not remember saying, and they are always true."*
- **Drive:** *"Heal the spiritual imbalance between Ogun and Oshun before it tears the cosmic fabric."*
- **Flaw:** *"Oshun's possession is becoming more frequent and harder to distinguish from my own thoughts."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

### Archetype 3: The Master Bronze-Caster of Benin
- **Concept:** The greatest living bronze-caster in the Igun-Eronmwon guild. His works are so precise that the orisha mistake them for living beings. He has been commissioned by the Oba to cast a bronze plaque depicting the balance between all the orisha, but every time he attempts the casting, the metal cracks at the point where Ogun and Oshun meet. Something in the spiritual world will not allow their reconciliation to be depicted.
- **Quote:** *"Bronze does not lie. When the metal cracks, it is telling you that the thing you are trying to capture does not exist yet. I cannot cast a reconciliation that has not happened."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Master bronze-caster whose works are so lifelike the orisha respond to them"*, *"My forge burns with fire given by Ogun himself--it melts any metal and burns no flesh"*, *"I once cast a portrait head of the Oba that blinked when the Oba blinked"*, *"The cracking bronze is telling me a story I do not want to hear."*
- **Drive:** *"Complete the Great Plaque: a bronze artwork that reconciles Ogun and Oshun, healing the cosmic rift."*
- **Flaw:** *"I am so absorbed in my craft that I fail to notice the human consequences of the spiritual crisis unfolding around me."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 1

## 6. Condition Palette

**Physical Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Crossroads Vertigo* (Eshu's presence causes disorientation), *Iron Burn* (from Ogun's forge-energy), *River Chill* (Oshun's water carries a cold blessing).
- **Moderate:** *Orisha Possession Aftershock* (physical exhaustion following divine possession), *Bronze Forge Scald*, *Sacred Grove Thorn Poison* (the grove's plants defend against intruders).
- **Severe:** *Ori Fracture* (the inner head is splitting--two destinies are competing for dominance), *Ogun's Mark* (the war orisha has claimed you for battle, and your body is hardening to iron), *River Drowning* (Oshun's waters are pulling you under--a warning, not an attack).

**Social Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Eshu's Miscommunication* (the trickster has garbled your message to another orisha), *Guild Rivalry* (another bronze-caster is undermining your work).
- **Moderate:** *Oba's Displeasure* (the king questions your spiritual authority), *Orisha Debt* (you promised an offering you have not delivered).
- **Severe:** *Ori Rejection* (your own destiny has turned against you--nothing you attempt succeeds), *Eshu's Full Attention* (the trickster is now personally invested in complicating your life).

**Psychological Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Odu Overload* (too many divination verses competing for attention in your mind), *Crossroads Anxiety* (you dread the choices that crossroads represent).
- **Moderate:** *Possession Blur* (difficulty distinguishing your thoughts from the orisha's), *Destiny Doubt* (questioning whether the destiny you chose before birth was the right one).
- **Severe:** *Ori Dissolution* (you no longer know who you are--the inner head is empty), *Eshu's Paradox* (the trickster has shown you that every possible choice leads to the same outcome, and the outcome is catastrophe).

## 7. Starter Scenario: The Weeping Bronzes

**Framing:** The bronze sculptures in the Oba of Benin's palace have begun to weep. Not metaphorically--actual tears of liquid bronze are running from the eyes of the portrait heads. Simultaneously, the Osun River is running backward, flowing upstream toward its source. And at every crossroads in the Yoruba city-states, Eshu's mounds are growing larger without anyone adding to them. The Babalawo's divination produces the same odu repeatedly: *Oyeku Meji*--the odu of darkness, endings, and transformation.

**3-Layer Scene Design:**

1. **Immediate Threat:** The weeping bronzes are not just crying; they are draining. The spiritual energy captured in the Benin bronzes over generations is leaking out, weakening the protective wards that have kept the kingdom safe. By nightfall, the wards will fail, and whatever has been kept out will enter.

2. **Ticking Clock:** The Osun River is almost dry. When it reaches its source and stops flowing, Oshun's connection to the mortal world will be severed. This will leave Ogun unopposed, and the war orisha without balance is pure destruction. 5 beats until the river dries.

3. **Opportunity:** Eshu has appeared at the main crossroads of Benin and offered a deal: he will restore the balance between Ogun and Oshun if someone can answer his riddle. The riddle is: "What do you sacrifice when you sacrifice nothing?" The answer is both philosophical and practical, and it determines the nature of the cosmic reconciliation.

**Antagonist:** ***Eshu Elegbara*** -- Not a villain but the divine trickster whose role is to ensure that every choice has consequences. He is not creating the crisis; he is ensuring that the crisis is resolved correctly. His riddle is genuine. His deal is fair. His laughter is because he already knows the answer and finds it funny that the humans do not.
- **Traits:** Omnipresent at crossroads, master of communication and miscommunication, cannot be fought (he is a force of nature), can be negotiated with (he respects wit), weakness to genuine humility (arrogance bores him), strength in paradox (he thrives on contradictions).
- **Condition Track:** ☐ Testing (observing the characters' approach) / ☐ Riddle Posed (the characters must solve it through roleplay, not rolling dice) / ☐ Answered Correctly (Eshu facilitates the reconciliation) / ☐ Answered Incorrectly (Eshu provides a different, harder path to reconciliation).

## 8. Why This Setting Matters

Yoruba cosmology is one of humanity's most sophisticated philosophical systems, yet it is dramatically underrepresented in tabletop RPGs. The Ifa divination system alone contains more coded information than many university libraries. The orisha are not simplistic deities but complex spiritual forces representing the fundamental tensions of human existence: war and love, order and chaos, destiny and free will. Eshu, the trickster at the crossroads, is one of the greatest literary characters in world mythology--a figure who makes sure that every choice matters and that complacency is impossible. This setting brings all of this richness to the gaming table with the respect it deserves.
