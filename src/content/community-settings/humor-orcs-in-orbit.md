## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The orcs are genuinely excellent at space exploration. They're also creating incidents at a rate that concerns mission command. These two facts are related.

The **Ironclaw Warband** operates by a code that is genuinely admirable in context: courage, honesty, directness, loyalty, and the conviction that strength is proven through action rather than claimed through status. In space, these qualities create a crew that will boldly go anywhere, refuse to lie even when lying would help, and absolutely will not back down from a genuine threat regardless of the threat's nature or the opinions of mission command.

The comedy emerges from the collision of honor culture with the diplomatic and political requirements of galactic relations, combined with the orcs' genuine excellence at the actual exploration work.

**Tone:** The orcs are played with complete respect and genuine affection. Their culture is not the joke -- the collision with other frameworks is the comedy, and the orcs are often right. The other species are also often right. The fun is in the negotiation between frameworks.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Ironclaw Warband in their section of the ship: their area is immaculate (they take care of things they are responsible for), their weapons maintained to a standard that impresses the ship's security team, their cultural markings displayed with pride, and one of them standing in full armor watching a sunrise on an alien world with what the other crew members are gradually understanding is a form of prayer.
2. **Sound:** The direct, unfiltered communication style of the orcs in contrast to the measured diplomatic language of the rest of the crew, the Warband's informal hierarchy established through brief, intense, completely open exchanges that other species find alarming and the orcs find efficient, and the very specific silence when Grak, the Warchief, is thinking, which the crew has learned means a significant statement is coming.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The physical culture of the orcs: their equipment is built for function and durability, their gestures are expansive and clear, their physical presence is substantial, and their cultural warmth (once you're in the inner circle) is expressed physically in ways the other species are learning to interpret correctly.

**The Core Tension:** The orcs' way works but doesn't translate. The diplomatic framework works but often requires compromise the orcs find dishonest. Neither is fully wrong.

## 2. The Mission

### The Ship: ISV Courage
A joint galactic cooperative exploration vessel with crew from five species. The Ironclaw Warband occupies the forward tactical section and has become informally responsible for first contact situations because, as the mission commander observed, "at least you know exactly what they're thinking."

### The Warband
- **Grak, Warchief:** The leader. Direct, honest to a fault, genuinely curious about the universe, extremely good at reading threats and people. Currently learning that diplomatic language is a type of weapon and is developing respect for it.
- **Korra, Lorekeeper:** The Warband's scholar and memory-keeper. Has become fascinated by xenoculture and is the bridge between the Warband and the rest of the crew. Her cultural translations are excellent and occasionally too accurate.
- **Rusk, Weaponmaster:** Responsible for tactical readiness. Has strong opinions about the crew's physical fitness and has started an unofficial training program that is voluntary-but-you'd-better-volunteer. Ship morale has improved.

### The Diplomatic Crisis (Ongoing)
The Warband's approach has created a formal diplomatic complaint from the Vellori Collective, requesting that orc crew members be prohibited from direct contact situations. Mission command must decide: support the complaint or defend the Warband's effectiveness. The orcs have been informed. They have opinions.

## 3. Adventure Seeds

1. **The Honest Negotiation:** A trade negotiation requires the players to secure terms with a civilization that negotiates exclusively through indirect implication and social inference. The orcs are terrible at this. They are also the most credible representatives because they physically cannot be deceptive. Somehow this becomes an advantage.

2. **The Challenge:** An alien species issues what the orcs recognize as a challenge to single combat. The diplomatic team insists this is a metaphor. The orcs insist it is not. Investigation reveals both are right, which creates a genuinely interesting challenge about what kind of response demonstrates strength in this cultural context.

3. **The Crew Member:** One crew member from another species has requested transfer to the Warband's section. The orcs are considering it. The admission criteria are clear: prove courage, prove honesty, prove loyalty. The admission process is also clear: it involves physical challenge. The crew member in question is the ship's doctor. This will be interesting.

4. **The Ancient Enemy:** Deep space exploration has brought the ship into contact with a species that the Warband's oral history identifies as an ancient enemy -- a conflict centuries old from their homeworld. The Warband's cultural obligation conflicts with the mission's diplomatic objectives. The crew must find a path that honors both.

5. **The First Contact:** A genuine first contact situation with a completely unknown species. The diplomatic team freezes. The orcs step forward. Their approach -- direct, honest, physically present, clearly neither afraid nor aggressive -- creates a first contact experience that the mission logs will study for generations.
