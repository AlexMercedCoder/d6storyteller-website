## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The greatest monsters in the world look exactly like whoever you trust most.

The world draws from the rich mythological landscape of the Hindu epics: a Bharata (ancient India) where dharmic order sustains civilization and the Rakshasas -- shapeshifting demons with immense power and intelligence -- represent the opposing force of adharma. This is not a simple good-vs-evil framing. The Rakshasas have courts, philosophies, and hierarchies. Some pursue adharma enthusiastically. Some are conflicted. A few have walked the path toward something resembling dharma.

The players operate in the forests of Kishkindha, the deep jungles of the Deccan, and the courts of ancient kingdoms, protecting the mortal world from infiltration while navigating the complex politics of both human and supernatural factions.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** Ancient stone temples rising from jungle that has grown around them over centuries, their carved figures illuminated by oil lamp light that makes the carved figures seem to move; a marketplace where one vendor sells exactly the same goods every day from memory, because the Rakshasa wearing the vendor's face remembered everything except the subtle asymmetry of the original's smile; a Vanara patrol moving through the upper canopy with the silence of beings for whom height is home.
2. **Sound:** The Sanskrit incantations of the Brahmin who travels with the party, a sound that makes Rakshasas uncomfortable without revealing them; the drums and conches of a kingdom's army on the march; and the very specific sound of a person who is almost but not quite right -- a hesitation in speech, a phrase remembered wrong, a laugh a half-breath off.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The heat of the Deccan plateau, the humidity of the ancient forest, the smell of marigolds and incense at a roadside shrine, and the cold of a place where a Rakshasa has been for too long -- something in the air that is wrong before the mind can name it.

## 2. Major Factions

### The Vanara of Kishkindha
Ancient forest people with monkey characteristics: quick, clever, loyal, and fiercely protective of the territories they guard. The Vanara have fought Rakshasas longer than human civilization has kept records of it and have developed detection techniques no human sage has been able to fully learn.

### The Brahmin Sages
Holders of the Vedic tradition, navigators of the dharmic order, and the world's most reliable source of information about Rakshasa nature and vulnerability. Their knowledge is encyclopedic; their courage is variable.

### The Rakshasa Courts
Hierarchical supernatural courts with their own politics, ambitions, and internal conflicts. Not all Rakshasas serve the same master or pursue the same goals. Some want to rule; some want to eat; some, strangest of all, are looking for something else.

### The Dharmic Warriors
Human fighters who have bound themselves to sacred duty, earning divine protection against Rakshasa deception through absolute commitment to their code. The protection is real. The cost is also real.

## 3. Adventure Seeds

1. **The Infiltrated Court:** A king's court has been penetrated. One advisor is a Rakshasa. Which one? All of them are behaving suspiciously, because they know there's a Rakshasa among them and nobody trusts anyone. The players must identify the impersonator without destroying the court's function.

2. **The Willing Sacrifice:** A Rakshasa is willing to help the players destroy a rival Rakshasa court -- for reasons that may be self-interested or may be something more complicated. Can a deal with a demon be honored? Can a demon honor a deal?

3. **The Ancient Temple:** An ancient temple in the deep jungle has been occupied by a Rakshasa for so long that the local community has begun to worship it as a deity. The "deity" is not malicious -- it has been performing the role for 200 years and has become attached to the community it protects. This situation has no clean solution.

4. **The Shapeshifter Child:** A Rakshasa who was defeated in combat left behind a child -- half Rakshasa, raised by humans, struggling with a nature they didn't choose. The players must help them navigate what they are, what their dharma demands, and whether dharma applies equally to all beings.

5. **The Final Illusion:** The Rakshasa Lord has constructed an illusion so perfect that it has become real -- an entire city that does not exist but which thousands of people live in and cannot leave. Unmaking the illusion will destroy the city. Not unmaking it will allow the Rakshasa to drain the life of its inhabitants indefinitely.
