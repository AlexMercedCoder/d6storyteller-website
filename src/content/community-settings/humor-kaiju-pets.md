## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The kaiju were domesticated. Mostly. The licensing is comprehensive. Mostly. The kaiju are very large. Definitely.

The world of **Catastrophic Critters** takes place roughly 80 years after the Great Kaiju Accords, which established that kaiju were sentient, domesticable, and technically covered under expanded species protection laws. The Miniaturized Companion Licensing Authority (MCLA) has since developed a comprehensive framework for regulated kaiju ownership in urban environments, using size-reduction technology to bring kaiju down to a legally manageable (if still enormous) scale.

The system works. Most of the time. The comedy emerges from the gap between "legally manageable" and "practically manageable" when your pet can accidentally demolish infrastructure by sitting in the wrong place.

**Tone:** This is animal control/veterinary comedy scaled to city-block dimensions. The kaiju are affectionate, intelligent, and genuinely loving toward their owners. They are also very large and occasionally destructive in the way any large animal is occasionally destructive, just at a different scale of consequence.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** A city that has been significantly redesigned to accommodate licensed kaiju residents: reinforced parkland designated for kaiju recreation, extra-wide roads with kaiju-traffic lanes, buildings with structural ratings that include "kaiju adjacency tolerance," and one enormous, gentle, well-socialized kaiju sitting in a designated park zone while children play nearby with the practiced ease of residents who have normalized this.
2. **Sound:** The subsonic rumble of a well-fed, content kaiju at rest, the specific alarm tone that indicates a kaiju has wandered outside its permitted zone, a MCLA officer communicating with a kaiju in the simplified gesture-and-tone language developed for human-kaiju communication, and the different sound of a kaiju that is distressed (significantly louder; structure-damaging at close range).
3. **Touch & Smell:** The odd weather effects of large kaiju body heat on their immediate environment, the very specific smell of kaiju (large animal smell at enormous scale; the city has adapted), the physical scale difference in all MCLA equipment designed for both human operation and kaiju interaction, and the warmth radiating from a kaiju whose resting body temperature is slightly above ambient.

**The Core Tension:** Kaiju are people-ish. Their size makes their feelings dangerous. Protecting the city from kaiju behavior is also protecting kaiju from consequences of their own emotions.

## 2. The MCLA Structure

### Field Officers
The people who go talk to kaiju and their owners on-site. The job requires equal skill in human communication and kaiju communication. Officers who are good at both are rare and well-compensated. Officers who are good at one but not the other create different problems.

### The Welfare Division
Kaiju welfare officers who assess whether licensed kaiju are being responsibly cared for. They have the authority to remove kaiju from negligent owners. Executing a kaiju removal order is a significant logistical event that requires substantial advance planning.

### The Veterinary Corps
Kaiju veterinarians. The technical challenges are immense (large scale, exotic biology) and the emotional rewards are significant (working with intelligent giant creatures who appreciate care). The MCLA's head veterinarian has been practicing for 30 years and maintains that kaiju patients are more cooperative than most human patients.

### The Licensing Division
Processes ownership applications, conducts home assessments (checking whether a property can safely accommodate kaiju ownership), and issues violation notices. The licensing division is the part of the MCLA most people interact with most often and most frustratingly.

## 3. Adventure Seeds

1. **The Unlicensed Operation:** Reports of an unlicensed kaiju in the warehouse district. Investigation reveals not one but a family of unlicensed kaiju whose previous owner lost their license and hid the kaiju rather than surrender them. They're healthy, well-bonded, and hiding in an area increasingly unable to contain them.

2. **The Expansion Crisis:** Something is causing the size-reduction effects to fail. Kaiju across the metro area are gradually growing. The cause is the unlicensed size-reduction spells mentioned in the premise. Locating the source requires investigation. Stopping the expansion requires urgency. Several kaiju are approaching legally non-manageable size.

3. **The Welfare Case:** A kaiju is showing signs of neglect: decreased activity, changed coloring, behavioral regression. The owners maintain everything is fine. MCLA investigation requires establishing cause of distress and either resolving the owner situation or preparing for removal. The kaiju is aware of the investigation and is trying to communicate something.

4. **The New Species:** A kaiju of a previously undocumented type has appeared in the city. It doesn't match any known classification. The MCLA's response protocols are calibrated for known species. The new kaiju is not exhibiting threatening behavior but also isn't exhibiting any known behavioral pattern. First contact with a new type requires improvisation within regulatory framework.

5. **The Public Incident:** A kaiju has made a significant infrastructure error (sat on something important; stepped through a building during a panic response). Nobody was hurt. The kaiju is distressed about what happened. The city council is holding hearings on kaiju licensing restrictions. MCLA must defend the program while investigating what triggered the incident.
