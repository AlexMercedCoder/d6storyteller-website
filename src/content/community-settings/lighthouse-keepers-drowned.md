## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

The Drowned Coast was once a prosperous shoreline: fishing villages, port towns, and a merchant fleet that traded with the inland kingdoms. The flooding began six years ago. The sea advances each night, testing the boundary that the lighthouses maintain. In the morning, it retreats, leaving salt deposits, waterlogged structures, and the specific damage of a tide that does not follow lunar cycles. The lighthouses were discovered, not built. They were already standing when the flooding began, ancient structures whose lights activate at dusk and shut off at dawn, holding a line that the water cannot cross as long as the beam holds.

The keepers are the people who chose to stay. They maintain the lights, fuel the beams, and repair equipment that is older than any living person understands. The work is lonely, repetitive, and essential. If the lights go out, the coast disappears.

**Tone:** Gothic, quiet, and weighted with responsibility. The setting is about people who hold a line against something that is patient, vast, and personally uninterested in their survival. The horror is not the water itself, which is indifferent. The horror is the isolation, the dwindling resources, and the certainty that the lighthouses will eventually fail unless someone figures out why the sea is rising.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** A lighthouse beam sweeping across dark water, the line it draws on the surface as sharp as a wall. The sea beyond the beam: black, still, and deeper than it should be, as if the ocean has gained vertical depth as well as horizontal reach. The coast at dawn: salt-crusted buildings, waterlines on walls, and the specific gray of a landscape that spends every night underwater. The Drowned Zones where lighthouses have failed: seamless ocean where towns used to stand, with no wreckage visible.
2. **Sound:** The slap of rising water against a seawall at dusk, growing louder as the tide tests the beam. The hum of a lighthouse mechanism, which is not mechanical but resonant, as if the structure itself is producing the sound. The silence after a lighthouse goes dark, which is the absence of the hum and the arrival of the water, both at once. The creak of a keeper's quarters in a storm, high above the waterline, swaying slightly in wind that carries salt and the smell of deep ocean.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The cold spray that reaches the lighthouse gallery on rough nights. The grit of salt on every surface, in clothing, in food, in hair. The warmth of the lighthouse mechanism room, which is the only reliably warm space on the coast because the light generates heat as a byproduct. The smell of the Drowned Coast: brine, wet stone, rotting seaweed, and the deep-ocean smell that rises from the advancing water, which is older and colder than ordinary salt air.

## 2. The Coast

**The Seventh Light** is the setting's primary lighthouse: a tower of dark stone, 120 feet tall, standing on a promontory that the sea cannot reach as long as the beam holds. Keeper Ashwin has maintained the Seventh for two years, replacing the previous keeper who left after the Sixth went dark. The light mechanism is ancient, complex, and partially incomprehensible. Ashwin maintains it through observation, careful notes, and the specific kind of repair that comes from treating a machine as if it has preferences.

**Harbor's Rest** is the largest remaining settlement on the coast: a fishing town of 400 people whose economy has shifted from commerce to survival. The town sits between the Seventh and Eighth Lights, protected by both beams. Its mayor, Elena Cross, organizes fuel supply, coordinates with the inland kingdoms for provisions, and manages a population that is divided between staying and evacuating.

**The Salt Road** connects the remaining lighthouses along the coast. It is the keepers' lifeline: a path used for fuel delivery, message relay, and emergency transit. The road floods every night and must be cleared every morning. Salt damage makes maintenance a constant task. The road crew, a team of ten, is the most physically demanding job on the coast.

**The Drowned Zones** are the areas where lighthouses have failed. They are ocean now: deep, still, and featureless. Sailors who have crossed the Drowned Zones report that the water is significantly deeper than the original coastal bathymetry would allow. Something is not just flooding the coast. Something is replacing it.

## 3. Factions

**The Keepers' Watch** is the informal organization that coordinates the twelve remaining lighthouses. Watch Commander Ashwin (who holds both titles because nobody else volunteered) distributes fuel, shares maintenance notes, and makes resource allocation decisions that determine which lighthouses survive. The Watch has no formal authority. Its power comes from the fact that without it, the lights go out.

**The Harbor Council** represents the coastal settlements. Mayor Cross leads the Council and manages the tension between residents who want to stay and defend their homes and residents who want to evacuate while the Salt Road is still passable. The Council's resources are thin: food from the inland kingdoms, fuel from diminishing coastal reserves, and labor from a population that is losing people to evacuation every month.

**The Inland Kingdoms** provide supplies to the coast but are increasingly reluctant to invest in what they consider a lost cause. Ambassador Keller has been stationed at Harbor's Rest to assess whether continued support is justified. Her preliminary report recommends evacuation. She has not submitted it because she has spent enough time at the Seventh Light to understand what will be lost if the coast is abandoned.

**The Deep Watchers** are a small group of former sailors and fishers who believe the rising sea is not a natural phenomenon but an intentional act. They have been diving in the shallow waters near the lighthouses and have found structures beneath the surface: not human structures, but organized formations of stone and coral that match patterns in the lighthouse architecture. The Watchers believe the lighthouses were built by whatever is under the water, which raises a question nobody has answered: why would the sea build its own boundary?

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Light Integrity

Each lighthouse maintains **Light Integrity** from 0 to 6, starting at 4.

**Natural decay:** Light Integrity decreases by 1 at the start of each session (fuel depletion, equipment wear).

**Raising Integrity (+1 each):**
- Successful fuel resupply run
- Maintenance that repairs or improves the mechanism
- Community support (Harbor providing labor, supplies, or morale)
- Discovery about the lighthouse's original function (understanding the technology improves maintenance)

**Lowering Integrity (-1 each, in addition to natural decay):**
- Storm damage
- Sabotage or neglect
- Supply line disruption (Salt Road blockage, inland supply cut)
- Attempting to modify the mechanism without understanding it

**Thresholds:**
| Integrity | Lighthouse Status |
|-----------|-------------------|
| 5-6 | Strong beam. The water stays well back. Safe working conditions. |
| 3-4 | Functional. The water approaches at night but does not breach the line. |
| 1-2 | Flickering. The waterline pushes past the beam during storms. Salt damage increases. |
| 0 | Dark. The lighthouse fails. The water claims the territory permanently. |

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Lighthouse Keeper

You maintain the Seventh Light. You check the mechanism at dawn and dusk, manage the fuel supply, and repair equipment you do not fully understand using notes left by keepers who came before you. You chose this work because someone had to. You stay because if you leave, the light goes out, and if the light goes out, Harbor's Rest goes underwater.

- **Drive:** *"Keep the light burning long enough for someone to figure out why the sea is rising, and hope the answer is not what the Deep Watchers think it is."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 1

### The Salt Road Runner

You maintain the road between lighthouses. You clear salt deposits, repair washed-out sections, and deliver fuel and messages to keepers who have not spoken to another person in weeks. You are the coast's circulatory system. Without you, the lighthouses are isolated and the keepers are alone. The work is exhausting, dangerous, and necessary. You have not taken a day off in four months.

- **Drive:** *"Keep the road open. If the lighthouses cannot talk to each other, they go dark one at a time."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 3 | Finesse 2 | Mind 1 | Presence 2

### The Deep Watcher

You dive in the shallow waters near the lighthouses and you have found things that should not be there: stone formations matching lighthouse architecture, coral patterns that look like circuitry, and a carved inscription on the seabed that you cannot read but that matches markings inside the Seventh Light's mechanism room. You believe the lighthouses were built by whatever lives beneath the rising sea, and the boundary they maintain is not a defense. It is an agreement.

- **Drive:** *"Understand the relationship between the lighthouses and the deep before one side breaks the arrangement the other does not know it made."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The Eighth Light:** The Eighth Lighthouse, three miles north of Harbor's Rest, has been flickering for a week. Its keeper has not responded to messages. The Salt Road to the Eighth was damaged in last night's tide. Ambassador Keller has informed the Harbor Council that the inland kingdoms will not fund a rescue mission for a single lighthouse. The Deep Watchers have reported unusual activity in the water near the Eighth: lights beneath the surface, moving in patterns that match the lighthouse beam's sweep. The characters must reach the Eighth Light, determine whether it can be saved, and decide what to do if the keeper found something in the mechanism room that changes what the lighthouses are for.

**Why It Matters:** The Lighthouse Keepers asks what it costs to hold a line against something larger than yourself. The keepers are not heroes in the traditional sense. They are maintenance workers, laborers, and stubborn people who refuse to let the dark water win. The setting is about institutional neglect, community survival, and the specific kind of courage that shows up at dawn every morning to check a machine you do not understand because if you do not, your neighbors lose their homes. The deeper mystery, what the lighthouses are and why the sea built them, is a question the characters may answer. The daily work of keeping the lights on does not wait for the answer.
