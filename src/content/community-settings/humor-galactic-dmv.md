## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** Somewhere between galaxies, on a station large enough to have its own weather system, the **Interstellar Bureaucratic Authority** processes the paperwork that keeps 847 civilizations technically legal. They have been doing this for 3,000 years. The queue has never been empty. Management remains optimistic.

The IBA is not evil. It is not even particularly incompetent by the standards of agencies managing thousands of civilizations simultaneously. It is simply an organization that has grown large enough that it operates on its own institutional logic, largely independent of any individual civilization's expectations of how things should work.

The players are IBA employees at various levels: counter staff who deal directly with bewildered aliens, field agents who go out into the galaxy to collect required documentation from civilizations that don't know they need to file, and senior processors who have been working on specific cases for decades.

**Tone:** This is *Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy* meets *Parks and Recreation*. The universe is genuinely wonderful and weird. The bureaucracy is genuinely sincere about helping but genuinely constrained by its own rules. The comedy comes from collision of cosmic scale with institutional smallness.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** A waiting room the size of a stadium filled with beings of every conceivable shape and biology, numbered tickets floating in species-appropriate formats (light pulses for phototropic organisms, chemical signatures for chemosensory species), counter staff wearing uniforms designed to be non-threatening to 847 different species simultaneously (they achieved this by being slightly awkward for everyone equally), and an "NOW SERVING" display that operates in 23 star system time zones.
2. **Sound:** The overlapping translation drones of hundreds of simultaneous conversations in dozens of languages, the mechanical thunk of a stamp that has been stamping forms for 200 years without maintenance, occasional sirens from Queue Management when an entity's patience reaches a critical threshold, and the very specific silence of a counter window marked "CLOSED" that has been closed for six years.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The regulated IBA atmospheric compromise (comfortable for no species, tolerable for most), the papery texture of Physical Form Substrate that IBA uses for species that cannot interface with digital documentation, and the faint smell of something burning in the copy room that maintenance has been notified about.

**The Core Tension:** The players genuinely want to help. The rules genuinely exist for reasons. These two facts are in constant conflict.

## 2. The IBA Structure

### Counter Operations
Front-line form intake. The most grueling job in the galaxy because it requires maintaining patience while beings who have traveled 40 light-years express frustration that Form 22-B requires notarization from a government that no longer exists.

### Field Operations
The glamorous-sounding division that involves traveling to civilizations and collecting required documentation. Less glamorous in practice: collecting required documentation from a civilization that considers paperwork a philosophical affront.

### Appeals & Exceptions
The division that reviews cases where standard processing has failed. They have the authority to grant exceptions. They use this authority extremely rarely. The current backlog of exception requests is 900 years.

### The Directors
Career IBA bureaucrats who have forgotten what any of the forms actually accomplish and now focus entirely on process compliance. They mean well. They are also the source of most policies that make the work harder.

## 3. Adventure Seeds

1. **The Sentient Planet Problem:** A gas giant in Sector 7 has achieved consciousness and wants to file for sentient species recognition. The form requires a physical address. A mailing address for a gas giant has never been established. Someone needs to figure out Form 7-GA-R Article 12, Addendum 3.

2. **The Lost Paperwork:** A civilization's original sentience documentation was lost 400 years ago when the office handling it underwent restructuring. They've been filing for a re-recognition ever since. The players discover the paperwork wasn't lost -- it was processed and then filed in the wrong section, which has since been decommissioned.

3. **The Audit:** The IBA is receiving an efficiency audit by an independent galactic commission. The players must either make the operation look functional (difficult) or help the auditors understand what functional means at this scale (more difficult).

4. **The Deadline:** An application expires in 48 hours and the applicant has waited 200 years in queue. The application, if approved, prevents a war. Getting it approved requires navigating seven approval stages in 48 hours. Normal processing time per stage: two years.

5. **The New Policy:** A director has implemented Form Revision 447-B which changes the format of core documentation. Retroactively. For all existing applications. Every case in the system is now non-compliant.
