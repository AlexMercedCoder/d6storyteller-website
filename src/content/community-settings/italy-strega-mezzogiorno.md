## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** Southern Italy's folk traditions preserve one of Europe's most vibrant and least understood magical systems. The **strega** (wise woman/folk witch) is the community's unofficial healer, counselor, and spiritual defender. The **malocchio** (evil eye) is a genuine cultural belief with elaborate protective and curative traditions involving olive oil, water, salt, and prayer. **Tarantism** was a documented phenomenon in Puglia from the 15th to 18th centuries: women (primarily) would fall into trance states attributed to spider bites and could only be cured through the frenzied **tarantella** dance, sometimes lasting days.

In this setting, all of it is real. The malocchio is a transmissible spiritual curse. The tarantella is a genuine exorcism. The spiders are not physical but spiritual entities emerging from the ruins of Magna Graecia (the ancient Greek colonies of southern Italy), where pre-Olympian Chthonic forces still slumber.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** Sun-blasted Puglian landscapes of silver olive groves and red earth; the frenzied beauty of the tarantella danced in a circle of musicians; the blue malocchio eye amulet (*cornicello*) hanging from every doorway.
2. **Sound:** The driving rhythm of the tarantella's tambourine and fiddle; the whispered prayers of the strega performing the oil-and-water divination to detect malocchio; cicadas screaming in summer heat.
3. **Touch & Smell:** Scorching Mediterranean sun on bare shoulders; the herbal scent of rosemary, basil, and rue (the strega's primary protective herbs); warm olive oil dripped into blessed water as the malocchio diagnostic tool.

## 2-3. Historical Context & Factions

**Tarantism** is documented in medical and ethnographic literature. Anthropologist Ernesto de Martino's landmark 1961 study *The Land of Remorse* treated it as a culturally legitimate healing practice rather than superstition.

**Factions:** The Streghe Circle (folk healers), the Tarantate (the bitten women who become channels for the Chthonic energy), and the Magna Graecia Scholars (researchers investigating the ancient Greek ruins).

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Malocchio (Evil Eye)

- **1-2 (Protected):** The character is spiritually shielded. +1d6 to Presence. Cannot be cursed. Cannot perceive curses on others.
- **3-4 (Exposed):** The character's protection has weakened. They can perceive Malocchio on others (visible as a sickly olive-green aura). Can perform basic protective rituals. **Risk:** They are now vulnerable to receiving the curse.
- **5-6 (Cursed):** The character radiates bad fortune. -1d6 to all rolls. Everything they touch spoils, breaks, or sours. The curse spreads to people who spend time near them. +1d6 to Mind (the curse sharpens perception of spiritual threats as compensation). **Cure:** The tarantella (danced to exhaustion) or the strega's oil-and-water ritual (performed by someone who cares about the cursed person).

## 6-7. Conditions & Starter Scenario

**Conditions include:** *Spider-Bitten* (uncontrollable dancing), *Malocchio Cursed*, *Tarantella Exhaustion*, *Olive Oil Divination Shock* (seeing the truth of a curse), *Community Exile* (accused of witchcraft), *Magna Graecia Awakening* (ancient spirits speaking through you).

**Starter Scenario: The Summer of the Spider.** An entire village's olive harvest is failing. Three women have been bitten. The priest is calling for an exorcism. The strega knows the bites are coming from beneath the ruins of a Greek temple to Dionysus, where the earth is cracking open. The tarantata must dance to close the crack. The musician must play for as long as it takes.

## 8. Why This Setting Matters

Southern Italian folk magic is a living tradition practiced by millions of Italian Americans and southern Italians today. The malocchio, the cornicello, the strega's oil-and-water diagnostic-these are not historical artifacts but active cultural practices. This setting honors them as a sophisticated spiritual system, born from centuries of resilience in a region that has been conquered, exploited, and dismissed by northern elites. The strega's power comes not from books or gods but from community knowledge passed from grandmother to granddaughter.
