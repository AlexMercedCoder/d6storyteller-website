## 1. Setting Overview and Tone

The Korrath Shipping Lane is a narrow corridor of navigable water between two island chains, connecting the fertile southern trade ports to the industrialized north. Without the Tidecallers' management, the lane would be impassable for six months of the year: the reef systems are intricate and shift with seasonal pressure changes, and the storm patterns that funnel through the corridor can sink a three-masted trader in under an hour.

The witches are not a government. They have no written law, no standing army, and no territorial claim that any empire would recognize on paper. What they have is three centuries of institutional knowledge about how the lane actually works, and the ability to make the crossing safe or lethal on any given morning.

**Tone:** Political, atmospheric, and grounded in the specific practicality of maritime life. This is not a setting about dramatic magic battles. It is about governance, leverage, and the gap between official authority and functional authority.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The lane at dawn: green water over reef shallows, the peculiar bioluminescent tide that the Tidecallers have cultivated as a navigational marker, and the shapes of merchant hulls queuing outside the passage point, waiting for the day's clearance signal. The witches' cliff stations: stone buildings with signal fires, sheltered on the island faces that catch the western wind.
2. **Sound:** The sound of the lane in good weather: water and rigging. In bad weather: the lane produces a harmonic resonance as wind moves through the reef formations, a deep chord that experienced sailors read like a barometer. The Tidecallers use specific whistle codes to signal the daily reef positions to passing ships.
3. **Touch and Smell:** The lane's salt is heavier than open ocean salt, concentrated by the reef system. Sailors who have crossed the Korrath many times can smell their position by the salt weight in the air. The Tidecallers' hands are permanently roughened by the gesture-work of weather management, a specific callusing pattern that merchants recognize and trust.

## 2. The Lane

**The Passage Point** is the northern entry to the Korrath: a quarter-mile gap between the outermost reef islands where ships must single-file through. The Tidecallers maintain a signal station on each island, and clearance is given by a pattern of lights visible in any weather. Ships that enter without clearance are not attacked; the reefs simply become less predictable.

**The Tithe Anchorage** is a protected harbor halfway through the lane where merchant ships stop to pay crossing fees. The payment is assessed by a Tidecaller factor who boards, inventories the cargo, and takes a percentage in goods. The system is efficient and has been calibrated over generations: merchants consider the tithe fair because the alternative (navigating without Tidecaller guidance) is worse.

**The Reef Interior** is the maze of shallow water and coral formation that fills most of the lane's width. The Tidecallers have memorized its structure across seasonal variations. The empire's naval charts show a simplified version that was accurate in 1803 and has not been updated since.

**The Southern Council Chamber** is where the Tidecallers hold their confederation meetings: a stone room on the largest island, open to the weather on one side, with charts covering the floor rather than the walls. The chamber has no permanent leader. Decisions are made by consensus among the twelve senior Tidecallers.

## 3. Factions

**The Tidecallers** are twelve senior witches and approximately forty junior practitioners distributed across the lane's island stations. They are not unified in opinion about the empire: some want to fight, some want to negotiate passage rights that protect their autonomy, and at least two are willing to discuss an arrangement with the empire in exchange for personal guarantees. Senior Tidecaller Maren Sull holds the confederation together through force of personality and the practical reality that she knows more about the lane's deep reef system than anyone alive.

**The Korrath Merchant Consortium** represents the traders who depend on the lane. The Consortium's factor, Oren Talle, has been crossing the Korrath for twenty years and trusts the Tidecallers' system more than he trusts any navy. He is quietly funding Tidecaller resistance because disrupted crossings cost him money. He is not an idealist. He is a realist who has done the math.

**The Imperial Squadron** is five warships under Commodore Brix, tasked with establishing free passage for all vessels. Brix is a competent officer who has been given an objective without adequate intelligence about the lane's actual navigational complexity. He is not cruel, but he is committed to his orders, and his charts are wrong in ways that will become apparent when he tries to navigate without the Tidecallers' help.

**The Rogue Pilots** are a group of former Tidecaller apprentices who were expelled from the confederation and who have been selling lane knowledge to imperial surveyors. Their leader, Dora Crane, knows enough about the lane to make the imperial charts 60% accurate, which is enough to navigate in good weather and fatal in bad weather. She does not believe this qualifies as betrayal because she believes the confederation expelled her unfairly.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Tide Favor

Track **Tide Favor** from 0 to 6, starting at 4.

**Raising Favor (+1 each):**
- Perform a major weather working on the lane's behalf
- Successfully negotiate with a new merchant fleet for tithe cooperation
- Restore a damaged reef marker or signal station
- Retrieve accurate seasonal reef data from the deep passages

**Lowering Favor (-1 each):**
- A Tidecaller is captured, expelled, or defects to the imperial side
- The empire successfully charts a safe passage route without Tidecaller guidance
- A tithe dispute results in a merchant fleet bypassing the lane or supporting imperial action
- A storm working fails publicly during an imperial approach

**Thresholds:**
| Favor | Lane Status |
|-------|-------------|
| 5-6 | Full control. Weather is cooperative. Reef positions are stable. The empire cannot navigate without Tidecaller assistance. |
| 3-4 | Contested. The lane is navigable with care. The empire makes slow progress but faces unexpected complications. |
| 1-2 | Slipping. The imperial charts are proving adequate. The Tidecallers' exclusivity is eroding. |
| 0 | Lost. The empire controls the passage point. The Tidecallers are reduced to resistance operations from the interior islands. |

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Senior Tidecaller

You have managed the lane's weather patterns for thirty years. You know every seasonal reef shift, every harmonic resonance pattern, and every merchant captain's preferred route through the inner passages. The empire's arrival is not frightening to you. What is frightening is the confederation's internal division, because three of your twelve are considering compromise and you need nine for a consensus decision.

- **Drive:** *"Keep the confederation united through the imperial pressure, and find a resolution that preserves the lane's management without requiring a fight the witches cannot win militarily."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

### The Merchant Factor

You are Oren Talle. You have crossed the Korrath 200 times. You have never lost a ship on the lane because you have always paid the tithe and respected the clearance signals. The empire's intervention threatens the most reliable shipping route on the chart, and you are using your considerable commercial relationships to build a merchant coalition that can apply diplomatic pressure on the imperial government without firing a shot.

- **Drive:** *"Protect the Tidecaller arrangement through commercial and political pressure before the imperial squadron's bad charts get someone killed on the reef."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### The Expelled Apprentice

You were removed from the confederation three years ago over a dispute that you believe was political rather than professional. You have been selling your knowledge to imperial surveyors because you need income and because you believe the confederation's monopoly on the lane is itself the problem. You are correct that the confederation has governance flaws. You are wrong about what happens to the lane when the empire takes over.

- **Drive:** *"Prove that the confederation's monopoly was the problem, not the solution, while keeping enough lane knowledge back that the empire can't navigate without needing you."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

## 6. Starter Scenario and Why It Matters

**The Survey Ship:** The imperial squadron has sent a single survey vessel into the lane with a Rogue Pilot navigator and orders to chart the deep passage. If the survey completes successfully, the empire will have enough data to attempt an unguided crossing within the month. The Tidecaller confederation is split: Maren Sull wants to engineer a navigational emergency that forces the survey to abort without casualties, demonstrating the lane's danger without provoking a military response. Two of the twelve want to simply sink the survey ship. Three want to open negotiations. The characters must find a way to abort the survey, manage the confederation's internal fractures, and create a diplomatic opening before the Commodore runs out of patience and commits the full squadron.

**Why It Matters:** Witches of the Shipping Lane is about functional authority versus official authority. The Tidecallers have governed the lane effectively for three centuries without any legal recognition. The empire's claim is legal and hollow: they can declare the lane free territory, but they cannot make it safe without the knowledge that the witches hold. The setting is about the difference between owning something on paper and being able to actually operate it.
