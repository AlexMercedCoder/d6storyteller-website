## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The celestial bureaucracy is meticulous, authoritative, and completely unable to deal with chaotic injustice. The Dokkaebi are chaotic, informal, and extremely motivated by injustice. This is the partnership.

Korean Dokkaebi tradition is genuinely distinctive: these are spirits born from inanimate objects that have accumulated human feeling over long use -- a worn broom, an old club, a tool that has been the companion of generations. They emerge with the energy of that accumulated human contact and are deeply invested in human affairs as a result. They are neither fully good nor fully evil, but they have strong opinions about fairness and are willing to act on those opinions dramatically.

The celestial framework draws on Korean spiritual tradition's parallel administrative structure: a heaven that governs through formal records and careful procedure, which cannot always match the needs of a world where injustice doesn't follow protocol.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Three Kingdoms landscape -- Goguryeo's northern mountains, Silla's southern plains, Baekje's western coast -- with its layered natural and spiritual geography; Dokkaebi in their various sizes and specializations, each carrying a club that represents their particular type of power, their appearance distinctive and somewhat alarming to those who don't know them; and celestial officials in their formal robes, absolutely in their own jurisdiction, and absolutely helpless in the Dokkaebi's.
2. **Sound:** The characteristic sound of Dokkaebi activity: they are not subtle, they enjoy music and noise, and their approach is often heralded by the sound of things happening faster and more chaotically than they should; the formal language of celestial procedure; and the particular sound of a Dokkaebi wrestling match, which is how they prefer to resolve most disputes.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The physical reality of the Three Kingdoms period: the smell of pine forests, the cold of northern mountains, the warmth of traditional architecture; the physical presence of a Dokkaebi, who are more solid than most spirits and make their presence known through contact; and the formal coolness of celestial documents that are always exactly the right temperature, which is unusual.

## 2. The Partnership Structure

### The Bureau Officials
Celestial administrators who prefer paperwork, prefer following procedure, and prefer sending the Dokkaebi to situations that would require them to bend the rules. They maintain the Bureau's official position that the Dokkaebi are informal assets rather than official agents. This is technically true and practically a distinction without a difference.

### The Dokkaebi Network
A loose community of Dokkaebi across the Three Kingdoms, each with their own history (the object they emerged from, the human relationships that shaped them), their own specialty, and their own approach to justice. They communicate through means that the Bureau does not fully understand and has decided not to investigate.

### The Human Element
The human communities where injustice occurs and where the Dokkaebi and Bureau must operate. Most humans cannot see the spirit-world dimension of their problems. Some can. Those who can are valuable to the partnership and endangered by that value.

### The Opposition
Forces that benefit from injustice continuing: corrupt officials protected by the procedural structure, spirits that feed on imbalance, and the occasional genuine supernatural actor who has found the loopholes in both earthly and celestial law.

## 3. Adventure Seeds

1. **The Unjust Verdict:** A court has ruled against someone through corruption, and the situation is technically legal under earthly law. The Dokkaebi know it's wrong and want to act. The Bureau knows it's wrong and cannot act through official channels. Working together to achieve just outcomes without technically violating the law requires creative interpretation of both earthly and celestial codes.

2. **The Dokkaebi Origin:** A specific Dokkaebi traces back to an object involved in an unresolved historical event. The object's history explains why the Dokkaebi is drawn to a particular type of injustice and suggests a specific resolution that would allow the spirit to rest. Following the history back requires both physical investigation and spirit-world navigation.

3. **The Bureau's Test:** The celestial Bureau is conducting an official evaluation of the Dokkaebi partnership's effectiveness. The evaluators are formal officials who deeply disapprove of the informality. The Dokkaebi are responding to this evaluation in characteristic fashion (enthusiastically and chaotically). The players must help the partnership look effective without preventing the Dokkaebi from being exactly what they are.

4. **The Wrestling Challenge:** A powerful local spirit has challenged the Dokkaebi to a wrestling match. The winner gains authority over a disputed spiritual territory. The Dokkaebi have accepted. The match is real and the stakes are real, but the preparation and the challenge itself reveal that the opposing spirit is not what it appears.

5. **The Dokkaebi War:** Two factions of Dokkaebi have developed a dispute about a justice question -- not about how to achieve justice, but about what justice means in a specific complicated situation. Both sides are sincere. Both are also escalating. The players must mediate between beings whose method of argument is physically intense and whose feelings run very deep.
