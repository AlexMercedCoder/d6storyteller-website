> *"The message from Kepler-442b is clear: 'Landing denied. Colony at capacity. Do not approach.' We are 14 light-years from Earth, carrying 12,000 people who sold everything they owned to be here, and our fuel margin is exactly zero. I need options that are not on this list because this list has one item and it says 'die.'"*
> - Captain Noor Hadid, emergency log, Day 1

--

## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The Sleep Fleet is a survival negotiation story set inside a generation ship. The characters are a skeleton crew awakened three months early by an automated emergency protocol. They are not commanders or heroes. They are the six people whose skill profiles matched the crisis: a diplomat, an engineer, a doctor, a navigator, a lawyer, and a supply officer. They have three months of approach time, a ship full of unconscious passengers, and a destination that has told them to turn around.

**Tone:** Pressured, intimate, and ethically heavy. Six people making decisions for 12,000 who cannot consent. The setting is a bottle episode: one ship, limited resources, a deadline. The emotional weight comes from the relationships between the six crew members and from the knowledge that every decision they make will be judged by people who were not awake to make it themselves.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** Rows of cryopods stretching to vanishing points, each one containing a person who left Earth expecting to wake up in a new home. Status lights: blue for stable, amber for degrading, red for the three pods that failed during transit. The view from the forward observation deck: a star growing brighter each day, orbited by a planet that does not want them.
2. **Sound:** The ship's background hum, designed to sound like a heartbeat at 60 BPM. The click and hiss of a cryopod releasing gas during a maintenance cycle. The delay on communications with the planet: 47 minutes each way, turning every conversation into a series of monologues.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The cold of a cryopod hull under bare hands. The staleness of air that has been recycled for decades. The particular warmth of the galley, the only room on the ship designed for prolonged human habitation, which smells faintly of the previous emergency crew's coffee from forty years ago.

## 2. The Ship

**The Meridian** is a generation ship built for a one-way trip. It is not elegant. It was designed to deliver people alive, not comfortably. The corridors are narrow, the lighting is institutional, and every surface serves a structural purpose. The ship's AI, called the Steward, is functional but not conversational. It provides data when asked, runs maintenance protocols, and woke the crew because its programming prioritized passenger survival over destination arrival.

**Cryodeck Alpha** holds 8,000 colonists: families, workers, teachers, and farmers who paid for passage with twenty-year labor contracts. Their pods are functioning. Their destination is not.

**Cryodeck Beta** holds 4,000 colonists with specialized skills: engineers, doctors, agricultural scientists, and administrators. Beta passengers paid higher fares and have contractual priority for early settlement plots. Those contracts are now worthless.

**The Bridge** is a six-person workspace with communication equipment, navigation controls, and a resource management console that displays the Wake Budget in real time. The number gets smaller every day.

**The Hold** contains seed stock, prefabricated shelter components, medical supplies, and agricultural equipment. All of it was packed for a specific planet. Some of it may be useful as trade goods if the planet can be persuaded to negotiate.

## 3. Factions

**The Awakened Crew** (the player characters and immediate NPCs) are the only people currently conscious. They disagree on strategy. Some want to negotiate landing rights. Others want to search for alternative destinations. One crew member has begun arguing that the passengers should be consulted, which would require waking them and spending resources the ship cannot spare.

**Kepler Colony** is the established settlement on the destination planet. Governor Yuen responds to communications with bureaucratic firmness: the colony's carrying capacity is calculated, additional arrivals would exceed sustainable resource limits, and the Meridian's passengers were not part of the original settlement charter. Yuen is not heartless. She is managing a colony that is itself only two decades old and still fragile. She fears that 12,000 new arrivals would collapse both communities.

**The Meridian Compact** is the legal entity that organized the voyage. It exists only on paper now: its offices on Earth closed a decade after departure. But its charter governs the ship, and the charter says the captain has authority over operations but not over the passengers' rights. The passengers signed contracts. Those contracts promise a destination. The crew is technically obligated to deliver on a promise the destination has refused.

**The Three Pods** are a problem nobody wants to discuss. Three cryopods failed during transit. The occupants died. Their families are among the 12,000 still sleeping. The crew must decide when and how to disclose this, knowing that the disclosure will define how the passengers view the crew's competence and authority.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Wake Budget

The ship sustains **6 awakened crew** at current resource levels, with **18 months of reserves** (3 months to arrival plus 15 months of contingency).

**Waking additional people:**
- Each additional awakened person costs 1 month of reserves
- Waking someone adds their skills to the crew's capabilities
- A specialist can be woken for a specific task and re-frozen, but re-freezing carries a 10% medical complication risk

**Resource drain events:**
- Communication equipment failure: -1 month (power rerouted to repairs)
- Cryopod section malfunction: -2 months (emergency life support for affected pods)
- Full passenger awakening: -12 months (game over unless landing is secured within 6 months)

**Current reserves** are displayed on the Bridge console. The Storyteller tracks this openly. The crew can see the number. The number creates the pressure.

The core tension: every problem can be solved by waking the right person, but every person woken brings the ship closer to a deadline where nobody survives.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Diplomat

You were frozen as a communications officer. You are now the only person on this ship trained to negotiate with a government that does not want to negotiate. Your counterpart on the planet is a bureaucrat who responds in 47-minute intervals and never says yes.

- **Drive:** *"Find a word, a trade, or a concession that turns 'landing denied' into 'landing conditional.' I do not need them to want us. I need them to let us land."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

### The Ship Engineer

You keep the Meridian running. You know every system on this vessel and you know which ones are failing. The Steward gives you data. The data says the ship was designed for arrival, not for waiting. You are now being asked to make a one-way ship hover.

- **Drive:** *"Buy the diplomat time. Every day this ship holds together is a day closer to a solution."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 1

### The Passenger Advocate

You are a lawyer. Your specialty was settlement contracts. You were frozen in Beta deck with the administrators. You were woken because the crew needed someone who could interpret the Meridian Compact charter. You have read the charter. It says the passengers have rights the crew is not currently honoring, starting with the right to be informed.

- **Drive:** *"These people did not consent to being managed by six strangers. Whatever we decide, they deserve to know before we do it."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 1 | Mind 3 | Presence 3

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The Counter-Offer:** Governor Yuen has sent a surprising response: the colony will accept 200 passengers with agricultural expertise, provided the Meridian surrenders its seed stock and prefabricated shelters as payment. The remaining 11,800 passengers must continue to another system. The crew's navigator has identified one marginally habitable candidate, 4 light-years further, that would require putting everyone back to sleep and consuming all remaining fuel with no margin for error. The Passenger Advocate argues that the passengers must be consulted before any agreement is made. The engineer warns that waking passengers for a vote costs months of reserves. The diplomat is drafting a counter-counter-offer that would increase the accepted number to 2,000 but requires concessions the captain is not sure she has the authority to make. The crew has 72 hours before Yuen's offer expires.

**Why It Matters:** The Sleep Fleet asks who gets to decide for people who cannot decide for themselves. The crew did not choose this authority. They were given it by an automated system that matched their skills to a crisis. Every choice they make will be evaluated by 12,000 people who were unconscious when it was made. The setting is about consent, representation, and the difference between leadership and custodianship.
