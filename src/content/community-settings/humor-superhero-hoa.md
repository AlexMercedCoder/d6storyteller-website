## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The hardest part of being a superhero isn't the villains. It's the paperwork.

**Paragon Heights** solved the problem of where superheroes live by creating a dedicated residential community with deed restrictions specifically calibrated for powered individuals. Reinforced foundations. Blast-resistant fencing. An emergency repair fund. Vendor relationships with contractors who don't ask questions about how the kitchen got destroyed. Insurance riders for "unexplained structural events."

It works very well. The community is genuinely functional, surprisingly pleasant, and governed by an HOA that takes its responsibilities seriously despite the extraordinary circumstances of its membership.

The comedy emerges from the HOA taking its responsibilities equally seriously for stopping a nuclear missile and for parking enforcement. The monthly newsletter covers both with the same measured bureaucratic tone.

**Tone:** Genuine superhero action in a community governance frame. Fight sequences happen. They are then reviewed by the Incident Committee. The committee's observations are sometimes actually useful.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** A well-maintained suburban community with subtle signs of its unusual residents: blast scoring on some walls that has been repainted but not quite matched, one resident's driveway that floats six inches off the ground (the resident has a density issue), a community center that was rebuilt twice in 2019 alone, and a mailbox that is chained to the ground because the previous occupant had strength issues.
2. **Sound:** The normal suburban sounds interrupted by distant sonic booms (patrol departures), the periodic clang of the community repair team, a heroic battle that can be heard from the community pool, and the HOA President's voice on the phone calmly discussing damage assessment while said battle continues in the background.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The community garden maintained by a hero whose powers include accelerated plant growth (it is very productive and slightly overwhelming), the faint ozone smell that follows electric-powered residents, the texture of incident report forms (substantial paper stock; they go through a lot of them), and the warm smell of the community cookout where someone's heat powers are handling the grill.

**The Core Tension:** Community membership requires compliance. Heroism requires action. These are not always compatible. The HOA understands this and has created extensive documentation to address it. The documentation is imperfect.

## 2. Paragon Heights Structure

### The HOA Board
Current President: a retired hero with administrative superpowers (not a joke; their power is bureaucratic optimization and it is formidable). The board governs community standards, manages the repair fund, and handles the challenging diplomacy of 200 people with superpowers who occasionally disagree about parking.

### The Probationary Members
The reformed supervillains and morally ambiguous members of the community on HOA probationary status. They have modified deed restrictions. Their neighbors are cautiously accepting. The one who can mind-control people is not on probation; the HOA board unanimously decided this was unfair discrimination and they're trying to manage it.

### The Retirees
Former heroes who have settled into community life. They are extremely effective at neighborhood watch duties. They occasionally mentor newer residents. They have very strong opinions about how heroism has changed since their day.

### The Kids
Powered children in the neighborhood who have not yet registered with any heroic organization. They're technically civilians. They are also very powerful and very bored during summer break.

## 3. Adventure Seeds

1. **The Villain Next Door:** The probationary ex-villain in unit 7 is exhibiting suspicious behavior. The HOA doesn't have authority to investigate. The heroes technically shouldn't use their powers for personal surveillance. But something is definitely happening.

2. **The Incident Review:** Last month's battle caused significant overrun of the community damage allowance. The Incident Review Committee wants a full accounting. The heroes must reconstruct the fight, justify each decision, and negotiate their fine. The villain is also submitting a statement.

3. **The HOA Election:** The current President is not running for re-election. The race to replace them has become, somehow, more fraught than most villain encounters. Multiple candidates with super-powers. One of them can see the future. This is considered a conflict of interest by the Elections Committee but the bylaws don't specifically address it.

4. **The New Neighbor:** A new resident has moved in with powers that are unclear, a vague heroic history, and no apparent registration. The background check came back clean but something is off. The HOA's response options are limited. The heroes' response options are extensive but legally complicated.

5. **The Repair Fund:** The repair fund is running low after an unusually eventful quarter. Someone needs to fundraise, cut costs, or identify the hero who keeps filing duplicate claims. The annual gala is coming up and the community needs it to go well.
