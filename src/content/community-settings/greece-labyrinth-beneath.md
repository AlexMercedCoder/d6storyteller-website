## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** Greek mythology is the most familiar fantasy setting in Western culture, but this supplement goes beneath the surface. The Olympian gods are not the oldest powers in the Greek cosmos. Beneath them lie the **Titans** (imprisoned in Tartarus), the **Chthonic deities** (gods of the underworld, earth, and death), and the **pre-Olympian primordial forces** (Chaos, Nyx, Erebus). The Labyrinth is not merely a maze but a cosmological seal designed by Daedalus at the gods' command to keep the Chthonic realm separate from the mortal world. The Minotaur was its guardian.

The game is set in the Bronze Age Aegean, during the twilight of Minoan civilization and the rise of Mycenaean Greece. Oracles are going mad, speaking in Titan-tongues. Heroes are receiving menos from unknown gods who are not Olympians. The sea is behaving strangely. Something beneath the earth is waking up.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The brilliant azure Aegean Sea contrasting with the dark volcanic rock of Thera (Santorini); the painted frescoes of Knossos coming alive with Chthonic energy; the Labyrinth's corridors extending impossibly deep beneath Crete.
2. **Sound:** The Pythia's voice shifting from prophecy to screaming as Chthonic influence corrupts the Oracle; the subsonic rumble of Titan movement deep underground; the crash of Mediterranean waves on volcanic shores.
3. **Touch & Smell:** Hot Mediterranean sun on bare skin; the sulfurous stench of Chthonic vents; the cool, disorienting darkness of the Labyrinth; sacred incense from the Eleusinian Mysteries.

## 2. Historical & Mythological Context

The Minoan civilization (c. 2700-1450 BCE) on Crete was the first advanced civilization in Europe. The palace of Knossos was an enormous, complex structure that may have inspired the Labyrinth legend. The Eleusinian Mysteries were the most important secret religious rites in ancient Greece, promising initiates knowledge of death and the afterlife. The Chthonic deities (Hades, Persephone, Hecate, the Erinyes) represented the powers beneath the earth.

> **Note on Respectful Portrayal:** Greek mythology is presented in its original Bronze Age context, with attention to the pre-classical traditions that are often overshadowed by later Olympian-focused retellings.

## 3. Core Factions & Traditions

### The Eleusinian Initiates
Priestesses and priests of the Mysteries who understand the Labyrinth's true purpose.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I have seen what lies beyond death. It is not what Homer described."*
  - *"The Mysteries taught me that the Olympians are not the first gods. They are the latest."*

### The Bronze Shield Companions
Warriors trained in Mycenaean combat who serve as the mortal line of defense.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"My bronze shield was forged by a student of Hephaestus. It has never been breached."*
  - *"I fight for no king. I fight for the people the kings sacrifice to their ambitions."*

### The Thread-Walkers (Ariadne's Legacy)
Navigators of the Labyrinth who follow Ariadne's thread tradition.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"Ariadne did not merely give Theseus a thread. She taught him the path. I am her student's student."*
  - *"The Labyrinth changes. The thread remembers. Without it, even the gods get lost."*

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Menos (Divine Fury)

- **1-2 (Mortal):** Standard human abilities. +1d6 to Mind (mortal clarity).
- **3-4 (Inspired):** A god has breathed menos into the character. +1d6 to Force. Heightened senses. **Risk:** The god expects obedience. Refusing a divine command drops Menos to 0.
- **5-6 (Hero-Touched):** +1d6 to Force and Finesse. The character performs feats that enter legend. **Risk:** The character attracts divine jealousy. At Menos 6, a god demands a specific action, and refusal results in divine punishment (the Conditions are legendary-madness, transformation, eternal torment).

## 7. Starter Scenario: The Broken Seal

**Framing:** The Oracle at Delphi has stopped speaking Greek. For three days, the Pythia has prophesied in a language no one recognizes-until a scholar identifies it as Titan-tongue, the language spoken before the Olympians conquered heaven. Simultaneously, earthquakes shake Crete, and the entrance to the Labyrinth beneath Knossos has reopened. The characters must enter the Labyrinth, descend to the broken seal, and restore it before the Chthonic powers fully emerge.

**Antagonist:** ***The Minotaur's Ghost*** - Not a villain but a tragic guardian who failed. Its spirit haunts the Labyrinth, trying to warn anyone who enters that the seal is weakening. It does not attack; it tests. Only those who understand that the Minotaur was a protector, not a monster, can pass its final test and reach the seal.

## 8. Why This Setting Matters

Greek mythology is the foundation of Western fantasy, but most adaptations treat it as a collection of adventure stories. This setting goes deeper, exploring the pre-Olympian cosmology that the classical myths were built to contain. The Labyrinth as a seal, the Minotaur as a guardian, and the gods as a younger generation sitting atop an older, more dangerous power-these reframings invite players to question the stories they think they know.
