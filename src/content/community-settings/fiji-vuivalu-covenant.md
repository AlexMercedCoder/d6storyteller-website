## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The ocean is managed by spirits. The Dato manage the relationship. Both are failing, and discovering why requires going where humans rarely go.

Fijian spiritual tradition includes a rich ocean-spirit cosmology: the gods of the deep sea, the guardian spirits of specific reefs and currents, and the hereditary spiritual leadership of the Dato system (which has historical parallels in actual Fijian chiefly and spiritual institutions). The Vuivalu are the ocean spirits most relevant to navigation, fishing, and the reef ecology.

This setting engages Polynesian maritime culture at its most sophisticated: the Pacific Islander tradition of deep-sea navigation by stars, currents, and wave patterns was one of history's great navigational achievements, and it existed in relationship with spiritual practice that acknowledged the ocean as an inhabited, managed environment.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Fiji island chain from the perspective of those who live between islands as much as on them: the lagoon shallows where reef life is visible from the surface, the abrupt drop of the reef wall into the blue-dark of deep water, the island chain as a pattern of high points on a surface that is mostly ocean, and the way the water's color and behavior telegraphs information to those trained to read it.
2. **Sound:** The ocean in its varieties: the shallow-water sound over reef, the deep swell of open ocean, the sound of wind that reef-experienced navigators read for information, and the specific sound quality of water above a Vuivalu-inhabited reef (subtle harmonic differences from the surrounding ocean, audible to trained ears).
3. **Touch & Smell:** The physical reality of tropical ocean: the warm shallows and the abrupt cold of deep water, the texture difference between healthy reef (rough, alive) and damaged reef (smooth, dead), the smell of the ocean changing as the reef ecology changes, and the physical challenge of extended ocean navigation on traditional craft.

## 2. The Covenant Framework

### The Dato System
Hereditary spiritual leaders who inherit specific Vuivalu relationships along with their chiefly rank. Each Dato has primary relationships with specific ocean spirits and secondary relationships with the broader spirit community. The system has worked for generations. The current crisis is unprecedented in living memory.

### The Vuivalu Hierarchy
Ocean spirits organized by depth and domain: shallow-reef Vuivalu manage the coral systems; mid-water Vuivalu govern the fish populations and currents; the deep Vuivalu are ancient and rarely contacted, governing the deep-sea processes that the shallow Vuivalu depend on. The disruption is coming from the deep level.

### The Reef as Communication
The reef's health communicates the covenant's status: healthy, growing reef means the relationship is strong; bleaching, damage, and unusual fish behavior indicates spiritual disturbance. Reading the reef is a skill that the Dato have developed over generations.

### The Disruption
Something from the deep ocean -- deeper than the established Vuivalu territory -- is interfering with the covenant. The established Vuivalu are not able to address it directly and are disturbed by it. Human contact with whatever it is requires going deeper than humans have gone.

## 3. Adventure Seeds

1. **The Dead Reef:** A previously healthy reef section has died within one season -- not through known causes, but in a pattern that the Dato recognize as spiritual withdrawal. The Vuivalu responsible for that reef have gone silent. Investigation requires diving to the reef to assess what happened and then navigating the spirit world to find the Vuivalu who withdrew.

2. **The Current Change:** A regular current that the island communities depend on for inter-island navigation has changed direction. Navigation between specific islands is now significantly more dangerous. The Vuivalu governing that current cannot be reached through normal ceremony. Finding them requires following the current to wherever it's now going.

3. **The Deep Contact:** A Vuivalu from the deep ocean has made contact -- a completely unprecedented event. Deep Vuivalu do not communicate with humans under normal circumstances. What they have communicated is fragmentary: a warning, an image, a direction. Interpreting it and acting on it requires following it into territory the established covenant doesn't cover.

4. **The Competing Claim:** Another island community's Dato system has extended into what this community considers its covenant territory. The Vuivalu in the overlap area are responding to both claims. Negotiating shared jurisdiction between two Dato systems requires both human diplomacy and spirit-world mediation.

5. **The Covenant Renewal:** The ancient covenant is at a renewal point -- a cyclical event that every generation of Dato manages, but which this generation must manage under crisis conditions. The renewal ceremony requires contact with all major Vuivalu simultaneously. Several are currently unreachable. The ceremony must happen on schedule or the covenant lapses.
