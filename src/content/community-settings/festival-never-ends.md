> *"I have been a festival musician for twenty-three years. I play twelve hours a day. I have not had a vacation since I was nineteen. If I stop playing, the ward weakens, and something outside gets closer. I love music. I hate this."*
> - Lyra Goss, street musician, Revalia

--

## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

Revalia is a city that runs on happiness, which turns out to be an exhausting fuel source. The ward that protects the city is ancient, powerful, and fueled by collective positive emotion. The original festival was genuine: a week of dancing, feasting, and music to celebrate the city's founding. When the founders discovered the ward's requirements, they made the festival permanent. The city has adapted. Festival production is an industry. Joy is a civic duty. Burnout is a political crisis.

**Tone:** Bright, frantic, and heartbreaking in quiet moments. The setting is colorful and loud on the surface. Underneath, it is a story about people who are required to be happy and the toll that mandatory joy takes on genuine emotion. The darkest moments are the quietest: a musician playing a beautiful song at 3 AM in an empty square because the ward requires it, tears running down her face, playing beautifully because she is excellent at her job.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** A city in permanent celebration: banners on every building, stages on every corner, dancers in every square. The colors are vivid, deliberate, and slightly desperate. The ward itself: a shimmering dome visible at the edges of the sky, brighter over areas of intense celebration, dimmer over quiet neighborhoods. The visible difference between genuine joy and performed joy, which is subtle to outsiders and obvious to Revalians.
2. **Sound:** Music everywhere, always. Street musicians, sponsored orchestras, amateur singers, and automated instruments that play when the ward needs maintenance. The layering of sound in a Revalian street: three different songs from three different directions, laughter (some genuine, some professional), and the low hum of the ward, which sounds like a sustained chord that changes key with the city's mood.
3. **Touch & Smell:** Festival food: spiced bread, roasted nuts, sugar pastries, and the particular greasiness of a snack eaten while walking. Confetti underfoot, never fully cleaned because the sweepers cannot keep up and the ward responds positively to visual celebration. The warmth of a crowd pressed together in a dance square, which is comforting or suffocating depending on how many years you have been dancing.

## 2. The City

**The Grand Stage** is the city's central performance venue: a permanent amphitheater where the most important celebrations occur. Festival energy generated at the Grand Stage has the strongest effect on the ward. The Stage Director, a woman named Calista Venn, programs the schedule and is the closest thing Revalia has to a military commander. When the ward dims, she calls for an emergency performance. When the ward is strong, she allows rest days. She has not had a rest day herself in six years.

**The Quiet Wards** are residential neighborhoods where the festival is present but muted. Their residents are off-shift workers, families with young children, and people whose capacity for public joy has been exceeded. The Quiet Wards contribute less to the festival energy, which means they receive less city funding, which means they are quieter, which means they contribute less. The cycle is recognized and unaddressed.

**The Burnout Clinics** treat festival exhaustion: a real medical condition with symptoms including emotional numbness, chronic fatigue, performance anxiety, and an inability to experience genuine happiness because every positive emotion has been instrumentalized for four decades. The clinics are underfunded because acknowledging burnout implies that the festival is not working, which is politically unacceptable.

**The Outer Wall** is where the ward is thinnest and whatever the ward protects against is most visible. Guards patrol the wall and report fluctuations. On bad nights, when festival energy drops, the guards can see movement outside: shapes that press against the ward's surface, testing it. Nobody has seen clearly what is outside. The guards agree that it is large, patient, and interested.

## 3. Factions

**The Festival Authority** manages the city's celebration schedule and maintains the ward. Director Venn runs the Authority with the efficiency of a general and the creativity of an artist. She believes the festival can be sustainable if it is better managed: more rotation, more variety, more genuine community events instead of mandatory performances. She is fighting a bureaucracy that measures success in volume rather than quality.

**The Rest Movement** argues that forced celebration is not celebration and that the ward should be powered by something other than mandatory joy. Their leader, a retired dancer named Orin Voss, has been organizing quiet protests, which in Revalia means standing still in a public square, a radical act in a city where stillness is treated as civic negligence.

**The Festival Merchants** profit from the permanent celebration: food vendors, costume suppliers, instrument makers, and event sponsors. Their trade association controls significant economic power and opposes any reduction in festival hours because their businesses depend on mandatory participation. Guild president Hana Reis argues that the festival is Revalia's identity and that changing it would destroy the city's culture. She is conflating culture with commerce, but she is not entirely wrong.

**The Wall Watchers** are the guards who patrol the outer wall and monitor the ward. They know more about the threat outside than anyone in the city, and they are frustrated by a government that treats the ward as a celebration problem rather than a defense problem. Watch Captain Dren has been requesting permission to study the ward's structure for three years. His requests are denied because studying the ward might reveal that it is weaker than the Festival Authority reports.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Festival Energy

The city maintains a shared **Festival Energy** score from 0 to 10, starting at 6.

**Natural decay:** Energy decreases by 1 at the start of each session. The festival is always losing ground.

**Gaining Energy (+1 each):**
- Genuine public celebration (not mandatory, not performed)
- A community event that brings people together for reasons beyond the ward
- An artistic performance that moves the audience honestly
- Resolving a source of collective grief, resentment, or exhaustion

**Losing Energy (-1 each):**
- Forced participation without emotional authenticity
- Public burnout (a visible performer collapse, a protest, a refusal)
- Discovery of ward weakness (public knowledge of the threat reduces confidence)
- Festival Authority crackdown on dissent or rest

**Thresholds:**
| Energy | Ward Status |
|--------|-------------|
| 7-10 | Strong. The ward is visible, bright, and the thing outside is quiet. |
| 4-6 | Stable. The ward holds but flickers during low periods. |
| 2-3 | Critical. The ward is translucent. Movement outside is visible to civilians. |
| 1 | Emergency. The ward is failing. The thing outside is pressing inward. |
| 0 | Collapse. The ward fails. Whatever is outside enters the city. |

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Festival Musician

You play for twelve hours a day because the ward requires music and you are good at it. You love music. You hate this. You have been performing for twenty-three years and you cannot remember the last time you played a song because you wanted to, rather than because the schedule required it.

- **Drive:** *"Find a way to play music that is mine again, not the ward's."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 3 | Mind 1 | Presence 3

### The Rest Advocate

You stand still in public squares. This is the most radical act available to you. You are a retired dancer whose body gave out after nineteen years of mandatory performance. You believe that the ward can be powered differently, and you are willing to test that belief by organizing a citywide rest day, knowing that if you are wrong, the ward may fail.

- **Drive:** *"Prove that the city can survive without mandatory joy, or discover what we are actually protecting ourselves from."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 1 | Mind 3 | Presence 3

### The Wall Watcher

You guard the outer wall. You have seen the thing outside more clearly than anyone in the city. You have also seen the ward from the outside, which gives you a perspective the Festival Authority does not share: the ward responds to specific emotions, not general celebration. You have a theory about which emotions strengthen the ward and which ones the Authority has been wasting. You need someone inside the city to test it.

- **Drive:** *"Understand the ward well enough to make the festival voluntary without making the city vulnerable."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 1

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The Silent Hour:** Orin Voss has organized Revalia's first public rest period: one hour of silence and stillness in the Grand Stage amphitheater. 3,000 people have committed to attend and do nothing. The Festival Authority has declared the event a public safety threat. The Wall Watchers have reported increased activity outside the ward. The Festival Merchants are threatening an economic boycott. Director Venn is privately sympathetic but cannot publicly support an event that might weaken the ward. The characters must decide whether to support the Silent Hour, protect it from interference, or stop it. They must also prepare for the possibility that one hour of silence will provide the first real data about what the ward actually needs, and the answer may not be what anyone expects.

**Why It Matters:** The Festival That Never Ends asks whether an obligation to be happy can coexist with genuine happiness. The ward needs joy. Mandatory joy is not joy. The contradiction has been driving the city for 47 years, and the system is breaking down because people cannot sustain an emotion they are required to feel. The setting is about burnout, authenticity, and the difference between performing well-being and experiencing it. The characters will not solve the ward's power problem. They will decide whether the city can learn to celebrate for its own reasons rather than its survival, and whether that distinction matters to whatever is waiting outside.
