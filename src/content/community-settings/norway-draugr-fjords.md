## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** Norway's conversion to Christianity (c. 995-1030 CE) was one of the most violent religious transitions in European history. King Olaf Tryggvason used force, bribery, and threats to convert the Norse population, destroying temples (*hov*), felling sacred groves, and desecrating burial sites. In this setting, the dead respond. **Draugr**--the fearsome undead of Norse mythology, far more powerful than modern zombie concepts--rise from their burial mounds wreathed in corpse-fire (*haugeld*), possessing superhuman strength, shape-shifting ability, and burning hatred for those who have disturbed their rest.

But the stave churches being built from sacred wood carry their own power. The collision between the dying Norse spiritual system and the arriving Christian one creates a spiritual vacuum that both the draugr and newer supernatural entities rush to fill.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** Dramatic Norwegian fjords cutting deep into the mountains, their dark water reflecting the corpse-green glow of draugr emerging from hillside burial mounds; the architectural beauty of stave churches with their dragon-headed roof peaks and intricate wood carvings; a draugr king standing atop his mound at midnight, ten feet tall, wreathed in sickly green fire.
2. **Sound:** The crack of frozen ground as a draugr forces its way from the mound; the chanting of Christian monks competing with the keening of a völva in trance; the deep groan of fjord ice; wind howling through the dragon heads on stave church rooftops.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The charnel cold radiating from a draugr's body; the warm, resinous scent of freshly carved stave church pine; the metallic taste of death-energy after entering a burial mound; salt spray from the fjord.

**The Core Tension:** Old Faith vs. New Faith vs. the Dead. The draugr do not represent the old gods' vengeance; they represent the consequences of transition without respect. The characters must find a way to honor the dead while building something new.

## 2. Historical & Mythological Context

Norse draugr are far more formidable than most modern undead depictions. In the sagas, draugr possess superhuman strength (often growing larger after death), can change shape (into seals, cats, or smoke), wield weapons, generate a foul death-stench, and are nearly impossible to destroy. The traditional method involves wrestling the draugr back into its mound and then burning the body--a physically grueling and spiritually dangerous process.

The **völva** (plural *völur*) were Norse seeresses who practiced *seiðr* (a form of shamanic magic involving trance, prophecy, and spirit communication). They were among the most respected and feared figures in Norse society. Norway's stave churches (28 survive today) are architectural masterpieces built using techniques derived from Viking shipbuilding.

> **Note on Respectful Portrayal:** Norse spiritual traditions, both pagan and Christian, are treated as genuine expressions of community faith, and the Christianization process is presented with its full historical complexity rather than as a simple good-vs-evil narrative.

## 3. Core Factions & Traditions

### The Mound Wardens (Haugvaktere)
Warriors and wise-folk who maintain the boundary between the living and the draugr.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"My grandmother taught me the nine rune-wards that can seal a burial mound from the inside"*
  - *"I have entered four mounds and returned. Each time, I left a piece of myself behind."*
  - *"The draugr do not want to fight. They want to go back to sleep. They cannot, because someone built a church on their pillow."*

### The Stave Builders (Stavmestere)
Master carpenters who build stave churches that serve as both Christian sanctuaries and spiritual anchors.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I carve dragon heads on the roof to ward off the draugr. The priest thinks they are decorative."*
  - *"The wood speaks to me. It remembers being a living tree in the sacred grove. It still carries that power."*

### The Last Völva's Circle
The remnants of the Norse seeress tradition, keeping the old ways alive in secret.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I am the last woman in Norway who can sing seiðr. When I die, a thousand years of prophecy die with me."*
  - *"My staff is carved from the World Tree's root. It lets me walk between the nine worlds--when the roads are open."*

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Haugbrot (Mound-Breaking)

Characters track a **Haugbrot Meter** (1-6):

- **1-2 (Untouched):** No death-energy. +1d6 to Presence with the living. Cannot perceive draugr until they physically manifest.
- **3-4 (Mound-Marked):** Has entered burial mounds and carries their mark. +1d6 to Force against draugr. Can perceive them before they fully manifest. Can read rune-inscriptions on burial goods. **Risk:** Animals shy away; children cry; the character smells faintly of grave-earth.
- **5-6 (Death-Carrier):** +1d6 to Force and Mind in all encounters with the dead. Can command minor draugr and read the memories of the ancient dead by touching their grave goods. **Risk:** Draugr begin treating the character as a peer. The living find the character increasingly unsettling. At Haugbrot 6, the character must perform a major act of life-affirmation (feasting, love, laughter) or begin transforming into a draugr themselves.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### Archetype 1: The Shield-Maiden of Geiranger
- **Concept:** A warrior woman trained in the old Norse martial tradition who refuses to convert but also refuses to let the draugr harm the living. She fights for people, not gods.
- **Quote:** *"Odin did not protect us from the Christians. Christ does not protect us from the draugr. I will protect us from both."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 4 | Finesse 2 | Mind 1 | Presence 1

### Archetype 2: The Stave Church Builder
- **Concept:** A master carpenter building a stave church who discovers that the sacred grove wood he is using still carries the old power--and that his church is becoming something neither fully Christian nor fully pagan.
- **Quote:** *"I built a church. The wood remembers being a grove. Now the church is dreaming, and I do not know which god answers when the congregation prays."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 1

### Archetype 3: The Last Völva
- **Concept:** The final Norse seeress in Norway, carrying the entire tradition of seiðr prophecy and spirit-communication. She is ancient, powerful, and dying. She needs to pass her knowledge to someone before she dies--or perform one final prophecy that will determine Norway's spiritual future.
- **Quote:** *"I have seen the end of my tradition and the beginning of yours. Neither is what you expect."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 1 | Mind 3 | Presence 3

## 6. Condition Palette

**Physical:** *Corpse-Fire Burn*, *Draugr Grip* (crushing damage from undead strength), *Mound Air Poisoning*, *Rune-Scar* (from handling ancient inscriptions).
**Social:** *Pagan Suspect* (the Christian court is watching), *Draugr Ally Accusation* (accused of conspiring with the dead), *Völva's Burden* (carrying the last tradition isolates you).
**Psychological:** *Mound Memory* (absorbing a dead warrior's battlefield memories), *Faith Crisis* (neither faith provides answers), *Death Attraction* (the draugr's world seems peaceful).

## 7. Starter Scenario: The Draugr King of Geiranger

**Framing:** King Olaf's priests have built a church on the burial mound of Jarl Hrothgar, a legendary warrior-king buried with a ship full of grave goods 200 years ago. Hrothgar's draugr has risen, destroyed the church's altar, and seated itself on the church's throne. It demands the return of its grave goods and the removal of the church. Olaf's commander demands the draugr be destroyed. The community is caught between an undead king and a living one.

**Antagonist:** ***Draugr Jarl Hrothgar*** -- Not evil, but absolutely unwilling to negotiate from a position of weakness. He was a just ruler in life and remains one in death. He will accept a respectful resolution (relocation of his mound with proper ceremony) but will not tolerate desecration.

---

## 8. Why This Setting Matters

Norway's Christianization is one of history's best-documented religious transitions, and the draugr mythology surrounding it speaks to a universal human concern: what do we owe the dead? Every society must negotiate between honoring its past and building its future. The draugr represent the cost of doing this badly--the fury of the ancestors when their graves are disturbed and their traditions dismissed. The stave church, built from sacred wood with dragon heads on the roof, represents the possibility of synthesis: a structure that honors both the old and the new, if the builders are wise enough to listen to the wood.
