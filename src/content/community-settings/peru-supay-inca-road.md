## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The Inca empire is one of the great administrative achievements of the ancient world -- and it runs on sacred reciprocity between the human and spirit realms as much as it runs on roads and quipus.

The setting draws on Andean cosmology: the three worlds of Hanan Pacha (upper world), Kay Pacha (this world), and Ukhu Pacha (inner/lower world); the apu mountain spirits who are active presences with personalities, preferences, and expectations; the huacas as living sacred sites that need maintenance and respect; and Supay as the complex ruler of the Ukhu Pacha who has his own logic and his own compacts with the living world.

The players walk the Qhapaq Nan (the royal road network, one of history's great engineering achievements) as spiritual custodians, maintaining the sacred infrastructure alongside the physical.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Andes in their full altitude and drama: peaks that pierce the clouds and whose upper reaches exist in a permanent condition of spiritual proximity, the ancient road cut into cliff faces with a precision that should be impossible given the available technology, terraced agricultural slopes that turn the mountain into a living garden, and the huacas -- sacred stones, springs, and crossroads markers -- that mark the road's spiritual geography as precisely as its physical one.
2. **Sound:** The thin silence of high altitude, different from lowland silence in its quality; the sound of wind in the passes that local knowledge says carries messages from the apu if you know how to listen; the rhythm of travel on the great road, the sound of sandals on fitted stone; and the absence of sound that indicates a previously active huaca has gone quiet.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The cold of altitude even in daylight, the warmth that vanishes the moment you step into shadow; the smell of coca leaves offered at a huaca shrine; the physical exertion of Andean altitude that changes everything about how the body works; and the unmistakable sense, reported by every Qhapaq-Nan mystic, of being watched by something that sees more than you can.

## 2. The Sacred Landscape

### The Apu
Mountain spirits with distinct personalities and specific domains. Some are ancient and immense, their awareness stretching across hundreds of miles of range. Some are local and particular, concerned with specific valleys and communities. All require acknowledgment and none appreciate being ignored.

### The Huacas
Thousands of sacred sites along the road system: springs, rocks, crossroads, peaks, caves, and sites of significant historical events. Each huaca is a point of contact with the spirit world. Maintaining them is both a spiritual and practical necessity -- a neglected huaca weakens the sacred infrastructure in its area.

### The Ukhu Pacha
Supay's realm, accessible through caves, springs, and certain mountain passes. Not a hell in the Western sense -- the dead live here, and Supay governs them, but the relationship between the worlds is one of exchange and reciprocity rather than punishment and reward. The dead can be consulted. Supay can be bargained with. Both require the proper protocol.

### The Compact
The ancient agreement between Supay and the Qhapaq-Nan order that allows the mystics to walk between worlds without being taken. Something has violated this compact, and Supay is no longer holding back what he normally holds back.

## 3. Adventure Seeds

1. **The Silent Apu:** The great mountain whose apu guides the most traveled section of the north road has gone silent. No warnings, no guidance, no response to offerings. Communities that depend on the apu's warnings for weather, avalanche, and spiritual protection are vulnerable. The players must determine what happened and whether it can be fixed.

2. **The Missing Compact:** Investigation of Supay's violated compact reveals it was broken by a Qhapaq-Nan mystic three generations ago, whose transgression was covered up. The compact can be renewed, but renewal requires acknowledgment of the original violation -- which would implicate several current sacred offices.

3. **The Returning Dead:** Dead ancestors are returning to their communities -- not as threats but as visitors, clearly trying to deliver a warning. But the dead cannot speak clearly in Kay Pacha, and the messages are fragmentary. The players must enter the Ukhu Pacha to hear the full warning from the source.

4. **The Road That Moved:** A section of the sacred road has changed. Not physically -- the stones are the same -- but spiritually: the huacas along it no longer mark the same locations, and travelers are ending up in the wrong places. Something has shifted the road's spiritual geography without touching its physical form.

5. **Supay's Offer:** Supay appears to the players directly and offers them what they need in exchange for something he wants. His price is not unreasonable. His motives are opaque. And refusing a direct offer from the lord of the dead has its own complications.
