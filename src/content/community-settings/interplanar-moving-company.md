## 1. Setting Overview and Tone

Meridian Relocations was founded by a planar cartographer who noticed that people frequently needed to move between planes and had no professional moving services available. Sixty years later, the company operates in fourteen planes, employs 200 movers and 40 planar navigators, and has a legal department that spends most of its time on cases where client property acquired properties, opinions, or independent existence during transit.

The company does not advertise the 13% failure rate. They advertise the 87% success rate, which, given that they are moving furniture through dimensions where the laws of physics are decorative suggestions, is genuinely impressive.

**Tone:** Workplace comedy with a genuinely strange workplace. The movers are professionals dealing with impossible logistics with the equanimity of people who have accepted that their industry operates under conditions nobody else would recognize as normal.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The dispatch office: a large room with planar transit maps covering every wall, job boards showing active moves across fourteen planes, and the Stability monitoring system that tracks every active shipment in real time. When a shipment's Stability drops, the monitoring system makes a specific sound that every dispatcher has learned to hear in their sleep. The loading dock: where the dimensional portal equipment is staged, maintained by a team of portal engineers who treat the equipment with the specific combination of expertise and wariness that people have toward things that, if mishandled, open holes in the fabric of reality.
2. **Sound:** The company's communication system across planes: a series of message crystals that transmit voice across dimensional boundaries with occasional translation errors that produce surreal operational updates. The sound of furniture hitting the loading dock after a chaotic transit: not always the sound furniture makes when handled normally.
3. **Touch and Smell:** Cargo that has been through the Plane of Elemental Fire smells like it, which is one of the standard quality indicators the company uses in its delivery confirmation process. The standard-issue mover's dimensional anchor bracelet: a piece of equipment that prevents the wearer from experiencing the same planar drift as the cargo, worn at all times during active moves and responsible for the three-tan-line joke that all long-service movers share.

## 2. The Company

**The Dispatch Office** coordinates all active moves. Dispatch Director Nora Pell manages the daily logistics of fourteen simultaneous cross-planar operations with the specific efficiency of someone who has determined that most emergencies have a standard response if you have seen enough of them. She has seen enough of them.

**The Field Crews** are the movers themselves: two-to-four person teams who physically handle the cargo through transit. Field crews develop specializations: some are best at residential moves, some handle fragile magical items, and a small elite team handles what the company calls "Unusual Client Situations," which is the category for clients who are deities, entities, or beings whose household items have will of their own.

**The Planar Navigators** are the dimensional routing specialists who plan the transit paths for each move. Navigator expertise determines which planar shortcuts are safe, which boundary points are stable, and which paths should never be used regardless of how much faster they would be. Senior Navigator Dax has been with the company for thirty years and has personally mapped more planar transit routes than any other navigator in the profession. He has opinions about shortcuts.

**The Legal Department** is the company's most overworked division, handling insurance claims for items lost, transformed, or opinionated in transit, contracts with clients who have unusual property needs, and the growing category of cases where company property (usually portal equipment) has ended up in a plane that does not recognize the company's legal standing to retrieve it.

## 3. Factions

**Meridian Relocations Management** is trying to maintain operational excellence in an industry where excellence is inherently provisional. CEO Wren Marsh founded the company, is now seventy-two, and still comes in to personally supervise unusual client situations because she finds them interesting. Her management style is to hire people who know more than she does about specific domains and then trust them, which is the reason the company has survived sixty years of cross-planar logistics.

**The Movers' Association** is the industry union. Their relationship with Meridian is positive: Meridian was the first company to recognize dimensional hazard pay and to provide standard-issue dimensional anchor bracelets, both of which were Association priorities for a decade before Meridian implemented them voluntarily. The Association's current priority is a formal rescue protocol for movers who experience dimensional drift during operations.

**The Dimensional Oversight Board** regulates cross-planar commerce. They issue the operating licenses that make Meridian's work legal and the safety certifications that their insurance requires. Board Inspector Sela views Meridian's operation with a combination of genuine professional respect and concern about the specific creative interpretations of "within normal parameters" that the company's field reports regularly produce.

**The Unusual Clients** are the customers whose moves require the elite field team. Current active unusual clients include a deity relocating a vacation island, a collector whose artifacts have developed strong preferences about where they are stored, and a client who is moving between planes and whose household includes a library where the books are alive and have opinions about their transport conditions.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Dimensional Stability

Track cargo **Stability** from 0 to 6 through each transit leg.

**Transit leg rolls:** For each planar boundary crossed, roll based on the path quality:
- Standard approved route: no roll required
- Expedited shortcut: Finesse roll (difficulty 2)
- Emergency improvised path: Mind roll (difficulty 3)
- Through unstable boundary: difficulty 4

**Stability effects:**
| Stability | Cargo Status |
|-----------|-------------|
| 5-6 | Clean transit. Cargo arrives as expected. |
| 3-4 | Minor drift. Cargo arrives with minor changes: color shift, temperature change, slight temporal displacement (arrives 20 minutes in the past or future). |
| 1-2 | Significant drift. Cargo arrives in altered state: material change, partial temporal displacement, or acquired planar characteristics. |
| 0 | Lost in transit. Retrieval Operation required. Cargo is somewhere in the planes, possibly with opinions about its situation. |

**Retrieval Operations:** When cargo is lost, a separate operation is required to locate, negotiate with, and return the cargo to the client. Lost cargo frequently develops awareness of its situation and has preferences about its return. These must be addressed.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Field Crew Lead

You have been moving things across planes for fifteen years. You have recovered a sentient piano from the Plane of Resonant Frequencies. You have supervised the relocation of a minor deity's beach house, including the beach. You have talked a client's grandmother's china collection out of a brief obsession with the Plane of Sharp Angles. You have opinions about shortcuts that are not adequately captured by the Navigator's route planning software and a professional relationship with your crew based on absolute trust and shared reference to experiences that nobody outside the company would believe.

- **Drive:** *"Complete the current unusual client situation without losing anything to the planes or acquiring any new company obligations to dimensional entities that will still be active in twenty years."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 3 | Mind 1 | Presence 2

### The Dispatch Director

You are Nora. You are managing fourteen simultaneous cross-planar operations, one lost piano retrieval, a complaint from the Dimensional Oversight Board about a recent field report, and a new unusual client intake that your CEO has just personally approved and assigned to your best team, who are currently in the Plane of Resonant Frequencies negotiating with a piano. You have a working system for managing normal operations. You have a different working system for unusual situations. You have a third system, developed over twenty years, for situations that fall outside both of those categories, which this appears to be becoming.

- **Drive:** *"Manage all fourteen active operations, retrieve the piano, satisfy the Oversight Board, and complete the unusual client intake without losing a single mover to dimensional drift."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### The New Navigator

You graduated from the Planar Cartography Institute three months ago with the highest route-planning marks in your cohort. You have now been on your first six actual moves and have discovered that the Institute's simulation environment was accurate about dimensional boundary physics and completely unprepared you for the experience of a client's armchair developing brief sentience and needing to be talked through its return to its home plane. You are good at your job. You are also reconsidering several assumptions about what your job would involve.

- **Drive:** *"Develop a functional professional equilibrium with the reality of cross-planar moving work before the current unusual client situation adds to the list of experiences the Institute did not prepare you for."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

## 6. Starter Scenario and Why It Matters

**The Library Move:** A client is moving between planes and the household includes a living library: a collection of about three thousand books, all of which are aware, most of which have preferences, and several hundred of which have formed opinions about the moving company based on reviews they have read in other books. The client needs the library moved intact. The library requires that each book be individually consulted about its transport conditions, which the company's standard timeline does not accommodate. The most senior books are also demanding a guarantee about the destination plane's library culture before they agree to go. CEO Wren has committed the company's best team. Senior Navigator Dax is planning a route. The characters must negotiate with a library, move three thousand opinionated books across planar boundaries without losing any of them to dimensional drift, and arrive at a destination plane whose library culture the company has not previously assessed.

**Why It Matters:** The Interplanar Moving Company is about the specific comedy and genuine professionalism of people doing impossible work with the equanimity of experience. The setting is also, underneath the comedy, about the ethics of moving things that might not want to be moved, the logistics of care at scale, and the question of what you owe to the objects in your charge when they develop opinions about the matter.
