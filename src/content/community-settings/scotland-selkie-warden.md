You're standing at the shore watching the sea and what you're actually watching is the sea and everything moving through it that isn't water. Both things at once. It's exhausting. It's also, some days, genuinely beautiful."*
> - Account of a Highland Second Sight holder

## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The Selkie have never asked for human help before. That they're asking now is the most alarming sign that things are badly wrong.

Scottish folklore's Selkie tradition is among the most melancholy of Celtic supernatural traditions: the seal folk who remove their skins to become human, and who can be trapped in human form by anyone who finds and hides the skin. The stories are usually about longing: a Selkie trapped as a human wife who eventually finds her skin and returns to the sea, leaving her human family. The tragedy is in the ambiguity -- the Selkie loves but belongs elsewhere.

This setting takes the established tradition and extends it: Selkies as a genuine community with their own politics, their own relationship to the human shore, and their own capacity to ask for help when the situation requires it.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The outer islands of Scotland at their most atmospheric: low cloud that sits on the water, sea that is grey-green in all weathers, the distinctive shapes of the seal population on the rocks -- including the ones that are too large and too aware to be entirely seals; and through the Second Sight, the additional layer visible only to Wardens: the Selkie in their spiritual dimension, the paths they travel, and the points where the shore and the deep communicate.
2. **Sound:** The Scottish Atlantic's sound vocabulary: the sea on stone, the wind across the open islands, the cry of seabirds that Second Sight holders learn to read as sometimes more than bird behavior, and the specific sound (reported by those who've heard it) of a Selkie singing in their true voice under the water.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The bone-cold of the Scottish Atlantic, the smell of salt and kelp and the particular quality of air near the seal rookeries, the feel of a Selkie skin (reportedly both wet and dry simultaneously, warm and cold at once, and profoundly wrong to hold), and the physical sensation of the Second Sight engaging -- experienced differently by different holders, but always unmistakable.

## 2. The Warden System

### The Selkie Communities
Not solitary creatures but organized communities with their own leadership, territories, and social structures. They have no need for human contact under normal circumstances. The relationship with Wardens is a formality maintained for extreme situations. This is an extreme situation.

### The Second Sight Holders
Scattered across the outer islands, identifiable to each other by specific signs, maintaining the Warden tradition through informal apprenticeship. They are not always comfortable with their gift. They are always competent with it.

### The Skin Trade
Someone has identified the value of Selkie skins and is building a business in them: trapping Selkies in human form, selling or using the skins for purposes ranging from the practical (the skins have supernatural properties) to the disturbing (the trapped Selkie as a supernatural servant). The operation is larger than initially apparent.

### The Shore Communities
Fishing communities with centuries of complicated relationship with the Selkie tradition: some acknowledge it openly, some privately, some don't believe it at all. All of them have been affected by the supernatural disruption without understanding why their relationship with the sea has changed.

## 3. Adventure Seeds

1. **The First Contact:** A Selkie approaches a Warden for the first time in living memory. The contact itself is a crisis indicator. Understanding what they're asking requires both Second Sight and the specific communication protocols that Wardens theoretically know but have never had to practice. The information the Selkie provides is partial and urgent.

2. **The Recovered Skin:** A Selkie skin has been recovered from the operation and needs to be returned to its owner -- who is currently trapped somewhere in the human world, living as a human, and doesn't know help is coming. Finding them without alerting the operation requires investigation through both human and supernatural channels.

3. **The Human Accomplice:** The skin trade has a local human connection -- someone in the shore community who has been providing information about Selkie locations in exchange for payment or protection. Identifying them without destroying the community's already-fragile relationship with the Selkie requires care.

4. **The Second Sight Inheritance:** A Warden is dying without a designated successor. The Second Sight doesn't transfer through training -- it's either there or it isn't -- but identifying someone who has it and doesn't know it yet is possible. Finding the next Warden in a crisis situation, training them sufficiently, and integrating them into the current work is simultaneously urgent and delicate.

5. **The Trapped Selkie Elder:** The Selkie who was stolen is their community's eldest and most significant member. Their capture is both a personal tragedy and a leadership crisis. Recovering them requires navigating the operation's center directly, which is where the most danger and the most information are concentrated.
