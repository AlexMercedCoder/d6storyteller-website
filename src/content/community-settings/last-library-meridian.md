## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

Meridian's civic calendar contains gaps everyone has learned not to notice. A district called Old Vela has no founding record, though its residents still use the name. A veteran remembers a war nobody else can place. These are the scars left by the Ministry of Continuity, which removes dangerous volumes from the Last Library and calls the resulting revision public safety.

The characters work in the pressure between official preservation and active resistance. They may be librarians, street historians, printers, defectors from the Archive Guard, or ordinary people whose lives no longer match the record. Every investigation can become a rescue operation when the Ministry discovers a book worth burning.

**Tone:** Intellectual adventure with real stakes. Knowledge is physical, civic, and contested. The question is not whether history is neutral, but who gets to decide what it is allowed to remember. The setting is serious about libraries as institutions and about the people who work in them, but it is also a thriller: the clock is always running, the censors are always coming, and the books sometimes disagree about what happened.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Last Library rising through the city in tiers, its upper floors visible from every street: bridges connecting reading rooms, vaults with copper doors, and glass-walled catalogue halls where the spines of shelved books glow faintly when their contents are referenced by a living person. The Ministry's removal wagons, painted white, parked outside a ward entrance while censors work inside. The flickering of a book being erased: its pages turning blank one line at a time while the world outside shifts to match.
2. **Sound:** The rustle of the Library at night, thousands of pages settling like a forest in wind. The snap of a Ministry seal being broken on a locked catalogue. The voice of a Margin Press reader in a basement workshop, dictating endangered text to a row of copyists who write as fast as they can. The silence that follows an erasure, which is not the absence of sound but the absence of a sound that should be there, a gap in the city's ambient noise where a name, a street, or a bell used to be.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The weight of a chained book in the deep stacks, iron links cold against the wrist. The dry, papery smell of the oldest levels: vellum, binding glue, and dust that is centuries old. The heat of a Margin Press printing stone, which is warm to the touch because the ink carries a trace of the fact it preserves. The particular chill of a passage between wards, where the Library's structure meets the city's infrastructure, and the air smells like both stone and paper.

## 2. Meridian and the Last Library

**The Stacks** rise through the city in wards, bridges, vaults, and public reading rooms. The oldest levels are below street level, where books are chained to shelves because their facts are too consequential to move casually. The deep stacks are cold, poorly lit, and organized by a cataloguing system that predates the Ministry. Nobody fully understands the system. The books seem to.

**The Catalogue Halls** are the Library's public face: glass-walled rooms where citizens can request access to non-restricted materials. The halls are crowded, well-lit, and monitored by Archive Guard officers who record every request. The request logs are, themselves, a kind of surveillance: they tell the Ministry who is interested in which histories.

**The Ministry of Continuity** occupies a wing of the Library converted to government offices. Its censors are trained historians, which makes them more dangerous than zealots. They can explain precisely why a truth is inconvenient and draft a removal order that cites preservation rather than suppression. The Ministry's director, Chancellor Marek, is a former librarian who believes that some truths cause more harm than their absence.

**The Margin Press Basements** are scattered across Meridian, each one a workshop where illegal printers produce broadsheets containing fragments of endangered history. The largest press is run by a woman named Riva, who coordinates the Free Stacks network and maintains a master list of which books are scheduled for removal. The list is itself a dangerous document.

## 3. Factions

**The Archive Guard** protects the Library's collection and enforces Ministry removal orders. Its captain, a man named Torel, is a career officer who joined the Guard to preserve books and now spends his days supervising their destruction. Some members believe the system can be reformed from within. Others enjoy deciding what is real.

**The Free Stacks** are a network of borrowers, copyists, and librarians who duplicate endangered knowledge before it disappears. Their greatest problem is scale: copying a book may preserve its words, but no one knows how many copies are needed to preserve its reality. A single copy in a basement might not be enough to keep a fact true. A thousand copies in a thousand hands might be.

**The Margin Press** produces cheap broadsheets containing fragments of endangered history. They know that a story repeated in kitchens and workshops can outlast an official catalogue. Their editor, Riva, is fearless, organized, and aware that the press itself has become a target. The Ministry has begun classifying broadsheets as unauthorized historical documents, which makes distributing them a crime.

**The Remembered** are people altered by past erasures. They carry memories of events the world no longer supports: a sister who never existed, a street that now leads somewhere else, a language that no living person speaks. They form support circles, trade evidence of impossible memories, and are divided over whether restoring the past would heal them or erase the lives they built afterward. Their coordinator, a man named Doss, remembers a war that, according to every record in the Library, never happened. He has a scar from a battle that did not take place.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Memory Integrity

Create a **Memory Integrity** clock (6 segments) whenever a historical truth becomes the focus of play. A truth can be a person, an event, a neighborhood, or a promise.

**Marking the clock (+1 each):**
- Censors seize or destroy evidence
- Public belief turns against the truth (propaganda, discrediting witnesses)
- A key witness is rewritten by a related erasure
- The Ministry issues a formal removal order

**Protecting the clock (-1 each):**
- Find a primary source that independently confirms the truth
- Create copies distributed to multiple locations
- Recruit public witnesses who will repeat the truth openly
- Make the truth materially necessary to people (demonstrate that erasing it would damage something the city depends on)

**When the clock fills:** Establish the revised version of events with the table. Characters remember the old truth, but the world no longer supports it. Streets have changed. Names have disappeared. The people affected by the truth may or may not remember what was lost. The characters are the exception: they remember because they were fighting for it when it fell.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Catalogue Diver

You descend into sealed stacks to find records that were never officially removed. You have learned to navigate catalogue systems that rearrange themselves around forbidden subjects. You carry a lamp, a notebook, and a map of the deep stacks that you update after every expedition because the stacks move.

- **Drive:** *"Find the book that proves my father existed before the Ministry decides I am a clerical error too."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### The Guard Defector

You signed removal orders before you understood their consequences. You watched a neighborhood disappear because you approved the destruction of its founding charter. Now you know which histories are scheduled for destruction and which colleagues will come after you. You are leaking information to the Free Stacks. You have not told them your real name.

- **Drive:** *"Save enough people from the system I served that the apology becomes more than a private ritual."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

### The Street Historian

You do not work in the Library. You work in kitchens, workshops, and market squares, collecting oral histories from people who remember things the official record does not contain. Your archive is a box of notebooks under your bed. It is the most dangerous collection in Meridian because it cannot be erased by burning a single book. It can only be erased by finding you.

- **Drive:** *"Keep the memory alive in people, not in pages. The Ministry can burn a book. They cannot burn every person who remembers."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The Missing Founders:** The Ministry announces that Meridian's founding charter is a forgery and schedules a public burning. That night, a woman arrives at the Free Stacks with an impossible claim: she is one of the charter's signatories, and she has not aged in two centuries because the charter has kept her role in history intact. If the charter burns, she will vanish, and so will every institution the charter established, including the Library itself. The characters must determine whether she is genuine, find the original charter before the ceremony, negotiate with a Guard captain who suspects the burning is wrong but fears the consequences of disobedience, and decide what version of Meridian deserves to survive.

**Why It Matters:** The Last Library at Meridian asks whether history is a public resource or a political tool. The Ministry is not burning books at random. It is selecting which truths are safe and which are dangerous, and it is making those decisions on behalf of people who do not get to vote on them. The characters are fighting for the principle that the past belongs to everyone, not just the people with the authority to edit it. Every book they save is a fact the city gets to keep. Every book they lose is a piece of reality someone decided the public did not deserve.
