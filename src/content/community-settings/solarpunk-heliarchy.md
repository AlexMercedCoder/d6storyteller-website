## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The **Heliarchy** is a solarpunk civilization that has existed long enough to feel natural and fragile at the same time. The cities are beautiful-genuinely, achingly beautiful-with living architecture that integrates photovoltaic cells into flowering facades, neighborhood solar councils that function as direct democracies, bioluminescent street lighting grown from engineered organisms, and food forests that double as public parks. The technology feels organic because it has been designed to be: Heliarchy engineers follow an aesthetic philosophy called **Integrated Naturalism**, which holds that technology should extend natural systems rather than replace them.

But the Heliarchy is not a paradise without enemies. The **Ashcrown Compact**-three petro-states that refused the Solar Accords-have spent a century perfecting asymmetric warfare against the Heliarchy's infrastructure. Their recent masterpiece: **Shade Protocol**, a series of atmospheric aerosol deployments that have reduced solar yield in the Heliarchy's northern cities by 60% for an estimated 90-day window. In a civilization that runs on sunlight, this is an act of war. The Heliarchy council is divided between those who want diplomatic resolution and those who want retaliation. The characters navigate this crisis while also dealing with the Ashcrown agents embedded in their communities.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** Terraced building facades covered in flowering solar-mosaic tiles that shift color as their photovoltaic cells charge; public plazas where bioluminescent trees glow gently at night from stored solar energy; sky gardens on residential tower tops visible from street level as explosions of green and gold; the disquieting gray pall of the Shade Protocol sky-a haze that turns golden light silver and makes garden plants droop.
2. **Sound:** The near-silence of Heliarchy cities-no combustion engines, only the hum of electric transit, the sound of wind through vertical gardens, birdsong in the food forests, the conversational noise of a community market; the eerie contrast of Ashcrown agents' diesel-powered tools, which sound like violence to Heliarchy-born residents.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The warmth of a Yield-charged tool in the palm-solar devices run warm and smooth; the green, complex smell of integrated living architecture (not quite garden, not quite building); the clean taste of food from a community forest; the acrid chemical smell of Shade aerosol where it concentrates near the ground, which Heliarchy chemists are only just beginning to identify.

**The Core Tension:** Utopia Under Siege. The Heliarchy works-it is a genuinely better way to organize a civilization, and most residents know it and love it fiercely. But it requires collective good faith to function, and the Ashcrown's embedded agents are systematically targeting that good faith: spreading distrust between solar councils, framing community members as Compact sympathizers, and engineering small scarcities that fracture neighborly relationships. The characters must protect both the infrastructure and the social fabric simultaneously.

## 2. Historical & Cultural Context

The **Solar Accords** were signed 100 years ago by 147 nations after a series of climate cascades that destroyed three coastal metropolitan regions. The Accords established the Heliarchy governance model: solar councils, Integrated Naturalism engineering standards, collective grid ownership, and the Sun Dividend (a guaranteed allocation of solar power to every resident as a social right). The transition took thirty years and was difficult, but by Year 30, quality of life metrics in Heliarchy nations had surpassed all historical records.

The **Ashcrown Compact**-Valdris, Kerenoch, and the Sulphur Reaches-refused the Accords on economic grounds and have sustained petroleum extraction through increasingly extreme methods: deep synthetic drilling, chemical fracturing of bedrock reservoirs, and atmospheric carbon management systems that allow them to deny the visible effects of their emissions. Their populations have lower life expectancy, higher inequality, and deep cultural identity tied to industrial heritage.

**Shade Protocol** represents the Ashcrown's most sophisticated attack. It is not physically destructive-no bombs, no deaths (yet). It is a precise attack on the Heliarchy's power source using the atmosphere as a weapon, one that the Compact can plausibly deny in international forums. The Heliarchy has 90 days to respond before the aerosol dissipates-or escalates.

## 3. Core Factions

### The Solar Councils (Heliarchy Governance)
Direct democratic assemblies at neighborhood, district, and continental scale. Decisions require consensus, which is slow but creates genuine buy-in.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I represent the Verdant Quarter solar council. We've been running at 40% yield for three weeks. My constituents need answers."*
  - *"The Continental Assembly has been in emergency session for six days and hasn't reached consensus on whether to call Shade Protocol an act of war. That's not dysfunction. That's what it looks like when you don't make decisions casually."*

### The Ashcrown Compact's Operatives (Hidden Antagonists)
Embedded agents whose goal is to fracture Heliarchy social cohesion. Not uniformly villainous-some genuinely believe the Heliarchy is fragile utopianism that will eventually collapse anyway and are trying to control the collapse.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I've been living in this community for three years. I have friends here. I genuinely don't know if what I'm doing is right anymore."*

### The Photoneer Corps (Heliarchy Technical Defenders)
Engineers and scientists who maintain the solar grid and respond to infrastructure attacks. The closest thing the Heliarchy has to a military.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I can restore yield to a shaded district in 72 hours with secondary reflection arrays. I can also track the aerosol to its deployment site within 48 hours. Which would you prefer I do first?"*

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Solar Yield Tracking

**Sun Intensity (set by Storyteller per scene):**
- **1:** Night / Full overcast / Shade Protocol maximum
- **2:** Cloudy / Dawn or dusk / Partial shade
- **3:** Bright day / Clear sky / Standard solar
- **4:** Peak solar / Solstice noon / Mirror-amplified light

**Yield Points = Solar Attunement × Sun Intensity**

**Spending Yield Points:**
- **1 YP:** Activate any standard biotech tool for one use.
- **2 YP:** Add +1d6 to any action that involves solar infrastructure, community networks, or living technology.
- **3 YP:** Charge a Sun Burst-a focused discharge of stored solar energy (blinding pulse, kinetic beam, system override).
- **1 YP (refund):** Any action that demonstrably helps other characters generates this refund, encouraging generosity.

**Community Bonus:** If three or more characters pool their Yield Points for a single collective action, the action gains +2d6 regardless of total pooled amount.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### Archetype 1: The Living Architect
- **Concept:** Designs and maintains Heliarchy's integrated living architecture-buildings that are simultaneously solar arrays, food sources, water purifiers, and air processors. Has spent twelve years perfecting a self-healing building material called Solarite that repairs minor solar panel damage using symbiotic mycelium networks.
- **Quote:** *"A building should grow, breathe, and die like anything else. We stopped designing things that way for two hundred years and called it progress. I design things that live."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### Archetype 2: The Council Advocate
- **Concept:** A traveling negotiator who moves between solar councils during crisis periods, facilitating consensus in communities fractured by the Shade Protocol. Has an uncanny ability to identify Ashcrown misinformation before it spreads and a legitimate talent for rebuilding trust that has been deliberately damaged.
- **Quote:** *"The Compact's best weapon isn't the aerosol. It's the story that the aerosol is your neighbor's fault. My job is to make sure that story dies before your neighbor does."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

### Archetype 3: The Photoneer Field Agent
- **Concept:** A grid engineer turned investigator, tracking the Shade aerosol deployment to its source while also running interference against Ashcrown sabotage teams. Carries a Yield-charged multi-tool that functions as scientific instrument, communication device, and last-resort weapon.
- **Quote:** *"They chose the atmosphere because they thought we couldn't fight back in a medium we don't militarize. They forgot that we built the most sophisticated monitoring network in human history because we care about weather. We can find them."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

## 6. Condition Palette

**Physical:**
- **Minor:** *Shade Fatigue* (reduced yield from blocked sun), *Pollen Overwhelm* (living architecture allergic response).
- **Moderate:** *Grid Disconnect* (cut off from community solar network), *Aerosol Exposure* (respiratory effects from Shade Protocol chemical).
- **Severe:** *Full Shade* (extended yield loss, aquipment offline), *Biomaterial Rejection* (living tech interface failure).

**Social:**
- **Minor:** *Council Skepticism* (community doubts your assessment).
- **Moderate:** *Compact Suspicion* (accused of Ashcrown sympathies-devastating in a trust-based community).
- **Severe:** *Council Exile* (removed from community decision-making pending investigation).

**Psychological:**
- **Minor:** *Gray Sky Grief* (the Shade Protocol is viscerally distressing to people born in full sunlight).
- **Moderate:** *Utopia Doubt* (beginning to wonder if the Heliarchy's ideals are survivable under sustained attack).
- **Severe:** *Trust Collapse* (unable to extend good faith to community members after manipulation).

## 7. Starter Scenario: The Shade Council

**Framing:** Three days into the Shade Protocol's fifth week, a Photoneer field station in the mountains has detected what appears to be the aerosol dispersion network-but the detection data points to a location inside the Verdant Quarter, the most celebrated solar community in the Heliarchy. Either the data is wrong (which has never happened), or someone in the Verdant Quarter is hosting an Ashcrown dispersion node. The council cannot search private residences without a consensus vote that will take 72 hours to convene. The aerosol is intensifying. The characters must investigate without violating the community rights that make the Heliarchy worth defending.

**3-Layer Scene Design:**
1. **Immediate Threat:** The dispersion node is disguised as a community composting unit in a residential courtyard. It is on a timer. It will activate within 18 hours regardless of anything else happening.
2. **Ticking Clock:** The Photoneer's detection signature has also been noticed by the Ashcrown operative running the node-who is now methodically destroying any evidence trail while maintaining perfect community persona.
3. **Opportunity:** The Verdant Quarter's annual Solstice Festival begins in 8 hours and every resident's movements and locations will be publicly logged as part of community tradition. Cross-referencing festival attendance with the dispersion node's activation windows could identify the operative without any rights violation-but requires someone to do the analysis while appearing to simply enjoy the festival.

## 8. Why This Setting Matters

Solarpunk as a genre asks the question most science fiction refuses to ask: what would a genuinely good future look like in practice, not as a static endpoint but as a living process that still has problems, still has enemies, and still requires active work to sustain? The Heliarchy setting uses that question as its dramatic engine. The antagonism is not internal corruption of the utopia (a tired trope) but external pressure from a world that has chosen differently-and the genuine challenge of defending values that cannot be defended by abandoning them. Players must protect the Heliarchy using Heliarchy methods: community trust, collective action, transparency. The moment they resort to Ashcrown methods, the setting's central question-can the better civilization survive?-becomes its central tragedy.
