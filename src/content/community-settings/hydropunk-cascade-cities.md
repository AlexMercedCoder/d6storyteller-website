## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** The **Cascade Cities** occupy a world reshaped by one civilizational bet: that water could power everything fossil fuels once did—and could do it forever. Two hundred years after the Great Cessation (the depletion of the last oil reserves), that bet has paid off technologically but extracted a social cost no one foresaw. The **Turbine Guilds**—the engineering dynasties who built the first great dams—have become governments, landlords, and gods simultaneously. Control a river, control a city. Control a city, control a thousand lives.

The world is spectacularly alive with water: canyon megacities where turbines hum constantly beneath the streets, aqueduct highways spanning mountain ranges, floating market platforms on reservoir lakes, underground cistern districts lit entirely by bioluminescent algae cultivated for power generation. It is also a world of acute injustice: the Spray Districts at canyon bottom, perpetually misted and half-flooded, house the majority of the population in cramped efficiency pods; the Crestline Towers above the waterline are walled estates of Guild executives whose every luxury is powered by the labor below.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The perpetual blue glow of hydroelectric conduits tracing lines through canyon walls like luminous veins; the breathtaking spectacle of a Turbine Cathedral—a dam face covered in engineered falls that cascade into turbine chambers in a controlled roar of white foam; the Spray Districts at dusk, every surface glistening, neon signs reflected in standing puddles, the distant hum of the great machines felt in the chest before the ears.
2. **Sound:** The ceaseless, deep-frequency roar of turbines that residents of Cascade Cities cease to consciously hear within six months but which causes immediate unease in visitors; the percussive bang of Guild Enforcement hydro-cannons; the silence of a dam that has been sabotaged—a silence so complete and wrong that canyon-born residents describe it as vertigo.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The permanent damp of Spray District air—clothes never fully dry, wood swells, metal corrodes; the clean, cold taste of reservoir water straight from the mountain; the chemical scent of aqua-tech lubricant that marks Guild technicians as distinctly as a uniform; the strange warmth of a capacitor charge—water energy stored in a backpack unit buzzes faintly against the spine like held breath.

**The Core Tension:** Access vs. Control. Water is the most democratic resource imaginable—it falls from the sky—and simultaneously the most controlled commodity in the Cascade world. The Freecurrent movement insists water energy should be a public right. The Guilds insist civilization depends on managed infrastructure that requires centralized control. Both are partially correct, and the people caught between them are running out of dry ground.

## 2. Historical & Technical Context

The **Great Cessation** occurred 217 years ago when the last viable petroleum reserves were exhausted simultaneously with the global financial infrastructure built on them. The crisis, called the **Dry Collapse** in Guild histories, killed approximately 40% of the global population within a generation. Hydroelectric power was not new, but scaling it to replace all other energy sources required engineering at a previously unimaginable scale. The engineers who made this transition possible organized themselves into **Turbine Guilds** to protect their technical knowledge and negotiate politically for the massive land and water rights needed to build.

Within two generations, the Guilds had become inseparable from government. The **Cascade Alliance**—the loose treaty network of Guild-controlled city-states—manages power distribution across six major river systems. Water law has replaced property law as the foundation of social order. **Current Rights** (the legal entitlement to a measured flow of hydroelectric power) are the primary currency, traded on **Flow Markets** like stock exchanges.

The **Freecurrent Movement** began 30 years ago as a civil rights campaign for minimum guaranteed Current Rights regardless of district status. It has radicalized after three decades of peaceful protest accomplished nothing: the most recent faction, the **Cascade Anarchists**, have begun systematically sabotaging dam control systems with a device called the **Overflow Spike**—an aqua-tech tool that triggers controlled cascade failures in turbine networks.

## 3. Core Factions & Districts

### The Turbine Guilds (The Establishment)
The engineering dynasties who built and now control the dams. Each Guild controls a river system and the cities along it.
- **The Volta Guild:** Controls the largest dam network, the Volta Cascade. The most powerful and most corrupt. Its current Director is a former hydraulic engineer named **Syndra Vol** whose infrastructure expertise is matched only by her political ruthlessness.
- **The Whitecap Engineers:** A breakaway Guild faction that still believes in the original mission of universal access. Internally divided and politically squeezed between the Volta Guild and the Freecurrent radicals.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"Third-generation Guild technician. I know where every valve in this canyon is. Including the ones not on the official maps."*
  - *"I enforce Guild current law. I don't write it. That distinction matters more than you'd think."*

### The Freecurrent Movement
A spectrum of activists, saboteurs, and idealists who believe water energy must be freed from Guild monopoly.
- **Moderate Wing:** Negotiate for reform, organize Spray District labor strikes, document Guild corruption.
- **Cascade Anarchists:** Armed saboteurs. Overflow Spike attacks. Willing to cause localized blackouts to demonstrate Guild fragility.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I've lived in the Spray my whole life. My current ration is 4.2 kWh per day. The Crestline exec above me uses 340. I didn't radicalize. I did math."*
  - *"We're not trying to destroy the dams. We're trying to make the Guilds afraid of what happens if they don't share."*

### The Spray District Communities
The majority population of canyon-bottom residents. Fiercely communal, ingeniously adaptive, and deeply mistrustful of anyone from above the waterline.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"We built our own microturbine from salvage parts. It powers twelve apartments. The Guild calls it illegal current skimming. We call it staying warm."*

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Flow State Dice

Each character has a **Flow Rating** (1-6) representing their attunement to and access to hydroelectric energy.

**Charging the Capacitor:**
- At the start of each scene, roll Flow Rating dice.
- Each **even** result generates 1 **Capacitor Point (CP)**.
- Each **odd** result drains 1 CP (minimum 0).

**Spending Capacitor Points:**
- **1 CP:** Power an aqua-tech device (door lock override, comm jammer, water jet tool) for one scene.
- **2 CP:** Add +1d6 to any action that involves hydroelectric infrastructure, machines, or environmental water.
- **3 CP:** Trigger an Overflow—a dramatic surge of stored energy used for one spectacular action (blow a sealed bulkhead, supercharge a weapon, send a broadcast across the entire district network).
- **5 CP:** Seize momentary control of a local turbine grid (lights out, pressure redirected, gates opened or closed).

**Environmental Flow Modifiers:**
- **Spray District / underwater / near active falls:** +1 Flow Rating for the scene.
- **Arid plateau / rationed zone / Guild lockdown:** -2 Flow Rating for the scene.
- **During a dam failure or Overflow Spike event:** All Flow rolls are Wild (reroll any result once).

## 5. Character Archetypes

### Archetype 1: The Spray District Engineer
- **Concept:** Grew up in Cascade City's bottom district, became self-taught in aqua-tech by reverse-engineering Guild equipment. Survived three current shutoff events that the Guild called "maintenance" and she calls war crimes. She can build a functional microturbine from a broken conduit pipe and a salvaged motor.
- **Quote:** *"You learn to fix machines down here because the Guild won't send a tech to the Spray unless you have a work order, and work orders require Current Credits, and Current Credits require power, and power is the thing that's broken. So you fix it yourself."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Can coax electricity out of a broken dam like water from a wrung cloth"*, *"Spray District born—can navigate canyon infrastructure blindfolded"*, *"Carries a Flow Meter that detects current theft by either side"*, *"Knows which Guild officials live on doctored power bills."*
- **Drive:** *"Build a microgrid that makes the Spray District independent of Guild current—and prove it can scale."*
- **Flaw:** *"I am so focused on the infrastructure problem that I sometimes miss the human cost of my solutions."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 3 | Mind 3 | Presence 1

### Archetype 2: The Guild Defector
- **Concept:** Spent fifteen years as a senior hydraulics engineer for the Volta Guild, designing the very Overflow Spike countermeasures the Cascade Anarchists now defeat with infuriating ease. Left after discovering that the Guild's flagship Crestline Dam had a critical structural flaw that management had covered up for twelve years to avoid a costly repair shutdown.
- **Quote:** *"I designed three dams that power two million people. I am also the reason the Guild is one bad season away from a cascade failure that will destroy the Spray District they've been slowly drowning for fifty years. I am very motivated to fix this."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Master hydraulic engineer with full Guild network access credentials—for now"*, *"Carries the only copy of the Crestline Dam structural assessment that management burned"*, *"Every Guild exec in the Alliance has reason to want me silent"*, *"I know exactly how much it costs to keep a city powered and exactly who is being made to pay it."*
- **Drive:** *"Force the Guild to repair the Crestline Dam and publish the structural report before the next monsoon season. Everything else is secondary."*
- **Flaw:** *"I spent fifteen years helping the system I now oppose. I don't know if that makes me an asset or a liability."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### Archetype 3: The Freecurrent Courier
- **Concept:** A young bike messenger who runs packages through the aqueduct maintenance tunnels between Spray Districts—tunnels that are technically Guild property and technically off-limits to anyone without engineering credentials. She is the informal postal system, intelligence network, and social glue of three canyons' worth of Freecurrent cells.
- **Quote:** *"I don't believe in the Cascade Anarchists' methods. I also deliver their messages, their parts, and their lunch. Technically I'm neutral. In practice I'm how any of this works."*
- **Suggested Descriptors:** *"Knows the maintenance tunnel network of six canyon districts by feel in total darkness"*, *"Guild enforcement can't catch her—she's been running these tunnels since she was eleven"*, *"Carries nothing she can't explain but never gets searched because she smiles too much to be suspicious"*, *"Has memorized the name, face, schedule, and family situation of every Guild checkpoint guard on the Volta run."*
- **Drive:** *"Keep the network alive. Keep people connected. If the canyon falls apart, at least it falls apart knowing its neighbors."*
- **Flaw:** *"I am everybody's friend and nobody's confidant. I know everything and can tell no one."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

## 6. Condition Palette

**Physical Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Current Burn* (mild electrical discharge from malfunctioning aqua-tech), *Spray Lung* (persistent chest congestion from canyon damp), *Capacitor Drain* (exhausted Flow reserves).
- **Moderate:** *Hydraulic Pressure Injury* (caught near a burst conduit), *System Lockout* (Guild access credentials revoked), *Overflow Backlash* (aqua-tech device overloaded).
- **Severe:** *Dam Crush* (structural collapse injury), *Current Death* (full electrical discharge from a Grid Seizure gone wrong), *Flood Zone* (caught in a controlled release event).

**Social Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Spray Suspicion* (outsider in the district), *Guild Watch List* (your credentials are being monitored).
- **Moderate:** *Current Suspended* (your personal flow ration has been cut off), *Freecurrent Association* (known affiliation with the movement).
- **Severe:** *Guild Exile* (expelled from all Cascade Alliance networks—no power, no water credits, no identity), *Cascade Criminal* (military warrant for your arrest for infrastructure terrorism).

**Psychological Conditions:**
- **Minor:** *Turbine Numbness* (the constant roar has begun to disappear from your conscious awareness), *Flow Anxiety* (obsessive monitoring of current reserves).
- **Moderate:** *Class Guilt* (if from the Crestline, reckoning with what your comfort costs), *Current Paranoia* (convinced the Guild is monitoring your power usage to track you—and you're probably right).
- **Severe:** *Dam Fatalism* (resigned belief that the system cannot be changed and your efforts are meaningless), *Overflow Addiction* (using Capacitor Overflow for increasingly minor problems, burning through reserves recklessly).

## 7. Starter Scenario: The Whitecap Gambit

**Framing:** The Whitecap Engineers—the reform Guild faction—have obtained internal documents proving the Volta Guild has been falsifying flow distribution records for six years, siphoning current from Spray District rations to supplement Crestline executive accounts. They want to release these documents publicly but cannot do so without corroboration: the documents require a technical authentication signature from a neutral third party—someone with Guild credentials but no Guild loyalty. The Whitecaps have 24 hours before the Volta Guild's intelligence arm figures out what they have. The characters must authenticate the documents, get them to a Freecurrent media contact, and survive the Volta Guild's very motivated attempts to make everyone involved disappear quietly into the canyon.

**3-Layer Scene Design:**
1. **Immediate Threat:** The authentication must happen at a working Guild technical terminal—which means getting credentials past a Guild checkpoint or finding an unmapped terminal in the maintenance tunnels. The Cascade Anarchists, independently, have also learned about the documents and want them for their own purposes (public release + simultaneous dam sabotage for maximum chaos). They are not enemies, exactly, but their timeline and methods will cause the authentication window to close.
2. **Ticking Clock:** The Volta intelligence arm has 4 Beats before they pinpoint the Whitecap faction member who leaked the documents. At Beat 3, they begin a district-wide current shutdown to flush out the characters. At Beat 5, they send Guild Enforcement—armored aqua-tech units with hydro-cannon and override spikes.
3. **Opportunity:** The Freecurrent media contact has an emergency transmission relay hidden in the base of Dam 7's turbine cathedral—deep in Guild infrastructure, accessible only through an unmapped maintenance tunnel, but with broadcast reach across six canyon networks simultaneously. Getting the authenticated documents to that relay in a single transmission would be unblockable by Guild censors. It would also require someone to physically remain at the relay during the broadcast—in a turbine chamber during an active transmission.

**Antagonist: Director Syndra Vol** — The Volta Guild's current Director. She built her career on optimizing flow distribution systems and is genuinely brilliant. She is also genuinely convinced that centralized Guild control is the only thing preventing a civilizational collapse, and that short-term inequities are acceptable costs for long-term stability. She is not lying to herself. She believes it. This makes her far more dangerous than a simple villain.
- **Traits:** Complete command authority over Volta Guild infrastructure, can trigger district-wide current shutdowns as a tactical tool, has a network of informants in both Freecurrent factions, weakness in that she cannot bring herself to flood the Spray Districts (her one ethical line), will negotiate if convinced the documents' release will be more destabilizing than controlled reform.
- **Condition Track:** ☐ Containment Mode (sends enforcement, assumes this will be handled quietly) / ☐ Information Blackout (shuts down communications across the district) / ☐ Desperate Bargaining (offers genuine reform concessions to contain the damage) / ☐ Controlled Release (publishes a curated version of the documents first, framing the narrative on her terms).

## 8. Why This Setting Matters

Hydroelectric power is humanity's oldest large-scale renewable energy source—the first commercial hydroelectric plant opened in 1882. But every major dam project in history has involved the forced relocation of communities, the flooding of valleys, the redirection of ecosystems. The Cascade Cities extrapolate this real tension to its logical conclusion: a civilization entirely powered by water that has also organized every social injustice around that power. The setting asks whether a renewable energy source can be liberatory if it is monopolized, and whether the engineering expertise required for large-scale infrastructure inevitably creates a technocratic elite. These are not hypothetical questions. They are contemporary policy debates, and putting them in a genre fiction frame makes them approachable, urgent, and immediate.
