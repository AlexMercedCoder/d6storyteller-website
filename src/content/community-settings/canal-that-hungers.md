The first entry reads: 'Given to the water, one barrel of the good salt and three fish, whole. Passage clear.' That is not an unusual entry. The ones that are unusual appear approximately every six months. I am not going to describe what those entries contain."*
> - Dr. Rana Vox, Canal Authority Historian, internal memo

## 1. Setting Overview and Tone

The Velk Canal is a commercial artery. Twenty barges a day move through its locks, carrying grain, coal, manufactured goods, and occasionally passengers who do not know that the quiet stretches of the canal between locks have a particular stillness that experienced canal workers recognize and do not discuss in front of customers.

The Canal Authority employs four hundred people: lock keepers, maintenance crews, towpath workers, and administrative staff. A small subset of these employees know about the offering requirement. A smaller subset know what the offering log's unusual entries actually describe. The authority's official position is that the canal is a feat of engineering that requires routine maintenance. This is true. The maintenance the public knows about is accurate as far as it goes.

**Tone:** Quiet dread administered through bureaucratic procedure. The horror of the Canal is not dramatic. It is the specific horror of an institution that has normalized something monstrous by treating it as a maintenance schedule item. The canal workers who know are not sadists. They are people who have inherited a responsibility they did not choose and who manage it the way they manage everything else: professionally.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The canal at operating hours: a steady procession of barges, the green water reflecting the towpath willows, lock gates operating on schedule, and the lock keepers' neat stations with their maintenance logs. The canal at night: the same structures emptied of traffic, the water darkening, and the specific visual quality of the Hunger-high stretches where the water is too still, even in wind.
2. **Sound:** The canal's operational sounds: barge engines, lock machinery, the calls between towpath workers and barge crews. The sounds that indicate rising Hunger: the absence of the normal water sounds, a low resonance from certain stretches that experienced workers describe as the canal "breathing."
3. **Touch and Smell:** The towpath mud: a specific consistency that experienced workers can read for flood risk. The water's smell: clean in well-managed stretches, and something else in Hunger-high sections, a mineral depth that does not belong to the water table. The lock machinery: brass and iron, cold and precise, the most reliable physical feature of the canal operation.

## 2. The Canal

**The North Lock Complex** handles the highest traffic volume: the junction between the canal and the northern industrial distribution network. Lock Master Bren Soto manages seven locks and a crew of forty. Bren knows about the offering requirement. Bren has been managing it for twelve years.

**The Long Reach** is the twenty-mile central stretch with no locks, where barges travel under their own power or by towpath horse. The Long Reach is where Hunger manifests most visibly: the current changes here first, and the canal surface has qualities that barge captains learn to watch without asking about.

**The Authority Office** in the canal-side town of Velk manages the operational and administrative functions. Director Nora Dale runs the office efficiently and is one of three senior staff with full knowledge of the offering log's complete contents. She is fifty-three, has been with the Authority for twenty-eight years, and has made every required offering during her tenure.

**The Archive Room** in the basement of the Authority Office holds the complete offering log, all three hundred years of it. The room requires a key held by the Director. The log is written in the authority's standard operational format: date, offering description, result, keeper's initials. The unusual entries are indistinguishable in format from the routine ones.

## 3. Factions

**The Canal Authority** manages the canal's operation, including the offering requirement. The Authority's inner circle (Director Dale, Lock Master Soto, and two others) maintain the offering schedule and keep the requirement out of the official record. They consider this responsible management of a situation they inherited. They are also aware that their handling of the requirement has never been reviewed by any outside body.

**The Canal Workers' Union** represents the four hundred employees. Union representative Dak Pell has been investigating a pattern of worker incidents in the Long Reach: small accidents, unexplained equipment failures, and three workers who requested reassignment away from the Reach in the past year and were quietly transferred without explanation. He does not know what he is investigating. He suspects the Authority is concealing something about working conditions.

**The Commercial Users' Association** represents the barge operators and trading companies that depend on the canal. Their primary concern is reliability: the canal must be passable on a predictable schedule. The Association's factor, Mira Fen, tracks operational incidents and has noticed that incident rates follow a six-month pattern. She has filed three inquiries with the Authority. She has received three anodyne responses about routine maintenance cycles.

**The Canal Historian** is Dr. Rana Vox, an academic from the university who was given archive access to write a definitive history of the canal's construction. She has found the offering log. She has read all three hundred years of it. She is currently deciding what to do with the information, a decision complicated by the fact that the Authority Director has not denied its contents and has instead asked Vox, very quietly, what she thinks a reasonable person would do in the Authority's position.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Hunger Clock

Track **Hunger** from 0 to 6. Hunger starts at 2 and rises by 1 each game session without an offering.

**Hunger effects:**
| Hunger | Canal Status |
|--------|-------------|
| 0-1 | Satisfied. The canal is cooperative. Navigation is smooth. Incidents are rare. |
| 2-3 | Neutral. Standard operation. Occasional minor incidents within normal parameters. |
| 4 | Rising. Current irregularities increase. Equipment failures require more maintenance. Towpath workers are uneasy. |
| 5 | Urgent. Navigation requires more care. Barge captains notice the water. Two unexplained incidents per week. |
| 6 | Taking. The canal selects its own offering from passing traffic. The Authority cannot predict what it will be or when. |

**Offerings:** The ritual offering reduces Hunger by 3. An incomplete or poorly executed offering reduces Hunger by 1 and increases it by 1 the following session.

**Discovery risk:** Each offering the characters perform or witness, each investigation of the log, adds to the risk of public knowledge. If the offering requirement becomes publicly known, the Canal Authority faces a crisis that could shut down the canal entirely while the political response unfolds.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Inherited Keeper

You are Lock Master Bren Soto. You learned about the offering requirement from your predecessor, who learned from hers, who learned from the Authority's records. You did not choose this inheritance. You have managed it professionally for twelve years because the alternative, allowing Hunger to reach 6, was demonstrated to you through the historical record, and you will not allow that to happen on your watch. You are also aware that the Union's Dak Pell is getting close to something, and you do not know whether to stop his investigation or help it.

- **Drive:** *"Keep the canal safe through the next offering cycle while determining whether the current arrangement can continue without the kind of external review that might improve it or destroy it."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

### The Investigating Historian

You are Dr. Rana Vox. You have the complete offering log. You have three hundred years of evidence that the Canal Authority has been managing a ritual requirement that it has never disclosed to the public, its users, or its workers. You also have the Director's quiet admission that the requirement is real and the canal's operation depends on it. You are an academic who values transparency. You are also a person who understands what happens if the canal stops operating. You have not yet filed your findings.

- **Drive:** *"Determine what public disclosure would actually achieve and whether there is a responsible version of transparency that doesn't require choosing between the truth and the canal's operation."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### The Union Representative

You are Dak Pell. You do not know about the offering requirement. You know that three workers requested transfers from the Long Reach after incidents that were classified as "routine accidents" and that the Authority declined to discuss in detail. You know that the incident pattern follows a schedule that does not correlate with maintenance cycles or seasonal weather. You are filing worker safety complaints with the Labor Board and getting procedurally blocked at every step. You are getting very close to the wrong kind of answer.

- **Drive:** *"Protect the canal workers from whatever the Authority is concealing, even if protecting them requires confronting information that is considerably worse than a safety violation."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

## 6. Starter Scenario and Why It Matters

**The Missing Week:** A barge crew went dark in the Long Reach four days ago. The barge was found drifting, cargo intact, crew absent. The Authority classified it as a disappearance pending investigation and quietly noted in the offering log that Hunger was at 5 when the crew went missing. The regular offering is due in four days. Director Dale is preparing it. Union representative Pell is filing a missing persons report that will bring the Regional Labor Board to the canal within the week. Historian Vox has told the Director she needs to make a decision about the log. The characters are either the Authority staff managing the crisis, investigators looking for the missing crew, or outside parties who have just been told something true and impossible about a canal that has been running on schedule for three hundred years.

**Why It Matters:** The Canal That Hungers is about institutional inheritance: what do you owe to a system you did not create, when maintaining it requires participating in something you cannot justify to an outsider? The Authority staff are not monsters. They are managers of a maintenance schedule that contains something monstrous, and they have made the calculation that the canal's operation is worth the cost. The setting asks whether that calculation can ever be right, and what it does to the people who make it year after year.
