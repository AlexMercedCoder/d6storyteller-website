> *"In the church I am baptized, confirmed, absolved. In the mountains I am Táltos. These two things do not contradict each other because God made both the church and the mountain. The priest would not agree with this. The spirit of the mountain would not disagree with it. I serve both. I am afraid of neither. This is either wisdom or foolishness and I have not decided which."*
> - Personal account of a Carpathian Táltos, 18th century

## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** Two spiritual worlds occupy the same landscape. The people in between them serve both and answer to both.

Hungary's spiritual history is genuinely layered: the Magyar people arrived in the Carpathian Basin in the 9th century with a Turkic shamanic tradition (the Táltos) and converted to Christianity in the 10th-11th centuries under King Stephen I. The shamanic tradition did not disappear -- it went underground, persisting in the rural highlands and the Carpathian margins in forms that blended with the new faith.

The Táltos were identified by birth signs (extra teeth, extra fingers, sometimes born with a caul), trained in spirit knowledge by older practitioners, and recognized by their communities as necessary even when the church disapproved. They performed spiritual combat in animal form, negotiated with mountain spirits, and dealt with supernatural threats that the church's tools couldn't address.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The Carpathian highlands in their summer and winter extremes: the dense forest, the mountain meadows where the old spirits are most active, the villages with their dual architecture of church spire and older sacred sites just outside the village boundary that everyone knows about and most don't acknowledge.
2. **Sound:** The sound of the Hungarian plains and mountain mix: wind through the passes, the specific music that the Táltos tradition uses to enter trance states, the sound of a community gathering after dark for practices that the priest has not been invited to, and the sound of the spirit world in the old hills, which old people still know how to listen for.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The physical intensity of a Táltos trance (the tradition describes it as physically painful during the transformation period), the smell of the herbs and preparations used in shamanic practice, the mountain cold that the spirits of the Carpathians bring with them when they manifest, and the warmth of a village hearth where old stories are still told in the old forms.

## 2. The Spiritual Landscape

### The Táltos Network
Scattered across the highlands, Táltos practitioners maintain informal relationships, sharing knowledge, warning each other of threats, and gathering at certain times and places for the spiritual combats that the tradition requires. They are not organized. They recognize each other.

### The Ancient Spirits
The pre-Christian Magyar spiritual world is not gone -- it has been renegotiated. The spirits of mountains, rivers, and the earth still exist and still maintain their relationships with the humans who acknowledge them. Some are indifferent to the church's presence. Some are actively opposed to it. Most are pragmatic.

### The Lidérc
The shapeshifting demonic spirits of Hungarian folklore: they attach to specific individuals, masquerade as loved ones, drain life energy, and can take animal forms. They are the most active threat in the setting and the most personal -- a Lidérc attack is always specifically targeted.

### The Church
Present, influential, and aware that something it cannot fully address persists in the communities it serves. Most priests manage this with pragmatic ignorance. Some are actively hostile to the Táltos tradition. A few have developed their own hybrid understanding that would not survive scrutiny from Rome.

## 3. Adventure Seeds

1. **The Lidérc Attack:** A community member is being drained by a Lidérc that has taken the form of their deceased spouse. The person knows, on some level. They don't want to know. Confronting the Lidérc requires the victim's cooperation, which requires them to accept what they've been refusing to accept.

2. **The Spirit Combat Challenge:** A Táltos practitioner has received a formal spirit challenge at a traditional combat site. The challenge is from a spirit they've never encountered. Investigating the spirit's origins reveals it was displaced by recent land changes and is challenging the Táltos who holds responsibility for the land where its original home was.

3. **The Church Investigation:** The bishop has sent an investigator to examine "unusual practices" in the mountain villages. The investigator is more curious than hostile but their investigation threatens Táltos practitioners who need to continue their work. Navigating the investigation while protecting the tradition requires careful diplomacy.

4. **The Missing Táltos:** An elder Táltos has vanished during what should have been a routine spiritual errand. Investigation reveals they entered a spirit combat that went wrong and are trapped between worlds. Getting them back requires entering the spirit world directly, which is dangerous without the elder's guidance.

5. **The Inheritance:** An older Táltos who has no trained successor is dying. They have identified the players as the only candidates available. The initiation process is traditionally voluntary and traditionally involves a series of experiences that the initiate does not choose. Deciding whether to accept the calling, and navigating what accepting it means, is the heart of this arc.
