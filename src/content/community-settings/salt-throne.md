## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

The Keshari have governed the desert for centuries through a system that combines mining, governance, and ancestor worship into a single practice. The salt mines are simultaneously an economic resource, a library, and a temple. The voices preserved in the deepest veins belong to leaders, scholars, and ordinary people whose words crystallized under specific geological conditions that the clans have learned to cultivate but not fully control.

**Tone:** Reverent, familial, and politically sharp. The setting treats the ancestral voices with the same mixture of respect and exasperation that any living family produces. The voices are not oracles. They are people, preserved in crystal, with opinions that were formed in their own time and may not apply to the present. Consulting them is valuable and exhausting, like calling a grandparent who has strong views about how you should live your life.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** The desert at midday: white salt flats shimmering under a sky bleached almost colorless by heat. The mine entrances: arched doorways cut into sandstone cliffs, each one marked with the clan sigil of its keeper. The deep chambers: walls of translucent salt crystal that glow amber when a voice activates, casting moving shadows that follow the rhythm of speech.
2. **Sound:** The voices themselves: clear, human, and unmistakably individual. A grandmother's laugh preserved for two centuries. A war chief's orders, still sharp with urgency. The ambient hum of a salt chamber, which the miners say is the collective murmur of every voice that has not yet been individually accessed. Wind across the desert surface, carrying fine salt dust that tastes like history.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The cool of the deep mines, a relief after surface heat. The texture of salt crystal under fingertips: smooth, slightly warm, and vibrating faintly when a voice is active. The dry, mineral smell of the mines, which is different from surface salt: deeper, older, with an undertone that the clans describe as "the smell of someone remembering."

## 2. The Desert and the Mines

**The Throne Chamber** is the deepest accessible mine, containing the oldest voices. Entry requires the Throne-holder's permission. The voices here date back to the founding of the Keshari confederation, and their counsel has shaped desert policy for centuries. The chamber is cold, quiet, and humbling. Even experienced miners speak softly here.

**The Clan Veins** are mid-depth mines maintained by individual clans. Each clan tends its own ancestral voices, adding new recordings through a crystallization ritual performed at death. The quality of preservation varies: well-maintained veins produce clear, reliable voices. Neglected veins produce garbled fragments or silence.

**The Salt Road** connects the major mines and the trading towns that service them. It is the desert's primary infrastructure: a packed-salt highway maintained by clan labor. Control of Road junctions is a source of political power, and disputes over maintenance obligations are a reliable source of inter-clan tension.

**The Free Veins** are unclaimed salt deposits that contain voices from before the clan system was established. The voices here speak in older dialects, reference unfamiliar places, and occasionally describe events that contradict the official clan histories. The Free Veins are technically open to anyone. In practice, several clans have posted guards.

## 3. Factions

**The Throne-Holder, Keshar Varos**, has governed for twenty years with steady, conventional leadership. He consults the deep voices regularly and has always reported their guidance faithfully. He is now facing a challenge from his own heir, who claims the voices are saying things Varos never mentioned. Varos insists the heir is misinterpreting. The clans are watching carefully.

**The Heir, Lira Keshar**, entered the Throne Chamber for the first time six months ago. The voices told her that the founding confederation was not a voluntary alliance but a conquest, that two of the current clans were originally subjugated peoples, and that the Salt Throne's authority rests on a history the voices themselves describe as incomplete. Lira wants the truth made public. She has not fully considered what the truth would do to the confederation's political stability.

**The Miners' Council** represents the workers who maintain the veins. Their concerns are practical: mine safety, fair wages, and the growing problem of voice degradation in older veins. Council leader Harun Bel has been warning for years that the crystallization process is becoming less reliable. Fewer voices are preserving clearly. If the trend continues, the mines will eventually fall silent, and the clans' entire system of governance will lose its foundation.

**The Memory Brokers** trade in voice access. They operate in the gray market, selling consultations with ancestral voices to people who lack clan connections or who want to consult voices outside their own family line. The brokers argue they are democratizing access. The clans argue they are commodifying the dead. Both are correct.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Ancestral Consultation

Before a major decision, a character may enter a salt chamber and consult the preserved voices.

**Roll Mind:**

| Result | Outcome |
|--------|---------|
| **Best (6,6)** | The voice provides clear, detailed guidance directly relevant to the problem. The character gains +1d6 on their next action related to the guidance. |
| **Good (4-5)** | The voice provides accurate guidance, but it carries a personal cost: a demand ("Restore my name to the clan rolls"), an obligation ("Protect my granddaughter from the Throne-Holder's decision"), or an uncomfortable truth. |
| **Adequate (2-3)** | The voice is fragmented. The character receives partial information that requires interpretation. The Storyteller provides one true detail and one ambiguous one. |
| **Failure (1)** | The voice provides counsel that sounds authoritative but is based on circumstances that no longer apply. The character does not know the guidance is outdated until they act on it. |

**Limitations:** A character can consult one voice per session. Consulting the same voice twice in a row produces diminishing returns (the voice begins repeating itself, as the living do). Consulting a voice from a hostile clan requires either permission or stealth.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Throne Heir

You heard the voices for the first time six months ago. They told you things your father never reported. You believe the truth should be public. You have not yet reckoned with the possibility that truth, released without care, can be as destructive as a lie.

- **Drive:** *"Make the Throne accountable to the full record, not the curated version."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 3

### The Vein Keeper

You maintain a clan's ancestral salt vein. You know every voice in the collection: their names, their stories, their habits. You have noticed that the crystallization process is weakening. Fewer voices are preserving clearly. You are watching the clans' memory dissolve, one generation at a time, and nobody with political power is listening.

- **Drive:** *"Save the voices before the mines go quiet, even if that means telling the clans that their immortality has an expiration date."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

### The Memory Broker

You sell access to voices that the clans want to keep private. You have built a client list that includes scholars, merchants, estranged family members, and political operatives. You justify the work by arguing that the dead belong to everyone. You also profit from it, which complicates the argument.

- **Drive:** *"Keep the voices accessible to people who need them, not just people who inherited the right mine."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The Founding Voice:** Lira Keshar has requested a public consultation with the oldest voice in the Throne Chamber: the voice of the original Salt Throne, who founded the confederation. Varos has refused, citing tradition. The Miners' Council has discovered that the Throne Chamber's deepest vein is degrading, and the founding voice may be lost within the year if the vein is not reinforced. Reinforcement would require opening the chamber to the miners, which would mean the public would hear whatever the founding voice says during the repair. The characters must navigate the succession dispute, decide whether the founding voice should be heard publicly, and confront the possibility that the voice they are trying to save may say things that make the confederation's authority impossible to maintain.

**Why It Matters:** The Salt Throne asks what happens when ancestral authority meets inconvenient truth. The clans govern through tradition, and the tradition is built on a curated history. The voices are real, but they have been selectively consulted, and the selection has always favored the powerful. Lira's discovery threatens the system not because it reveals a conspiracy, but because it reveals that the system was always incomplete. The characters must decide whether preserving the confederation requires preserving its founding story, or whether honest history is worth the political cost.
