## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

**High-Concept Pitch:** Set in 17th-century Panama - the narrow crossroads of the world - *The Golden Road of Guna Yala* weaves pirate history, Cimarrón escapee alliances, and Guna Yala indigenous marine mastery into a swashbuckling setting. As Spanish treasure fleets collect Peruvian silver and gold in Panama City to transport across the *Camino Real* to Portobelo, Guna canoe navigators and Cimarrón freedom fighters control the jungle shortcuts and hidden river passages of the Chagres River.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** Heavy wooden chests brimming with Peruvian silver bars piled on pack mules; the pristine turquoise waters and coconut palms of the 365 islands of Guna Yala; the colorful hand-stitched *mola* textile patterns worn by Guna defenders.
2. **Sound:** The clinking of silver coins in leather saddlebags; the thunder of Spanish flintlock muskets across jungle river bends; the rhythmic splashing of wooden paddles cutting through Chagres River rapids.
3. **Touch & Smell:** Tropical downpours soaking canvas jackets in seconds; the salty sea spray of the Caribbean mixing with woodsmoke; the heavy metallic smell of wet silver and gunpowder.

**The Core Tension:** Liberty vs. Exploitation. The Isthmus is the wealthiest trade choke point on earth. Controlling its passages means freedom for indigenous nations and escaped communities; losing it means total colonial domination.

## 2. Historical & Mythological Context

Panama's history as a global transit hub dates back to the *Camino Real* and Chagres River transport routes used by the Spanish Empire to ship Inca gold and silver to Europe. The Guna (Kuna) nation maintained fierce independence in their island realm of Guna Yala (San Blas), often allying with privateers like Francis Drake and Cimarrón leader Mandinga communities against Spanish forces.

> **Note on Respectful Portrayal:** The Guna Yala nation has one of the most successful records of indigenous self-governance in the Americas. This setting highlights Guna maritime sovereignty and the historic Cimarrón-Indigenous anti-colonial alliance.

## 3. Core Factions & Traditions

### The Sail-Masters of Guna Yala (Saila Keepers)
Maritime navigators who pilot swift wooden dugouts through the reefs of San Blas.
- **Cultural Association:** Experts in reef navigation, mola symbolism, and island defense.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I can navigate the 365 islands of Guna Yala in a storm with eyes closed"*
  - *"My mola shirt encodes the secret sea channels no Spanish chart records"*

### The Cimarrón Trail-Cutters
Escaped African warriors who built autonomous strongholds in the Chagres river basin.
- **Cultural Association:** Masters of jungle ambush, trail traps, and freedom fighting.
- **Descriptor Examples:**
  - *"I know every footstep of the Camino Real between Panama City and Portobelo"*
  - *"Spanish steel shatters when it hits our jungle barricades"*

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Isthmian Flow

Characters track an **Isthmian Flow Meter** (1–6):
- **1–2 (Stagnant):** Land travel is slow. Finesse rolls suffer in mud.
- **3–4 (Navigable):** +1d6 to Finesse and Mind when travelling by water or river shortcuts. Costs 1 Momentum.
- **5–6 (Torrential):** +1d6 to Force and Finesse. Double movement speed, but failure capsizes boats or triggers mudslides.

## 5. Character Archetypes

### Archetype 1: The Guna Island Sail-Master
- **Concept:** A marine navigator who defends the San Blas archipelago.
- **Descriptors:** *Master of the dugout sailing canoe*, *Wears hand-stitched mola protective patterns*, *Respected by Caribbean privateers*.
- **Drive:** *"Keep Guna Yala free from foreign garrisons."*
- **Flaw:** *"Reluctant to travel far inland away from salt water."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 3 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

### Archetype 2: The Cimarrón Scout
- **Concept:** A veteran jungle fighter who raids Spanish mule trains.
- **Descriptors:** *Knows every ambush point on the Camino Real*, *Unbreakable spirit*, *Fluent in Spanish, Patois, and Guna*.
- **Drive:** *"Liberate every captive on the treasure trail."*
- **Flaw:** *"Refuses to negotiate with Spanish officers under any terms."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 3 | Finesse 3 | Mind 1 | Presence 1

### Archetype 3: The Chagres River Pilot
- **Concept:** A smuggler who guides privateer ships through river delta mazes.
- **Descriptors:** *Reads Chagres River currents like a map*, *Friend to privateer captains*, *Knows where gold chests are hidden*.
- **Drive:** *"Secure wealth and independence for our river community."*
- **Flaw:** *"Greedy when silver bars are on the table."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

## 7. Starter Scenario: The Ambush at Cruces

- **Framing:** A massive Spanish silver mule train departs Panama City for Portobelo. The characters organize a joint raid at the Chagres River crossing.
- **3-Layer Scene:**
  1. *Immediate Threat:* Disarming the Spanish guard vanguard on the narrow stone trail.
  2. *Ticking Clock:* Reinforcements arriving from Venta de Cruces in 4 beats.
  3. *Opportunity:* Diverting the silver mules onto a secret Guna river barge.
- **Antagonist:** *Captain Don Gonzalo de la Vega* (Armada Treasure Guard).
  - *Traits:* Heavy breastplate, veteran steel sword master.
  - *Condition Track:* ☐ Guard Unit Ready / ☐ Line Broken / ☐ Captured.

## 8. Why This Setting Matters

Panama's Isthmus is a historical nexus of global commerce, piracy, and human liberation. This setting highlights the crucial role of Indigenous Guna and Cimarrón communities in shaping the destiny of the Americas.
