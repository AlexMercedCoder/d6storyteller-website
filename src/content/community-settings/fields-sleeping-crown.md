> *"I planted wheat in March. By April I was dreaming siege plans for a castle I have never seen. By May I knew the names of every noble who betrayed the queen. By June my neighbor and I were arguing about which of us should lead the assault. We are farmers. We do not assault things. But the dreams are very convincing."*
> - Testimony of Josek Thane, Caldera inquest

## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

Caldera is farming country. Its people grow wheat, tend orchards, raise livestock, and argue about weather, land boundaries, and market prices. They are practical, stubborn, and deeply suspicious of anyone who claims authority they did not earn through labor. The dreams complicate this. Every generation, a portion of the farming population receives the queen's memories, and every generation, some of them act on those memories in ways that range from productive (organizing a community defense) to catastrophic (marching on the provincial capital with pitchforks and a three-century-old battle plan).

**Tone:** Earthy, stubbornly domestic, and increasingly unsettling. The horror of the setting is not the dreams themselves, which are vivid and occasionally beautiful. The horror is watching practical, self-reliant people slowly lose the boundary between their own desires and the ambitions of a dead monarch who never cared about wheat prices.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** Golden fields stretching to a flat horizon. The shimmer of heat over grain at midday, which dreamers say sometimes resolves into images of the queen's court. A farmer standing in a field at night, eyes open, speaking in a formal register they have never used while awake. The subtle change in a dreamer's posture over weeks, standing straighter, gesturing with more authority, looking at neighbors as if assessing them for loyalty.
2. **Sound:** The rustle of wheat in wind, which dreamers hear as whispered court language. The sound of a community meeting where half the speakers are arguing about irrigation and the other half are debating military strategy they learned in their sleep. The creak of a farmhouse at night, when the dreamer beside you begins murmuring orders to soldiers who have been dead for three centuries.
3. **Touch & Smell:** Soil under fingernails: the foundation of Calderan identity and the medium through which the queen transmits her consciousness. The weight of a harvest sheaf, which feels heavier to dreamers, as if the grain carries intention. The smell of baking bread, which is both the most domestic scent in Caldera and a trigger for the queen's strongest memories, because she was eating bread when she died.

## 2. The Farmlands

**The Crown Fields** are the original farmland where the queen scattered her crown. They produce the most vivid dreams and the most reliable harvests. Working the Crown Fields is economically desirable and psychologically risky. Families rotate Crown Field duty, and the rotation schedule is the most contested political document in Caldera.

**Threshold Town** is Caldera's largest settlement: a market town at the edge of the Crown Fields where farmers sell grain, buy supplies, and attend the seasonal inquest, where dreamers report what they have seen. The inquest is part therapy, part intelligence briefing, and part community theater.

**The Waking Barns** are communal sleeping houses built away from the fields. Non-dreamers use them to avoid accidental exposure. Dreamers use them during the worst of the season, when sleeping in the fields produces dreams too intense to manage alone. The barns are staffed by volunteer watchers who wake dreamers before they sleepwalk.

**The Queen's Cairn** is a stone mound at the center of the Crown Fields where the queen died. No grain grows within twenty feet of the cairn. The soil is warm year-round. Dreamers who sleep beside the cairn receive the queen's final memories: the moment of death, the scattering of the crown, and the intention behind it. The intention varies depending on who asks, which suggests the queen is still making decisions.

## 3. Factions

**The Harvest Council** governs Caldera and manages the dream problem with a combination of practical regulation and deliberate understatement. Council elder Brin Thane (Josek's mother) has handled three dream seasons and considers the dreams a manageable nuisance, like locusts. She is wrong about the current season's intensity but right about most things.

**The Crown Dreamers** are farmers who have embraced the queen's memories and believe her unfinished work should be completed. Their leader, a wheat farmer named Dael Corr, can quote the queen's military strategy from memory and has begun organizing a militia. Dael is not power-hungry. He is a farmer who has been dreaming the same woman's desperate plans for six months and can no longer tell whether the urgency is hers or his.

**The Waking Movement** wants the dreams stopped entirely. Its members include non-dreamers frightened by the seasonal changes, former dreamers who lost years to the queen's influence, and healers who treat the psychological damage. Their spokesperson, Dr. Asa Wren, has been studying the Crown Fields' soil and believes the queen's consciousness can be neutralized through a specific chemical treatment. The treatment would also render the Crown Fields permanently infertile.

**The Provincial Authority** is the government of the larger kingdom that contains Caldera. They have historically dismissed the dreams as local superstition. The current season's reports have changed their assessment. The Authority is sending an investigator, and the investigator's mandate includes the possibility of quarantining the Crown Fields, which would destroy Caldera's economy.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Dream Influence

Each farmer-character tracks **Dream Influence** from 0 to 6.

**Rising (+1 each):**
- Sleep in the Crown Fields during planting season
- Act on the queen's tactical or political memories
- Visit a location from the queen's dreams
- Convince another person to follow the queen's guidance

**Thresholds:**
| Influence | Effect |
|-----------|--------|
| 1-2 | Vivid dreams. The character remembers the queen's court clearly. No mechanical effect. |
| 3 | Access to tactical knowledge. +1d6 on strategic, political, or military actions informed by the queen's memory. |
| 4 | The character begins using the queen's vocabulary, gestures, and decision-making patterns without noticing. |
| 5 | Difficulty distinguishing personal goals from the queen's. The Storyteller may suggest actions aligned with the queen's agenda. |
| 6 | Each session, resist with a Presence roll or act entirely on the queen's priorities for one scene. |

**Reducing Influence (-1 each):**
- Sleep in a Waking Barn for a full session
- Take a deliberate action that contradicts the queen's known agenda
- Share the dream's content honestly with someone who is not a dreamer
- Work a non-Crown Field for a full growing cycle

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Reluctant Dreamer

You are a good farmer. You did not ask for a dead queen's war plans. The dreams started in March and they are getting louder. You can feel the queen's anger seeping into your own opinions, and you are not sure where she ends and you begin. You have a family, a mortgage, and a field that needs tending. You also have a detailed knowledge of cavalry tactics that you acquired in your sleep.

- **Drive:** *"Stay myself while figuring out what the queen wants, and decide whether she deserves to get it."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

### The Dream Healer

You treat the psychological damage caused by prolonged dream exposure. Your patients include farmers who cannot distinguish memory from experience, veterans of previous dream seasons who still flinch at bread, and children who inherited fragments of the queen's personality from parents who worked the Crown Fields during pregnancy.

- **Drive:** *"Protect people from a dead woman's ambition without dismissing the possibility that her grievances are legitimate."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### The Provincial Investigator

You were sent by the Authority to assess the situation in Caldera. Your mandate includes quarantine, containment, and, if necessary, destruction of the Crown Fields. You arrived expecting rural superstition. You have been here three days and you have already started dreaming. Your report is due in two weeks.

- **Drive:** *"Write an honest report before the dreams make honesty impossible."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The Queen's Demand:** The dreamers have all received the same dream simultaneously for the first time: the queen is asking for a vote. She wants Caldera to declare independence from the province. The dream includes her reasoning, her proposed governance structure, and the names of three provincial officials she considers enemies. Dael Corr has called a town meeting to discuss the vote. The Harvest Council wants to suppress the meeting. The Waking Movement wants to begin the soil treatment immediately. The Provincial Investigator's report is due in four days. The characters must decide whether the queen's demand is a genuine political vision or the final escalation of a three-century haunting, and they must make that decision while dreaming her arguments every night.

**Why It Matters:** Fields of the Sleeping Crown asks whether the dead have a right to the future. The queen's grievances may be legitimate. Her methods are coercive. The farmers caught in between are not choosing between loyalty and rebellion. They are choosing between their own identity and a borrowed one that comes with tactical advantages and a very compelling case. The setting is about autonomy: the personal kind (can I trust my own thoughts?) and the political kind (who gets to decide what this land becomes?), and the uncomfortable possibility that a dead queen's vision for the future might be better than the one the living are building.
