> *"My grandmother told me never to walk past the end of Lockwood Road after September. I asked why. She said the trees get hungry. I asked what they eat. She changed the subject. I am forty-seven years old and I still do not walk past the end of Lockwood Road after September, and my granddaughter is asking the same questions I asked, and I am changing the subject the same way my grandmother did."*
> - Ruth Callander, Harrow's Bend lifetime resident

--

## 1. Setting Overview & Tone

Harrow's Bend is a small farming community that does everything right. Its fields are productive. Its community events are well-attended. Its school is funded. Its people are healthy, prosperous, and deeply, specifically quiet about the orchard at the end of Lockwood Road, where the trees have bark that is too smooth, roots that are too organized, and fruit that is not fruit.

The offering happens every October. The town council handles the arrangements. The details are not discussed. The result is another year of good harvests, healthy livestock, and the particular quality of life that makes neighboring towns envious and Harrow's Bend residents unlikely to move away. The system has worked for five generations. The new family, the Harpers, did not grow up with the silence, and their daughter Maya has found something in the orchard that the town has been successfully not-seeing for over a century.

**Tone:** Quiet, domestic, and saturated with the specific horror of community complicity. The town is not evil. Its residents are kind, helpful, and genuinely good neighbors. They are also participants in a system that requires them to look away from something once a year, and the cost of looking away has been low enough that most of them have convinced themselves there is nothing to see.

**Sensory Pillars:**
1. **Sight:** Harrow's Bend in autumn: golden fields, red barns, the kind of landscape that appears on postcards. The orchard at the end of Lockwood Road: trees planted in rows that are too regular, trunks that are pale and smooth like bone, branches that curve inward as if sheltering something. The fruit that grows on the trees: pale, translucent, and shaped like nothing in any agricultural catalogue. At night, the fruit glows faintly with a light that comes from inside.
2. **Sound:** The silence around the orchard, which is not natural quiet but the absence of birdsong, insect noise, and wind, as if the air around the trees has been pressed flat. The sound of the town during festival preparation: music, conversation, and the careful omission of any reference to what happens at the orchard. The sound that the trees make when the wind blows through them, which is not rustling but clicking, like teeth chattering.
3. **Touch & Smell:** The soil near the orchard, which is warm regardless of season and smells like turned earth and something sweeter, like overripe fruit and copper. The bark of the trees, which is smooth, dry, and warm to the touch, like skin rather than wood. The fruit itself, which no one in town will touch with bare hands, picked using gloves passed down through families along with the instruction to never bite into one.

## 2. The Town

**Lockwood Road** ends at the orchard gate: a wrought-iron fence that the town maintains without discussion. The gate is unlocked. Nobody locks it because nobody wants to imply that the orchard is a place you need to be locked out of. The road beyond the gate is unpaved and leads into the orchard's interior, which is deeper than its acreage should allow.

**Town Hall** is where the council meets and where the October arrangements are organized. The arrangements are recorded in a ledger that is kept in a locked drawer in the mayor's office. The ledger lists dates, participants, and quantities without specifying what is being arranged. Reading the ledger requires context that the ledger itself does not provide.

**The Harper House** is the new family's residence, located on Lockwood Road, closer to the orchard than any other home. The Harpers moved to Harrow's Bend for the quality of life, the good schools, and the affordable property. They were not told about the orchard. Maya Harper, age fourteen, found a pale fruit on the ground behind their house and brought it inside. It is sitting on the kitchen windowsill. It is still glowing.

**The Root Cellar** beneath the oldest farmhouse on Lockwood Road contains the original agreement: a document written on material that is not paper, in a hand that is not quite human, describing the terms of the offering and the consequences of default. The cellar's current owner, Elias Callander, knows the document exists. He has not read it. He maintains the cellar because his family has maintained it for five generations, and maintaining is different from understanding.

## 3. Factions

**The Town Council** manages the offering through established procedure. Mayor Nora Voss has organized three October ceremonies and considers the arrangement a community obligation, like maintaining the roads or funding the school. She does not enjoy the offering. She considers it necessary, and she considers the distinction between enjoyment and necessity important. She is terrified that the Harpers will force the town to discuss what it has successfully avoided discussing for over a century.

**The Callander Family** has been the orchard's closest neighbor and the offering's primary custodian for five generations. Elias Callander is the current keeper. He maintains the gate, tends the boundary, and performs the October preparation. He does this without enthusiasm and without complaint. He was taught by his mother, who was taught by her mother, and the chain of instruction is longer than anyone's individual memory. Elias knows the practice. He does not know the reason.

**The Harper Family** is new. They do not know the town's arrangement. Dan and Liz Harper are friendly, community-minded, and increasingly confused by the way their neighbors avoid discussing the orchard that is visible from their kitchen window. Maya Harper is fourteen, curious, and in possession of a fruit she found behind her house that her mother thinks is a "weird apple" and that the town would very much prefer she had not picked up.

**The Orchard** is not a faction in the human sense, but it has expectations. The trees respond to the offering with continued productivity: good soil, healthy crops, and the general prosperity that Harrow's Bend enjoys. When the offering is neglected, the orchard responds with withdrawal: soil quality drops, livestock sickens, and the trees' clicking becomes audible from the road. The orchard is patient. It has been patient for longer than the town has existed. Its patience has limits that the original agreement describes in detail, and the detail is why Elias Callander has never read the document in the root cellar.

## 4. Unique Mechanic: Offering Debt

Track the town's **Offering Debt** from 0 to 6, starting at 0 (the October offering is current).

**Debt rises (+1 each):**
- The offering is delayed, reduced, or questioned publicly
- An outsider investigates the orchard or the October ceremony
- The original agreement is read aloud or its contents are shared outside the custodian family
- A fruit is removed from the orchard and kept (the Harper situation has already raised Debt by 1)

**Debt effects:**
| Debt | Consequence |
|------|------------|
| 0 | Prosperity. Harvests are excellent. The town thrives. |
| 1 | Tension. The orchard's clicking is audible on calm nights. Soil near Lockwood Road cools slightly. |
| 2 | Warning. Livestock near the orchard behave erratically. Crops nearest the boundary produce less. |
| 3 | Harvest failure begins. Town-wide crop yields drop. The fruit on the trees glows brighter. |
| 4 | Spread. The orchard's influence extends: new trees appear outside the fence line. Roots surface in neighboring properties. |
| 5 | Demand. The orchard communicates directly through the fruit, the roots, or the original agreement. Its terms are clear and its patience is measured. |
| 6 | Collection. The orchard takes what it is owed. The terms of the original agreement's default clause activate. |

**Reducing Debt (-1 each):**
- Perform the October offering as specified
- Return the removed fruit to the orchard
- Renew the agreement through the original ceremony
- Negotiate new terms (requires reading the original agreement and responding to the orchard's communication)

## 5. Character Archetypes

### The Mayor

You manage a town that runs on a secret. You have organized three October ceremonies. You do not enjoy them. You do not question them. You are a practical woman who inherited a practical arrangement and who measures its value in crop yields, school funding, and the health of the community you are responsible for. The Harpers are your problem because they are the first people in a generation who might force the town to say out loud what it has been doing in silence.

- **Drive:** *"Keep the town safe, the offering current, and the Harpers from asking questions I do not have honest answers for."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 2 | Mind 2 | Presence 2

### The Newcomer's Daughter

You are Maya Harper. You are fourteen. You found a fruit behind your house that glows and is warm and that nobody in town will talk about. You are not frightened. You are curious, which in Harrow's Bend is more dangerous. You have noticed that adults change the subject when you mention the orchard. You have noticed that the trees click. You have noticed that the fruit on your windowsill is growing.

- **Drive:** *"Find out what the orchard is and why nobody will tell me, and decide for myself whether the answer is something to be afraid of."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 1 | Finesse 2 | Mind 3 | Presence 2

### The Reluctant Custodian

You are Elias Callander. Your family has tended the orchard's boundary for five generations. You maintain the gate, prepare the October arrangement, and keep the root cellar locked. You have never read the original agreement. You have never asked why the offering works. You have performed your role because your mother performed it and her mother before her, and the chain is older than your understanding. The Harper girl has picked a fruit, and for the first time in your life, the orchard is responding.

- **Drive:** *"Manage the orchard's response without reading the agreement, or read the agreement and learn what my family has been custodians of."*
- **Approach Distribution:** Force 2 | Finesse 2 | Mind 1 | Presence 3

## 6. Starter Scenario & Why It Matters

**The Growing Fruit:** The fruit on Maya's windowsill has doubled in size in three days. It is warm, luminescent, and producing a sound that Maya describes as breathing. The orchard's clicking has become audible from the Harper house at all hours. Elias Callander has found new roots surfacing in his yard. Mayor Voss has called an emergency council meeting, but she cannot explain the emergency without explaining the orchard, and explaining the orchard to a council that includes Dan Harper (newly elected to the planning committee) would break the silence that has held for over a century. Granny Callander (Elias's mother, age 89) has told Elias to read the agreement. Elias does not want to. The characters must return the fruit, manage the orchard's rising debt, and decide whether the town's silence can hold, whether it should, and what the community becomes when the thing it has been looking away from finally demands to be seen.

**Why It Matters:** The Orchard of Teeth asks what people will agree not to see in exchange for a comfortable life. The town is not trapped. It is complicit. The offering is a choice renewed every October by people who have decided that prosperity is worth the cost and that not discussing the cost is part of the arrangement. The setting is about community secrets, generational complicity, and the moment when a fourteen-year-old's simple question, "What is this?", threatens to unravel an agreement that adults have maintained through silence for longer than anyone can remember.
