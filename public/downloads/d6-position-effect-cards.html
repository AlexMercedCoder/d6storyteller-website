Position & Effect Cards

Print these cards, cut them out, and use them at the table. When a player declares an action, place the appropriate Position and Effect card face-up so everyone can see the stakes before the dice hit the table.

Tip: Print on cardstock for durability. Use Ctrl+P → Landscape → Save as PDF.

Position
🛡️
Controlled

You have the upper hand. The situation is manageable. Even if things go wrong, the fallout is minor.

On Failure
Minor setback or complication. You lose your advantage or the situation shifts, but nothing catastrophic happens.
When to Use
The character has leverage, preparation, surprise, or safety. The worst realistic outcome is inconvenience.

Position
⚠️
Risky

The default. Things could go either way. Failure here carries real consequences.

On Failure
Meaningful consequence: harm, lost resources, escalation, or an opportunity that closes. The situation gets harder.
When to Use
Standard action under pressure. No special advantage or disadvantage. Most rolls in play are Risky.

Position
🔥
Desperate

You are cornered, outmatched, or out of options. This is your last shot. Failure is devastating.

On Failure
Severe harm, major loss, capture, or the worst-case scenario you were afraid of. The fiction escalates hard.
When to Use
Outnumbered, unarmed, trapped, acting against overwhelming odds, or trying something with no safety net.

Effect
⚡
Great

On success, you accomplish more than expected. Faster, bigger, and with lasting impact.

What It Means
The action achieves extra: you disarm and intimidate, you heal fully instead of partially, you bypass the entire obstacle. Multiple 6s or exceptional fictional advantage often earn Great Effect.
Example
Picking a lock while the guards are away and the lock is a simple model you've cracked before.

Effect
✅
Standard

On success, you accomplish what you set out to do. No more, no less.

What It Means
The expected result for the action. You achieve your goal as described. A single 6 under normal circumstances typically produces Standard Effect. This is the default.
Example
Persuading a cautious merchant to share information. He tells you what you asked for, nothing extra.
D6 Storyteller
Effect
⏳
Limited

On success, you make progress, but it's partial or constrained. More work is needed.

What It Means
Success is real but reduced. You weaken the guard instead of knocking him out. You open the door a crack, not all the way. The fiction constrains how much one action can achieve.
Example
Trying to break down a reinforced steel door with your bare hands. Even on a 6, you dent it. You'll need another approach.
